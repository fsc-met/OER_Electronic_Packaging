## Suggested Content for Figure 4.2.1

### Figure title for authoring/reference only

`Representative PCB Assembly Factory Flow`

### Instructional purpose

Provide the whole-factory visual anchor for Section 4.2.

The figure should help a reader who has never worked in an electronics factory understand how a representative PCB assembly operation is organized before later sections zoom into individual machines.

The figure should show three ideas together:

1. a **conveyorized SMT line** as the central physical production path;
2. **material preparation / kitting** feeding the line rather than being part of the conveyor itself;
3. **product-dependent branches and offline/support areas** such as X-ray, THT/secondary assembly, rework, and downstream test.

The figure should reinforce the section's main idea that a PCB assembly factory is a coordinated **flow system**, not simply a row of machines.

### Central teaching message

> **A representative PCB assembly factory combines a core SMT flow with material support, board handling, product-dependent branches, and downstream verification. The exact route depends on the product and factory.**

The figure should also make clear:

> **Printer -> SPI -> placement -> reflow is the core SMT process sequence shown here; X-ray, THT/secondary operations, rework, and downstream test are not universal inline stations for every product.**

### Overall layout

Use a **factory/process-map composition**, not a photorealistic factory scene.

Recommended organization:

#### Central horizontal band — conveyorized SMT line

Show a left-to-right product path:

`Loader / board input -> Solder-paste printer -> SPI -> Pick-and-place -> Reflow oven -> Post-reflow AOI / optical inspection -> Unloader / buffer`

A simple conveyor line should visually connect these stations.

#### Upper-left support area — material preparation / kitting

Show a separate support zone labeled:

`Material receiving / storage / kitting`

From this support zone, use concise feeder arrows to show:

- `Solder paste / stencil / tooling` supplied to the printing area;
- `Components / feeders / trays` supplied to the placement area;
- `Verified PCB / panel` supplied to the loader.

Do not turn this into the detailed Section 4.4 kitting workflow.

#### Product-dependent inspection branch

Near the post-reflow inspection / unloader side of the line, show a separate branch:

`X-ray / AXI when required`

Use a dashed product-dependent branch rather than placing X-ray directly on the solid inline path. It should be visually obvious that X-ray is **conditional**, not a mandatory station for every board.

#### Product-dependent THT / secondary branch

After the SMT path, show a dashed branch or downstream work-cell area labeled:

`THT / secondary assembly as required`

Within this area, use compact sublabels such as:

- `THT insertion`
- `Wave OR selective OR hand soldering`
- `Press-fit / odd-form / depanelization awareness`

Do not draw wave, selective, and hand soldering in series. They are alternative or product-dependent methods.

#### Downstream verification area

Show a separate downstream block labeled:

`Downstream quality / electrical / functional test`

This area should be visually downstream of the assembly process but should not be drawn as part of every inline SMT machine sequence.

#### Rework / repair support area

Show one small separate support cell labeled:

`Rework / repair area — when authorized`

Connect it only from an inspection/test evidence area using a restrained dashed relationship. Do **not** draw a return arrow back into normal production unless the future figure-generation QA establishes a specific, non-misleading need for it.

Do not show rework as a normal required step in every board's main flow.

### Required technical relationships

- The **bare PCB or manufacturing panel** enters the SMT line through the loader.
- The **solder-paste printer must precede SPI**.
- **SPI must precede component placement** in the representative sequence because it measures the printed paste deposit.
- **Pick-and-place must precede reflow** in the representative SMT sequence.
- **Reflow must precede the shown post-reflow AOI/optical inspection**.
- The figure may show AOI after reflow as the representative line arrangement used in Section 4.2, but it must not imply that AOI can only exist at that location.
- X-ray/AXI must be shown as **product-/inspection-plan-dependent**, not mandatory for every assembly.
- Material receiving/kitting must feed the production line but remain physically distinct from the conveyorized SMT path.
- The verified PCB/panel must feed the loader.
- Solder paste/stencil/tooling must feed the printing process.
- Components/feeders/trays must feed the placement process.
- THT/secondary operations must be shown as a **product-dependent route after or around the main SMT path**, not as mandatory operations for every product.
- Wave, selective, and hand soldering must **not** be shown as a required serial sequence.
- Rework/repair must be shown as a **conditional support activity**, not a standard step that every board must pass through.
- Downstream electrical/functional test must remain a separate verification activity; it must not be drawn as if it physically forms solder joints.
- The figure must not imply that X-ray, AOI, electrical test, or functional test are interchangeable.
- The figure must not imply that every optional/offline branch reconverges in one universal order.
- The figure must not imply that the exact factory layout shown is universal.

### Flow-line / arrow semantics

Use visually distinct path styles:

- **solid navy arrows/line** = representative main product flow through the SMT line;
- **dashed blue-gray arrows** = product-dependent or offline branch (X-ray, THT/secondary, rework/test routing as appropriate);
- **thin muted blue arrows** = material supply into a process station.

Include a small unobtrusive legend only if necessary for clarity.

Do not use red for normal product flow.

### Spatial / geometry requirements

- Keep the conveyorized SMT line as the dominant horizontal visual element.
- Arrange the seven inline stations in one left-to-right row with consistent baselines.
- Keep station widths proportional enough that the reflow oven reads as longer than the other machines without dominating the entire canvas.
- Place `Material receiving / storage / kitting` above or upper-left of the SMT line.
- Material-supply arrows should terminate at the correct station:
  - PCB/panel -> loader;
  - paste/stencil/tooling -> printer;
  - components/feeders/trays -> placement.
- Avoid crossing material-supply arrows.
- Place X-ray/AXI near the inspection side of the line, preferably below or above the AOI/unloader region, with a dashed conditional branch.
- Place the THT/secondary area after the main SMT line and visually separate it from the inline conveyor.
- Place downstream test after assembly operations, with enough spacing to make it a later verification area. Do not force every optional branch to reconverge into one universal sequence.
- Place rework/repair off to the side of inspection/test, not on the main solid-arrow path.
- Keep all arrowheads clear of text, box borders, and machine silhouettes.
- Use safe outer margins of at least approximately 4–5% of canvas width/height.
- Do not use a floor-plan perspective that forces tiny labels or decorative building geometry.
- Keep all text horizontal.
- Avoid more than two lines of text in any station label where possible.
