## Suggested Content for Figure 4.1.1

### Figure title for authoring/reference only

`Manufacturing Handoff from Released Design to PCBA`

### Instructional purpose

Show the manufacturing handoff that opens Chapter 4.

The figure should make clear that a released design does not directly become a PCBA. Controlled manufacturing information, verified physical inputs, production resources, and process controls must converge at the **PCB assembly process** to create the **PCBA product**.

The figure should also preserve the manufacturing boundaries established earlier in the book:

- **PCB fabrication** is upstream and produces the bare PCB;
- **PCB assembly** is the board-level manufacturing process;
- **PCBA** is the assembled board produced by that process;
- **system final assembly** is downstream and integrates the PCBA into the finished product/system.

### Central teaching message

> **PCB assembly is the manufacturing process; PCBA is the assembled product.**

and

> **A controlled manufacturing handoff combines released information, verified physical inputs, production resources, and process controls before the intended PCBA can be built repeatably.**

- Authoring canvas: approximately `1800 x 1000 px`.

### Overall layout

Use a **left-to-right manufacturing-handoff diagram** with one strong central convergence.

Recommended structure:

1. **Upstream boundary**
   - `PCB fabrication`
   - arrow to `Verified bare PCB`

2. **Manufacturing-handoff inputs/resources**
   - `Released manufacturing information`
   - `Verified bare PCB`
   - `Components + joining materials`
   - `Equipment + tooling`
   - `People + process controls`

3. **Central process**
   - `PCB assembly`
   - smaller qualifier: `Manufacturing process`

4. **Immediate output**
   - `PCBA`
   - smaller qualifier: `Assembled board product`
   - optional very small note: `Downstream inspection/test status not implied`

5. **Downstream boundary**
   - arrow from `PCBA` to `System final assembly`
   - optional small generic product/system icon after that stage

The figure should visually emphasize the transformation:

`controlled inputs -> PCB assembly -> PCBA`

while keeping `PCB fabrication` and `System final assembly` visibly outside the PCB-assembly process.

### Required technical relationships

- `PCB fabrication` must produce the **bare PCB**, not the PCBA.
- The **verified bare PCB** must enter PCB assembly as one of the physical production inputs.
- Released manufacturing information must enter or control PCB assembly; it must not be drawn as if it physically becomes the board.
- Components/materials and equipment/tooling must remain distinct input categories.
- `People + process controls` should influence the PCB-assembly process rather than being shown as part of the PCBA product.
- The figure must not imply that the label `PCBA` means the assembly has already passed all required inspection, electrical test, or functional test.
- `PCB assembly` must be shown as the **process**.
- `PCBA` must be shown as the **product/output** of that process.
- The PCBA must remain downstream of PCB assembly and upstream of system final assembly.
- `System final assembly` must not be drawn as part of PCB assembly.
- The figure must not imply that a released CAD/layout file alone is sufficient to start correct production.
- The figure must not imply that every PCBA follows the same detailed SMT/THT route.
- The figure must not imply that every board-level connection is soldered; other product-specific attachment/interconnection methods may exist.

### Spatial / geometry requirements

- Use a clear left-to-right reading direction.
- Place `PCB assembly` near the center and make it the convergence point.
- Arrange the manufacturing-handoff inputs/resources so their arrows enter the central process cleanly without crossings.
- Use no more than one short secondary sublabel inside each input card at the 900 px design size; detailed document/process examples belong in later sections.
- Keep `PCB fabrication` visually upstream of `Verified bare PCB`.
- Keep `PCBA` immediately to the right of `PCB assembly`.
- Keep `System final assembly` farther right and visually secondary.
- The bare PCB and PCBA should be comparable in general board shape/scale so the student can immediately see the state change from unpopulated to populated board.
- Do not draw a literal one-to-one transformation of every component/file into the PCBA; the diagram is conceptual.
- Avoid a crowded radial layout. A structured horizontal flow with grouped input cards is preferred.
- Maintain generous whitespace and safe margins around arrows and labels.
- No arrow should terminate ambiguously between two objects.
- Avoid crossed arrows.
- Reserve enough lower space so the external mdBook caption does not visually collide with the artwork.

### Color / style guidance

- Clean engineering-textbook appearance consistent with the current EPAC visual baseline.
- Explicit white background.
- Flat 2D or restrained isometric engineering illustration.
- Restrained palette:
  - bare PCB / PCBA: muted green or blue-green;
  - copper/pads where visible: muted copper/gold;
  - controlled information/documents: muted blue;
  - equipment/tooling: neutral gray;
  - people/process-control elements: muted blue-gray;
  - primary process arrows: navy or dark neutral;
  - downstream system context: neutral gray.
- Use labels and geometry, not color alone, to distinguish categories.
- Sans-serif labels sized for comfortable reading at 900 px.
- Moderate, consistent line weights.

- Do not embed the external figure number, public caption, or a large duplicate overall title inside the artwork.

### Scope boundaries

The figure should **not** teach:

- the detailed factory line or station order from Section 4.2;
- the full NPI/job-package document set from Section 4.3;
- kitting/barcode/lot-control workflow from Section 4.4;
- ESD/MSL controls from Section 4.5;
- detailed SMT/THT process selection from Section 4.6;
- soldering physics;
- stencil printing, placement, reflow, wave, selective, or hand-solder process detail;
- AOI/X-ray/test systems;
- SPC, MRB, root cause, or corrective-action systems from Chapter 5;
- system-level mechanical/thermal integration detail.

Do not add specific process settings, temperatures, speeds, tolerances, yields, or acceptance limits.

A separate SMT-versus-THT comparison is intentionally not included because that physical recognition concept is already established earlier in the book and receives process-specific treatment later in Chapter 4.

### Generation guidance

A **controlled vector/SVG-first workflow is preferred** because the figure depends on exact labels, process/product distinction, grouped inputs, and unambiguous arrow geometry.

Recommended workflow:

1. Build the first concept from this approved specification.
2. Use generic original vector objects for documents, board states, components, tooling, and process blocks.
3. Keep the bare PCB and PCBA visually related so the manufacturing state change is obvious.
4. Verify the exact text of every label.
5. Verify every arrow origin and endpoint.
6. If AI image generation is used for an early visual concept, preserve that output only as a concept revision unless all labels and geometry can be controlled reliably.
7. Once a strong visual baseline is established, freeze:
   - layout;
   - aspect ratio;
   - palette;
   - typography;
   - major object positions;
   - illustration style.
8. Make targeted corrections only; do not redesign correct portions unless the instructional concept is wrong.
9. Preserve every meaningful PNG/SVG revision under a different filename.
10. Do not create/copy the canonical publication filename until explicit project-owner acceptance.
