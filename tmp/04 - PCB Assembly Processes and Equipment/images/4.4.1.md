## Suggested Content for Figure 4.4.1

### Figure title for authoring/reference only

`From Approved Part Requirement to Correct Feeder Loading`

### Instructional purpose

Show the **wrong-part-prevention identity chain** that Section 4.4 introduces.

The figure should help a reader see that correct line-side loading requires agreement among several controlled identities:

1. the **approved job requirement**;
2. the **physical component container**;
3. the **verification/scanning step**;
4. the **assigned feeder and feeder location**;
5. the **active placement-machine job/program**.

The figure should also show that a mismatch at any point is a **stop/hold condition**, not something an operator resolves by guessing, visual similarity, or an undocumented substitution.

### Central teaching message

> **Wrong-part prevention depends on preserving the identity chain from the approved job requirement to the physical component container, feeder assignment, and active placement job.**

A second message should remain visually clear:

> **Scanning verifies identity against controlled data; it does not create the approval.**

`4.4.1 - From Approved Part Requirement to Correct Feeder Loading.png`

### Overall layout

Use a **left-to-right controlled identity-chain diagram** with one main verification path and one clearly separated mismatch branch.

Recommended main sequence:

`Approved job requirement`
→
`Physical component container`
→
`Scan / verify identity`
→
`Assigned feeder + slot/location`
→
**compare with** `Active placement job / feeder map`
→
`Correct loading authorized`

A separate lower branch should show:

`Mismatch at any check`
→
`STOP / HOLD — resolve identity or authorization before loading`

The figure should not look like a detailed pick-and-place machine diagram. Its unique role is material-identity control.

### Required objects and labels

#### Approved job requirement

Required label:

- `Approved job requirement`

Required representative information:

- `Internal part number`
- `Approved MPN / alternate`
- `Product / revision`

Use a simple controlled BOM/job-record symbol.

Do not imply that an operator creates or approves alternates at the line.

#### Physical component container

Show one generic **reel**, **tray**, or **tube/stick** as the main container example. A reel is preferred because feeder loading is the final step shown.

Required label:

- `Physical component container`

Required representative label fields on or beside the container:

- `MPN`
- `Internal PN` where available/used by the factory
- `Lot / batch`
- `Quantity`
- `Barcode / 2D code`

Optional small status cue:

- `MSL / use status where applicable`

Do not create a vendor-specific label layout.

#### Verification/scanning step

Required label:

- `Scan / verify identity`

Required comparison cues:

- `Job requirement`
- `Container identity`
- `Feeder identity / location`

The scanner/checking symbol should be generic and should not resemble a proprietary handheld scanner model or software UI. The visual should make clear that the scan/check result is evaluated against both controlled part identity and the active feeder/job assignment.

#### Assigned feeder and location

Required labels:

- `Assigned feeder`
- `Feeder ID`
- `Slot / location`

Use a generic tape feeder or feeder-cart representation.

A representative location such as `Slot 12` may be used only if it is clearly marked as an example and does not imply a universal slot numbering scheme.

#### Active placement job / feeder map

Required labels:

- `Active placement job`
- `Expected part-to-feeder map`

Use a generic placement-machine/job-program symbol.

The figure should make clear that the machine/job expects a particular controlled part identity at a particular feeder assignment. The active job/program is therefore a **comparison reference**, not merely the last physical step in the chain.

#### Correct loading authorization

Required label:

- `Correct loading authorized`

Optional supporting label:

- `Part + feeder + active job agree`

Use a restrained green check/enable symbol.

Do not label this as product acceptance or quality approval.

#### Mismatch / hold branch

Required labels:

- `Mismatch at any check`
- `STOP / HOLD`
- `Resolve identity or authorization before loading`

Use a restrained amber/red-orange stop symbol or hold box.

Do not show an operator choosing a substitute or bypassing the mismatch.

### Required technical relationships

- The **approved job requirement** is the controlling source for what component is allowed for the active product/revision.
- The physical reel/tray/tube must be positively identified and matched against the controlled requirement.
- A visually similar component must not be treated as approved merely because package/value markings appear similar.
- Barcode/2D scanning must be shown as an **identity-verification method**, not the source of engineering approval.
- The assigned feeder identity/location must be verified in addition to the component-container identity.
- The active placement job/program must expect the same part-to-feeder relationship.
- A correct reel in the wrong feeder location must be shown as a mismatch condition.
- A feeder/location that is correct for a different job/revision must still be shown as a mismatch for the active job.
- `Correct loading authorized` should occur only after the relevant identities agree **for the active job/revision and feeder assignment**.
- Any mismatch in part identity, approval status, feeder identity/location, or active-job mapping should route to `STOP / HOLD`, not directly to loading.
- Alternate/substitute approval must exist **before** line-side verification; the figure must not imply that scanning or an operator can create an alternate approval.
- Lot/date-code/MSL/use-status fields may be shown as identity/status information, but the figure must not turn them into universal accept/reject thresholds.
- The figure must not imply that every factory uses barcode automation; the verification logic is the same whether implemented by scanner, software interlock, controlled manual check, or a combination.

### Arrow / status semantics

Use visually distinct relationships:

- **solid navy arrows** = intended controlled identity chain;
- **thin muted blue comparison arrows/lines** = data checked against the active job;
- **amber/red-orange branch** = mismatch/hold condition;
- **muted green check** = loading authorization after successful verification.

Do not use a green arrow to imply the component has passed incoming quality or all manufacturing acceptance requirements.

### Spatial / geometry requirements

- Maintain a clear left-to-right reading direction.
- Keep the five main identity-chain stages on one horizontal visual baseline where possible.
- Place the mismatch/hold branch below the scan/verification, feeder, and active-job comparison region so it is clearly a conditional path.
- Do not route the mismatch branch back into the main chain without an explicit `resolve / reverify` concept.
- Avoid crossed arrows.
- Use enough space around the feeder and active-job blocks so labels remain readable at `900 px`.
- Keep the container label large enough to distinguish `MPN`, `lot`, and `barcode` without turning the figure into a realistic supplier label.
- Keep all text horizontal.
- Preserve safe outer margins of approximately 4–5% of the canvas.
- No arrowhead should touch a box border or label.
- The approved job requirement should visually remain upstream of scanning and feeder loading.

### Generation guidance

A **controlled SVG/vector-first workflow is strongly preferred** because this figure depends on exact identity relationships and mismatch logic.

Recommended workflow:

2. Confirm the job requirement is clearly upstream and controlling.
3. Add the physical container and simplified label.
4. Add scan/verify comparison logic.
5. Add feeder identity/location and active-job expectation.
6. Add successful-loading authorization only after all required identities agree.
7. Add the mismatch/hold branch below the main chain.
8. Verify that a wrong feeder location creates a mismatch even when the reel itself is correct.
9. Verify that scanning does not visually create part approval.
