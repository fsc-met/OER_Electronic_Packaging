## Suggested Content for Figure 2.7.1

**Figure title for authoring/reference only:**  
`Solder Mask and Surface Finish Protect Different Copper Areas`

**Instructional purpose:**  
Make the Section 2.7.1 distinction physically obvious:

> **Solder mask protects copper that should remain covered. Surface finish protects copper that must remain exposed and usable.**

The figure should prevent readers from treating solder mask and surface finish as two names for the same protective coating.

**Filename:**  
`2.7.1 - Solder Mask and Surface Finish Protect Different Copper Areas.png`

**Intended public width:** 900 px

**Recommended aspect ratio:** approximately 16:9

# Overall Layout

Use an original **two-part engineering diagram** on a white or very light background.

Recommended composition:

- **Left:** simplified top view of a PCB region;
- **Right:** enlarged cross-section through one masked trace and one exposed finished pad.

The top view establishes where mask openings occur.  
The cross-section establishes which material actually touches the copper.

# Left Side - Top View

Show a small, generic PCB region with:

- one or two copper traces beneath solder mask;
- one exposed component pad;
- a clearly visible solder-mask opening around the pad;
- optionally one exposed test pad if space allows.

Required labels:

- `Solder mask`
- `Exposed pad`
- `Mask opening`

Optional:

- `Copper trace under solder mask`

The exposed pad should not appear to be bare copper. Its exposed area should have a visually distinct thin surface-finish layer/appearance.

Do not show:

- populated components;
- solder paste;
- solder joints;

- unrelated vias or routing detail.

# Right Side - Cross-Section

Show two adjacent simplified copper features on the same PCB substrate.

### Covered trace

Required stack from bottom to top:

- dielectric substrate;
- copper trace;
- solder mask covering the copper.

Required label:

- `Copper protected by solder mask`

### Exposed pad

Required stack from bottom to top:

- dielectric substrate;
- copper pad;
- thin surface finish over the pad;
- no solder mask over the active exposed pad area.

Required labels:

- `Copper pad`
- `Surface finish`
- `Solder-mask opening`

The mask may overlap or approach the pad edge depending on the generic drawing, but the finish must clearly appear on the intentionally exposed conductive surface.

---

# Required Technical Relationships

The final figure must correctly communicate:

- solder mask and surface finish are different materials;
- solder mask normally covers most outer-layer copper;
- solder mask intentionally leaves openings where conductive surfaces must remain accessible;
- surface finish is applied to the exposed copper area;
- the finish may be metallic or organic depending on finish type;
- the surface finish prepares/protects the exposed surface for soldering, contact, testing, or another downstream use;
- the drawing is generic and must not imply one universal solder-mask-defined or copper-defined pad geometry.

# Technical Verification Basis

## Primary curriculum framing

Use the Chapter 2 instructional material for the core relationship:

- solder mask protects most outer copper;
- surface finish is applied near the end of bare-board fabrication;
- finish protects exposed copper pads;
- finish preserves usability/solderability for later assembly.

## Current OER context

Use current Section 2.7.1:

- solder mask and surface finish are not the same;
- mask covers copper intended to remain insulated/protected;
- finish covers copper intentionally exposed for later use.
