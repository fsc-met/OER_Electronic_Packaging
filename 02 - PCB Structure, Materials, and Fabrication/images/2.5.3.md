## Suggested Content for Figure 2.5.3

**Figure title for authoring/reference only:**  
`Annular-Ring Geometry and Manufacturing Variation`

**Instructional purpose:**  
Provide one clear engineering-geometry figure that supports Section 2.5.5 by showing both the **nominal annular-ring geometry** and why the actual ring may become nonuniform when the hole and copper feature are not perfectly aligned after fabrication.

The figure should reinforce the Section 2.5.5 relationship:

$$
a = \frac{D_p-D_h}{2}
$$

where:

- $D_p$ = pad / land diameter;
- $D_h$ = finished hole diameter;
- $a$ = nominal radial annular-ring width for the ideal centered-hole case.

The figure should answer:

> **How is nominal annular-ring width defined, and what happens visually when manufacturing variation shifts the hole relative to the copper pad?**

The central teaching point is:

> **The annular ring is the copper surrounding the finished plated hole. A centered hole gives a uniform nominal ring, while drill or registration variation can reduce the ring on one side.**

# Required Technical Relationships

The final figure must correctly communicate all of the following:

- the annular ring is the copper surrounding a finished plated hole on a conductive layer;
- the hole opening and the annular ring are different physical features;
- $D_p$ refers to the pad / land diameter;

- for the ideal centered circular geometry, $a=(D_p-D_h)/2$;
- the nominal annular ring is uniform only in the ideal centered case;

- the figure is conceptual and does not establish an acceptance criterion or minimum design rule;
