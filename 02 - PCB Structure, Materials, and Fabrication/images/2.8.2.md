## Suggested Content for Figure 2.8.2

**Figure title for authoring/reference only:**  
`From Copper-Clad Core to Patterned Inner Layer`

**Instructional purpose:**  
Show how a uniform copper-clad core becomes a patterned inner copper layer.

The figure should make two concepts visually obvious:

> **Imaging creates a temporary resist pattern. Etching creates the actual copper circuit pattern.**

and

> **Inner layers are patterned and inspected before they are buried by lamination.**

**Canonical filename:**  
`2.8.2 - From Copper-Clad Core to Patterned Inner Layer.png`

**Intended public width:** 900 px

**Recommended authoring canvas:** approximately `1800 × 1000` px unless the final composition clearly benefits from a modestly different aspect ratio.

**Recommended aspect ratio:** approximately 16:9 to 9:5, with enough vertical room for labels and safe margins.

# Overall Layout

Use a **four- or five-state left-to-right process sequence** with the same cross-sectional/top-view board fragment evolving across the figure.

Recommended states:

1. `Copper-clad core`
2. `Photoresist applied`
3. `Imaged + developed resist`
4. `Copper etched`
5. `Resist stripped -> patterned inner layer`

A small final `Inspect before lamination` cue may be added after the patterned layer, but do not make AOI a separate major panel unless there is sufficient room.

Use a simplified copper trace/plane pattern that is easy to follow through all states.

Do not place the external figure number, final public caption, revision notes, or a large decorative title inside the artwork.

The artwork should remain readable when reduced to the 900 px mdBook display width.

# Required Technical Relationships

- The starting core must already be a cured dielectric laminate with copper bonded to it.
- Photoresist is temporary and must be visually distinct from copper.
- The imaged/developed resist protects the copper intended to remain in the representative subtractive inner-layer route.
- Etching removes exposed/unprotected copper.
- Resist stripping exposes the final patterned copper.
- Do not imply that optical/direct imaging itself removes copper.
- Registration targets may be shown conceptually, but avoid detailed phototool mechanics.
