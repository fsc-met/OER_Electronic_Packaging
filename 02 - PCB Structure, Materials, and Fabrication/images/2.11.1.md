## Suggested Content for Figure 2.11.1

**Figure title for authoring/reference only:**  
`In-Plane and Through-Thickness Heat Flow in a PCB`

**Instructional purpose:**  
Help MET students immediately distinguish the two important PCB heat-flow directions and understand why copper and dielectric layers affect them differently.

**Central teaching message:**

> **Copper provides efficient in-plane heat-spreading paths, while through-thickness heat flow is often limited by low-conductivity dielectric unless vertical copper paths are available.**

**Canonical PNG filename:**  
`2.11.1 - In-Plane and Through-Thickness Heat Flow in a PCB.png`

**Intended mdBook display width:** 900 px

**Recommended canvas:** approximately `1800 × 1050` px

**Recommended aspect ratio:** approximately 12:7 or 16:9

---

# Overall Layout

Use one clean multilayer-PCB cross-section with a small hot component or hot region on the top surface.

Show two visually distinct heat-flow directions:

- **X-Y / in-plane:** broad lateral arrows spreading through copper planes or pours;
- **Z / through-thickness:** vertical arrows crossing dielectric layers.

Include a small orientation indicator for X-Y and Z if it improves clarity.

Avoid a poster layout with separate explanation boxes. The cross-section itself should carry the teaching message.

---

# Required Objects and Labels

Required:

- hot component or heat-source region;
- top copper land/pour;
- at least one internal copper plane;
- dielectric/core/prepreg regions;
- lateral heat-flow arrows;
- vertical heat-flow arrows;
- label `In-plane (X-Y) heat spreading`;
- label `Through-thickness (Z) heat flow`;
- label `Copper plane`;
- label `Dielectric / laminate`;
- concise cue such as `High-k copper path`;
- concise cue such as `Low-k dielectric bottleneck`.

Optional:

- representative values `Copper ≈ 390 W/(m·K)` and `FR-4 ≈ 0.3 W/(m·K)` if they remain readable and do not clutter the figure.

---

# Required Technical Relationships

- Lateral heat flow should visually favor copper planes/pours.
- Vertical heat flow should visibly cross dielectric unless a via is shown only as a minor secondary object.
- The figure must not imply that heat flows only in one direction at a time.
- The figure must not imply that copper eliminates all thermal resistance.
- The figure must not imply that every multilayer PCB automatically has good thermal performance.
- Heat arrows should represent qualitative flow direction, not vector magnitude.

---

# Spatial / Geometry Requirements

- Keep the PCB cross-section large enough to read at 900 px.
- Copper layers should be thin relative to dielectric thickness but still visibly distinguishable.
- Avoid excessive layer count; four to six conductive layers are enough.
- Keep arrows outside dense label regions.
- Prevent leader-line crossings.
- Preserve safe margins for later SVG editing.
