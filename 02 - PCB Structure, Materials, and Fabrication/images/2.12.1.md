# Figure 2.12.1 Suggested Content

## Figure title for authoring/reference only
**Figure 2.12.1 - Common Copper Pattern Defects on a Bare PCB**

## Instructional purpose
Help MET students visually distinguish the most common **bare-board copper-pattern defect categories** and understand that some defects cause immediate electrical failure while others create **geometric nonconformance** before complete failure.

## Central teaching message
Copper-pattern defects should be identified by the **physical copper condition**:
- **open conductor** = broken path;
- **short / copper bridge** = unintended connection;
- **over-etch** = too little copper remaining;
- **under-etch** = too much copper remaining / spacing reduced.

`900`

## Recommended canvas size / aspect ratio
- Preferred canvas: **1800 × 1200 px**
- Aspect ratio: **3:2**
- White background with safe outer margins.

## Overall layout
Use a **single clean comparison plate** with **five equally important panels** arranged left-to-right or in a 2×3 grid with one empty margin-balanced slot if needed.

Recommended order:
1. Normal reference
2. Open conductor
3. Short / copper bridge
4. Over-etch
5. Under-etch / reduced spacing

Each panel should show a simplified **top view** of copper on a PCB surface.

## Required objects and labels
Each panel must include:
- a small board region with light neutral substrate;
- copper traces/pads in a warm copper color;
- a short panel title.

Required panel labels:
- `Normal reference`
- `Open conductor`
- `Short / copper bridge`
- `Over-etch`
- `Under-etch / reduced spacing`

Required feature callouts:
- `Broken conductor path` or equivalent on the open panel;
- `Residual copper bridge` or equivalent on the short panel;
- `Trace width reduced` on the over-etch panel;
- `Spacing reduced` and/or `Residual copper remains` on the under-etch panel.

## Required technical relationships
- The **normal panel** should show the intended trace width and intended spacing.
- The **open panel** must show a true physical break in one conductor.
- The **short panel** must show two conductors unintentionally connected by residual copper.
- The **over-etch panel** must show a narrower-than-intended conductor, not simply a different nominal design.
- The **under-etch panel** must show extra copper or insufficiently removed copper that reduces spacing.
- Do **not** mix assembly solder bridges into this figure; keep it strictly bare-board copper pattern geometry.

## Spatial / geometry requirements
- Keep all traces large enough to remain legible at 900 px display width.
- Use identical nominal geometry in all panels before defect variation so comparisons are visually fair.
- Maintain enough white space between panels to prevent label overlap.
- If arrows/callouts are used, keep them short and clear.
- If dimension indicators are included, they should be minimal and conceptual, not IPC-style acceptance drawings.

## Color / style guidance
- White background.
- Simplistic, textbook-like, clean engineering-diagram style.
- Copper features in a muted copper/orange tone.
- Substrate in light neutral beige or pale green.
- Callout text in dark gray or dark blue-gray.
- Avoid gradients, shadows, decorative icons, or photo realism.

## Scope boundaries
Include only **bare-board copper-pattern defect recognition**.
Do not include:
- solder mask;
- surface finish;
- plated-hole cross sections;
- assembly solder defects;
- detailed etching chemistry;
- numerical fabrication tolerances.

## Technical-verification basis
Verify against Section 2.12 text and the surrounding Chapter 2 context:
- open conductor;
- short circuit;
- over-etch;
- under-etch;
- geometric nonconformance can exist without complete electrical failure.
