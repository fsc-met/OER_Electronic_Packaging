## Suggested Content for Figure 2.5.2

**Figure title for authoring/reference only:**  
`Basic Dimensions of a PCB Trace`

**Instructional purpose:**  
Provide one simple engineering-geometry figure that gives physical meaning to the trace-resistance relationship introduced in Section 2.5.2.

The figure should reinforce the Section 2.5.2 relationship:

$$
R = \rho \frac{L}{wt}
$$

and the corresponding trace cross-sectional area:

$$
A = wt
$$

The figure should help readers identify the three geometric variables used in these relationships:

- trace length, $L$;
- trace width, $w$;
- copper thickness, $t$.

The figure should answer:

> **Where are $L$, $w$, and $t$ measured on a physical PCB trace?**

The central visual teaching point is:

> **Longer $L$ increases resistance, while wider $w$ or thicker $t$ decreases resistance when the other quantities remain unchanged.**

**Filename:**  
`2.5.2 - Basic Dimensions of a PCB Trace.png`

**Intended width:** 900 px in Markdown

**Recommended aspect ratio:** approximately 16:9 to 9:5; use enough horizontal width that all dimension arrows and labels remain fully inside the canvas.

---

# Overall Layout

Use a clean **two-view engineering diagram** on a white or very light neutral background.

Recommended composition:

- **Left, larger panel:** simplified top view of one straight PCB trace connecting two pads/lands.
- **Right, smaller panel:** enlarged cross-section through the straight trace showing trace width and copper thickness above the PCB dielectric/laminate.
- **Bottom:** one concise takeaway sentence only, if space allows.

The two views should use the same copper and substrate visual language.

The figure should look like a simplified engineering textbook diagram, **not** like a populated PCBA, CAD screenshot, marketing infographic, or design-rule chart.

Do not place the external figure number, final caption, revision information, or an oversized overall title inside the artwork.

# Left Panel - Top View of the Trace

Show a simple rectangular PCB fragment containing:

- one straight copper trace;
- one pad/land at each end of the trace;
- enough substrate area around the trace to make the geometry easy to read.

The trace should be intentionally straight so that the geometry can be understood without introducing path-length complications.

## 1. Trace Length, $L$

Show a horizontal dimension arrow parallel to the trace.

Required label:

- `Trace length, L`

Required relationship:

- the dimension must run along the direction of the trace;
- the extension lines should terminate at clearly defined locations associated with the trace span;
- the arrow must not be mistaken for current direction.

For this conceptual figure, $L$ represents the conductor length used in the resistance relationship.

Do not add a numerical length.

Do not imply that all real PCB traces are straight.

---

## 2. Trace Width, $w$

Show a short dimension arrow across the trace, perpendicular to the trace direction.

Required label:

- `Trace width, w`

Required relationship:

- the arrow must span the copper trace width;
- it must clearly be perpendicular to the trace length direction;
- the width callout should not overlap the current-direction arrow or pad geometry.

Do not add a numerical width.

Do not imply that the shown width is a recommended or universal PCB design value.

---

Show a simple pad/land at each end of the trace.

Required label:

- `Pad / land`

The pads are included only to make the trace look like a physical PCB interconnect rather than an isolated rectangle.

- `Copper thickness, t`

- the symbol must be lowercase italic $t$ in the surrounding text/math notation.

- it must not include any dielectric thickness;
- it must not point to solder mask or surface finish.

Do not add a numerical thickness or copper-weight conversion in this figure.

# Required Technical Relationships

The final figure must correctly communicate all of the following:

- a PCB trace is a three-dimensional copper conductor with finite length, width, and thickness;
- $L$ is measured along the conductor path;
- $w$ is measured across the trace in the plane of the PCB;
- $t$ is the copper thickness measured normal to the PCB surface;
- for the rectangular conceptual cross-section, $A = wt$;
- the Section 2.5.2 first-order resistance relationship is $R = \rho L/(wt)$;
- increasing $L$ increases resistance when the other quantities remain unchanged;
- increasing $w$ decreases resistance when the other quantities remain unchanged;
- increasing $t$ decreases resistance when the other quantities remain unchanged;
- the figure shows physical geometry only and does not provide a current-rating rule;
- the trace and pads are copper features on a **bare PCB**, not a populated assembly.
