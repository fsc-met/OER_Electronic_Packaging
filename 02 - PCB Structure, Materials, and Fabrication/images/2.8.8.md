## Suggested Content for Figure 2.8.8

**Figure title for authoring/reference only:**  
`Bare-Board Electrical Test and Final Quality Verification`

**Instructional purpose:**  
Give the final verification stage the same pedagogical depth as the earlier fabrication operations.

The figure should clearly separate two different questions:

1. **Electrical test:** Are the intended nets continuous and unintended nets isolated?
2. **Final quality verification:** Does the finished bare board meet required physical, dimensional, surface, hole, cleanliness, and documentation expectations?

The central message is:

> **A board can look correct and still fail electrically; a board can pass continuity/isolation and still have another physical nonconformance.**

**Canonical filename:**  
`2.8.8 - Bare-Board Electrical Test and Final Quality Verification.png`

**Intended public width:** 900 px

**Recommended authoring canvas:** approximately `1800 × 1000` px unless the final composition clearly benefits from a modestly different aspect ratio.

**Recommended aspect ratio:** approximately 16:9 to 9:5, with enough vertical room for labels and safe margins.

# Overall Layout

Use a **two-stage left-to-right verification flow** after a completed bare PCB.

### Left - Electrical test
Show the same finished bare board with two conceptual test-method examples:

- `Flying probe` — a few probe tips contacting test points/pads;
- `Dedicated fixture` — simplified bed-of-nails / fixture plate beneath or above a board.

Do not show both as simultaneous required operations; use them as representative alternatives.

Visually distinguish:

- `Continuity`
- `Isolation`

A small net example may show:
- intended connected nodes -> pass;
- separate nets -> no short.

### Right - Final quality verification
Show a checklist-style engineering inspection around the bare board:

- visual surface/mask/legend;
- hole/slot condition;
- outline/dimensions;
- surface finish;
- cleanliness;
- warpage where required;
- test records / traceability;
- coupon/microsection evidence where required.

End with:

`Accepted bare PCB / assembly-ready array`

Do not make the figure look like a consumer quality-control poster; keep it an engineering diagram.

Do not place the external figure number, final public caption, revision notes, or a large decorative title inside the artwork.

The artwork should remain readable when reduced to the 900 px mdBook display width.

# Required Technical Relationships

- Electrical testing must be shown as bare-board continuity/isolation verification, not component functional test.
- Flying probe and dedicated fixture are representative alternatives, not simultaneous mandatory steps.
- A continuity check confirms required connections; an isolation check confirms unintended shorts are absent.
- Electrical test must not be depicted as replacing visual/dimensional/material inspection.
- Final inspection may include coupons/microsections or warpage checks only where required by the quality plan/specification.
- The board must remain unpopulated.
- The final state is accepted bare PCB / array, not PCBA.
