## Suggested Content for Figure 2.6.5

**Figure title for authoring/reference only:**  
`Thermal Via Concept Beneath a Heat Source`

**Instructional purpose:**  
Provide one applied cross-sectional figure that helps MET readers visualize **thermal vias as through-thickness heat-conduction paths**.

The figure should connect the Section 2.6 thermal-via concept to the Chapter 2 lecture's thermal-behavior emphasis without turning this subsection into a full thermal-design treatment.

The figure should answer:

> **How can a group of plated vias help move heat from a hot surface region into internal and opposite-side copper?**

The central teaching point is:

> **Thermal vias provide multiple copper-wall conduction paths through the low-conductivity dielectric structure, allowing heat to reach copper regions where it can spread laterally or continue toward another thermal interface.**

**Filename:**  
`2.6.5 - Thermal Via Concept Beneath a Heat Source.png`

**Intended width:** 900 px in Markdown

**Recommended aspect ratio:** approximately 16:9

# Heat-Flow Arrows

Use restrained arrows to show conceptual heat transfer.

Required relationships:

- arrows originate at or near the heat source;
- arrows pass into the top copper;
- multiple arrows travel down through the via locations;
- arrows then spread laterally in internal/bottom copper.

Do not draw arrows crossing empty dielectric far from the vias as though FR-4 were the preferred vertical conduction path.

Some heat transfer through dielectric exists physically, but the figure's purpose is to highlight the enhanced copper path.

Use a short label:

- `Heat flow`

---

# Required Technical Relationships

The final figure must correctly communicate:

- thermal via = functional use of plated via structure for heat conduction;
- thermal vias improve through-thickness conduction by adding copper-wall paths;
- multiple vias act as multiple parallel heat paths;
- heat can then spread laterally in larger copper regions;
- thermal vias are not the same as thermal reliefs;
- thermal-via effectiveness depends on the complete thermal path, not only the presence of holes;
- the figure is conceptual and does not establish via count, spacing, diameter, or thermal-resistance values.
