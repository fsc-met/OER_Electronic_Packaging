## Suggested Content for Figure 2.4.1

**Figure title for authoring/reference only:**  
`PCB Material Properties and Engineering Consequences`

**Instructional purpose:**  
Provide one compact technical figure that helps students connect important PCB material properties to their **physical meaning** and to the **engineering consequences** that appear later in the book.

The figure should visually reinforce the Section 2.4 teaching pattern:

> **Property -> Physical meaning -> Packaging consequence**

The figure should not be a generic material-property poster. It should show how a conventional rigid PCB laminate/copper system participates simultaneously in:

- thermal behavior;
- thermomechanical behavior;
- mechanical stiffness;
- moisture-related behavior;
- electrical behavior;
- manufacturing/process robustness.

The figure should answer:

> **Why do PCB laminate and copper properties matter to an engineering technologist?**

**Filename:**  
`2.4.1 - PCB Material Properties and Engineering Consequences.png`

**Intended width:** 900 px in Markdown

**Recommended aspect ratio:** approximately 16:9

---

## Overall Layout

Use a clean **central-PCB + property callout** engineering diagram.

Recommended composition:

- center: simplified rigid multilayer PCB cross-section;
- left side: thermal / thermomechanical / mechanical properties;
- right side: electrical / moisture / copper properties;
- each property connected to one concise engineering consequence;
- use arrows or short callout lines only where they clarify a direct relationship.

The figure should remain readable at textbook scale and should not become a dense infographic.

Recommended central board features:

- top copper;
- inner copper layers;
- bottom copper;
- dielectric/core region;
- one plated through-hole or via;
- one or two simplified surface copper features.

Do not use a full fabrication-flow drawing.

Do not place the external figure number or final caption inside the artwork.

# Core Teaching Relationships

The figure should include the following property-to-consequence relationships.

## 1. Thermal Conductivity \(k\)

### Physical meaning

Thermal conductivity describes how readily heat conducts through a material.

### Figure relationship

Use a simple heat-flow arrow or temperature-gradient cue through the board.

Show:

- copper as a strong heat-spreading path;
- dielectric as a comparatively poor heat conductor.

### Required consequence label

Use a concise statement such as:

> `Heat spreading / through-thickness heat flow`

or:

> `Controls heat-flow path`

### Technical caution

Do not place a universal numerical thermal conductivity value inside the figure.

Do not state that adding more copper always solves a thermal problem.

---

## 2. Coefficient of Thermal Expansion (CTE)

### Physical meaning

CTE describes dimensional change with temperature.

### Figure relationship

Show directional arrows:

- `X/Y` along the board plane;
- `Z` through the board thickness.

The figure should communicate that these directions can behave differently.

### Required consequence label

Use a concise statement such as:

> `Dimensional change / mismatch stress`

and, near the plated hole or via:

> `Via / barrel strain`

### Technical caution

Do not imply that X/Y and Z CTE are equal.

Do not add universal CTE numbers.

---

## 3. Glass-Transition Temperature \(T_g\)

### Physical meaning

\(T_g\) marks a temperature region where polymer mechanical behavior changes significantly.

### Figure relationship

Use a small conceptual temperature indicator or polymer-state cue.

### Required consequence label

Use:

> `Modulus and CTE behavior change`

or:

> `High-temperature dimensional behavior`

### Technical caution

Do not present \(T_g\) as:

- melting point;
- decomposition temperature;
- maximum operating temperature;
- reliability guarantee.

---

## 4. Decomposition Temperature \(T_d\)

### Physical meaning

\(T_d\) is associated with chemical degradation under a defined thermal test.

### Required consequence label

Use:

> `Severe thermal-exposure robustness`

or:

> `Chemical degradation threshold (test-defined)`

### Technical caution

Do not combine \(T_g\) and \(T_d\) into one temperature.

Do not imply that \(T_d\) is a normal operating-temperature rating.

---

## 5. T260 / T288

### Physical meaning

T260 and T288 are time-to-delamination measurements at specified elevated temperatures.

### Required consequence label

Use:

> `Time-to-delamination awareness`

### Figure treatment

This can be shown as a small secondary label near \(T_d\) rather than a full independent callout.

### Technical caution

The reported result is **time**, not another transition temperature.

Do not turn this figure into a laminate-qualification test chart.

---

## 6. Elastic / Flexural Modulus

### Physical meaning

Modulus describes resistance to material deformation.

### Figure relationship

Use a subtle board-bending sketch or small force/deflection icon.

### Required consequence label

Use:

> `Material stiffness / deformation`

### Technical caution

The figure must not imply that laminate modulus alone determines whole-board bending stiffness.

If space permits, use a short qualifier:

> `Board stiffness also depends on geometry`

---

## 7. Moisture Absorption

### Physical meaning

Moisture absorption describes the tendency of the material system to take up moisture.

### Figure relationship

Use a restrained water-vapor or moisture-arrow cue entering the laminate.

### Required consequence label

Use:

> `Electrical / dimensional / thermal-process effects`

or:

> `Can contribute to delamination risk during heating`

### Technical caution

Do not imply that moisture absorption alone predicts product failure.

Do not show dramatic "popcorn" failure imagery in this introductory material-property figure.

---

## 8. Dielectric Constant \(D_k\)

### Physical meaning

\(D_k\) is the relative permittivity of the dielectric material.

### Required consequence label

Use:

> `Capacitance / propagation / impedance`

### Figure relationship

Optionally show an electric-field cue between a signal layer and reference plane.

### Technical caution

Do not include impedance formulas.

Do not treat one \(D_k\) number as universal for all FR-4 materials.

---

## 9. Dissipation Factor \(D_f\)

### Physical meaning

\(D_f\) describes dielectric loss.

### Required consequence label

Use:

> `Signal attenuation at higher frequency`

or:

> `Dielectric-loss behavior`

### Technical caution

Do not imply that \(D_f\) matters only above one universal frequency.

Do not include detailed signal-integrity calculations.

---

## 10. Copper Thickness / Copper Weight

### Physical meaning

Copper thickness describes how much conductor material is present; copper weight is a common mass-per-unit-area specification method.

### Figure relationship

Use one enlarged copper trace or plane edge.

### Required consequence label

Use:

> `Resistance / current / heat spreading / manufacturability`

### Optional note

If space allows, one tiny note may say:

> `1 oz/ft² ≈ 35 µm nominal`

Only include this if it remains clearly labeled as **nominal / approximate**.

### Technical caution

Do not imply that finished outer-layer copper always equals the starting foil thickness.

Do not turn this figure into a copper-weight conversion table.

---

Recommended structure:

- top copper;
- dielectric / prepreg-derived region;
- one or two inner copper layers;
- core dielectric;

- bottom copper;

# Directional CTE Representation

This is one of the most important technical parts of the figure.

Include:

- horizontal double-headed arrow labeled `X/Y`;
- vertical double-headed arrow labeled `Z`.

Place them beside the PCB cross-section.

Recommended supporting label:

> `CTE is direction-dependent`

The visual should help students understand that:

- in-plane behavior is influenced strongly by glass reinforcement;
- through-thickness behavior is more strongly influenced by resin-dominated expansion.

Do not use numerical values.

# Required Technical Relationships

The final figure must correctly communicate that:

1. PCB laminate properties are not one fixed "FR-4" property set.
2. Thermal conductivity affects heat flow.
3. Copper generally provides a much stronger heat-spreading path than glass-reinforced epoxy laminate.
4. CTE is direction-dependent.
5. X/Y and Z expansion behavior are different.
6. Z-direction expansion can strain plated holes or vias.
7. \(T_g\) is a polymer transition region, not melting or decomposition.
8. \(T_d\) relates to chemical degradation under a defined test.
9. T260/T288 represent time-to-delamination measurements at fixed elevated temperatures.
10. Modulus is a material property and does not alone determine whole-board stiffness.
11. Moisture absorption can affect electrical, dimensional, and thermal-process behavior.
12. \(D_k\) influences capacitance, propagation, and impedance.
13. \(D_f\) influences dielectric loss.
14. Copper thickness affects electrical, thermal, manufacturing, and mechanical behavior.
15. Properties depend on the actual laminate/construction and test conditions.

Do **not**:

- imply all FR-4 materials have identical \(T_g\), CTE, \(D_k\), or \(D_f\);
- imply \(T_g\) is the melting point;

- imply \(T_d\) is a normal operating-temperature limit;
- label T260/T288 as temperatures rather than time-to-delamination tests;
- imply X/Y and Z CTE are equal;
- show a via barrel without copper plating;
- imply modulus alone determines PCB bending stiffness;
- imply moisture absorption alone causes failure;
- include controlled-impedance equations;
- include detailed thermal-resistance equations;
- include detailed material-selection tables;
- use proprietary laminate product names as the figure's main content;
- copy a laminate-vendor datasheet graphic;
- reproduce an IPC figure or table;
- include figure number or final caption inside the artwork.
