# Figure 2.12.2 Suggested Content

## Figure title for authoring/reference only
**Figure 2.12.2 - Common Hole and Via Fabrication Defects**

## Instructional purpose
Help MET students distinguish **registration-related** hole defects from **plating-integrity** defects in plated-through structures.

## Central teaching message
A drilled/plated hole can fail in more than one way:
- the hole may be **misregistered** relative to the land;
- the annular ring may **break out**;
- the plated barrel may contain a **void**;
- the plating may be **thin or nonuniform**;
- the barrel may be **cracked**.

These are different physical problems and should not be collapsed into one generic “bad via” idea.

## Overall layout
Use a **multi-panel cross-sectional comparison** with one **normal reference** plus four defect cases.

Recommended panel set:
1. Correctly registered plated hole
2. Hole registration error / annular-ring breakout
3. Plating void
4. Thin or nonuniform plating
5. Barrel crack awareness

All panels should use the same basic board cross-section for comparison.

## Required objects and labels
Required board elements:
- top and bottom copper pads;
- laminate / dielectric body;
- drilled hole;
- plated copper barrel where appropriate.

Required labels:
- `Normal reference`
- `Registration error / annular-ring breakout`
- `Plating void`
- `Thin or nonuniform plating`
- `Barrel crack`

Useful callouts:
- `Reduced annular ring`
- `Breakout`
- `Local plating void`
- `Thin barrel wall`
- `Crack in plated barrel`

## Required technical relationships
- The normal panel should show centered hole geometry and continuous plating.
- The breakout panel must show **off-center hole-to-pad relationship** causing partial loss of annular ring continuity.
- The plating-void panel must show a **local discontinuity in the plated copper wall**, not a missing drilled hole.
- The thin/nonuniform plating panel must show the barrel present but with visibly uneven or insufficient thickness.
- The barrel-crack panel must show a crack in the plated barrel wall, not merely a cosmetic line elsewhere in the laminate.
- Keep this figure at **recognition level**; do not attempt full fatigue analysis.

## Spatial / geometry requirements
- Use simple cross-sections large enough to remain legible at mdBook size.
- Keep copper thickness exaggerated slightly for teaching clarity.
- Ensure defect callouts point precisely to the relevant geometry.
- Maintain consistent panel scale across all cases.
