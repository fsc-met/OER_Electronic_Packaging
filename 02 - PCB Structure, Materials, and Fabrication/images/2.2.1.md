## Suggested Content for Figure 2.2.1

**Figure title for authoring/reference only:**  
`Physical Anatomy of a Multilayer PCB`

**Instructional purpose:**  
Give readers one clear visual reference for the **physical anatomy of a conventional rigid multilayer bare PCB** before later sections discuss stack-up design, materials, vias, surface finishes, fabrication, DFM, thermal behavior, or reliability in more detail.

The figure should help readers connect two ways of looking at the same board:

1. the **surface view**, where traces, pads, holes, solder mask, legend, and mechanical features can be seen; and
2. the **cross-sectional view**, where copper layers, dielectric layers, plated hole walls, solder mask, and surface finish can be understood through the board thickness.

The figure should answer the question:

> **What physical layers and features make up a rigid multilayer bare PCB?**

The main teaching goal is not to show every possible PCB technology. It is to establish a correct physical model that later sections can build on.

**Filename:**  
`2.2.1 - Physical Anatomy of a Multilayer PCB.png`

**Intended width:** 900 px in Markdown

**Recommended aspect ratio:** 16:9

---

### Overall layout

Use a clean **two-panel engineering-textbook figure** on a white or very light neutral background.

Recommended arrangement:

- **Left panel:** simplified top/surface view of a bare PCB.
- **Right panel:** enlarged cutaway/cross-sectional view of the same conceptual board.

The cross-section should receive slightly more space than the surface view because it carries the main instructional burden.

Recommended approximate width allocation:

- left surface view: 35-40%;
- right cross-section: 60-65%.

The panels should be visually related but do not need to be a literal geometric cut from the exact same artwork. The goal is conceptual continuity, not photorealistic reconstruction.

Use short panel headings only if useful, such as:

- `Surface view`
- `Cross-section`

Do **not** place the external figure number, final caption, or an oversized overall title inside the artwork.

## Left Panel - Surface View of a Bare PCB

### Required visual concept

Show a clean, simplified **bare PCB with no mounted electronic components**.

The board should appear as a realistic engineering drawing rather than a decorative green rectangle.

The surface view should contain enough features to let readers identify the board anatomy discussed in Section 2.2.

### Required surface features

Include:

- board outline;
- visible solder mask covering most of the board surface;
- several exposed copper pads with surface finish;
- a few copper traces visible where pedagogically useful;
- at least one small via;
- one plated through-hole associated with a larger pad;
- one mounting/mechanical hole;
- one slot or cutout;
- several short legend/silkscreen markings;
- optional reference designator text such as `R1`, `C1`, or `J1` only if it remains clearly a **bare-board marking** and does not imply that a component is already mounted.

### Surface-view labeling

Use concise callouts for:

- `Trace`
- `Pad / land`
- `Via`
- `Plated through-hole`
- `Solder mask`
- `Legend`
- `Mounting hole`
- `Slot / cutout`

If all eight callouts make the panel crowded, the label set may be reduced by using the cross-section to identify some features, but the following should remain visible somewhere in the full figure:

- trace;
- pad;
- via;
- plated through-hole;
- solder mask;
- legend;
- mounting/mechanical feature.

### Surface-view technical relationships

The artwork must show correctly that:

- pads intended to remain accessible are **not covered by solder mask**;
- the surface finish is on the **exposed conductive pad**, not on top of the solder mask;
- a via is smaller and primarily represents an interlayer electrical connection;
- a plated component hole is physically larger and surrounded by a copper land;
- the mounting hole is visibly different from the via;
- a slot/cutout removes board material rather than being drawn as a copper feature;
- legend markings sit on the board surface and are not copper traces;
- no mounted components, solder joints, or component leads are present because this is a **bare PCB**.

### Suggested surface-view style

Use a restrained, lightly isometric or near-orthographic top view.

Avoid strong 3-D perspective because the goal is identification rather than product rendering.

A simple flat technical plan view with slight thickness at the edge is acceptable.

## Right Panel - Multilayer Cross-Section

### Required visual concept

Show a simplified cross-section of a **conventional rigid four-layer PCB**.

A four-layer example is preferred because it is complex enough to show:

- outer copper;
- inner copper;
- a central core;
- prepreg-derived dielectric regions;
- plated vertical connections;
- outer solder mask;
- exposed pads with surface finish;

while remaining readable at textbook size.

The cross-section is an **illustrative construction**, not a universal stack-up.

### Recommended conceptual stack

From top to bottom, show:

1. top solder mask;
2. top copper (Layer 1);
3. prepreg-derived dielectric;
4. inner copper (Layer 2);
5. cured core dielectric;
6. inner copper (Layer 3);
7. prepreg-derived dielectric;
8. bottom copper (Layer 4);
9. bottom solder mask.

On at least one exposed outer pad, add a thin surface-finish layer above the copper.

The figure does not need numerical thicknesses.

### Required cross-section labels

Label clearly:

- `Solder mask`
- `Top copper`
- `Prepreg-derived dielectric`
- `Core`
- `Inner copper layer`
- `Bottom copper`
- `Surface finish`
- `Plated barrel`
- `Annular copper / pad`

Optional if space allows:

- `Dielectric`
- `Via`
- `Plated through-hole`

Do not duplicate labels unnecessarily. For example, one callout may identify both inner copper layers as `Inner copper layers`.

### Core and prepreg representation

The figure must support the Section 2.2 distinction:

- the **core** is a cured laminate construction element;
- **prepreg** is used during multilayer lamination and becomes part of the cured dielectric structure of the finished board.

Because the artwork shows the **finished PCB**, use the label:

> `Prepreg-derived dielectric`

rather than simply `uncured prepreg`.

If a small explanatory note is needed, it may say:

> `Prepreg before lamination`

but only if the label remains concise and does not overcrowd the figure.

Do not depict liquid resin or uncured prepreg inside the finished board.

---

## Vertical Interconnects in the Cross-Section

### Plated through-hole

Include one clearly drawn **plated through-hole** extending from the top surface to the bottom surface.

It should show:

- drilled opening;
- conductive plating on the hole wall;
- copper land/pad at the outer surface;
- electrical connection to one or more selected copper layers.

The plated wall must be visually continuous.

Do not fill the center of the hole with solid copper.

Do not show a component lead or solder joint in the hole because the board is still bare.

### Via

If space permits, include a second smaller **through via** as a separate feature.

It should show:

- smaller drilled opening;
- plated wall;
- connection between selected copper layers;
- no component lead.

This allows students to visually distinguish a plated component hole from a via without introducing blind/buried/microvia structures before Section 2.6.

### Mounting hole

The surface panel should show a mounting/mechanical hole.

If a matching hole is also shown in cross-section, make it clearly **non-plated** unless a different intentional construction is specifically labeled.

Do not imply that every mounting hole in industry is non-plated; the figure simply uses one non-plated mechanical hole as a clear contrast.

---

## Copper Features to Show

The figure should visually distinguish at least three kinds of copper geometry:

### Trace

A relatively narrow copper conductor on the surface view.

### Pad / land

A larger local copper area around or beneath an intended connection point.

### Plane / large copper region

At least one inner copper layer may be shown as a broad continuous copper region.

The figure does not need to identify that plane specifically as power or ground.

Use the general label:

> `Inner copper layer / plane`

if needed.

This avoids introducing Chapter 5 electrical design assumptions too early.

---

## Solder Mask and Surface Finish

The figure must make this distinction visually unambiguous.

### Solder mask

Show solder mask as a coating over selected outer-board regions.

It should:

- cover portions of the top and bottom copper;
- stop at openings where pads must remain exposed;
- not fill the entire board thickness;
- not be shown as a hermetic shell.

### Surface finish

Show surface finish only on selected exposed copper features.

It should:

- appear as a thin layer directly on an exposed copper pad/land;
- not appear on top of solder mask;
- not coat the entire board.

The figure should therefore visually reinforce:

> **Solder mask covers selected board-surface regions; surface finish protects selected exposed conductive regions.**

---

## Legend / Silkscreen

Show one or two simple surface markings in the left panel.

Examples:

- `R1`
- `C1`
- `J1`
- polarity/orientation mark

The markings should:

- be visually distinct from copper;
- sit above the solder-mask surface;
- remain secondary in visual emphasis;
- not imply that the corresponding component has already been placed.

The figure does not need to explain legend-printing methods.

---

## Mechanical Features

The left panel should include:

### Board outline

Clearly show the external board boundary.

### Mounting hole

Show one mechanical mounting hole.

Prefer a simple non-plated example for visual contrast with plated holes/vias.

### Slot or cutout

Show one elongated slot or internal cutout.

The slot should visibly remove substrate material.

### Tooling feature

A separate tooling hole is optional.

Do not add panel rails, fiducials, mouse bites, V-scores, or detailed panelization unless they are essential for visual balance. Those topics belong mainly in Chapter 3.

---

## Required Technical Relationships

The final figure must preserve all of the following:

1. The board is a **bare PCB**, not a PCBA.
2. Copper and dielectric layers alternate through the multilayer construction.
3. The core is a cured laminate region.
4. Prepreg-derived regions are shown as cured dielectric in the finished board.
5. Outer copper is selectively covered by solder mask.
6. Exposed pads are openings in the solder mask.
7. Surface finish is located on exposed conductive features.
8. A plated through-hole has conductive plating on the hole wall.
9. A through via also has conductive wall plating but is primarily an interlayer electrical connection.
10. A mechanical/mounting hole can be non-plated and should be visibly distinguished from a via.
11. Copper layers may contain narrow traces or broad plane regions.
12. Legend markings are surface information, not electrical copper.
13. Slots/cutouts remove board material.
14. No mounted components are present.
15. No semiconductor die, IC package construction, or package substrate is shown as part of the PCB stack.
16. The figure is an illustrative conventional four-layer rigid-board anatomy, not a universal construction for all PCBs.

## Spatial and Drawing Requirements

### Cross-section geometry

- Keep copper layers thin relative to the dielectric layers, but slightly exaggerate copper thickness for readability.
- Maintain parallel layer geometry.
- Do not let copper layers float outside the dielectric stack.
- Hole plating should pass through the dielectric stack continuously.
- Annular copper should align with the plated-hole wall.
- Surface finish should be visibly thinner than the underlying copper pad.
- Solder mask should be visibly thinner than the dielectric body.

### Callouts

- Use clean leader lines.
- Every label must point to exactly one intended feature.
- Avoid crossed leader lines.
- Avoid placing callout text over the PCB geometry.
- Keep labels horizontal where possible.
- Use left- and right-side label columns if needed.

### Scale

The cross-section is schematic.

Do not imply that:

- copper, solder mask, and surface finish are physically the same thickness;
- core and prepreg-derived dielectric are always equal thickness;
- all four-layer boards use this exact geometry.

No dimensions should be shown unless a later revision deliberately adds them for a teaching reason.

## Visual Style

Follow the established MET406 OER figure style:

- clean engineering-textbook appearance;
- white or very light neutral background;
- original vector-like technical illustration;
- restrained color palette;
- consistent line weights;
- simple geometry;
- no photorealistic PCB photograph;

- no poster-like design;
- no decorative gradients unless extremely subtle and needed for layer separation;
- no shadows that obscure layer boundaries.

### Suggested restrained color logic

Use color only to improve physical distinction.

Possible approach:

- copper: copper/orange-brown;
- dielectric/core/prepreg-derived layers: two related neutral or muted laminate tones;
- solder mask: muted green;
- surface finish: light metallic/gold or neutral metal tone;
- legend: white or light gray;
- hole interiors: white/background;
- callouts/text: dark gray/black.

Color must **not** be the only means of distinguishing core from prepreg-derived dielectric. Use labels and, if helpful, subtle pattern/tonal differences.

Do not make the figure dependent on exact real-world material colors.

## Prohibited or Misleading Elements

Do **not**:

- show mounted resistors, capacitors, ICs, connectors, or solder joints;
- label the bare board as a PCBA;
- show a semiconductor die inside the PCB stack;
- show an IC package substrate as though it were a PCB layer;
- show solder mask covering exposed solderable pads;
- show surface finish coating the entire solder-mask surface;
- show surface finish as a thick structural layer;
- describe or visually imply solder mask as airtight, waterproof, or hermetic;
- depict uncured/liquid prepreg inside the finished PCB;
- imply that the core alone determines board stiffness;
- imply that all plated through-holes are vias;
- imply that all mounting holes are non-plated in all products;
- include blind vias, buried vias, stacked microvias, filled vias, or via-in-pad structures in this introductory anatomy figure;
- include component leads in the plated through-hole;
- add numerical material properties or thickness values;
- present this four-layer construction as the universal PCB stack-up;
- show arrows implying a fabrication sequence;
- turn the figure into a PCB-fabrication-process diagram;
- reproduce or closely imitate a textbook, lecture-slide, fabricator, or manufacturer cross-section.
