# Figure 2.9.2 - Nominal Annular Ring and Manufacturing Variation

## Instructional Purpose

This figure should show why annular ring is a **tolerance-margin problem**, not only an ideal pad-and-hole geometry problem.

The central teaching relationship is:

> **A centered hole produces the nominal annular ring. Relative drill / layer-registration offset reduces the remaining ring on one side.**

The figure should visually support the simplified screening relationships used in Section 2.9:

$$
a_\text{nominal}=\frac{D_p-D_h}{2}
$$

and

$$
a_\text{min}\approx a_\text{nominal}-e
$$

where $e$ is a simplified combined relative pad-to-hole offset.

This is a conceptual screening model, **not an acceptance standard**.

## Recommended Composition

Use a clean **two-panel top-view geometry comparison**.

### Left panel - Ideal centered geometry

Show:

- one circular copper pad / land;
- one circular drilled/plated-hole opening centered in the pad;
- pad center and hole center coincident;
- equal radial annular ring all around the hole.

Required labels:

- `Pad / land`
- `Hole`
- `Pad center = hole center`
- `Nominal annular ring`
- `$D_p$`
- `$D_h$`
- `$a_\text{nominal}$`

Use a radial dimension arrow from the hole edge to the pad edge to indicate $a_\text{nominal}$.

Use a double-arrow diameter dimension for $D_p$ and $D_h$ only if spacing remains clean at 900 px. If both diameter dimensions make the panel crowded, place the diameter definitions in a small equation/legend band below the panels instead.

### Right panel - Relative manufacturing offset

Show the **same pad diameter and same hole diameter**, but move the hole center laterally relative to the pad center by a visible but not extreme offset.

Required labels:

- `Design pad center`
- `Actual hole center`
- `Relative offset, e`
- `Reduced annular ring`
- `$a_\text{min}$`
- `Larger ring on opposite side`

The right panel should clearly show:

- less copper on the narrow side;
- more copper on the opposite side;
- no automatic failure symbol;
- no claim that the illustrated ring is acceptable or unacceptable.

The figure should teach **loss of geometric margin**, not pass/fail acceptance.

## Bottom Equation / Interpretation Band

Use a short, clean band below the two panels.

Include:

$$
a_\text{nominal}=\frac{D_p-D_h}{2}
$$

and

$$
a_\text{min}\approx a_\text{nominal}-e
$$

Definitions:

- `$D_p$ = pad / land diameter`
- `$D_h$ = relevant hole diameter`
- `$e$ = simplified relative drill / layer-registration offset`

Add one concise qualifier:

> `Screening model only — actual acceptance depends on the applicable supplier / product requirements.`

Do not add a numerical minimum annular-ring requirement inside the figure.

## Required Technical Relationships

The artwork must preserve all of the following:

- The left and right pads have the **same pad diameter**.
- The left and right holes have the **same hole diameter**.
- Only the relative center position changes between the panels.
- In the centered case, the annular ring is equal around the circumference.
- In the offset case, the annular ring decreases on one side and increases on the opposite side.
- The offset $e$ represents a simplified **relative pad-to-hole displacement**, not necessarily only drill-machine error.
- The figure should acknowledge both drilling position and layer-registration variation conceptually.
- The right panel should not imply total breakout unless a separate optional "extreme case" inset is intentionally added.
- The equations are conceptual first-order screening relationships, not IPC acceptance criteria.

- show radial $a_\text{min}$ at the narrowest side.

## Technical Verification Basis

### Primary curriculum basis

The Chapter 2 material identifies manufacturing limits as process-dependent and emphasizes via/drill constraints as fabrication capability issues.

The Section 2.9 public text develops annular ring explicitly as a tolerance-margin problem.

### Secondary verification basis

PCB manufacturing references support that:

- annular-ring outcome depends on pad registration and manufacturing tolerance;
- misaligned padstacks can produce unpredictable annular-ring results;
- annular ring below manufacturing registration/tolerance capability can reduce yield or create open-connection risk.

The figure should preserve the project's policy of **not assigning one universal numerical annular-ring limit**.

---

## Relationship to Section 2.6 and Chapter 3

### Section 2.6

Section 2.6 already teaches:

- what a plated hole/via is;
- hole anatomy;
- annular ring as copper around the hole;
- drilling versus plating;
- tolerance and registration awareness.

Figure 2.9.2 should therefore **not repeat basic via anatomy**.

Its new teaching contribution is the effect of manufacturing variation on the remaining ring.

### Chapter 3

Detailed annular-ring DFM dimensions and preferred design rules belong in Chapter 3.

Figure 2.9.2 should not preempt those detailed design rules.

2. verify both holes have identical diameters;

5. verify $a_\text{min}$ dimensions the narrow side;
6. verify $e$ is center-to-center offset;
7. inspect native and 900 px renders;
8. preserve each revision under a new filename.
