## Suggested Content for Figure 2.10.3

**Figure title for authoring/reference only:**  
`Bow and Twist Are Different PCB Warpage Shapes`

**Instructional purpose:**  
Provide direct visual recognition of the two common PCB warpage shapes introduced in Section 2.10.

The figure should help MET readers understand three linked ideas:

1. **warpage** is a broad term for loss of flatness;
2. **bow** and **twist** are different **geometric deformation shapes**;
3. these terms describe the shape of the deformation, not by themselves the root cause.

The central teaching message is:

> **Bow and twist are different observable forms of PCB warpage and should not be confused with one another.**

**Canonical filename:**  
`2.10.3 - Bow and Twist Are Different PCB Warpage Shapes.png`

**Intended public width:** 900 px

**Recommended authoring canvas:** approximately `1600 × 1000` px unless a modestly different canvas improves clarity.

**Recommended aspect ratio:** approximately 4:3 or 8:5.

---

# Overall Layout

Use a clean **side-by-side two-panel comparison**.

Recommended composition:

- **Left panel:** `Bow`
- **Right panel:** `Twist`

In each panel, show a simplified rectangular PCB above or relative to a reference plane.

Recommended drawing cues:

- a light `Reference plane` beneath each board;
- a perspective view or lightly angled view so the out-of-plane deformation is obvious;
- concise leader notes identifying the key geometric characteristic of each shape.

For the bowed board, show a smooth, continuous curvature.

For the twisted board, show diagonal torsional deformation so the corners are visibly non-coplanar.

Do not place the external figure number, final public caption, revision notes, or a large decorative title inside the artwork.

The figure should remain legible when reduced to the 900 px mdBook display width.

---

# Required Technical Relationships

- The bowed board must show smooth curvature.
- The bowed example should communicate that the corners remain approximately coplanar or that the deformation is not the same as twist.
- The twisted board must show a diagonal/torsional shape with one corner displaced out of the plane of the others.
- The figure must clearly distinguish `bow` from `twist` based on geometry alone.
- The figure must not attempt to assign a specific cause to one shape or the other.
- The figure must not imply that bow always occurs only along one exact axis or that twist always has only one unique visual form.
- The figure should remain simple enough that the reader can recognize these shapes later during inspection or troubleshooting.
