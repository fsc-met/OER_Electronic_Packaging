## Suggested Content for Figure 2.8.5

**Figure title for authoring/reference only:**  
`Outer-Layer Imaging, Pattern Plating, and Etching`

**Instructional purpose:**  
Make the **inner-versus-outer fabrication difference** visible.

The figure should teach that a representative outer-layer **pattern-plating** route is not simply a repeat of inner-layer subtractive etching.

The key sequence is:

> **outer plating resist defines where copper will be built -> electroplate copper and hole barrels -> strip resist -> etch unwanted base copper -> final outer circuitry**

**Canonical filename:**  
`2.8.5 - Outer-Layer Imaging Pattern Plating and Etching.png`

**Intended public width:** 900 px

**Recommended authoring canvas:** approximately `1800 × 1000` px unless the final composition clearly benefits from a modestly different aspect ratio.

**Recommended aspect ratio:** approximately 16:9 to 9:5, with enough vertical room for labels and safe margins.

# Overall Layout

Use a **five-state cross-sectional / simplified surface sequence** through the same plated-through-hole region.

Recommended states:

1. `Metallized panel`
   - thin conductive copper over outer surfaces and hole walls.

2. `Outer plating resist imaged`
   - resist covers regions that should not receive pattern copper;
   - intended conductors/pads/hole areas remain exposed.

3. `Pattern copper plated`
   - thicker copper added to exposed traces/pads and hole barrel;
   - optional thin temporary tin/metal etch-resist layer may be shown only if clearly marked `where used`.

4. `Plating resist stripped`
   - unwanted base copper now exposed.

5. `Outer copper etched`
   - unwanted base copper removed;
   - final plated outer trace/pad and hole barrel remain.

Use one simple via/pad/traces region so the barrel build-up is easy to follow.

Do not place the external figure number, final public caption, revision notes, or a large decorative title inside the artwork.

The artwork should remain readable when reduced to the 900 px mdBook display width.

# Required Technical Relationships

- The starting panel must already have initial conductive metallization from the previous stage.
- Plating resist polarity must correctly expose the regions where metal is to be added.
- Electroplated copper must build both outer circuitry and hole-barrel copper in the representative pattern-plating route.
- The final outer etch removes unwanted base copper, not the desired plated conductor.
- If temporary tin/metal etch resist is depicted, label it as route-dependent / `where used`.
- Do not imply that every fabricator uses pattern plating; panel-plating alternatives exist.
- Do not confuse the final surface finish of Section 2.7 with temporary outer-layer etch resist.
