## Suggested Content for Figure 2.10.1

**Figure title for authoring/reference only:**  
`Thickness Has a Cubic Influence on First-Order PCB Bending Rigidity`

**Instructional purpose:**  
Make the Section 2.10 thickness-versus-stiffness idea immediately visible.

The figure should help MET readers understand three linked ideas:

1. a simple rectangular-section model uses

$$
I=\frac{bt^3}{12};
$$

2. thickness has a **cubic** first-order influence on bending rigidity for otherwise comparable board strips;
3. this is a **first-order geometry model**, not a complete mechanical description of every real PCB.

The central teaching message is:

> **Modest thickness changes can produce large changes in first-order bending rigidity because the thickness term is cubed.**

**Canonical filename:**  
`2.10.1 - Thickness Has a Cubic Influence on First-Order PCB Bending Rigidity.png`

**Intended public width:** 900 px

**Recommended authoring canvas:** approximately `1800 × 1000` px unless a modestly different canvas improves clarity.

**Recommended aspect ratio:** approximately 16:9 or 9:5.

---

# Overall Layout

Use a clean **two-zone technical teaching figure**.

Recommended composition:

- **Zone A (left or upper-left):** a simple rectangular board cross-section with width `b` and thickness `t` labeled, plus the equation `I = bt^3/12`.
- **Zone B (main area):** three otherwise comparable idealized PCB strips or board sections under the **same loading and span condition**, but with different thicknesses.
- show visibly decreasing curvature/deflection as thickness increases;
- include short relative labels such as `1.00t`, `1.25t`, `1.50t` and corresponding relative `I` values `1.00×`, `1.95×`, `3.38×`.

A good layout is a left-side cross-section/equation inset and a larger right-side progression of board strips. A vertical stacked format is also acceptable if the readability is strong at 900 px.

Do not place the external figure number, final public caption, revision notes, or a large decorative title inside the artwork.

The figure should remain legible when reduced to the 900 px mdBook display width.

---

# Required Technical Relationships

- The cross-section relation must be shown as a **rectangular-section first-order model**.
- The width `b` and thickness `t` labels must be visually tied to the cross-section.
- The figure must show that only **thickness** changes while the compared boards remain otherwise similar in the conceptual comparison.
- The compared boards should be identified as having the same or comparable:
  - material system or effective modulus;
  - width;
  - span/support condition;
  - loading condition.
- Thicker boards must show clearly reduced curvature or deflection under the same conceptual loading condition.
- The figure must communicate the cubic trend without implying that every real PCB follows the simple model exactly.
- The artwork should not imply that the figure is a full laminated-composite or finite-element analysis.
- The figure must distinguish **bending under load** from **warpage due to internal imbalance**. Figure 2.10.1 teaches bending rigidity, not warpage mechanism.

---

# Minimum Labels

Required or strongly recommended labels:

- `b`
- `t`
- `I = bt^3 / 12`
- `Same load`
- `Same span`
- `Same material` or `Same effective material comparison`
- `1.00t -> 1.00× I`
- `1.25t -> 1.95× I`
- `1.50t -> 3.38× I`
- `Less deflection as thickness increases`
- a short note such as `First-order rectangular-section model`

Keep labels concise.

# Spatial / Geometry Requirements

- Keep all compared board strips at the same horizontal scale except for thickness.
- Show thickness change clearly enough to be visible at reduced size.
- If support symbols are used, keep them simple and consistent.
- If a central load arrow is used, use the same load symbol and scale for all compared boards.
- Avoid cluttered numerical tables larger than necessary.
- Prevent leader-line crossings.
- Preserve safe outer margins for later SVG editing.
- If deflection curves are exaggerated for visibility, imply conceptually that the drawing is **not to scale** rather than adding unnecessary text blocks.
