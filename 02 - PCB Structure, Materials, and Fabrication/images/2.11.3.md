## Suggested Content for Figure 2.11.3

**Figure title for authoring/reference only:**  
`A Useful PCB Thermal Feature Must Connect to a Complete Heat Path`

**Instructional purpose:**  
Teach students to evaluate thermal features as parts of a complete path rather than as isolated design objects.

**Central teaching message:**

> **Copper near a hot component helps only when it connects the source to a larger region that can continue carrying heat away.**

**Canonical PNG filename:**  
`2.11.3 - A Useful PCB Thermal Feature Must Connect to a Complete Heat Path.png`

**Intended mdBook display width:** 900 px

**Recommended canvas:** approximately `1800 × 1000` px

---

# Overall Layout

Use a clean two-column comparison.

**Left:** `Incomplete thermal path`

- hot component;
- nearby copper;
- missing or ineffective vertical connection;
- heat remains locally concentrated.

**Right:** `Useful thermal path`

- hot component;
- thermal land;
- thermal vias;
- connected copper plane;
- larger lower/remote copper region;
- arrows showing heat continuing away from the source.

Do not use a good/bad poster style with large icons. Keep it as a technical comparison.

---

# Required Objects and Labels

Left panel:

- `Hot component`;
- local copper land/pour;
- dielectric bottleneck;
- short heat arrows that remain localized;
- label `Heat remains concentrated`.

Right panel:

- `Hot component`;
- `Thermal land`;
- `Thermal vias`;
- `Continuous copper plane`;
- lateral arrows;
- lower or remote heat-rejection/spreading region;
- label `Complete heat path`.

Shared optional label:

- `Collect -> conduct -> spread -> continue to next thermal destination`.

---

# Required Technical Relationships

- The incomplete case must fail because the copper path is poorly connected, not because copper itself is ineffective.
- The useful case must show continuity from source to larger spreading region.
- Do not imply that the right panel guarantees a safe component temperature.
- Do not imply that bottom copper automatically rejects all heat to ambient.
- The comparison should focus on path connectivity, not numerical performance.

---

# Spatial / Geometry Requirements

- Keep both cases at comparable scale.
- Use similar hot components so the path difference is the teaching variable.
- Keep stack-up complexity low.
- Avoid excessive arrows.
- Ensure the incomplete-path reason is obvious without a paragraph.
- Preserve safe outer margins.
