## Suggested Content for Figure 2.8.7

**Figure title for authoring/reference only:**  
`Manufacturing Panel Profiling and Depanelization Features`

**Instructional purpose:**  
Make the often-unfamiliar **manufacturing-panel and profiling** concept tangible for MET readers.

The figure should show that late PCB fabrication does not simply `cut out one board`. The fabricator may create:

- final board outlines;
- slots and cutouts;
- outer rails;
- tooling features;
- tabs / breakaway webs;
- optional score lines;
- an assembly-ready panelized array.

The central message is:

> **Profiling creates the required board or array geometry; complete depanelization may occur later.**

**Canonical filename:**  
`2.8.7 - Manufacturing Panel Profiling and Depanelization Features.png`

**Intended public width:** 900 px

**Recommended authoring canvas:** approximately `1800 × 1000` px unless the final composition clearly benefits from a modestly different aspect ratio.

**Recommended aspect ratio:** approximately 16:9 to 9:5, with enough vertical room for labels and safe margins.

---

# Overall Layout

Use a **single top-view manufacturing panel** with 3–4 repeated PCB images.

Recommended features:

- rectangular manufacturing panel boundary;
- left/right or top/bottom process rails;
- tooling holes/fiducials in the rails;
- routed board outlines;
- at least one internal routed slot or cutout;
- small breakaway tabs or mouse-bite-style tab locations shown conceptually;
- optional dashed `V-score` line only if clearly separated from routed geometry;
- one board image highlighted as the future individual PCB.

Add a small right-side inset:

`Manufacturing panel -> assembly-ready array -> individual PCB`

to show that final board separation can occur later.

Do not place the external figure number, final public caption, revision notes, or a large decorative title inside the artwork.

The artwork should remain readable when reduced to the 900 px mdBook display width.

# Required Technical Relationships

- The manufacturing panel must be clearly larger than one PCB image.
- Board outlines/slots/cutouts should be created by routing/profile geometry, not by copper etching.
- Rails/tooling features must be visually separate from the product PCB area.
- Tabs/scores must be shown as deliberate depanelization features.
- Do not imply every panel uses both tabs and V-score; use `example` or `where used` qualification.
- The figure should allow a board to remain in an array after PCB fabrication.
- Copper-to-edge rules may be suggested visually but not quantified.
