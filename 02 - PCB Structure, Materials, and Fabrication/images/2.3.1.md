## Suggested Content for Figure 2.3.1

**Figure title for authoring/reference only:**  
`Common PCB Types and Structures`

**Instructional purpose:**  
Give readers a recognition-level comparison of the main printed-board constructions introduced in Section 2.3 without turning the figure into a detailed fabrication or design chart.

The figure should help readers distinguish:

- single-layer PCB;
- two-layer PCB;
- multilayer rigid PCB;
- flex PCB;
- rigid-flex PCB;
- HDI as a high-density interconnection approach;
- metal-core / insulated-metal-substrate (IMS) construction.

The figure should reinforce two important teaching points:

> **PCB categories describe different physical constructions or interconnection approaches and are not always mutually exclusive.**

and:

> **For the remainder of Chapter 2, the conventional rigid multilayer PCB is the primary teaching platform.**

The figure should answer:

> **What are the main PCB constructions a manufacturing or product engineer should recognize?**

**Filename:**  
`2.3.1 - Common PCB Types and Structures.png`

**Intended width:** 900 px in Markdown

**Recommended aspect ratio:** approximately 16:9

---

### Overall layout

Use a clean **multi-panel comparison figure** on a white or very light neutral background.

Recommended structure:

- seven compact panels arranged in either:
  - two rows; or
  - one 4-panel row plus one 3-panel row.

Preferred panel sequence:

1. `Single-layer`
2. `Two-layer`
3. `Multilayer rigid`
4. `Flex`
5. `Rigid-flex`
6. `HDI`
7. `Metal-core / IMS`

Each panel should use the **same simplified visual language** so readers compare construction rather than artistic style.

Do **not** place:

- the external figure number;
- the final Markdown caption;
- an oversized overall title;
- a revision-history footer;
- a large legend box;
- paragraph-length explanatory notes

inside the artwork.

Short panel headings are appropriate.

## Panel 1 - Single-Layer PCB

### Required visual concept

Show one insulating substrate with **one patterned copper layer** on one side.

Suggested side or restrained isometric view:

- dielectric substrate;
- one copper trace/pad layer;
- optional solder mask only if it does not make the simple structure harder to read.

Required label:

- `Single-layer`

Optional concise note:

- `One copper layer`

### Required relationship

The visual must clearly show that only one conductive layer is present.

Do not imply that:

- single-layer means poor quality;
- single-layer must be paper-based material;
- single-layer must be physically thinner than every multilayer board.

---

## Panel 2 - Two-Layer PCB

### Required visual concept

Show one insulating board structure with:

- patterned copper on the top;
- patterned copper on the bottom;
- one simple plated via or plated through-hole connecting the two sides.

Required label:

- `Two-layer`

Optional concise note:

- `Copper on both sides`

### Required relationship

The figure should visually show increased routing opportunity compared with the single-layer panel.

Do not automatically label either side:

- `ground`;
- `power`;
- or `plane`.

A two-layer PCB does not inherently contain a dedicated continuous ground plane.

---

## Panel 3 - Multilayer Rigid PCB

### Required visual concept

Show a simplified rigid multilayer cross-section containing:

- top copper;
- at least two inner copper layers;
- bottom copper;
- dielectric layers;
- rigid overall form.

Required label:

- `Multilayer rigid`

Optional concise note:

- `Multiple copper + dielectric layers`

The panel may use a four-layer example for recognizability.

### Required relationship

Make clear that:

- conductive and dielectric layers are bonded into one rigid board;
- internal copper layers are physically inside the laminate construction.

Do not use this panel to teach exact stack-up dimensions. Figure 2.3.2 handles stack-up organization in more detail.

---

## Panel 4 - Flex PCB

### Required visual concept

Show a thin flexible circuit strip or ribbon with:

- patterned copper conductor(s);
- flexible dielectric;
- a gentle controlled bend.

Required label:

- `Flex`

Optional concise note:

- `Flexible circuit construction`

The panel should visually communicate flexibility through shape rather than a cartoon motion effect.

### Required relationship

Do not draw flex as:

- a rigid FR-4 board simply bent sharply;
- a loose wire harness;
- an ordinary ribbon cable.

The circuit conductors must remain integrated with the flexible dielectric construction.

---

## Panel 5 - Rigid-Flex PCB

### Required visual concept

Show:

- one rigid region;
- one flexible integrated region;
- a second rigid region or another rigid termination area.

Required label:

- `Rigid-flex`

Optional concise note:

- `Integrated rigid + flexible regions`

The flexible section should physically join the rigid regions as part of the same board construction.

### Required relationship

The panel must **not** look like two independent rigid PCBs joined by a detachable cable.

The teaching point is:

> **Rigid-flex integrates rigid and flexible circuit regions into one construction.**

---

## Panel 6 - HDI

### Required visual concept

Show a compact rigid multilayer example with visibly higher interconnection density.

Recommended cues:

- finer surface traces;
- smaller capture lands;
- at least one small microvia-like interconnection;
- dense local routing.

Required label:

- `HDI`

Optional concise note:

- `High-density interconnection`

### Important classification rule

This panel must not imply that HDI is mutually exclusive with rigid, flex, or rigid-flex board types.

Use one of these approaches:

- visually show HDI as an **overlay/callout concept** on a rigid example; or
- include a small secondary note:
  - `Can be used in rigid / flex / rigid-flex constructions`

Keep the note short.

### Scope limitation

Do not introduce:

- stacked versus staggered microvias;
- detailed sequential build-up;
- via reliability rules;
- exact line/space dimensions.

Those belong in later sections or specialized PCB design references.

---

## Panel 7 - Metal-Core / IMS

### Required visual concept

Show a simplified cross-section:

> `Circuit copper -> dielectric insulation -> metal base`

Use:

- top copper traces/pads;
- thin electrical insulation layer;
- clearly thicker metal base.

Required label:

- `Metal-core / IMS`

Optional concise note:

- `Metal base supports heat spreading`

### Required relationship

The dielectric must visibly separate copper from the metal base.

Do not show the copper directly shorted to the metal base.

Do not imply that all metal-core boards are identical.

Do not show a complex multilayer metal-core architecture unless specifically needed; this is recognition-level only.

## Comparison Logic

The figure should help readers compare two different ideas:

### Board physical form

- rigid;
- flexible;
- rigid-flex;
- metal-backed.

### Interconnection density / architecture

- single-layer;
- two-layer;
- multilayer;
- HDI.

The visual should therefore avoid suggesting that the seven panels form one strict linear progression from "simple" to "advanced."

Do not use arrows connecting all seven panels in sequence.

A simple neutral comparison layout is preferred.

## Required Technical Relationships

The final figure must correctly communicate that:

1. a single-layer board has one patterned conductive layer;
2. a two-layer board has copper on both board sides;
3. a multilayer rigid board contains internal conductive layers bonded within a rigid dielectric structure;
4. a flex circuit uses flexible circuit materials rather than simply a sharply bent rigid board;
5. rigid-flex contains physically integrated rigid and flexible regions;
6. HDI is an interconnection approach and can coexist with rigid, flex, or rigid-flex construction;
7. HDI may use fine features and microvia-scale interconnections;
8. a metal-core / IMS construction electrically isolates circuit copper from a metal base through dielectric material;
9. metal-core construction is commonly selected where an improved board-level heat-spreading/conduction path is useful;
10. these categories are not a universal ranking of board quality.

## Visual Style

Follow the established MET406 OER image-generation rules:

- clean engineering-textbook illustration;
- white or very light neutral background;
- original vector-like geometry;
- restrained palette;
- consistent line weights;
- concise sans-serif labels;
- adequate whitespace;
- no photorealistic product rendering;
- no commercial PCB photographs;
- no logos or brand markings;
- no poster-style badges;
- no large infographic banners;
- no decorative shadows that obscure layer construction.

### Suggested restrained color logic

Possible approach:

- copper: muted copper / orange-brown;
- conventional rigid dielectric: pale tan or neutral;
- solder mask if shown: muted green;
- flex dielectric: muted amber or pale gold;
- rigid-flex rigid region: same colors as rigid board;
- metal base: neutral gray;
- HDI highlight: use the same board colors with finer geometry rather than a special bright color.

Color must not be the only means of identifying a construction.

## Spatial Requirements

- Keep all seven panels large enough to read at 900 px display width.
- Use approximately equal panel size unless the multilayer or rigid-flex panels need slightly more room.
- Keep panel headings directly above or below each structure.
- Avoid crossed leader lines.
- Prefer labels inside empty panel margins rather than over geometry.
- Keep a consistent viewing direction across rigid-board panels.
- Keep flex and rigid-flex bend geometry gentle and physically plausible.
- Make the metal base clearly thicker than the insulating layer in the IMS panel for recognition, without implying exact scale.

---

## Prohibited or Misleading Elements

Do **not**:

- depict HDI as a completely separate material class;
- imply HDI always means a rigid board;
- imply rigid-flex is simply a cable plugged into two boards;
- show a flex circuit bending through an unrealistically sharp fold radius;
- imply every multilayer board is an HDI board;
- imply more copper layers automatically mean better electrical, thermal, or mechanical performance;
- imply metal-core construction eliminates the need for thermal design;
- show circuit copper directly contacting the metal base without dielectric isolation;
- show mounted electronic components unless needed as tiny orientation cues;
- make any panel look like a finished PCBA comparison;
- include numerical thicknesses, line widths, or via diameters;
- include proprietary stack-up information;
- use vendor-specific construction names as the main labels;
- copy the layout of a textbook, IPC document, PCB-fabricator brochure, or web infographic;
- embed the figure number or external caption.
