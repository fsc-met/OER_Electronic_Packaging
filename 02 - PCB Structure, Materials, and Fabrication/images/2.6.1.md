## Suggested Content for Figure 2.6.1

**Figure title for authoring/reference only:**  
`Plated Component Hole, Through Via, and Mechanical Hole`

**Instructional purpose:**  
Provide one recognition-level cross-sectional figure that makes the Section 2.6 opening distinction immediately visible:

> **A hole describes physical geometry. A via describes an interlayer electrical-connection function.**

The figure should compare three through-thickness features that can look similar from the board surface but serve different purposes:

1. a **plated component hole** intended for later component or connector attachment;
2. a **through via** used primarily for electrical interconnection between selected copper layers;
3. a **non-plated mechanical hole** used primarily for structural or tooling purposes.

The figure should answer:

> **How can three holes pass through the same PCB but differ in plating, copper connection, and intended function?**

**Filename:**  
`2.6.1 - Plated Component Hole Through Via and Mechanical Hole.png`

**Intended width:** 900 px in Markdown

**Recommended aspect ratio:** approximately 16:9

# Common PCB Cross-Section

Use a simplified rigid multilayer PCB construction in all three panels.

Recommended visual layers:

- dielectric;
- one or two inner copper layers;

# Panel A - Plated Component Hole

Show a relatively large through-hole extending through the full board thickness.

Required physical features:

- open center;
- continuous conductive copper plating on the hole wall;
- top and bottom annular rings / pads;
- electrical connection from the plated barrel to at least one selected copper layer;
- clearance on any intentionally unconnected copper layer if shown.

Required label:

- `Plated component hole`

Recommended supporting labels:

- `Plated barrel`
- `Annular ring`
- `Selected layer connection`

The board should remain **bare**.

Do not show:

- inserted component lead;
- connector pin;
- solder joint;
- assembly solder fillet.

The purpose is to show the **bare-board feature before assembly**.

---

# Panel B - Through Via

Show a smaller plated through-hole extending through the full board thickness.

Required physical features:

- open center;
- continuous plated barrel;
- smaller pad/annular-ring geometry than the component-hole example;
- intentional electrical connection between selected copper layers;
- visible clearance at at least one layer that is not electrically connected to the via.

Required label:

- `Through via`

Recommended supporting label:

- `Connects selected copper layers`

The figure must make clear that:

- the barrel can physically pass through many layers;
- electrical connection occurs only where intended copper features contact the barrel.

Do not show a component lead.

Do not imply that every copper layer automatically connects to the via barrel.

---

# Panel C - Non-Plated Mechanical Hole

Show a through-hole of similar visual prominence but with **no conductive wall plating**.

Required physical features:

- substrate edge visible directly at the hole wall;
- no copper barrel;
- no annular ring required;
- no electrical connection to copper layers.

Required label:

- `Non-plated mechanical hole`

Optional supporting label:

- `Structural / tooling function`

The example is intentionally non-plated for contrast.

The figure must not imply that **all** mounting holes in industry are non-plated. The Section 2.6 text already explains that some mounting holes may be plated by design.

---

# Required Technical Relationships

The final figure must correctly communicate all of the following:

- a plated component hole and a through via can both be structurally plated through-holes;
- the plated component hole is intended for later lead or pin attachment;
- the through via is intended primarily for interlayer electrical connection;
- a through via need not connect to every copper layer it passes;
- a non-plated mechanical hole has no conductive barrel in the illustrated example;
- the board is a **bare PCB**, not a populated PCBA;
- hole function cannot be determined from diameter alone;
- plating and layer connection are part of the feature definition.
