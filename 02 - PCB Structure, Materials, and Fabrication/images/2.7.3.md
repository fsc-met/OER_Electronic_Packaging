## Suggested Content for Figure 2.7.3

**Figure title for authoring/reference only:**  
`Surface Planarity and Solder-Paste Stencil Contact`

**Instructional purpose:**  
Show the downstream assembly consequence of surface-finish planarity.

The figure should answer:

> **Why can a less-planar finished pad make solder-paste stencil printing less consistent?**

The central teaching relationship is:

- **planar pad -> close stencil contact / better gasketing;**
- **less-planar pad -> local stencil gap -> increased opportunity for paste to spread beneath the stencil.**

This is a conceptual assembly bridge, not a detailed stencil-design figure.

**Filename:**  
`2.7.3 - Surface Planarity and Solder-Paste Stencil Contact.png`

**Intended public width:** 900 px

**Recommended aspect ratio:** approximately 16:9

# Right Panel - Less-Planar Finished Pad

- `Less-planar finished pad`
- `Local gap`

If paste is shown spreading beneath the stencil, exaggerate only enough to teach the concept.

Do not imply that every HASL board will produce printing defects.

# Required Technical Relationships

The final figure must correctly communicate:

- stencil printing depends in part on close contact/gasketing;
- a planar pad surface supports close stencil contact;
- a less-planar surface can create a local gap;
- the gap can increase paste-printing variability and possible paste spreading;
- the figure is conceptual and not a universal prediction of assembly failure;
- finish type is only one factor in stencil-print quality.
