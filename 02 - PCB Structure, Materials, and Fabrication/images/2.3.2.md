## Suggested Content for Figure 2.3.2

**Figure title for authoring/reference only:**  
`Example Multilayer PCB Stack-Up`

**Instructional purpose:**  
Give readers one clear example of how conductive and dielectric layers are arranged through the thickness of a conventional rigid multilayer PCB.

The figure should visually support the Section 2.3 concepts of:

- layer ordering;
- signal layers;
- internal plane layers;
- core;
- prepreg-derived dielectric;
- dielectric spacing;
- approximate symmetry about the board mid-plane;
- the fact that a stack-up is a **physical engineering construction**, not merely a layer count.

The figure should also reinforce this caution:

> **This is one illustrative four-layer stack-up, not a universal four-layer standard.**

The figure should answer:

> **What information does a basic multilayer stack-up communicate?**

**Filename:**  
`2.3.2 - Example Multilayer PCB Stack-Up.png`

**Intended width:** 900 px in Markdown

**Recommended aspect ratio:** approximately 16:9

---

### Overall Layout

Use a clean **single cross-sectional stack-up diagram** on a white or very light neutral background.

Recommended arrangement:

- central vertical stack showing one illustrative four-layer rigid PCB;
- left-side labels identifying layer number and example function;
- right-side labels identifying dielectric construction;
- a subtle centerline or mid-plane indicator to support the symmetry discussion;
- optional small dimension-style brackets showing that dielectric spacing exists, but **no numerical values**.

Use only short labels.

Do **not** place:

- the external figure number;
- final Markdown caption;
- oversized overall title;
- large legend box;
- revision history;
- long explanatory notes

inside the artwork.

A small label such as:

- `Illustrative 4-layer stack-up`

is acceptable only if needed to prevent the example from being mistaken as universal. Prefer nearby public prose/caption for that message.

## Required Layer Order

The primary cross-section should use this conceptual order from top to bottom:

1. top solder mask if shown;
2. **Layer 1 - top copper**;
3. **prepreg-derived dielectric**;
4. **Layer 2 - inner copper**;
5. **core dielectric**;
6. **Layer 3 - inner copper**;
7. **prepreg-derived dielectric**;
8. **Layer 4 - bottom copper**;
9. bottom solder mask if shown.

The central teaching stack is therefore:

> **Top copper -> prepreg-derived dielectric -> inner copper -> core -> inner copper -> prepreg-derived dielectric -> bottom copper**

This is consistent with the physical anatomy already introduced in Section 2.2.

Do not add multiple cores to this introductory four-layer example.

## Copper Geometry

### Outer copper

Show the top and bottom copper as patterned layers rather than necessarily full solid planes.

A few simple trace/pad segments may be shown on L1 and L4 to reinforce their illustrative signal/component-connection role.

### Inner copper

Show L2 and L3 as broader continuous copper regions to suggest plane use.

Do not make the plane geometry look like it fills every possible area of a real board.

The purpose is conceptual recognition.

### Copper thickness

Copper layers should be visibly thin relative to dielectric regions but may be exaggerated for readability.

Do not imply exact scale.

---

## Core

Show one central **core** region between L2 and L3.

Required label:

- `Core`

Optional more explicit label:

- `Core dielectric`

The core should be visually distinct from the prepreg-derived dielectric regions through:

- a different neutral tone;
- a subtle pattern;
- or both.

Do not rely on color alone.

The figure should reinforce that the core is a cured laminate construction element.

Do not show liquid resin or uncured material.

---

## Prepreg-Derived Dielectric

Show one dielectric region:

- between L1 and L2;

and a corresponding dielectric region:

- between L3 and L4.

Required label:

- `Prepreg-derived dielectric`

The wording **prepreg-derived dielectric** is preferred because the figure depicts the finished board after lamination.

Do not label the finished dielectric regions simply as:

- `uncured prepreg`.

The upper and lower prepreg-derived regions should be drawn with similar thickness in this **illustrative symmetric example**.

---

## Dielectric Spacing

The figure should visually communicate that the vertical spacing between copper layers matters.

Recommended approach:

- add small bracket markers beside:
  - L1-L2 spacing;
  - L2-L3 core spacing;
  - L3-L4 spacing.

Possible concise label:

- `Dielectric spacing`

Do not provide numerical dimensions.

Do not imply:

- smaller spacing is always better;
- larger spacing is always stronger.

The purpose is only to establish that spacing is a controlled part of the stack-up.

---

## Mid-Plane and Symmetry

Add a subtle dashed horizontal line through the middle of the core region or at the board mid-plane.

Label:

- `Board mid-plane`

or:

- `Approximate mid-plane`

The upper and lower construction should be visually balanced:

- L1 corresponding to L4;
- L2 corresponding to L3;
- upper prepreg-derived dielectric corresponding to lower prepreg-derived dielectric.

The figure should communicate:

> **A reasonably symmetric vertical construction helps reduce warpage tendency.**

Do not state inside the artwork that symmetry **guarantees** flatness.

Do not require top and bottom trace patterns to be exact mirror images.

---

## Stack-Up Symmetry Versus Copper Balance

The figure may include one small secondary visual cue if it remains uncluttered.

For example:

- show similar copper thickness for L1/L4;
- show similar upper/lower dielectric thickness.

Do **not** attempt to fully illustrate copper balancing patterns in this figure.

Copper balance is addressed textually in Section 2.3 and visually in later copper-feature material if needed.

The teaching distinction remains:

- stack-up symmetry = vertical construction balance;
- copper balance = copper amount/distribution balance.

## Solder Mask

Solder mask is optional in this figure.

If shown:

Do not let solder mask obscure the main four-layer stack-up.

Do not show surface finish unless needed for orientation.

Figure 2.2.1 already teaches solder mask versus surface finish.

## Required Technical Relationships

The figure must correctly communicate that:

1. the example contains exactly four conductive copper layers;
2. L1 and L4 are outer copper layers;
3. L2 and L3 are inner copper layers;
4. L1-L2 are separated by dielectric;
5. L2-L3 are separated by the central core dielectric;
6. L3-L4 are separated by dielectric;
7. the finished outer dielectric regions may be described as prepreg-derived dielectric;
8. the upper and lower construction are approximately symmetric in this example;
9. layer function is an engineering choice, not a universal four-layer rule;
10. dielectric spacing is part of the stack-up definition;
11. the stack-up is both an electrical and physical construction;
12. layer count alone does not determine stiffness, thermal performance, or electrical performance.

## Prohibited or Misleading Elements

Do **not**:

- present the example as the only correct four-layer stack-up;
- label L2 as ground and L3 as power without making clear these are example roles;
- show more or fewer than four copper layers;
- show two or more cores in this simple example;
- show uncured/liquid prepreg in the finished board;
- imply that all four-layer boards have equal dielectric spacing;
- imply exact symmetry is mandatory in every design;
- imply symmetry guarantees zero warpage;
- show exact mirrored trace patterns as the definition of symmetry;
- imply more layers automatically improve cooling or stiffness;
- include numerical dielectric thicknesses;
- include controlled-impedance values;
- include trace-width rules;
- include manufacturer stack-up tables;
- include proprietary material designations;
- add vias, plated holes, or component structures unless a tiny orientation cue is essential;
- turn the figure into a fabrication-process flow;
- reproduce a PCB-fabricator's published four-layer stack-up diagram;
- embed the figure number or external caption.
