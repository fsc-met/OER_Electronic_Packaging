## Suggested Content for Figure 2.7.5

**Figure title for authoring/reference only:**  
`Surface Finishes During Soldering`

**Instructional purpose:**  
Prevent the misconception that a PCB surface finish always remains as an unchanged permanent top layer after assembly.

The figure should compare, at a **simplified conceptual level**, what happens to the three primary finishes during soldering:

- **HASL:** solder-based finish remelts and mixes with assembly solder;
- **ENIG:** thin immersion gold dissolves into molten solder, exposing the nickel interface;
- **OSP:** organic preservative is displaced/removed so solder can wet the underlying copper.

The central teaching relationship is:

> **Surface finish protects the bare PCB before assembly, but different finishes participate differently when soldering begins.**

**Filename:**  
`2.7.5 - Surface Finishes During Soldering.png`

**Intended public width:** 900 px

**Recommended aspect ratio:** approximately 16:9

# Column A - HASL

## Before soldering

Show:

- copper pad;
- solder-based HASL coating.

Required labels:

- `Copper`
- `HASL solder coating`

## During soldering

Show:

- molten assembly solder above/around the pad;
- original HASL solder coating no longer drawn as a separate rigid layer;
- visual indication that it has remelted and joined/mixed with the assembly solder.

Required note:

`HASL coating remelts and mixes with assembly solder`

Do not show detailed intermetallic layers.

Do not imply that HASL chemically disappears.

# Column B - ENIG

## Before soldering

Show:

- copper;
- electroless nickel;
- thin immersion gold.

Required labels:

- `Copper`
- `Nickel`
- `Thin immersion gold`

## During soldering

Show:

- molten solder above the nickel;
- the thin gold layer no longer present as a continuous top film;
- optional small dots/trace cue in the solder to indicate dissolved gold, but keep it conceptual.

Required note:

`Thin gold dissolves; solder interfaces primarily with nickel`

Do not imply that the nickel barrier immediately disappears.

Do not show ordinary ENIG as thick hard gold.

# Column C - OSP

## Before soldering

Show:

- copper;
- very thin organic OSP coating.

Required labels:

- `Copper`
- `OSP`

## During soldering

Show:

- molten solder contacting the copper;
- OSP coating displaced/removed from the wetted interface.

Required note:

`OSP is displaced so solder can wet copper`

Do not show OSP remaining as a barrier between solder and copper.

# Technical Verification Basis

## Current OER context

Use Sections 2.7.4-2.7.6 and 2.7.13:

- HASL leaves a solder-based finish;
- ENIG has thin immersion gold over nickel;
- during soldering, ENIG's thin gold dissolves into molten solder and the soldering interface develops primarily with nickel;
- OSP is removed/displaced during soldering so solder can react with copper;
- the finish is a bridge from bare-board fabrication to assembly.
