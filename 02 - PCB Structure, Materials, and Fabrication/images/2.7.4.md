## Suggested Content for Figure 2.7.4

**Figure title for authoring/reference only:**  
`Different Surface Functions May Require Different Finishes`

**Instructional purpose:**  
Show that one PCB can contain several exposed conductive regions with **different downstream functions**, so one universal finish may not be ideal for every exposed surface.

The figure should visually distinguish three example functions:

1. ordinary solder pads;
2. durable connector/contact fingers;
3. specialized wire-bond pads.

The central teaching relationship is:

> **Surface-finish selection follows the required function of the exposed surface.**

The figure should introduce **selective finishing** conceptually without prescribing one universal finish combination.

**Filename:**  
`2.7.4 - Different Surface Functions May Require Different Finishes.png`

**Intended public width:** 900 px

**Recommended aspect ratio:** approximately 16:9

Use an original **single simplified PCB top view** with three clearly separated callout regions.

Recommended regions:

- left or center: ordinary component solder pads;

# Selective-Finishing Concept

Include one concise teaching cue:

`Different regions can require different finish specifications`

`Selective finishing may be used`

# Required Technical Relationships

The final figure must correctly communicate:

- exposed PCB surfaces can serve different functions;
- ordinary solder pads prioritize solderability and often planarity;
- connector/contact surfaces may require durability and stable electrical contact;
- wire-bond surfaces require compatibility with the selected bonding process;
- a finish suitable for soldering is not automatically ideal for contact wear or wire bonding;
- selective finishing may be used where different functions coexist;
- no one universal finish combination is prescribed.

# Technical Verification Basis

## Current OER context

Use Section 2.7.11:

- solder pads, connector/contact surfaces, and wire-bond pads can have different finish requirements;
- OSP is generally unsuitable for durable exposed contacts;
- ENIG can support some contact functions;
- durable edge contacts may use separately specified electrolytic nickel/gold;
- wire bonding requires a finish qualified for the bonding process;
- selective finishing may therefore be used.

## Source hierarchy

Use the Chapter 2 framing first and approved PCB references to verify the contact/bonding distinction.

Do not copy a selective-plating example from a vendor or textbook.
