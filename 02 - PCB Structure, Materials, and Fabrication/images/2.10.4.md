## Suggested Content for Figure 2.10.4

**Figure title for authoring/reference only:**  
`PCB Warpage Can Disrupt Assembly and System Integration`

**Instructional purpose:**  
Show why PCB warpage matters in practice rather than as a flatness number only.

The figure should help MET readers connect warpage to four practical consequence categories:

1. uneven contact with tooling or support surfaces;
2. changed component / solder-joint geometry during or after assembly;
3. connector, card-guide, or enclosure misalignment;
4. uneven contact with inspection or test fixtures.

The central teaching message is:

> **Warpage changes the physical relationships that later manufacturing, test, and system-integration steps depend on.**

**Canonical filename:**  
`2.10.4 - PCB Warpage Can Disrupt Assembly and System Integration.png`

**Intended public width:** 900 px

**Recommended authoring canvas:** approximately `1800 × 1100` px unless a modestly different canvas improves clarity.

**Recommended aspect ratio:** approximately 16:10 or 5:3.

---

# Overall Layout

Use a clean **four-panel consequence figure** arranged as a 2×2 matrix or a clear four-panel horizontal/vertical composition.

Recommended panel set:

1. `Uneven support / tooling contact`
2. `Changed component / solder-joint geometry`
3. `Connector / card-guide misalignment`
4. `Uneven inspection / test-fixture contact`

Each panel should show a warped PCB in a simplified engineering setting.

Recommended examples:

- Panel 1: a warped board resting unevenly on support rails, tooling, or a process support surface;
- Panel 2: a surface-mount package on a curved board with visibly altered board-to-component geometry and uneven solder-joint gap/stand-off;
- Panel 3: a warped board misaligning with a card guide, edge connector, or mating connector interface;
- Panel 4: a warped board making uneven contact with a simple bed-of-nails/test or inspection fixture.

A small shared cue such as `Warped PCB` may be used consistently across panels.

Do not place the external figure number, final public caption, revision notes, or a large decorative title inside the artwork.

The figure should remain legible when reduced to the 900 px mdBook display width.

---

# Required Technical Relationships

- Each panel must show a **physical consequence**, not just a repeated warped-board sketch.
- The panel sequence must remain at **recognition level** rather than becoming a detailed Chapter 4 or Chapter 7/8 analysis.
- Uneven support/tooling contact should show incomplete or nonuniform board contact.
- Changed component/solder-joint geometry should show that board curvature alters the geometry between the PCB and the mounted component.
- Connector/card-guide misalignment should show difficulty aligning the board with a mating mechanical/electrical interface.
- Inspection/test-fixture contact should show incomplete or inconsistent interface contact.
- The figure must communicate that warpage can matter during:
  - assembly;
  - inspection/test;
  - installation/system integration.
- The figure should support the section's claim that forcing a warped board flat can transfer loads into interfaces, but it should not require a detailed stress analysis.
