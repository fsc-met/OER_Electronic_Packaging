## Suggested Content for Figure 2.6.3

**Figure title for authoring/reference only:**  
`Through, Blind, Buried, and Microvia Structures`

**Instructional purpose:**  
Provide a recognition-level comparison of four common via extents through a multilayer PCB stack-up.

The figure should help MET readers visually distinguish:

- **through via**;
- **blind via**;
- **buried via**;
- **microvia**.

The figure should answer:

> **How far does each via structure extend through the PCB, and which layers can it connect?**

The central teaching point is:

> **Via types differ mainly in the depth and layer range of the interconnection. More localized vias preserve routing area but generally require more specialized fabrication.**

**Filename:**  
`2.6.3 - Through Blind Buried and Microvia Structures.png`

**Intended width:** 900 px in Markdown

**Recommended aspect ratio:** approximately 16:9

# Overall Layout

Use a clean **four-panel multilayer cross-section comparison**.

Recommended arrangement from left to right:

1. `Through via`
2. `Blind via`
3. `Buried via`
4. `Microvia`

Use the same simplified layer stack and same board-thickness scale in all four panels.

The figure is a **structural comparison**, not a sequential fabrication diagram.

Do not place the external figure number, final caption, or revision information inside the artwork.

---

# Common Stack-Up

Use a simplified multilayer rigid PCB with enough copper layers to make the differences obvious.

Recommended:

- top copper;
- several internal copper layers;
- bottom copper;
- dielectric between layers.

Use 6 or more copper levels visually if needed, but keep the graphic readable.

Do not present the layer count as universal.

---

# Panel A - Through Via

Show:

- via opening extending from top surface to bottom surface;
- continuous plated barrel through the full board thickness;
- connection to at least two selected copper layers;
- clearance on one or more non-connected layers if space allows.

Required label:

- `Through via`

Optional note:

- `Full board thickness`

---

# Panel B - Blind Via

Show:

- via starting at the top outer layer;
- terminating on an internal layer;
- no opening on the bottom surface.

Required label:

- `Blind via`

Optional note:

- `Outer layer to internal layer`

The via may connect more than one layer if desired, but the main geometry must remain unambiguous.

---

# Panel C - Buried Via

Show:

- via entirely inside the multilayer construction;
- no opening on either outer surface;
- connection between internal copper layers.

Required label:

- `Buried via`

Optional note:

- `Internal layers only`

The surrounding top and bottom portions of the finished board should visibly continue over the buried via region.

Do not accidentally draw a blind via.

---

# Panel D - Microvia

Show a **small, shallow** interconnection.

Recommended geometry:

- surface layer to the immediately adjacent or nearby layer;
- tapered or slightly conical opening is acceptable to suggest laser formation;
- visibly shallower than the blind-via example.

Required label:

- `Microvia`

Optional note:

- `Small, shallow HDI interconnection`

Do not assign a numerical diameter or depth.

Do not imply that every microvia is identical in shape or must connect only one exact layer pair.

---

# Required Technical Relationships

The figure must correctly communicate:

- through via = full-thickness interconnection;
- blind via = outer surface to internal layer(s), without reaching the opposite surface;
- buried via = internal-only interconnection;
- microvia = small, shallow localized HDI interconnection;
- localized via structures can preserve routing area on layers not penetrated by a through via;
- blind/buried/microvia structures generally require more specialized fabrication planning;
- the figure is recognition-level and does not define a complete HDI architecture.
