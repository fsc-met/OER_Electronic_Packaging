## Suggested Content for Figure 2.6.4

**Figure title for authoring/reference only:**  
`Via-in-Pad, Filled Via, and Capped Via`

**Instructional purpose:**  
Provide a compact mixed-view figure that prevents three related PCB terms from being treated as synonyms:

- **via-in-pad** describes **where the via is located**;
- **filled via** describes **material placed inside the via cavity**;
- **capped / plated-over via** describes **additional metal covering the via opening**.

The figure should answer:

> **How do via location, via filling, and surface capping differ physically?**

The central teaching point is:

> **A via can be in a pad without being filled, can be filled without being in a pad, and can be capped/plated over as an additional surface-processing step.**

**Filename:**  
`2.6.4 - Via-in-Pad Filled Via and Capped Via.png`

**Intended width:** 900 px in Markdown

**Recommended aspect ratio:** approximately 16:9

---

# Overall Layout

Use a clean **four-panel instructional comparison**.

Because via-in-pad is easiest to recognize from the top and fill/cap is easiest to recognize in cross-section, a **mixed-view layout is preferred**:

- **Panel A - Top view:** conventional pad with nearby via
- **Panel B - Top view:** via-in-pad
- **Panel C - Cross-section:** filled via
- **Panel D - Cross-section:** capped / plated-over filled via

Clearly label view type where useful.

Do not force all four concepts into identical views if that reduces clarity.

Do not place the external figure number, final caption, or revision information inside the artwork.

---

# Panel A - Conventional Pad with Nearby Via

Use a simple top view.

Show:

- one rectangular or rounded component pad;
- a separate small via nearby;
- short trace connecting pad to via if useful.

Required label:

- `Conventional pad + nearby via`

Optional note:

- `Via outside pad`

This provides a baseline for comparison with via-in-pad.

---

# Panel B - Via-in-Pad

Use the same top-view scale as Panel A.

Show:

- same general component pad shape;
- via opening located **inside the pad area**.

Required label:

- `Via-in-pad`

Optional note:

- `Via located within component pad`

Do not automatically show fill/cap in this panel unless a small secondary note explains that those are separate processing choices.

The key concept is **location**, not fill material.

---

# Panel C - Filled Via

Use a simple cross-section.

Show:

- plated via barrel;
- internal cavity occupied by a visually distinct fill material;
- copper wall remaining visible around the fill.

Required labels:

- `Filled via`
- `Fill material`
- `Plated barrel`

Important:

- fill material must not visually replace the copper barrel;
- fill material must not be automatically labeled conductive;
- use a neutral fill color/pattern distinct from copper.

Recommended note:

- `Fill may be conductive or nonconductive depending on process/application`

Keep that note concise or place it outside the artwork if crowded.

---

# Panel D - Capped / Plated-Over Via

Use a cross-section directly comparable to Panel C.

Show:

- filled via cavity;
- plated barrel;
- additional copper cap / plated-over surface spanning the via opening;
- top surface visually continuous enough to communicate the concept.

Required labels:

- `Capped / plated-over via`
- `Copper cap / plated-over surface`

Optional:

- `Planarized fill`

Do not show a solder joint or component lead.

The purpose is bare-board structure.

# Required Technical Relationships

The final figure must correctly communicate:

- conventional pad + nearby via and via-in-pad differ primarily in via location;
- via-in-pad does not automatically mean filled or capped;
- filled via contains added material inside the cavity while retaining plated-wall structure;
- fill material is not necessarily conductive;
- capped/plated-over via includes added metal over the opening;
- fill and cap are fabrication/process choices that may be combined;
- the four terms describe related but different geometry/process attributes.

## Primary curriculum source

## Current OER source

- Current Section 2.6.6:
  - via-in-pad = via located within component pad;
  - fill = material intentionally occupying some or most of the via void;
  - fill may be conductive or nonconductive;
  - capped/plated-over via = additional metal over the opening;
  - the terms are related but not synonyms.
