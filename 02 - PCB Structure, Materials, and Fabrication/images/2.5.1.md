# Left Panel - Common Surface Copper Geometry

Show a generic rectangular bare-PCB fragment with only enough detail to identify the required copper features.

## 1. Trace

Show one clearly visible narrow copper conductor running between two local copper features.

Required label:

- `Trace`

Required relationship:

- the trace must physically connect to at least one pad/land;
- it must lie on the PCB surface as patterned copper rather than float above the board;
- its width should be visibly narrower than the large plane/pour region.

Do not add numerical trace width or spacing values.

Do not imply that one shown width is a universal design rule.

---

## 2. Surface-Mount Pads / Lands

Show at least two simple rectangular surface-mount pads/lands.

Recommended arrangement:

- two or more rectangular copper pads;
- one pad connected to the visible trace;
- enough separation that the pad shape can be recognized clearly.

Required label:

- `Pad / land`

The pads should be shown as copper features only. Do not place an actual electronic component on them.

Do not introduce solder-joint geometry or footprint-design dimensions; those belong in later chapters.

---

## 3. Large Plane / Copper-Pour Region

Show one broad copper-filled region occupying a visibly larger area than the traces and pads.

Required label:

- `Plane / copper-pour region`

The region may use a simple rectangular or polygonal boundary.

The purpose is to communicate a **large continuous copper area**, not a specific CAD implementation.

Do not imply:

- every plane fills an entire layer;
- every copper pour is necessarily ground;
- plane and pour are universally identical software objects;
- a large copper region automatically solves every thermal problem.

A small connection from a trace or pad into the region is acceptable if it remains physically clear.

# Right Panel - Enlarged Plated-Hole Connections

Use two enlarged circular examples at similar scale.

These are conceptual **top views**. They should be large enough that the hole, annular ring, spokes, and clearance gaps remain readable at 900 px display width.

## 4. Plated-Hole Pad and Annular Ring

Show:

- a circular hole at the center;
- a thin copper-colored inner-wall/rim cue at the hole edge so the feature reads as plated rather than non-plated;
- a surrounding copper pad/land;
- a visible copper ring around the hole.

Required labels:

- `Plated hole`
- `Annular ring`

Optional label:

- `Pad / land`

Required relationship:

- the annular ring must be the copper **surrounding the finished plated-hole opening**, not the thin barrel-plating cue at the inner wall;
- the label/leader must point to the copper ring, not to the hole opening;
- the hole should be centered in this idealized recognition-level example.

Do not show a minimum annular-ring dimension or acceptance criterion.

Do not imply that actual manufactured annular rings are always perfectly concentric.

---

## 5. Thermal-Relief Connection

Show a circular plated-hole pad located inside or adjacent to a large copper region.

The pad should connect to the surrounding copper through **four narrow spokes** unless a different simple spoke count gives better clarity.

Required labels:

- `Thermal relief`
- `Copper spokes`
- `Clearance gaps`

The surrounding large copper region should remain visibly continuous beyond the clearance pattern.

Required technical relationship:

- the spokes must provide real copper paths from the pad to the surrounding plane/pour;
- the clearance gaps must interrupt the otherwise solid connection;
- the geometry must clearly show that the pad is electrically connected but has a more restricted thermal-conduction path than a solid connection.

Do not use heat-flow arrows if they make the figure crowded. The surrounding Section 2.5 text already explains the soldering purpose.

Do not depict the thermal relief as a thermal via.

# Required Technical Relationships

The final figure must correctly communicate all of the following:

- traces and pads/lands are patterned copper features on the PCB;
- a trace is narrower than the large plane/pour region and physically connects intended copper features;
- a pad/land is a localized copper connection area;
- a large plane/pour region is a broad copper area, not a floating sheet above the PCB;
- the annular ring is the copper surrounding the drilled/plated hole;
- the hole opening and annular ring are different physical features;
- a thermal relief uses narrow spokes to maintain electrical continuity to a large copper region;
- clearance gaps interrupt the otherwise solid copper connection around the thermal-relief pad;
- the thermal-relief geometry is physically different from a solid annular-ring connection;
- all shown features belong to a **bare PCB**, not a populated PCBA.

# Prohibited or Misleading Elements

Do not:

- show mounted electronic components;
- make the figure look like a finished PCBA;
- show traces floating above the board;
- confuse a pad/land with the annular ring;
- point the `Annular ring` label to the hole opening;
- show a thermal relief with no electrical connection to the plane/pour;
- show clearance gaps that completely isolate the thermal-relief pad;
- depict a thermal relief as a cluster of thermal vias;
- imply that every plated hole uses a thermal relief;
- imply that every plane/pour is ground;
- imply that plane and pour are universally identical CAD terms;
- add trace width, annular-ring, clearance, or spoke dimensions;
- introduce IPC acceptance values or manufacturer capability limits;
- add controlled-impedance or high-speed routing details;
- add thermal-via arrays;
- use a real commercial PCB layout as the basis of the artwork;
- trace or closely redraw a textbook, lecture-slide, manufacturer, or IPC figure.
