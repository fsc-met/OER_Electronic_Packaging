# Figure 2.12.6 Suggested Content

## Figure title for authoring/reference only
**Figure 2.12.6 - From Bare-Board Defect Observation to Corrective Action**

## Instructional purpose
Visually support the section’s major MET-level learning objective: **a visible defect is not the same as its root cause**. This figure should turn the recurring troubleshooting logic into a simple usable workflow.

## Central teaching message
Bare-board troubleshooting should follow:
**Observation → Defect location → Structure/process mechanism → Plausible causes → Evidence → Confirmed cause → Corrective action**

The engineer/technologist should not jump directly from observation to assumed cause.

- Preferred canvas: **1800 × 1100 px**

## Overall layout
Use a **clean workflow diagram**.
Recommended layout:
- main horizontal or vertical flow from left to right or top to bottom;
- a small compact example beneath or beside the main flow using **open net / plating void**.

Main flow boxes:
1. Observed condition
2. Locate defect family
3. Identify physical mechanism
4. List plausible causes
5. Gather evidence
6. Confirm cause
7. Correct process / design / inspection control

Under `Locate defect family`, visually indicate categories such as:
- Copper pattern
- Hole / plating
- Laminate / interface
- Surface condition
- Geometry

## Required objects and labels
Required workflow labels:
- `Observed condition`
- `Locate defect family`
- `Identify physical mechanism`
- `List plausible causes`
- `Gather evidence`
- `Confirm cause`
- `Corrective action`

Required evidence examples:
- `Electrical test`
- `Optical inspection`
- `Dimensional measurement`
- `Microsection`
- `Process / lot history`

Required compact example labels (or equivalent):
- `Open net`
- `Plated-hole discontinuity`
- `Possible plating void`
- `Microsection confirms void`
- `Correct hole preparation / plating control`

## Required technical relationships
- The flow must emphasize that **evidence comes before confirmed cause**.
- The workflow should make `plausible causes` clearly different from `confirmed cause`.
- The example should be consistent with Section 2.12.9: an open net may lead to investigation of a plated-hole discontinuity and evidence such as microsection.
- Keep the workflow specific enough to be useful but general enough to apply beyond plating voids.

## Spatial / geometry requirements
- Keep box count manageable and labels concise.
- Use enough spacing that the workflow remains legible at 900 px.
- The compact example should not overpower the main workflow.
- If arrows are used, direction must be unmistakable and should not cross excessively.
