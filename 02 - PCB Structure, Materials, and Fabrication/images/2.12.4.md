# Figure 2.12.4 Suggested Content

## Figure title for authoring/reference only
**Figure 2.12.4 - Moisture and Electrical Bias Can Enable CAF Growth in PCB Laminate**

## Instructional purpose
Give MET students a **recognition-level conceptual model** of conductive anodic filament (CAF) development inside reinforced laminate, since CAF is one of the least intuitive defect mechanisms in the section.

## Central teaching message
CAF is an **internal electrochemical degradation mechanism**, not simply a visible surface copper bridge. It can develop when:
- two conductors are separated by reinforced laminate;
- moisture is present;
- electrical bias exists;
- ionic/electrochemical transport occurs along a susceptible glass-resin path.

`900`

- Preferred canvas: **1800 × 1100 px**

## Overall layout
Use one clear **conceptual cross-section** or **cutaway-style schematic** showing:
- two separated conductive features;
- reinforced laminate between them;
- moisture ingress;
- bias direction or + / – conductor indication;
- conceptual filament path developing along a susceptible internal path.

A simple left-to-right or top-to-bottom causal story is acceptable, but keep it within one integrated figure rather than a poster with many text boxes.

## Required objects and labels
Required labels:
- `Conductor`
- `Moisture`
- `Electrical bias`
- `Glass-resin path` or `Susceptible glass-resin interface`
- `Conceptual CAF growth path`

Optional clarifying labels:
- `Ionic/electrochemical transport`
- `Initially isolated conductors`

Required objects:
- two conductive features separated by dielectric;
- visible reinforcement indication (stylized glass-resin path);
- arrows indicating moisture entry and filament-growth tendency;
- plus/minus or voltage-bias indication.

## Required technical relationships
- The conductive features must begin **electrically isolated**.
- CAF growth must be shown **inside the laminate**, not on the outer surface as a simple copper short.
- Moisture and electrical bias must both be visibly represented as enabling conditions.
- The growth path should follow a plausible internal path associated with reinforcement/glass-resin structure.
- Keep the mechanism at conceptual recognition level; do not overclaim exact chemistry.

## Spatial / geometry requirements
- Make the internal path clearly visible without clutter.
- Avoid too many arrows; each arrow should have one job.
- Keep labels short and readable at 900 px.
- Maintain safe margins.

## Color / style guidance
- White background.
- Conductor features in copper tone.
- Laminate in pale neutral color.
- Moisture arrows/drops in muted blue if needed.
- CAF path distinct but not alarming; a dark or contrasting line is acceptable.
- No dramatic warning graphics or decorative effects.

## Scope boundaries
Do not include:
- detailed electrochemistry equations;
- life-prediction curves;
- environmental chamber testing setups;
- advanced reliability statistics;
- unrelated corrosion mechanisms.

## Technical-verification basis
Verify against Section 2.12.6:
- moisture;
- electrical bias;
- ionic species / electrochemical transport;
- susceptible glass-resin path;
- initially isolated conductors becoming bridged by a conductive path.

## Originality / copyright requirements
- Original conceptual schematic only.
- No copied CAF micrographs or vendor figures.
- No traced published diagrams.

## Generation guidance
The figure should reduce student confusion about **where** CAF grows and **why it is different** from a surface copper bridge. Simplicity is more important than chemical detail.
