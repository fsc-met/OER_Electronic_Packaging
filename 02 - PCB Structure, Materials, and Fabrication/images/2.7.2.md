## Suggested Content for Figure 2.7.2

**Figure title for authoring/reference only:**  
`Simplified Structures of HASL, ENIG, and OSP`

**Instructional purpose:**  
Provide a direct physical comparison of the three primary finishes emphasized in Section 2.7:

- HASL / lead-free HASL;
- ENIG;
- OSP.

The figure should answer:

> **What physically sits on top of the exposed copper for each finish?**

The key teaching relationship is:

- **HASL:** solder coating over copper;
- **ENIG:** nickel barrier plus thin immersion-gold protection over copper;
- **OSP:** very thin organic preservative directly over copper.

The figure should also make the qualitative **planarity difference** visible without turning into a numeric thickness chart.

**Filename:**  
`2.7.2 - Simplified Structures of HASL ENIG and OSP.png`

**Intended public width:** 900 px

**Recommended aspect ratio:** approximately 16:9

---

# Overall Layout

Use a clean **three-panel cross-sectional comparison**.

Recommended left-to-right order:

1. `HASL / Lead-Free HASL`
2. `ENIG`
3. `OSP`

Use the same conceptual copper-pad width and substrate scale in all three panels.

The figure is a **structure comparison**, not a fabrication process sequence.

Do not place the external figure number, final caption, revision notes, or a large overall title inside the artwork.

---

# Common Base Geometry

Each panel should include:

- dielectric substrate;
- exposed copper pad;
- finish on the copper pad.

Solder mask may be shown at the pad edges if useful, but it should not distract from the finish stack.

Keep copper thickness visually consistent across all three panels.

---

# Panel A - HASL / Lead-Free HASL

Show:

- copper pad;
- solder-based coating over exposed copper;
- a top surface that is visibly **less planar / slightly rounded or uneven** relative to ENIG and OSP.

Required labels:

- `Copper`
- `Solder coating`
- `HASL / Lead-Free HASL`

Do not make the coating so exaggerated that it looks like a completed assembly solder joint.

Do not show:

- component lead;
- solder ball;
- stencil;
- hot-air knives;
- fabrication bath.

The coating should look like a bare-board finish.

---

# Panel B - ENIG

Show the simplified layer sequence:

> **copper -> nickel -> thin gold**

Required labels:

- `Copper`
- `Electroless nickel`
- `Thin immersion gold`
- `ENIG`

The nickel layer should be visibly thicker than the gold layer in the conceptual graphic.

The gold layer must be visible but clearly identified as **thin**.

The upper surface should be visually planar.

Do not show gold as a thick durable connector coating.

Do not call ENIG “hard gold.”

---

# Panel C - OSP

Show:

- copper pad;
- very thin organic coating directly on copper.

Required labels:

- `Copper`
- `Very thin organic coating`
- `OSP`

The top surface should be planar.

The organic coating must be visible enough to teach the concept, even though its thickness is necessarily exaggerated.

Do not draw a substantial metallic layer above the copper.

---

# Not-to-Scale Requirement

Because the immersion-gold layer and OSP coating are too thin to show realistically at textbook scale, include a short unobtrusive note:

`Finish thicknesses exaggerated for visibility — not to scale`

This is important technical QA.

Do not provide numerical thicknesses.

---

# Required Technical Relationships

The final figure must correctly communicate:

- all three finishes protect intentionally exposed copper;
- HASL leaves a solder-based coating;
- ENIG contains a nickel barrier with thin immersion gold above it;
- OSP is a very thin organic coating directly on copper;
- ENIG and OSP are highly planar relative to the conceptual HASL example;
- the figure compares finish structure, not complete solder-joint metallurgy;
- the thicknesses are intentionally exaggerated and not quantitative.

# Technical Verification Basis

## Primary curriculum framing

Use the Chapter 2 instructional material for the role of surface finish in protecting exposed copper and preserving solderability.

## Current OER context

Use Sections 2.7.4-2.7.7:

- HASL = solder-based surface coating;
- ENIG = copper / electroless nickel / thin immersion gold;
- OSP = very thin organic preservative on copper;
- ENIG and OSP are very planar;
- HASL is generally less planar.

## Approved PCB references

Use approved reference books to verify structure/terminology only.

Do not copy their diagrams.
