# Figure 2.12.6 Suggested Content

## Figure title for authoring/reference only
**Figure 2.12.6 - Common Solder-Mask and Surface-Finish Defects on a Bare PCB**

## Instructional purpose
Help MET students visually distinguish common **bare-board surface-condition defects** from the copper-pattern, hole/plating, laminate/interface, and CAF defects already covered in Figures 2.12.1-2.12.4.

The figure should make clear that:

- **solder-mask defects** primarily concern coverage, registration, and local integrity of the mask layer; and
- **surface-finish defects** primarily concern the condition and continuity of the intended solderable/contact surface.

## Central teaching message

> **Solder-mask nonconformance changes where the mask covers or exposes the PCB surface, while surface-finish nonconformance changes the quality or continuity of the solderable/contact surface itself.**

The figure should teach **visual recognition**, not detailed finish chemistry or acceptance criteria.

## Overall layout

Use a clean **five-case comparison** with one normal reference and four defect cases.

Recommended order:

1. **Normal reference**
2. **Solder-mask misregistration / pad encroachment**
3. **Missing or damaged solder mask**
4. **Incomplete / nonuniform surface finish**
5. **Contaminated / oxidized solderable surface**

A 3-over-2 panel arrangement is recommended so each panel remains large enough to be readable at 900 px.

Keep the same nominal pad/trace geometry across cases whenever possible so the **surface-condition difference** is the main variable.

## Required objects and labels

### Common geometry

Each panel should use a simplified **top view of a bare PCB surface** containing:

- PCB substrate;
- copper pad and/or short copper trace;
- solder-mask layer around the pad;
- exposed pad surface;
- generic surface finish on the exposed pad where appropriate.

### Required panel labels

- `Normal reference`
- `Solder-mask misregistration / pad encroachment`
- `Missing / damaged solder mask`
- `Incomplete / nonuniform surface finish`
- `Contaminated / oxidized surface`

### Required feature callouts

#### Normal reference
Show:
- centered solder-mask opening;
- full pad exposure where intended;
- uniform generic finish coverage.

Optional concise label:
- `Correct mask opening`
- `Uniform finish`

#### Solder-mask misregistration / pad encroachment
Show:
- mask opening shifted relative to the copper pad;
- part of the intended solderable pad covered by solder mask.

Required callout:
- `Mask covers part of pad`

#### Missing / damaged solder mask
Show:
- local unintended loss or break in solder-mask coverage;
- copper exposed where mask should normally protect it.

Required callout:
- `Unintended exposed copper`

A small lifted/blistered edge may be shown only if it remains visually simple and does not confuse the panel with laminate blistering from Figure 2.12.3.

#### Incomplete / nonuniform surface finish
Show:
- intended exposed pad;
- finish layer covering only part of the pad or appearing clearly nonuniform in thickness/coverage.

Required callout:
- `Incomplete finish coverage`

The artwork should remain **generic** and should not imply one specific finish chemistry unless explicitly labeled as conceptual.

#### Contaminated / oxidized solderable surface
Show:
- otherwise correctly exposed pad;
- localized contamination/discoloration/oxidation cue on the solderable/contact surface.

Required callout:
- `Surface contamination / oxidation`

Avoid implying that any specific color alone proves a particular chemical mechanism.

## Required technical relationships

The figure must show the following distinctions correctly:

1. **Mask registration defect**
   - the copper pad itself remains present;
   - the solder-mask opening is shifted or incorrectly positioned;
   - the mask partially covers a region that should be exposed.

2. **Mask damage / missing mask**
   - the defect is in the mask layer;
   - copper becomes exposed where mask coverage was intended;
   - do not depict the copper trace itself as etched away.

3. **Incomplete / nonuniform surface finish**
   - the copper pad remains geometrically correct;
   - the problem is the finish layer on the exposed pad;
   - do not confuse this with missing copper or solder-mask coverage.

4. **Contaminated / oxidized surface**
   - the exposed pad remains physically present;
   - the surface condition is abnormal;
   - the drawing must remain generic and should not claim that visual discoloration alone identifies the exact chemistry.

5. The **normal reference** must make the intended relationship among copper pad, mask opening, and finish obvious.

## Spatial / geometry requirements

- Keep all five cases at comparable scale.
- Use large pads and simple traces so the defect remains visible at 900 px.
- Avoid dense component-like geometry.
- Keep panel titles outside active artwork.
- Keep leader lines short and noncrossing.
- Preserve enough white space between panels.
- If a mask blister/lift is included as a secondary cue, use a small profile/inset only; do not convert the figure into a cross-sectional laminate-defect figure.
- Ensure no text or callout approaches the outer canvas edge.

## Color / style guidance

Use the established EPAC textbook style:

- **background:** explicit white;
- **PCB substrate:** muted pale green or neutral beige;
- **copper:** muted copper/orange;
- **solder mask:** restrained green or blue-green clearly distinguishable from the substrate;
- **surface finish:** neutral metallic tone appropriate for a generic finish, such as pale silver/gold-gray;
- **contamination/oxidation cue:** modest darker/brown-gray irregular area;
- **labels/arrows:** dark navy or dark gray.

The figure must remain understandable in grayscale through geometry and labeling.

Do not use:

- photorealistic textures;
- dramatic metallic reflections;
- strong gradients;
- warning icons;
- decorative backgrounds;
- embedded external figure number;
- final public caption inside the artwork;
- large duplicate title inside the figure.

## Scope boundaries

This figure is **bare-board defect recognition only**.

Do not add:

- solder-joint bridging;
- insufficient solder;
- tombstoning;
- component placement errors;
- reflow-process defects;
- detailed HASL, ENIG, OSP, immersion-silver, or immersion-tin chemistry;
- solderability test procedures;
- numerical IPC acceptance criteria;
- corrosion mechanisms beyond generic surface-condition recognition;
- assembly contamination scenarios.

Those topics belong in Section 2.7, Chapter 4, or later reliability content.

## Technical-verification basis

Primary basis:

- current Section 2.12.7 text;
- current Section 2.12 bare-board defect scope;
- earlier Section 2.7 treatment of solder mask and surface finishes.

The figure must be consistent with the section statements that solder-mask defects can include:

- misregistration;
- mask covering part of a pad;
- exposed copper where coverage was intended;
- local bubbles/blisters/separation or physical damage;

and that surface-finish problems can include:

- incomplete/nonuniform coverage;
- contamination;
- oxidation/discoloration;
- damaged contact surfaces;
- solderability-related surface-condition problems.

The figure should remain at **recognition level** and should not imply that visual inspection alone determines the exact finish chemistry or root cause.
