# Figure 3.8.1 Suggested Content Specification

## Figure title for authoring/reference only
**Trace Width, Local Neck-Down, and Etch Margin**

## Instructional purpose
Show that copper manufacturability is controlled by the **narrowest local trace width**, not only by the nominal width assigned to the rest of a route.

## Central teaching message
A trace may be routed mainly at a comfortable width, but a short local neck-down becomes the controlling manufacturability feature. Small etching variation matters more when the local feature is already near the process limit.

## Canonical PNG filename
`3.8.1 - Trace Width Local Neck-Down and Etch Margin.png`

## Intended mdBook display width
`900`

## Recommended canvas size / aspect ratio
- Recommended aspect ratio: **5:3**
- Recommended working canvas: **2000 × 1200 px** minimum
- SVG may use any equivalent proportional viewBox with safe margins

## Overall layout
Use a **single-row, three-panel top-view comparison** on a white background:

1. **Panel A – Uniform trace**
2. **Panel B – Trace with local neck-down**
3. **Panel C – Same neck-down with representative etch loss / reduced margin**

All panels should be aligned horizontally with consistent board/copper styling and equal visual weight.

## Required objects and labels
### Panel A
- one straight copper trace of uniform width;
- dimension arrow labeling **Nominal trace width**;
- label such as **Uniform width** or **No local bottleneck**.

### Panel B
- one trace of larger nominal width with one visibly narrower short section;
- dimension arrow labeling the wider nominal section;
- dimension arrow labeling the narrow short section as **Local minimum width**;
- label such as **Short neck-down controls manufacturability**.

### Panel C
- same general geometry as Panel B;
- visibly reduced copper margin at the narrow section to represent process variation after etching;
- label such as **Less remaining margin after etching variation**;
- optional dashed or ghosted outline showing nominal geometry if it helps clarity.

### Shared/General labels
- **Nominal trace width**
- **Local minimum width**
- **Representative etch loss** or **Representative etching variation**
- optional example values:
  - nominal width: **0.20 mm**
  - neck-down: **0.10 mm**
  - more comfortable alternative: **0.15 mm**

## Required technical relationships
- The narrowest local section must be clearly smaller than the surrounding trace.
- The figure must visually support the statement that the **local minimum width** is the controlling manufacturing feature.
- If numeric examples are shown, they must be consistent with the section:
  - common mainstream minimum reference: **0.10 mm (4 mil)**
  - more comfortable planning value: **0.15 mm (6 mil)**
  - wider surrounding example may be **0.20 mm**
- The “etch variation” panel must **not** imply guaranteed failure; it should communicate reduced margin, not deterministic open circuit.

## Spatial / geometry requirements
- Keep all traces horizontal for easy width comparison.
- Neck-down section should be short but clearly visible at mdBook size.
- Dimension arrows must not overlap trace edges or text.
- Use generous whitespace around labels and arrows.
- Keep linework simple and textbook-like; avoid decorative perspective effects.

## Color / style guidance
- White background.
- Use EPAC-consistent simple educational styling.
- Copper may be shown in a muted copper/orange tone.
- Dimension lines, leader lines, and labels should use dark gray/black.
- Minimal accent use only where needed for clarity.
- No gradients, shadows, textures, or poster-like styling.

## Scope boundaries
- This figure is about **trace-width manufacturability**, not current-carrying capacity, impedance, or thermal effects.
- Do not turn this into a Section 3.5 spacing figure.
- Do not include high-speed signal integrity discussion.
- Do not overload with fabrication tables.

## Technical-verification basis
Grounded in Section 3.8 and the project’s verified teaching inventory:
- trace manufacturability is controlled by the narrowest local width;
- common engineering-scale references used in the section:
  - **0.10 mm (4 mil)** mainstream minimum reference;

  - **0.20 mm** acceptable wider nominal example;
- representative finished-width variation concept is allowed, but must be shown qualitatively unless exact tolerance context is needed.
