# Figure 3.8.2 Suggested Content Specification

## Figure title for authoring/reference only
**Pad and Via Entry Geometry with Teardrops**

## Instructional purpose
Show how trace-to-pad and trace-to-via entry geometry can either reduce or improve local copper robustness, and illustrate where a teardrop can add manufacturing margin.

## Central teaching message
Poor pad/via entry geometry can create fragile local copper necks or grazing connections. A centered entry and, when appropriate, a teardrop can provide a more robust transition without claiming that teardrops are mandatory everywhere.

## Canonical PNG filename
`3.8.2 - Pad and Via Entry Geometry with Teardrops.png`

## Intended mdBook display width
`900`

## Recommended canvas size / aspect ratio
- Recommended aspect ratio: **5:3**
- Recommended working canvas: **2000 × 1200 px** minimum

## Overall layout
Use a **two-row comparison layout** or a **single-row four-panel layout** with compact cases:

- **Poor / less robust examples**
- **Improved / more robust examples**

Suggested cases:
1. abrupt narrow trace entering a round pad or via;
2. tangential/grazing trace entry;
3. centered radial entry;
4. centered entry with teardrop.

Optional small inset:
- shifted drill example showing why extra junction copper can preserve connection.

## Required objects and labels
Required feature types:
- one **round pad** example;
- one **via** example (may also be drawn as drilled circular pad);
- narrow trace segment connecting to them.

Required labels:
- **Pad**
- **Via**
- **Abrupt entry** or **Thin local neck**
- **Tangential / grazing entry**
- **Centered entry**
- **Teardrop**
- optional **Drill shift direction**
- optional **More remaining copper margin**

## Required technical relationships
- Poor examples must visibly show a smaller or more fragile copper junction.
- Improved examples must show smoother or better-centered copper attachment.
- If the drill-shift inset is used, it must show conceptually that the teardrop provides more copper around the junction; do not exaggerate into a dramatic failure cartoon.
- The figure must support the section statements that teardrops:
  - smooth the transition;
  - add copper at the junction;
  - may reduce disconnect risk under drill misregistration.
- The figure must also support the statement that teardrops are **optional**, not universal.

## Spatial / geometry requirements
- Keep all examples top-view and visually consistent.
- Use equal scale across comparable examples.
- Keep trace widths and pad/via sizes simple and not to exact IPC footprint detail.
- Leave safe space for leader lines and labels.
- If using poor/good columns, maintain consistent alignment to allow fast comparison.

## Color / style guidance
- White background.
- Simple educational line art, consistent with EPAC Chapter 3.
- Copper features in muted copper/orange.
- Dimension/leader lines and text in dark gray or black.
- Optional subtle green or neutral board backdrop only if it improves readability, but avoid heavy fill.

## Scope boundaries
- Do not turn this into a detailed land-pattern sizing figure from Section 3.6.
- Do not turn this into a hole-sizing figure from Section 3.7.
- Do not discuss solder-mask-defined geometry here.
- Do not imply that every connection needs a teardrop.

## Technical-verification basis
Based on verified Section 3.8 content:
- a centered entry provides clearer intended connection geometry;
- tangential/grazing entries can leave small local copper margin;
- teardrops add local copper and smooth the transition;
- teardrops can reduce risk that drill misregistration disconnects a trace from a drilled pad or via;
- teardrops must still obey spacing and related geometry rules.
