## Suggested Content for Figure 3.13.1

### Figure title for authoring/reference only

`Functional Anatomy of an Assembly Panel`

### Instructional purpose

Introduce the panel as a **temporary manufacturing object** that contains repeated product PCBs plus process geometry needed for handling, alignment, tooling, orientation, and later separation.

This figure should establish the vocabulary students need before the section discusses specific separation, fiducial, and conveyor/support details.

### Central teaching message

> **An assembly panel is not just several boards placed together. Rails, alignment features, handling edges, orientation cues, and separation features work together as one manufacturing interface.**

### Canonical PNG filename

`3.13.1 - Functional Anatomy of an Assembly Panel.png`

### Intended mdBook display width

`900 px`

### Recommended canvas size / aspect ratio

- Authoring canvas: approximately `1800 x 1100 px`.
- Recommended aspect ratio: approximately `16:10` to `5:3`.
- Use a top-view panel so rails, repeated boards, tooling holes, fiducials, and orientation are spatially clear.

### Overall layout

Use one large **top-view rectangular assembly panel**.

Recommended composition:

- four repeated PCB images in a `2 x 2` array;
- process rails on the outer panel edges;
- three clearly visible panel/global fiducials distributed asymmetrically around the panel, with one deliberately offset from a perfectly symmetric pattern;
- at least two non-plated tooling/alignment holes on the rails;
- visible separation features between board images;
- one clear orientation cue/asymmetric feature so a 180-degree panel reversal is visually distinguishable.

The figure should show the panel as a complete manufacturing object rather than as four isolated finished boards.

### Required objects and labels

Required labels:

- `Process rail / handling edge`
- `Repeated PCB`
- `Panel fiducial`
- `Tooling hole`
- `Separation feature`
- `Panel orientation`

Optional labels:

- `Conveyor / clamp region`
- `Temporary panel material`

Required visible objects:

- outer panel boundary;
- four repeated board outlines;
- panel rails;
- three circular fiducial targets;
- tooling holes;
- score/tab/separation indicators between or around boards;
- one asymmetric orientation cue.

### Required technical relationships

- Repeated PCBs must sit **inside** the panel and remain mechanically connected until separation.
- Rails must exist outside the finished-board outlines, not as part of the final product PCB.
- Fiducials should be placed on panel/rail material and distributed so the arrangement is not rotationally ambiguous.
- Tooling holes must be clearly different from fiducials:
  - tooling holes = mechanical through-holes;
  - fiducials = optical surface targets.
- Separation features must occur at temporary board/panel or board/board boundaries; do not show decorative cut lines that fail to connect to a real separation boundary.
- Orientation cue should be independent of the repeated-board artwork so the panel cannot be mistaken for a perfectly symmetric object.
- Do not imply that every panel must have exactly four boards.
- Do not imply that every panel uses exactly the same rail width, fiducial count, or tooling-hole pattern.

### Spatial / geometry requirements

- Keep board images evenly aligned and clearly separate from rails.
- Use matched board geometry for the repeated PCBs.
- Reserve enough outer rail width to make fiducials and tooling holes readable at 900 px.
- Fiducials should be separated widely around the panel rather than clustered.
- Avoid placing fiducials immediately against holes, board edges, or labels.
- Tooling holes should be large enough to read as holes, not copper targets.
- If score or tab features are shown, keep them simple and schematic; do not duplicate the detailed separation geometry reserved for Figure 3.13.2.
- Use a light dashed or shaded treatment for temporary panel material if helpful, but do not imply transparency.


### Color / style guidance

- Clean engineering-textbook appearance consistent with the current EPAC figure set.
- Explicit white background; no transparent-canvas dependence.
- Flat 2D top-view, side-view, or restrained schematic/isometric geometry only where it improves physical understanding.
- Restrained engineering palette:
  - green/blue-green PCB areas;
  - neutral gray panel rails, fixtures, tools, and mechanical features;
  - copper/gold for exposed copper or fiducial targets;
  - muted blue for normal paths/alignment/handling arrows;
  - limited orange/red only for break zones, keep-outs, or interference/problem conditions.
- Sans-serif labels sized for the 900 px mdBook render.
- Moderate line weights and consistent visual hierarchy.
- No decorative gradients, glossy rendering, dramatic shadows, logos, watermarks, or branded commercial products.
- Do not rely on color alone; use geometry, line style, labels, arrows, and good/bad structure.
- Do not embed the external figure number, final caption, or an oversized duplicate overall title inside the artwork.


### Scope boundaries

This figure should **not** teach:

- detailed V-score geometry;
- mouse-bite drill patterns;
- routed cutter diameter;
- fiducial target dimensions;
- tooling-hole dimensions;
- conveyor mechanics;
- board-support fixtures;
- stencil-printer or pick-and-place programming;
- optimization of board nesting.

Figure 3.13.2 owns separation geometry.  
Figure 3.13.3 owns global/local fiducial detail.  
Figure 3.13.4 owns conveyor/support access.

### Technical-verification basis

Use as grounding:

- current Section 3.13.1 and 3.13.8 text;
- Chapter 2 profiling/panelization discussion for the existence of arrays, rails, tabs, and score lines;
- Coombs assembly-tooling analysis of fiducials, conveyed edges, tooling holes, and break features;
- current supplier guidance showing process rails, panel fiducials, tooling holes, and repeated-board arrays.

Representative values such as `5 mm` rails should remain in the section prose/table, not be turned into a mandatory dimension in this overview figure.

### Originality / copyright requirements

Create an original generic assembly-panel composition.

Do not:

- trace a fabricator panel drawing;
- reproduce a vendor panel template;
- reuse a customer PCB panel image;
- use manufacturer logos, board artwork, or proprietary connector/module geometry.

The panel should be independently drawn using generic board shapes and manufacturing features.


### Generation guidance

Use a controlled technical-figure workflow.

1. Establish the composition from this specification.
2. If AI image generation is used for an early concept, preserve it only as a concept revision.
3. Once a promising visual baseline exists, freeze the visual language and make only targeted corrections.
4. Rebuild/refine as controlled vector artwork when exact rail geometry, score lines, routed gaps, fiducials, tooling holes, support points, or labels cannot be controlled reliably in raster generation.
5. Verify every word, arrow, leader, keep-out, alignment feature, and mechanical relationship after each meaningful revision.
6. Preserve every meaningful revision under a new filename.
7. Do not copy or rename a candidate to the canonical publication filename before explicit project-owner acceptance.



### Editable SVG requirements

The strongest final candidate should include both a publication PNG and a **true editable SVG**.

The SVG must:

- use vector geometry rather than an embedded raster image as the primary artwork;
- keep text editable where practical;
- keep panel rails, PCB images, score lines, tabs, holes, fiducials, support points, arrows, leaders, and labels as separate editable objects;
- define explicit `width`, `height`, and `viewBox` values;
- use `preserveAspectRatio="xMidYMid meet"`;
- include an explicit full-canvas white rectangle as the first visible drawing element, e.g. `<rect x="0" y="0" width="100%" height="100%" fill="#ffffff"/>`;
- preserve adequate safe margins so no rail, feature, arrow, or label is clipped;
- use `markerUnits="userSpaceOnUse"` for normal engineering arrows unless a specific technical reason requires otherwise;
- remain clear when rendered at native size and approximately 1600 px, 1200 px, and 900 px width.

Before recommendation, render-check the final SVG at all four sizes.
