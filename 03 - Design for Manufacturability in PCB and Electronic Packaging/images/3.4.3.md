## Suggested Content for Figure 3.4.3

**Figure title for authoring/reference only:**  
`Placement Conditions that Contribute to Tombstoning`

### Instructional purpose

Teach that **placement offset can contribute to tombstoning, but tombstoning is not a placement-only defect**.

The figure should give MET readers a simple visual comparison among:

1. a centered component with approximately symmetric conditions;
2. an off-center component that reduces geometric symmetry;
3. a component with another asymmetric condition, such as unequal copper/thermal mass, showing that non-placement factors also matter.

The figure should support recognition-level DFM reasoning without turning into a detailed reflow-process or solder-wetting lesson.

### Central teaching message

> **Tombstoning is a multi-factor defect. Off-center placement can increase risk, but pad geometry, paste balance, copper/thermal balance, and reflow conditions also affect the two ends.**

The figure must avoid implying:

> **Off-center placement always causes tombstoning.**

### Overall layout

Use a **three-panel horizontal comparison**.

#### Panel A — Preferred / balanced placement

Show:

- a small rectangular two-terminal chip component;
- two symmetric pads;
- component centered between the pads;
- approximately equal overlap/position at both ends;
- optional small symmetric solder-paste deposits shown schematically.

Label:

- `Centered placement`
- `Approximately symmetric geometry`

This panel is the reference condition, not a guarantee against all tombstoning.

#### Panel B — Placement-related risk

Show:

- the same component and pads;
- component noticeably shifted toward one pad;
- unequal geometric overlap at the two ends.

Label:

- `Placement offset`
- `Reduced symmetry`

Optional small note:

- `Can contribute to unbalanced wetting forces`

Do not imply inevitability.

#### Panel C — Other asymmetry matters too

Show:

- centered component;
- symmetric-looking pads;
- one pad connected to a visibly larger copper region / thermal mass;
- or another clearly labeled non-placement asymmetry.

Preferred choice:

- unequal copper area / thermal mass because it is visually understandable.

Label:

- `Unequal copper / thermal condition`
- `Another contributing factor`

At the far right or below all panels, a small simplified tombstoned component icon may be shown as a **possible outcome**, connected with dashed `can contribute` arrows from Panels B and C.

Do not connect Panel A to the tombstoned outcome.

### Required objects and labels

Required across all panels:

- same generic two-terminal chip component;
- same basic pad geometry;
- same viewing style and scale.

Panel A required labels:

- `Centered placement`
- `Preferred geometry`

Panel B required labels:

- `Placement offset`
- `Can increase risk`

Panel C required labels:

- `Unequal copper / thermal condition`
- `Also affects balance`

Global bottom note:

- `Tombstoning is multi-factor`

Optional explanatory phrase:

- `Placement, pad/paste symmetry, copper/thermal balance, and reflow conditions can interact.`

If a tombstoned outcome icon is used, label:

- `Possible tombstoning outcome`

Do not label it `Result` or `Will tombstone`.

### Required technical relationships

The final figure must correctly communicate:

1. The component is a two-terminal SMT chip part such as a resistor/capacitor form factor.
2. Tombstoning involves unequal forces/wetting behavior at the two ends during reflow.
3. A centered component over symmetric pads generally provides better geometric symmetry than an offset placement.
4. Placement offset can contribute to imbalance but is neither necessary nor sufficient by itself.
5. Unequal copper/thermal conditions can also contribute by changing heating/wetting behavior between the two ends.
6. The figure is conceptual; it must not imply a quantitative probability of tombstoning.
7. The same basic package/pad scale should be used across panels so the comparison is meaningful.
8. If solder paste is drawn, the deposits must sit on the pads and should not float beside them.
9. If arrows representing force/wetting are used, use equal arrows in Panel A and visibly unequal arrows only in the risk panels.
10. Do not show a deterministic process sequence from offset → tombstone.

### Spatial / geometry requirements

- Use consistent panel widths.
- Use the same component body size in all panels.
- Use the same pad size in Panels A and B.
- In Panel B, shift the component laterally enough to see the imbalance, but keep both ends plausibly over/near the intended pads.
- In Panel C, keep the component centered while changing the copper/thermal condition so readers can see that placement is not the only variable.
- If a larger copper area is shown, connect it physically to one pad with a copper trace/pour.
- Use dashed arrows for `can contribute` relationships to emphasize non-deterministic causation.
- Keep the tombstoned icon small and secondary if included.
- Avoid complex solder-meniscus geometry.
- Keep safe margins of at least 60 px.

### Technical-verification basis

#### Current Section 3.4

Use the current public text:

- tombstoning is a small two-terminal SMT component standing upright during reflow;
- placement offset can contribute;
- other contributors include unequal solder paste, asymmetric pad geometry, unequal copper/thermal conditions, and reflow conditions;
- DFM actions include centering the component and avoiding unnecessary asymmetry.

#### Primary instructional basis

Use the Chapter 3 placement/orientation material:

- tombstoning is introduced as a placement-related DFM example;
- component placement, paste/pad symmetry, and thermal/copper asymmetry are relevant contributors.

#### Approved reference hierarchy

Use approved assembly references to verify the multi-factor mechanism.

The specification deliberately avoids any single-cause statement because tombstoning is influenced by interacting geometric, solder, thermal, and process conditions.
