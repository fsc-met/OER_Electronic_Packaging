## Suggested Content for Figure 3.5.2

**Figure title for authoring/reference only:**  
`Pad, Trace, and Copper-to-Edge Spacing`

### Instructional purpose

Show how **copper-feature spacing** is measured for:

- pad-to-pad;
- trace-to-trace;
- trace-to-pad;
- copper-to-board-edge.

The figure should also make one important distinction visually clear:

> **Copper-to-board-edge spacing is not the same rule as component-to-board-edge spacing.**

This figure supports Sections 3.5.4-3.5.6 and should help readers apply the numerical spacing tables without taking over the detailed copper-geometry treatment in Section 3.8.

### Central teaching message

> **Pad, trace, and copper-to-edge clearances are measured edge-to-edge between copper features or between copper and the board edge.**

Secondary message:

> **Component-to-edge and copper-to-edge rules serve different manufacturing purposes and should not be interchanged.**

### Overall layout

Use a **2 × 2 measurement grid** with a narrow bottom comparison strip.

#### Top-left — Pad-to-pad spacing

Show two adjacent SMT pads.

Required:

- two copper pads;
- dimension arrow between the nearest pad edges;
- label `Pad-to-pad spacing`.

#### Top-right — Trace-to-trace spacing

Show two parallel traces.

Required:

- two traces of equal width;
- dimension arrow perpendicular to the traces;
- label `Trace-to-trace spacing`.

#### Bottom-left — Trace-to-pad spacing

Show one trace passing near one copper pad.

Required:

- one trace;
- one pad;
- dimension arrow between nearest trace and pad edges;
- label `Trace-to-pad spacing`.

#### Bottom-right — Copper-to-board-edge spacing

Show a copper trace or copper area near a clearly defined routed board edge.

Required:

- board edge;
- copper feature;
- dimension arrow from nearest copper edge to board edge;
- label `Copper-to-board-edge spacing`.

#### Bottom comparison strip

Use a small, simple comparison:

- left small symbol: component near board edge;
- right small symbol: copper near board edge;
- concise text:
  `Component-to-edge ≠ copper-to-edge`

Do not repeat the 2.5 mm component value or any copper-to-edge number in the artwork.

### Required technical relationships

The final figure must correctly show that:

1. Pad-to-pad spacing is measured between the nearest copper-pad edges.
2. Trace-to-trace spacing is measured between the nearest trace edges, not trace centerlines.
3. Trace-to-pad spacing is measured between the nearest copper edges.
4. Copper-to-board-edge spacing is measured from the nearest copper edge to the physical board edge.
5. Copper-to-edge and component-to-edge rules are distinct.
6. The figure must not imply that the same numerical value applies to all four copper-spacing categories.
7. The figure must not imply that 0.10 mm, 0.20 mm, or 0.25 mm are universal requirements.
8. The figure must not imply that copper-to-edge is universally 1.0-1.5 mm.
9. Routed-edge geometry should be shown generically; do not teach V-score-specific numbers here.
10. The figure should not imply electrical-safety clearance/creepage criteria.

### Spatial / geometry requirements

- Use true top-view geometry.
- Use copper features with clearly visible edges.
- Dimension arrows must be perpendicular to the two measured boundaries where practical.
- Pad-to-pad: keep pad shapes simple and similar.
- Trace-to-trace: use straight, parallel traces.
- Trace-to-pad: place the trace close enough to the pad that the nearest-edge relationship is unambiguous.
- Copper-to-edge: show the board outline clearly and keep the copper fully inside the board.
- Keep all dimension arrows outside the copper features.
- Avoid dimension arrows that visually resemble trace geometry.
- Use a consistent dimension-arrow style across all four panels.
- Keep the bottom comparison strip visually secondary.
- Maintain approximately 60-80 px outer margin.
- Avoid crossing leader lines.
