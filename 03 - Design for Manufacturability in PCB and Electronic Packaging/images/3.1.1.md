## Suggested Content for Figure 3.1.1

**Figure title for authoring/reference only:**  
`DFM and Related DFX Perspectives on a PCB Assembly Design`

### Instructional purpose

Give readers one visual mental model for the relationship among:

- **Design for Manufacturability (DFM)**;
- **Design for Assembly (DFA)**;
- **Design for Test (DFT)**;
- the broader **Design for X (DFX)** family of design objectives.

The figure should reinforce the Section 3.1 idea that the **same physical PCB/PCBA design is examined from several related engineering perspectives before manufacturing release**.

It should help MET readers understand that these perspectives overlap in practice without requiring them to memorize a rigid taxonomy.

### Central teaching message

> **One electronic-product design can be evaluated through DFM, DFA, DFT, and other DFX perspectives; the perspectives overlap because one physical design decision can affect several manufacturing and product-life-cycle objectives.**

The figure should also preserve two important Section 3.1 distinctions:

1. **DFM is the main Chapter 3 lens** for pre-release manufacturing decisions.
2. **DFX is the broader family/umbrella**, not a separate fifth process that occurs after DFM, DFA, and DFT.

### Canonical PNG filename

`3.1.1 - DFM and Related DFX Perspectives on a PCB Assembly Design.png`

### Intended mdBook display width

`900 px`

- Preferred working canvas: **1600 × 900 px**
- Aspect ratio: **16:9**

The exact vector canvas may be adjusted slightly if needed for label spacing, but the final composition should remain close to 16:9.

## Overall layout

Use a clean **central-design-with-related-perspectives** composition.

Recommended structure:

1. **Center:** one simplified, generic PCB assembly design.
2. **Around the central design:** three clearly labeled primary perspective boxes:
   - `DFM`
   - `DFA`
   - `DFT`
3. **A fourth smaller box:** `Other DFX considerations`
4. **A subtle enclosing frame or band:** `DFX family of design objectives`

The enclosing DFX frame should visually communicate that DFM, DFA, DFT, and the other design-for-X objectives belong to a broader family.

The three main perspective boxes and the `Other DFX considerations` box should connect to the **same central PCB assembly design** using simple leader/connector lines.

Do **not** make the figure look like a manufacturing-process flowchart. There should be no left-to-right process sequence and no implication that DFM occurs first, then DFA, then DFT.

Do **not** use a Venn diagram as the main composition. A Venn diagram could imply rigid set membership and would make the figure harder to read at mdBook size.

---

## Required central object

Show one **generic, original PCB assembly design** in simplified top view or restrained isometric/top-oblique view.

The PCB assembly should include only enough detail to be recognizable:

- PCB body;
- one generic IC package;
- several small passive components;
- one connector;
- a few visible traces/pads or copper features;
- several vias;
- two or three generic test-point pads.

Optional concise label:

- `One PCB assembly design`

The PCB assembly should look like a **PCBA**, not a bare PCB.

Do not use a recognizable commercial board, branded product, or copied reference-board layout.

## Required perspective boxes and labels

### 1. DFM - primary Chapter 3 perspective

Required heading:

- `DFM`

Required supporting text:

- `Manufacturing capability`
- `Process margin`

Optional third short phrase if space remains:

- `Yield / complexity`

The DFM box should receive slightly stronger visual emphasis than the other boxes because DFM is the organizing lens for Chapter 3.

Do not make the DFM box so dominant that DFA and DFT appear unimportant.

### 2. DFA

Required heading:

- `DFA`

Required supporting text:

- `Placement`
- `Orientation`
- `Assembly access`

These phrases should remain at recognition level. Do not show detailed pick-and-place machinery, solder printing, reflow, or wave-soldering processes.

### 3. DFT

Required heading:

- `DFT`

Required supporting text:

- `Test points`
- `Probe access`
- `Diagnosability`

The DFT box should represent **testability**.

Do **not** label AOI, X-ray, or optical inspection as DFT in this figure. Section 3.1 explicitly distinguishes inspection access from electrical testability.

### 4. Other DFX considerations

Required heading:

- `Other DFX considerations`

Use a short list selected from:

- `Reliability`
- `Serviceability`
- `Cost`
- `Environment`
- `Safety`

All five may be shown if they remain readable at 900 px. If the visual becomes crowded, use four and retain the omitted concept in the surrounding section text rather than shrinking the text excessively.

### 5. DFX umbrella/frame

Required label:

- `DFX family of design objectives`

This should appear as a subtle enclosing frame, arch, or top band around the perspective boxes and central design.

The DFX treatment must visually communicate:

- DFM, DFA, and DFT are part of the broader DFX family;
- the boundaries are related and overlapping in practice;
- DFX is not a downstream manufacturing step.

## Required technical relationships

The final figure must communicate all of the following:

- one physical PCB assembly design is evaluated from multiple perspectives;
- DFM asks whether the design can be manufactured within real capability and process margin;
- DFA focuses on assembly-oriented design decisions such as placement, orientation, and access;
- DFT focuses on testability such as test points, probe access, and diagnosability;
- broader DFX objectives can include reliability, serviceability, cost, environment, safety, and other product-specific needs;
- the perspectives can overlap because a single design feature may affect more than one objective;
- DFM is the primary Chapter 3 focus;
- DFX is the umbrella/family, not a separate sequential process.

The figure should **not** imply:

- that one region of the PCB belongs exclusively to DFM, another exclusively to DFA, and another exclusively to DFT;
- that DFM, DFA, and DFT always have perfectly standardized boundaries in industry;
- that inspection access and electrical testability are identical;
- that one universal DFX checklist applies to every product.

## Spatial / geometry requirements

- Keep the central PCB assembly visually centered and large enough to remain recognizable at 900 px width.
- Recommended central PCBA footprint: approximately **35-45% of the canvas width**.
- Arrange the DFM, DFA, and DFT boxes around the PCBA with balanced visual weight.
- Place `Other DFX considerations` in the remaining quadrant or lower area.
- Use a subtle outer DFX frame/band around the complete group.
- Keep all callout lines short and non-crossing.
- Leader/connector lines should point to the **central design as a whole**, not assign individual components exclusively to one DFX category.
- Leave generous whitespace between boxes and the board.
- Keep at least approximately **60-80 px safe margin** around the 1600 × 900 working canvas.
- Avoid text wrapping beyond two or three short lines in any perspective box.
- Avoid dense lists inside the board drawing.
- Do not use dimensional arrows, numerical dimensions, or manufacturing tolerances in this figure.

No process-flow arrowheads are required. If an arrow is introduced during generation for a specific local reason, it must not imply a sequential process.

Do not include detailed:

- signal/power integrity;
- thermal analysis;
- mechanical stress/vibration;
- reliability-life prediction.

Recognition-level DFX terms such as `Reliability` are allowed only as labels within the broader framework.

## Technical-verification basis

Use the following as the technical grounding for the figure.

### Primary instructional basis

- Current Section 3.1 text:
  - DFM connects design intent to manufacturing reality;
  - DFM is considered before manufacturing release;
  - DFM, DFA, and DFT are related perspectives with overlapping boundaries;
  - DFX is the broader design-for-X family;
  - inspection access is related to but not identical to electrical testability.
- Approved Chapter 3 outline:
  - DFM remains the central Chapter 3 subject;
  - DFA, DFT, and DFX are framing concepts only where they support practical pre-release decisions.
- MET406 Chapter 3 instructional material:
  - DFM bridges design intent and factory reality;
  - DFM aims to reduce manufacturing problems/cost and improve yield;
  - placement, geometry, access, and manufacturability are pre-release design concerns.
- MET406 Lab #1:
  - frames DFM as an industry-style pre-release review of visible and quantitative design issues.

### Secondary technical verification

- Glenn R. Blackwell, ed., *The Electronic Packaging Handbook*:
  - concurrent engineering and design-for-manufacturability/testability context;
  - the need to consider manufacturing, assembly, test, rework, and product-life-cycle needs during design.
- Clyde F. Coombs Jr., *Printed Circuits Handbook*:
  - PWB design/manufacturing interaction;
  - board manufacturing, assembly, test/repair, process capability, yield, and cost as design considerations.
- Existing Chapter 1 and Chapter 2 OER:
  - manufacturability and testability as packaging concerns;
  - supplier capability, preferred capability, process margin, and producibility framework.

These sources are for **technical verification only**. The figure composition must be independently created.

- trace or closely redraw a lecture-slide diagram;

- create a new generic PCBA;
