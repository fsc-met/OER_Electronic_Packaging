## Suggested Content for Figure 3.7.4

**Figure title for authoring/reference only:**  
`Open Via-in-Pad versus Filled and Capped Via-in-Pad`

### Instructional purpose

Show why an ordinary open through-via located directly in a solderable component pad can create an unwanted path for solder, and contrast that with an intentionally processed via-in-pad that provides a closed, planar solderable surface.

The figure should reinforce the Section 3.7.7 decision:

- accidental open via-in-pad is a DFM risk;
- via-in-pad itself is not inherently wrong;
- controlled filled/planarized/plated-over construction is one common advanced solution;
- package-specific thermal-pad strategies can differ and should not be reduced to one universal rule.

### Central teaching message

> **An open via in a solderable pad can drain or wick solder into the barrel. A controlled filled and plated-over via-in-pad closes the solder path and restores a planar solderable surface.**

Secondary message:

> **Via-in-pad must be an intentional fabrication/assembly decision, not an accidental footprint detail.**

### Required technical relationships

The final figure must correctly show that:

1. The open via barrel creates a continuous opening from the component pad into the board.
2. The solder-path arrow in the open panel points **into the via**, not upward out of it.
3. The filled panel contains material inside the via opening.
4. The filled panel has a planarized, plated-over/capped top surface.
5. The capped surface is continuous enough to act as a controlled solderable pad surface.
6. The figure must not imply that via-in-pad is always defective.
7. The figure must not imply that every exposed-pad thermal-via design must be filled/capped.
8. The figure must not imply that simple solder-mask tenting is equivalent to filled/planarized/plated-over VIPPO construction.
9. The figure must not show via fill as copper unless the construction is explicitly intended to be copper-filled; generic fill should use a distinct material/color.
10. The figure must not present detailed solder-paste aperture design or reflow-profile behavior.

### Technical-verification basis

#### Current Section 3.7

The section establishes:

- open through-via in a solderable pad can wick/drain solder;
- this can reduce solder available at the intended joint or contribute to voiding/uneven standoff;
- filled and plated-over/capped via-in-pad is a common controlled solution for fine-pitch BGA and similar applications;
- via-in-pad is not automatically wrong;
- other package-specific exposed-pad strategies may exist.

#### Current technical verification already recorded in the section

The section notes that:

- current fine-pitch BGA guidance supports filled and plated-over PTH via-in-pad to control solder wicking;
- current QFN guidance documents solder-wicking risk and package-specific treatment such as fill or top-side tenting;
- therefore the figure must show one controlled filled/plated-over solution without presenting it as the only valid package-specific strategy.
