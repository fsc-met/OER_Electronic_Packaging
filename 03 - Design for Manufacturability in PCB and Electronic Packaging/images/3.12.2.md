## Suggested Content for Figure 3.12.2

### Figure title for authoring/reference only

`Localized Rework Heating and Neighboring Component Exposure`

### Instructional purpose

Show why local hot-air or thermal rework access is also a **neighboring-component placement** issue.

The figure should compare two otherwise similar target-component layouts:

- one with enough open local space that the heating region is concentrated mainly around the target;
- one with nearby components located within the affected heating region.

The figure should teach collateral thermal exposure without implying one universal hot-air clearance distance.

### Central teaching message

> **Localized rework heat does not stop exactly at the target component. Nearby parts can receive additional thermal exposure, so placement should support a controllable rework zone.**

### Canonical PNG filename

`3.12.2 - Localized Rework Heating and Neighboring Component Exposure.png`

### Intended mdBook display width

`900 px`

### Recommended canvas size / aspect ratio

- Authoring canvas: approximately `1800 x 1000 px`.
- Recommended aspect ratio: approximately `16:9` to `9:5`.
- Use a top-view PCB comparison so the heat-affected region is easy to interpret spatially.

### Overall layout

Use a **two-panel matched top-view comparison**.

Left panel:
- `Lower neighboring exposure`

Right panel:
- `Higher neighboring exposure`

Each panel should show:
- the same target IC;
- the same schematic hot-air/nozzle source above or beside it;
- a translucent heating/airflow zone centered on the target.

The main difference should be the location of neighboring components relative to that zone.

Left panel:
- nearby parts remain outside or near the edge of the heating region.

Right panel:
- several small parts and/or a plastic connector sit clearly inside the affected region.

### Required objects and labels

Both panels should include:

- PCB field;
- target IC labeled `Rework target`;
- simplified hot-air nozzle or hot-air direction cue;
- translucent `Localized rework heating region`;
- at least two neighboring components.

Left panel labels:

- `Nearby parts mostly outside heated region`
- optional green check.

Right panel labels:

- `Neighbor inside heated region`
- `Additional thermal exposure`
- optional red X.

If a plastic connector is used as a potentially heat-sensitive example, label it generically:
- `Plastic connector / heat-sensitive neighbor`

Do not label any temperature value.

### Required technical relationships

- Heating region must be centered on or directed toward the rework target.
- The right-panel neighboring part must actually overlap the illustrated heating region.
- The left-panel neighboring parts should be outside or near the boundary, not artificially far away.
- The heat zone must be schematic and clearly process-dependent.
- Do not imply a sharp real-world temperature boundary at the drawn zone edge.
- Do not imply that everything inside the region will necessarily be damaged; label the issue as **additional thermal exposure** or **higher neighboring exposure**, not guaranteed failure.
- Do not imply that everything outside the region is thermally unaffected.
- Do not specify a universal clearance radius.
- Do not show exact thermal profiles, temperatures, or airflow values.

### Spatial / geometry requirements

- Use identical target-component scale and board orientation across panels.
- Use the same heating-zone size in both panels so only neighboring placement changes.
- Use a translucent fill or soft contour bands for the heating region. The boundary must look intentionally schematic, not like a measured isotherm.
- Keep neighbor components visually distinct from the heating overlay.
- Avoid placing important labels over the translucent heat field if it reduces readability.
- Use arrows to show hot-air direction only if needed; do not overload the figure with fluid-flow detail.
- Keep all critical features recognizable at 900 px.


### Color / style guidance

- Clean engineering-textbook appearance consistent with the current EPAC figure set.
- White or very light neutral background.
- Flat 2D top-view, side-view, or restrained schematic/isometric geometry only where it improves physical understanding.
- Restrained engineering palette:
  - green/blue-green PCB field;
  - copper/gold pads or exposed conductive features;
  - dark gray component bodies;
  - muted blue/gray tools and motion arrows;
  - limited orange/red only for heat exposure or a blocked/problem condition.
- Sans-serif labels sized for the 900 px mdBook render.
- Moderate line weights and consistent visual hierarchy.
- No decorative gradients, glossy rendering, dramatic shadows, logos, watermarks, or branded commercial products.
- Do not rely on color alone; use geometry, labels, line style, arrows, and good/bad structure.
- Do not embed the external figure number, final caption, or an oversized duplicate overall title inside the artwork.


### Scope boundaries

This figure should **not** teach:

- rework temperatures;
- ramp/soak/reflow profiles;
- nozzle-size selection;
- airflow settings;
- shielding materials or detailed shielding procedure;
- package-specific rework instructions;
- pad-lift mechanics in detail;
- inspection/test access;
- rework tool/grip/removal geometry already owned by Figure 3.12.1.

### Technical-verification basis

Use as grounding:

- current Section 3.12.2 text;
- primary Chapter 3 teaching that dense placement increases adjacent-component damage risk;
- IPC-7711/7721 rework/repair context supporting avoidance of additional assembly damage;
- current package-rework guidance warning that component removal can damage the PCB, target package, and nearby components through thermal/mechanical stress;
- Chapter 4 discussion that rework adds thermal stress and develops detailed process methods.

The figure should preserve the DFM-level message only and avoid process-specific numerical settings.

### Originality / copyright requirements

Create an original schematic top-view rework-heating comparison.

Do not:

- copy a thermal-camera image;
- trace a rework-nozzle vendor diagram;
- reproduce a CFD/thermal simulation from another source;
- use a branded commercial board or connector.

The heat field should be an original schematic visualization, not copied measured data.


### Generation guidance

Use a controlled technical-figure workflow.

1. Establish the composition from this specification.
2. If AI image generation is used for an early concept, preserve it only as a concept revision.
3. Once a promising visual baseline exists, freeze the visual language and make only targeted corrections.
4. Rebuild/refine as controlled vector artwork when exact tool paths, heat zones, removal arrows, fastener access, or text placement cannot be controlled reliably in raster generation.
5. Verify every word, arrow, leader, tool/component relationship, and access path after each meaningful revision.
6. Preserve every meaningful revision under a new filename.
7. Do not copy or rename a candidate to the canonical publication filename before explicit project-owner acceptance.



### Editable SVG requirements

The strongest final candidate should include both a publication PNG and a **true editable SVG**.

The SVG must:

- use vector geometry rather than an embedded raster image as the primary artwork;
- keep text editable where practical;
- keep PCB features, components, tools, heat zones, arrows, leaders, fasteners, and labels as separate editable objects;
- define explicit `width`, `height`, and `viewBox` values;
- use `preserveAspectRatio="xMidYMid meet"`;
- include an explicit full-canvas white rectangle as the first visible drawing element, e.g. `<rect x="0" y="0" width="100%" height="100%" fill="#ffffff"/>`;
- preserve adequate safe margins so no tool, arrow, leader, or label is clipped;
- use `markerUnits="userSpaceOnUse"` for normal engineering arrows unless a specific technical reason requires another setting;
- remain clear when rendered at native size and approximately 1600 px, 1200 px, and 900 px width.

Before recommendation, render-check the final SVG at all four sizes.
