## Suggested Content for Figure 3.6.2

**Figure title for authoring/reference only:**  
`QFN and BGA Land-Pattern Relationships`

### Instructional purpose

Show two package-specific fine-pitch land-pattern relationships that are visually different from ordinary leaded-component lands:

- QFN perimeter terminals aligned to rectangular PCB lands;
- BGA solder balls aligned to circular PCB lands.

The figure should make the Section 3.6.3/3.6.6 pitch–land-width–gap reasoning visible while reinforcing that QFN and BGA land dimensions are package-specific.

### Central teaching message

> **Fine-pitch land patterns must match the package termination or ball geometry while preserving the remaining spacing needed for fabrication, solder mask, routing, and assembly.**

Secondary message:

> **The same package pitch does not imply the same land shape or land dimensions for different package types.**

### Required technical relationships

The final figure must correctly show that:

1. QFN perimeter terminals and PCB lands are rectangular and aligned on the same pitch.
2. QFN land width is measured in the pitch direction.
3. For equal rectangular lands centered on a uniform pitch, `g = p - w`.
4. With `p = 0.50 mm` and `w = 0.30 mm`, the shown gap is `0.20 mm`.
5. The QFN PCB land can extend farther outward than the package terminal length.
6. BGA PCB lands are circular and aligned to the solder-ball array.
7. BGA pitch is center-to-center.
8. The `0.50 mm pitch / 0.275 mm PCB land` values are one package-family example, not universal BGA rules.
9. QFN and BGA panels must not imply that their land geometries are interchangeable.
10. The figure must not imply a universal exposed-pad, mask-opening, or escape-routing rule.
