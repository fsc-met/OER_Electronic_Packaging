# Figure 3.8.3 Suggested Content Specification

## Figure title for authoring/reference only
**Copper Slivers and Narrow Webs Around Cutouts**

## Instructional purpose
Illustrate how copper pours, voids, and cutouts can unintentionally create thin copper slivers or narrow required webs, and show the preferred response: remove nonfunctional remnants or widen required copper.

## Central teaching message
Needle-like copper remnants and very narrow copper webs are poor DFM geometry. If the copper serves no useful function, remove it; if it must remain, widen it so the local feature meets the applicable fabrication capability.

`3.8.3 - Copper Slivers and Narrow Webs Around Cutouts.png`

`900`

- Recommended aspect ratio: **5:3**

## Overall layout
Use a **three-panel top-view comparison**:

1. **Copper sliver / isolated remnant**
2. **Narrow web around a cutout**
3. **Corrected geometry**

Alternative: four smaller panels if needed, but keep the overall instructional role unified.

## Required objects and labels
### Panel A
- copper region or pour leaving a narrow needle-like protrusion or isolated copper remnant;
- label: **Copper sliver** or **Needle-like remnant**.

### Panel B
- routed slot, void, or cutout surrounded by nearby copper;
- one remaining copper neck/web that is visibly narrow;
- label: **Narrow copper web**;
- optional routed-boundary label: **Cutout** or **Routed slot**.

### Panel C
- corrected version(s):
  - sliver removed;
  - web widened or copper boundary reshaped;
- labels such as:
  - **Remove nonfunctional sliver**
  - **Widen required copper web**

## Required technical relationships
- The sliver must appear functionally questionable and mechanically fragile.
- The narrow web must clearly look like a local minimum copper feature.
- The corrected panel must show one or both appropriate actions:
  - remove useless remnant;
  - widen necessary copper.
- Do not imply that every small copper island is invalid; focus on **nonfunctional or too-narrow** remnants.

## Spatial / geometry requirements
- Keep all panels in top view.
- The cutout should be clearly shown as a routed opening in the board.
- Use visually obvious before/after differences.
- Maintain enough spacing so that labels do not overlap the cutout boundary or copper edges.

## Color / style guidance

- Cutout/void may be white or a pale neutral fill with clear outline.
- Dark labels and leader lines.
- EPAC textbook-like style only.

## Scope boundaries
- This figure is about **copper-shape producibility**, not solder-mask slivers (Section 3.9).
- Do not turn this into a detailed board-edge clearance figure; Section 3.5 already teaches how clearance is measured.
- Avoid fabrication-process tables inside the figure.

## Technical-verification basis
Supported by verified Section 3.8 content:
- copper slivers can be over-etched, detached, or difficult to inspect consistently;
- narrow copper webs must satisfy the applicable minimum copper-feature capability;
- removing nonfunctional remnants is usually more robust than keeping fragile copper;
- required copper around cutouts must be intentionally wide enough.
