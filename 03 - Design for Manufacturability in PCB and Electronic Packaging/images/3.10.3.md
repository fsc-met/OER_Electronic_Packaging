## Suggested Content for Figure 3.10.3

### Figure title for authoring/reference only

`Marking Placement Before and After Component Assembly`

### Instructional purpose

Show why legend placement must be reviewed in the **assembled state**, not only on the bare PCB.

The figure should demonstrate that a reference designator or orientation mark can look perfectly clear in the CAD/bare-board view but become hidden or ambiguous after the component body is installed.

This figure supports Section 3.10.4 and provides the main good/bad visual comparison for the section.

### Central teaching message

> **A marking is useful only if it remains visible and clearly associated with the intended feature after the component is installed.**

### Canonical PNG filename

`3.10.3 - Marking Placement Before and After Component Assembly.png`

### Intended mdBook display width

`900 px`

### Recommended canvas size / aspect ratio

- Authoring canvas: approximately `1800 x 1050 px`.
- Recommended aspect ratio: approximately `12:7` to `16:9`.
- Maintain enough panel size that the actual board legend remains readable at 900 px.

### Overall layout

Use a **2 x 2 comparison** with a shared row/column structure.

Columns:

- `Poor placement`
- `Improved placement`

Rows:

- `Bare PCB`
- `After assembly`

Use the **same footprint, component, scale, and orientation** in all four panels.

Recommended component:

- one medium-size IC package such as a QFN/QFP/SOIC-like body.

Required poor case:

- `U3` and/or the board-level pin-1 mark is placed within the future component-body area;
- on the bare PCB the marking is readable;
- after assembly the component body hides it.

Required improved case:

- the same information is placed just outside the component body;
- after assembly it remains visible and clearly tied to the correct component/corner.

### Required objects and labels

Required panel headings:

- `Poor placement`
- `Improved placement`
- `Bare PCB`
- `After assembly`

Required board content:

- identical copper pad/footprint geometry in all panels;
- reference designator `U3`;
- board-level pin-1/orientation mark;
- component body in the assembled-state panels;
- visible package pin-1 cue if useful to verify that the board-level mark points to the correct corner.

Optional concise annotations:

- `Hidden after assembly`
- `Visible after assembly`

Avoid paragraph text.

### Required technical relationships

- The component body must cover the poor-case marking in the assembled state.
- The improved-case `U3` must remain close enough to be associated with the IC, but outside the component body.
- The improved-case pin-1 mark must remain adjacent to the correct pin-1 corner.
- Do not move or rotate the component between bare-board and assembled views.
- Do not change the footprint between poor and improved cases; only the **legend placement** should change.
- Do not imply that every reference designator must remain on the board if space is extremely constrained; the figure teaches the preferred DFM principle when the marking is intended to support assembly/service.
- The figure should not imply that the legend replaces controlled assembly drawings or machine data.

### Spatial / geometry requirements

- Use the exact same board crop and footprint scale in all four panels.
- Align the two bare-board panels horizontally and the two assembled panels horizontally.
- Use a vertical arrow or simple `Component installed` cue between rows if needed.
- Place poor-case legend clearly inside the package-body envelope but not on a pad.
- Place improved-case legend outside the body with enough visual separation from nearby unrelated features.
- Keep the pin-1 mark at the same logical corner before and after assembly.
- A faint dashed component-body outline should be shown in the bare-board panels to make the future component-body envelope explicit; label it once as `Component body envelope` and keep it visually secondary.
- Do not add numerical clearance dimensions; those belong to Figure 3.10.2.


### Visual style baseline

- Clean engineering-textbook figure, not a poster or marketing graphic.
- White or very light neutral background.
- Flat 2D top-view geometry unless a restrained isometric cue materially improves recognition.
- Consistent sans-serif labels and moderate line weights.
- Restrained PCB palette: muted green/blue-green solder mask, copper/gold pads, neutral gray components, white/light legend, navy/dark-neutral callouts.
- No photorealism, dramatic lighting, glossy rendering, decorative shadows, logos, watermarks, or branded commercial products.
- Do not rely on color alone; geometry, labels, and spatial relationships must remain understandable in grayscale.
- No embedded external figure number, external caption, or oversized overall title inside the artwork.


### Scope boundaries

This figure should **not**:

- teach exact silkscreen clearance values;
- teach text height or line width;
- become an AOI line-of-sight figure;
- introduce X-ray or ICT/flying-probe concepts;
- prescribe a universal reference-designator location for every package;
- imply that on-board legend is the only assembly documentation;
- teach detailed rework-access dimensions.

### Technical-verification basis

Use as grounding:

- current Section 3.10 text and outline requirement to avoid markings beneath components where they provide no useful information;
- primary instructional DFM checklist requirement that silkscreen remains readable after assembly;
- Chapter 2 identification of silkscreen/legend as an assembly and inspection aid;
- Eurocircuits guidance that important legend information can be covered by assembled components and that legend placement should follow the assembly/manufacturing partner's rules;
- Coombs's general nomenclature/legend producibility treatment.

No numerical process capability needs to appear in this figure.

### Originality / copyright requirements

Create an original four-panel board fragment.

Do not:

- copy a PCB CAD screenshot;
- trace a reference figure;
- reuse a vendor before/after graphic;
- use a recognizable commercial footprint drawing or product board.

The component and footprint should be generic, simplified, and independently drawn.


### Generation / drawing guidance

Because the figure contains technical geometry and exact text relationships, use a controlled drawing workflow.

Preferred approach:

1. construct the geometry as editable vector artwork;
2. use AI image generation only for an early concept if useful, not as the uncontrolled final technical source;
3. verify every word, dimension, leader, arrow, and physical relationship after drawing;
4. preserve the selected baseline and make only targeted corrections during cyclic QA;
5. save every meaningful revision under a new filename;
6. do not copy or rename a candidate to the canonical publication filename until explicit project-owner acceptance.

If a generated concept contains text or geometry defects that cannot be corrected reliably, rebuild the figure as controlled SVG rather than repeatedly regenerating the entire composition.



### Editable SVG requirements

The strongest final candidate should include both a publication PNG and a **true editable SVG**.

The SVG should:

- use vector geometry rather than an embedded raster as the primary artwork;
- keep text editable where practical;
- keep PCB features, components, legend strokes, dimension arrows, callouts, and labels as separate editable objects;
- define explicit `width`, `height`, and `viewBox` values;
- use `preserveAspectRatio="xMidYMid meet"`;
- preserve adequate outer margins so no text, dimension line, or leader is clipped;
- use a canvas large enough that important text remains readable when rendered to approximately 900 px width;
- preserve consistent stroke weights after scaling.

Before recommendation, render and inspect the figure at native size and at approximately 1600 px, 1200 px, and 900 px width.
