## Suggested Content for Figure 3.11.1

### Figure title for authoring/reference only

`Optical Inspection Line of Sight and AOI Shadowing`

### Instructional purpose

Teach the physical meaning of **inspection access** by showing how the same small solder-joint region can be optically visible in one placement arrangement and partially or fully blocked in another.

This figure should make the Section 3.11.1 concept concrete:

- optical inspection depends on usable viewing geometry;
- a nearby tall component becomes a DFM concern when it blocks the viewing path to the feature that must be inspected;
- component height by itself is not the defect — **blocked line of sight** is the issue.

The figure should focus on inspection geometry rather than becoming a general component-placement figure.

### Central teaching message

> **AOI/manual optical inspection needs a usable line of sight to the feature being checked. Tall components matter when they obstruct that required viewing geometry.**

### Canonical PNG filename

`3.11.1 - Optical Inspection Line of Sight and AOI Shadowing.png`

### Intended mdBook display width

`900 px`

### Recommended canvas size / aspect ratio

- Authoring canvas: approximately `1800 x 1000 px` or larger.
- Recommended aspect ratio: approximately `16:9` to `9:5`.
- Use enough vertical space for camera/viewing geometry above the PCB.

### Overall layout

Use a **two-panel matched side-view comparison**.

Left panel:
- `Clear optical access`

Right panel:
- `Blocked / shadowed access`

The PCB, small inspected component, inspected solder-joint region, camera position, and nominal viewing direction should remain as similar as possible between panels.

The only meaningful change should be the addition/repositioning of a nearby tall component that blocks the required optical path in the right panel.

### Required objects and labels

Each panel should include:

- a horizontal PCB cross-section/profile;
- one small fine-pitch leaded component with at least one externally visible solder-joint/lead region as the inspection target;
- clearly indicated `Inspection target`;
- a simplified optical camera/sensor above the board;
- one or more viewing rays/paths from the camera toward the inspection target.

Left panel:
- no obstruction between camera and target;
- label `Clear viewing path`.

Right panel:
- one taller neighboring component, connector, or shield-like obstruction;
- a blocked/interrupted viewing ray;
- label `Tall component`;
- label `Blocked viewing path` or `Shadowed region`.

Optional:
- a small check mark on the clear-access panel and X symbol on the blocked panel.

Do not include paragraph-length annotations.

### Required technical relationships

- The inspection target must be a real board-level feature such as an exposed lead/solder fillet or visible solder-joint edge.
- The camera/viewing rays must terminate at the inspection target in the clear case.
- In the blocked case, the tall component must physically intersect the required viewing ray before it reaches the target.
- Do not imply that every tall component causes AOI failure.
- Do not imply that only a perfectly vertical top-down camera exists; use an oblique representative viewing ray so the line-of-sight concept is obvious.
- Do not imply that optical inspection can see through opaque component bodies.
- Do not imply that AOI defect detection depends only on geometry; the surrounding section already qualifies equipment, lighting, angle, and algorithm dependence.

### Spatial / geometry requirements

- Use the same board baseline and target location in both panels.
- Keep the small component and its solder joint at the same scale/orientation in both panels.
- Place the tall component only in the right panel or move it into the ray path only in the right panel.
- Keep viewing rays visually separate from component outlines.
- If a shadow region is shown behind the obstruction, use a separate shaded/dashed region rather than continuing the optical ray through the opaque component.
- Avoid crossing labels/leaders.
- Keep the target and viewing path large enough to remain clear at 900 px.


### Color / style guidance

- Clean engineering-textbook appearance consistent with current EPAC figures.
- White or very light neutral background.
- Flat 2D schematic geometry or restrained cross-section drawing; no photorealism.
- Restrained engineering palette:
  - green/blue-green PCB or solder-mask field;
  - copper/gold conductive features;
  - dark gray component bodies;
  - muted blue/gray inspection or probe geometry;
  - limited red only for a blocked/problem condition when needed.
- Sans-serif labels with enough size for the 900 px mdBook render.
- Consistent line weights and moderate visual hierarchy.
- No decorative gradients, dramatic shadows, glossy rendering, logos, or watermarks.
- Do not rely on color alone; use labels, line style, geometry, symbols, and/or check/X cues.
- Do not embed the external figure number, final caption, or an oversized overall title inside the artwork.


### Scope boundaries

This figure should **not** teach:

- detailed AOI hardware architecture;
- lighting wavelengths or illumination recipes;
- AOI software/algorithm settings;
- defect acceptance thresholds;
- X-ray imaging;
- ICT, flying probe, or boundary scan;
- rework-tool access;
- universal component-height or spacing limits.

Figure 3.11.2 owns visible-versus-hidden package-joint inspection. Figure 3.11.3 owns physical electrical test access.

### Technical-verification basis

Use as technical grounding:

- current Section 3.11.1 text;
- primary Chapter 3 instructional material on poor inspection access, blocked joints, AOI shadowing, and tall-component obstruction;
- Section 3.4's earlier placement-level preview of AOI obstruction, without duplicating that broader placement figure;
- Chapter 4 inspection material only to confirm terminology and boundary;
- Coombs/Blackwell design-for-manufacturing/testability context.

No universal numerical AOI clearance or component-height rule should appear in the figure.

### Originality / copyright requirements

Create an original side-view inspection geometry.

Do not:

- copy an AOI vendor screenshot;
- trace a lecture-slide illustration;
- reproduce a textbook camera/board arrangement;
- use a recognizable commercial inspection machine;
- use vendor logos or proprietary package drawings.

The optical relationship is factual; the composition must be independently authored for this OER.

### Editable SVG requirements

The strongest final candidate should include a publication PNG and a **true editable SVG**.

The SVG must:

- use vector geometry rather than an embedded raster image as the primary artwork;
- keep text editable where practical;
- keep board features, component bodies, package cross-sections, rays, arrows, probe tips, leaders, labels, and symbols as separate editable objects;
- define explicit `width`, `height`, and `viewBox` values;
- use `preserveAspectRatio="xMidYMid meet"`;
- include an explicit full-canvas white rectangle as the first visible drawing element;
- preserve adequate outer margins so no label, ray, leader, or arrow is clipped;
- use `markerUnits="userSpaceOnUse"` for normal engineering arrows unless there is a specific technical reason not to;
- remain readable when rendered at native size and approximately 1600 px, 1200 px, and 900 px width.

Before recommendation, render-check the final SVG at all four sizes.
