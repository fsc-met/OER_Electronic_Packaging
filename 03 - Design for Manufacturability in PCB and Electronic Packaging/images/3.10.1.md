## Suggested Content for Figure 3.10.1

### Figure title for authoring/reference only

`Markings That Support Assembly Inspection and Service`

### Instructional purpose

Introduce the **human-information role of the PCB legend** by showing several common markings on one representative assembled PCB region.

This figure should let an Engineering Technology reader recognize what useful board markings look like and understand what physical feature each marking identifies.

It should visually support Section 3.10.1 without becoming a manufacturability-dimension figure. Clearance, text height, and line width are owned by Figure 3.10.2.

### Central teaching message

> **Useful PCB markings communicate identity, orientation, polarity, connector function, and test-point location clearly enough to support assembly, inspection, troubleshooting, and service.**

A marking is useful only when its physical association is unambiguous.

### Canonical PNG filename

`3.10.1 - Markings That Support Assembly Inspection and Service.png`

### Intended mdBook display width

`900 px`

### Recommended canvas size / aspect ratio

- Authoring canvas: approximately `1800 x 1000 px` or larger.
- Recommended aspect ratio: approximately `16:9` to `9:5`.
- Preserve generous outer margins for callouts.

### Overall layout

Use **one representative top-view PCBA region** in the center with a small number of surrounding callouts.

The central board should include enough different component types to support five marking examples without looking like a complete commercial product.

Required marking examples:

1. component reference designator;
2. IC pin-1/orientation indicator;
3. polarity indicator on a polarized component;
4. connector identification/orientation label;
5. test-point label.

This is primarily a **recognition and association** figure. Do not add good/bad comparison panels here.

### Required objects

Show a generic populated PCB with at least:

- one integrated-circuit package, preferably a simple QFN/QFP/SOIC-like shape;
- one polarized component such as a diode or polarized capacitor;
- one board connector;
- one clearly exposed circular or rectangular test point intended for probe contact;
- a few other generic passive components for context;
- visible solder mask and exposed pads only where needed for recognition;
- printed legend markings on the board surface.

### Required board markings and labels

The artwork should contain representative board legend such as:

- `U3` next to the IC;
- a board-level pin-1 dot, triangle, corner mark, or short line adjacent to the correct IC corner;
- `C8` or `D2` next to the polarized component;
- `+` / `-` or another technically appropriate polarity mark associated with that component;
- `J1` and optionally a concise function label such as `POWER`;
- `TP1` next to the test point.

Add concise external callouts identifying the **type of information**, not repeating the entire board legend:

- `Reference designator`
- `Pin-1 / orientation`
- `Polarity`
- `Connector identification`
- `Test-point identification`

Do not add paragraph-length explanations.

### Required technical relationships

- `U3` must clearly belong to the IC rather than to a nearby passive.
- The pin-1 board mark must be adjacent to the actual pin-1 corner/orientation of the IC package or footprint.
- The polarity mark must agree with the polarized component orientation shown.
- `J1` / `POWER` must visibly belong to the connector.
- `TP1` must be adjacent to the intended test point, not another pad.
- Markings should appear on the board legend layer, not as text printed on top of component bodies.
- The figure should show all required markings still visible in the assembled state, but should not add a separate before/after comparison; that visual role belongs to Figure 3.10.3.

### Spatial / geometry requirements

- Keep the board approximately centered.
- Separate the five marking examples spatially so callout leaders do not cross.
- Place each legend marking close enough to its feature that the association is obvious at 900 px.
- Leave enough board area around each example to avoid a cluttered "Where's Waldo" appearance.
- Keep component bodies opaque enough to make clear that legend beneath them would not be visible.
- Do not place any required teaching mark where it would be covered in the illustrated assembled state.
- Callout leaders should terminate at the actual printed legend mark or the relevant board region, not ambiguously between objects.


### Visual style baseline

- Clean engineering-textbook figure, not a poster or marketing graphic.
- White or very light neutral background.
- Flat 2D top-view geometry unless a restrained isometric cue materially improves recognition.
- Consistent sans-serif labels and moderate line weights.
- Restrained PCB palette: muted green/blue-green solder mask, copper/gold pads, neutral gray components, white/light legend, navy/dark-neutral callouts.
- No photorealism, dramatic lighting, glossy rendering, decorative shadows, logos, watermarks, or branded commercial products.
- Do not rely on color alone; geometry, labels, and spatial relationships must remain understandable in grayscale.
- No embedded external figure number, external caption, or oversized overall title inside the artwork.


### Scope boundaries

This figure should **not** teach:

- legend-to-pad clearance dimensions;
- minimum text height or line width;
- finite registration numerically;
- CAM clipping rules;
- AOI line-of-sight;
- X-ray inspection;
- electrical test-access geometry;
- rework procedures;
- assembly-documentation datasets;
- detailed IPC marking requirements.

Those topics either belong to Figure 3.10.2, Figure 3.10.3, Section 3.11, or later assembly content.

### Technical-verification basis

Use as technical grounding:

- current Section 3.10 text and approved Section 3.10 outline;
- primary Chapter 3 instructional material stating that silkscreen provides component identification, orientation, and polarity information;
- Chapter 2 treatment of silkscreen/legend as an assembly and inspection aid;
- Clyde F. Coombs Jr., *Printed Circuits Handbook*, for nomenclature/legend producibility and CAM-layer interactions;
- current fabricator guidance only as a secondary check that legend is a manufacturing layer and may be clipped when it conflicts with functional features.

No numerical manufacturing capability needs to appear in this figure. The figure is verified for terminology and physical association rather than process dimensions.

### Originality / copyright requirements

Create an original generic board region and original arrangement of markings.

Do not:

- trace a lecture-slide board image;
- reproduce a manufacturer's reference-designator example;
- use a recognizable commercial PCB;
- copy a textbook layout;
- use company logos, part numbers, trademarks, or distinctive product geometry.

The engineering concepts are factual; the composition and artwork must be independently created for this OER.


### Generation / drawing guidance

Because the figure contains technical geometry and exact text relationships, use a controlled drawing workflow.

Preferred approach:

1. construct the geometry as editable vector artwork;
2. use AI image generation only for an early concept if useful, not as the uncontrolled final technical source;
3. verify every word, dimension, leader, arrow, and physical relationship after drawing;
4. preserve the selected baseline and make only targeted corrections during cyclic QA;
5. save every meaningful revision under a new filename;
6. do not copy or rename a candidate to the canonical publication filename until explicit project-owner acceptance.

If a generated concept contains text or geometry defects that cannot be corrected reliably, rebuild the figure as controlled SVG rather than repeatedly regenerating the entire composition.



### Editable SVG requirements

The strongest final candidate should include both a publication PNG and a **true editable SVG**.

The SVG should:

- use vector geometry rather than an embedded raster as the primary artwork;
- keep text editable where practical;
- keep PCB features, components, legend strokes, dimension arrows, callouts, and labels as separate editable objects;
- define explicit `width`, `height`, and `viewBox` values;
- use `preserveAspectRatio="xMidYMid meet"`;
- preserve adequate outer margins so no text, dimension line, or leader is clipped;
- use a canvas large enough that important text remains readable when rendered to approximately 900 px width;
- preserve consistent stroke weights after scaling.

Before recommendation, render and inspect the figure at native size and at approximately 1600 px, 1200 px, and 900 px width.
