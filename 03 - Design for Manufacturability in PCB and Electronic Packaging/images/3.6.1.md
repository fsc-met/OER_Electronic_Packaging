## Suggested Content for Figure 3.6.1

**Figure title for authoring/reference only:**  
`Component Termination and PCB Land Geometry`

### Instructional purpose

Show the physical relationship between a leaded surface-mount component termination and its PCB land so readers can see why land width, land length, and placement margin must be matched to the actual package.

The figure should make the Section 3.6.2 terminology visible:

- lead/terminal width;
- land width;
- toe region;
- heel region;
- side margin;
- solderable overlap.

It should also support the section's illustrative `0.30 mm lead / 0.45 mm land` side-margin calculation without turning that example into a universal sizing rule.

### Central teaching message

> **The PCB land should extend beyond the component termination in the directions required by the package so normal placement and fabrication variation still leave useful solderable area.**

Secondary message:

> **Lead width, land width, heel, toe, and side margins are package-geometry relationships—not one universal percentage rule.**

### Overall layout

Use a **two-panel horizontal composition**.

#### Left panel — Top view: width and side margin

Show a simplified rectangular component body with one representative gull-wing or flat lead/termination extending over a rectangular PCB land.

Required visual relationships:

- component body on the package side;
- one lead/termination centered over the PCB land;
- land visibly wider than the lead;
- dimension arrows for `Lead width` and `Land width`;
- two small side-margin indicators between lead edges and land edges;
- optional small note `Illustrative geometry` if the numerical example is shown.

If numerical values are included, use only the current section example:

- `Lead width = 0.30 mm`
- `Land width = 0.45 mm`
- `Nominal side margin = 0.075 mm each side`

The numbers must be clearly identified as an example, not a universal design rule.

#### Right panel — Side view: heel, toe, and land length

Show the same lead/termination in simplified side view above or on the PCB land.

Required visual relationships:

- component body;
- lead/termination extending from the body toward the toe;
- PCB land beneath the lead;
- land extension beyond the outer lead end = `Toe region`;
- land extension toward/under the inner lead region = `Heel region`;
- visible overlap region between termination and land;
- concise label `Solderable overlap`.

Do not draw a detailed solder fillet shape or acceptance geometry.

### Required technical relationships

The final figure must correctly show that:

1. The component termination is narrower than the PCB land in the section's illustrative example.
2. The lead/termination is centered on the land in the nominal example.
3. The nominal side margin exists on **both sides** of the lead.
4. If numerical values are shown, `(0.45 - 0.30)/2 = 0.075 mm` is represented correctly.
5. The toe is on the **outer end** of the termination, away from the component body.
6. The heel is on the **inner end**, toward the component body.
7. Land length provides solderable area at both heel and toe according to package geometry.
8. The figure must not imply that every SMT land should be 20–50% wider than the lead.
9. The figure must not imply a universal toe or heel dimension.
10. The figure must not present solder-joint acceptance criteria.

### Spatial / geometry requirements

- Use a true top view for the width/side-margin panel.
- Use a simple orthographic side view for heel/toe relationships.
- Keep lead and land proportions visually plausible.
- The top-view land must extend equally beyond the centered lead on both sides.
- Dimension arrows must be perpendicular to the dimensions being measured.
- Do not run dimension lines through text.
- In the side view, place the component body to the left and the toe direction to the right for intuitive reading.
- The heel label must point to the inner land/termination region near the body.
- The toe label must point to the outer land extension beyond the termination end.
- Maintain approximately 60–80 px outer margins.
- Avoid crossing leader lines.
- Avoid perspective/isometric distortion because this figure teaches dimensional relationships.
