## Suggested Content for Figure 3.14.2

### Figure title for authoring/reference only

`Engineering Disposition of Supplier DFM Findings`

### Instructional purpose

Show how a supplier DFM finding moves through a controlled engineering disposition rather than being informally ignored, automatically accepted, or changed outside the controlled design.

The figure should make the **change/recheck loop** explicit.

### Central teaching message

> **A supplier DFM finding is an engineering input. It must receive a clear disposition, and any change to the controlled design or release data must be regenerated and rechecked before the finding is closed.**

### Canonical PNG filename

`3.14.2 - Engineering Disposition of Supplier DFM Findings.png`

### Intended mdBook display width

`900 px`

### Recommended canvas size / aspect ratio

- Authoring canvas: approximately `1800 x 1100 px`.
- Recommended aspect ratio: approximately `16:10` to `5:3`.
- Use a controlled flowchart with clear branches and one explicit recheck loop.

### Overall layout

Recommended flow:

1. top/left start box:
   - `Supplier DFM finding`
2. next review box:
   - `Engineering review / determine intent`
3. branch into four disposition boxes:
   - `Accept`
   - `Revise`
   - `Clarify`
   - `Deviation / exception`
4. merge into:
   - `Disposition documented`
5. decision diamond:
   - `Did the controlled design or any applicable release data change?`
6. `Yes` branch:
   - `Update controlled source`
   - `Regenerate affected outputs`
   - `Recheck relevant rules / findings`
7. `No` branch:
   - bypasses regeneration/recheck
8. final box:
   - `Finding closed when resolved`

The `Yes` branch must pass through controlled-source update/regeneration and then recheck before closure. The `No` branch may proceed to closure only after the disposition itself is documented and resolved.

### Required objects and labels

Required labels:

- `Supplier DFM finding`
- `Engineering review`
- `Accept`
- `Revise`
- `Clarify`
- `Deviation / exception`
- `Disposition documented`
- `Did design or release data change?`
- `Yes`
- `No`
- `Update controlled source`
- `Regenerate affected outputs`
- `Recheck relevant rules / findings`
- `Finding closed when resolved`

Recommended brief sublabels:

- under `Accept`: `Approved within controlled intent`
- under `Revise`: `Change design or released data`
- under `Clarify`: `Repair ambiguity / missing information`
- under `Deviation / exception`: `Document scope, rationale, approval, supplier/process, revision`

Do not add a fifth vague `other` category.

### Required technical relationships

- Every supplier finding must pass through engineering review before closure.
- `Accept`, `Revise`, `Clarify`, and `Deviation / exception` are **dispositions**, not necessarily four separate manufacturing processes.
- `Revise` must necessarily imply a controlled design/data change.
- `Clarify` may or may not change controlled data.
- `Accept` and `Deviation / exception` may close without changing geometry/data if properly documented and authorized.
- The decision `Did design or release data change?` determines whether regeneration/recheck is mandatory.
- When data/design change:
  - update the controlled source;
  - regenerate affected outputs;
  - recheck;
  - only then close the finding.
- The flow must not allow a changed output to bypass recheck.
- The figure should not imply that a supplier can unilaterally change controlled product intent.

### Spatial / geometry requirements

- Keep the four disposition boxes aligned as a clear row or 2 × 2 block.
- Use equal visual weight for the four disposition options; do not present `Accept` as the default.
- Use a single merge point after the four dispositions.
- The `Yes` and `No` branches from the change-decision diamond must be clearly labeled.
- Use a curved or orthogonal loop only where needed to show rechecking before closure.
- Avoid crossing arrows.
- Keep arrow directions unambiguous.
- If color is used:
  - neutral/blue for finding/review;
  - green for accept/close;
  - orange for clarify/deviation;
  - red should be limited to unresolved/change-required warning emphasis, not entire boxes.


### Color / style guidance

- Clean engineering-textbook appearance consistent with the current EPAC figure set.
- Explicit white background; never rely on viewer transparency/default canvas color.
- Flat 2D vector/schematic style rather than photorealistic software screenshots.
- Restrained engineering palette:
  - blue/blue-gray for normal review/check paths;
  - neutral gray for controlled documents, review frames, and secondary objects;
  - muted green for acceptable/closed/consistent states;
  - muted orange for caution or engineering-review states;
  - limited red only for mismatches, unresolved findings, or blocked release states.
- Sans-serif labels sized for the 900 px mdBook render.
- Moderate line weights and consistent visual hierarchy.
- Do not rely on color alone; pair color with labels, arrows, line style, symbols, or panel structure.
- No decorative gradients, glossy rendering, vendor logos, software UI chrome, watermarks, or branded screenshots.
- Do not embed the external figure number, final caption, or an oversized duplicate overall title inside the artwork.


### Scope boundaries

This figure should **not** teach:

- the whole Section 3.2 product-development workflow;
- supplier CAM-editing procedures;
- detailed organizational change-control forms;
- corrective-action systems;
- nonconformance/MRB workflows;
- production defect troubleshooting;
- the Chapter 3 applied feature checklist.

The visual is one **pre-release DFM finding-resolution loop**.

### Technical-verification basis

Use as grounding:

- current Sections 3.14.3 and 3.14.4;
- approved outline requiring:
  - fabricator/assembler findings;
  - engineering accept/revise/clarify/deviation response;
  - rechecking after changes;
- current supplier engineering-review practice showing that findings may require approval, repair, clarification, or exception handling;
- Chapter 2 release-control continuity that controlled design changes require synchronized output updates.

Important nuance:
- routine supplier process compensation inside an agreed process is different from a change to controlled product intent;
- only changes to controlled design/release data trigger the explicit regeneration/recheck branch in this figure.
