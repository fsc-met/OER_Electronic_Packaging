## Suggested Content for Figure 3.6.3

**Figure title for authoring/reference only:**  
`Solder-Mask-Defined and Non-Solder-Mask-Defined Lands`

### Instructional purpose

Make the geometric definition of **non-solder-mask-defined (NSMD)** and **solder-mask-defined (SMD)** lands visually unambiguous.

The figure should allow a reader to answer:

- Is the mask opening larger or smaller than the copper land?
- What feature defines the exposed solderable area?
- Which pad edges are exposed versus overlapped by solder mask?

This figure supports Section 3.6.7 and should introduce the distinction without expanding into detailed solder-mask design rules from Section 3.9.

### Central teaching message

> **NSMD: the solder-mask opening is larger than the copper land, so the copper defines the exposed solderable land.**

> **SMD: the solder-mask opening is smaller than the copper land, so the mask opening defines the exposed solderable area.**

### Overall layout

Use a **two-column side-by-side comparison**.

Each column should include:

1. a simple top-view schematic;
2. a simple cross-section directly below it.

This paired view is recommended because:

- the top view makes the relative opening diameter/size intuitive;
- the cross-section makes mask overlap versus exposed copper-edge geometry explicit.

#### Left column — NSMD

Top view:

- circular copper land;
- larger circular solder-mask opening;
- visible annular clearance between copper edge and mask edge.

Cross-section:

- copper land on PCB substrate;
- solder mask ending outside the copper-land edge;
- copper top and side edge visibly exposed.

Required concise labels:

- `NSMD`
- `Mask opening larger than copper land`
- `Copper defines exposed land`

#### Right column — SMD

Top view:

- circular copper land;
- smaller solder-mask opening;
- solder mask visibly overlapping the copper-land perimeter.

Cross-section:

- copper land on PCB substrate;
- solder mask overlaps the outer copper edges;
- only center portion of copper exposed through the opening.

Required concise labels:

- `SMD`
- `Mask opening smaller than copper land`
- `Mask opening defines exposed area`

At the top or bottom, include the clarification:

`SMD here = solder-mask-defined`

This prevents confusion with `surface-mount device`.

### Required technical relationships

The final figure must correctly show that:

1. In NSMD geometry, the solder-mask opening is larger than the copper land.
2. In NSMD geometry, solder mask does not overlap the copper land edge.
3. In NSMD geometry, the copper land geometry defines the solderable area.
4. In SMD geometry, the copper land is larger than the solder-mask opening.
5. In SMD geometry, solder mask overlaps the copper-land perimeter.
6. In SMD geometry, the mask opening defines the exposed solderable area.
7. The figure must not imply that NSMD is always superior.
8. The figure must not imply that SMD is always inferior or only used for defects.
9. No fixed mask-expansion/web dimension should be presented.
10. The figure must not show solder paste or reflow behavior as part of the definition.

### Spatial / geometry requirements

- Use the same copper-land size in the NSMD and SMD comparison where practical so the mask relationship is the only primary difference.
- Use the same PCB substrate thickness and copper thickness in both cross-sections.
- For top views, center the mask opening on the copper land.
- NSMD opening must visibly extend beyond the copper edge.
- SMD opening must visibly lie inside the copper edge.
- In cross-section, solder-mask edges must align with the top-view relationship.
- Make the mask layer thick enough to see, but do not imply a realistic thickness scale.
- Use labels/leaders that do not cross.
- Do not use perspective.
- Maintain safe margins and balance between columns.

### Technical-verification basis

#### Current Section 3.6

The immediate basis is the strongest current Section 3.6 candidate:

- NSMD = mask opening larger than copper land;
- SMD = mask opening smaller than copper land;
- `SMD` in this subsection means solder-mask-defined;
- package/process guidance controls which strategy is appropriate;
- detailed mask geometry is deferred to Section 3.9.

#### Independent verification already recorded in Section 3.6 QA

Current Intel BGA guidance confirms the geometric definitions:

- NSMD: solder-mask opening larger than the copper land;
- SMD: solder-mask opening smaller than the copper land.

Intel recommends NSMD for many referenced BGA applications.

Current TI guidance demonstrates that this is not universal: at some very fine pitches, insufficient mask web can make an SMD approach appropriate.

The figure must therefore teach the definitions without ranking one construction as universally better.

#### Reference-book cross-check already recorded

Coombs discusses both NSMD and SMD constructions and supports treating the distinction as part of the package-to-board interface.
