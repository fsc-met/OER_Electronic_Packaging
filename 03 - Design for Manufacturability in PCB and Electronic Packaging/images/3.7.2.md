## Suggested Content for Figure 3.7.2

**Figure title for authoring/reference only:**  
`Annular-Ring Design Margin and Breakout Risk`

### Instructional purpose

Show why annular ring is not just a static geometric feature but a **manufacturing margin** that can be reduced by relative hole-to-copper positional variation.

The figure should let students visually connect:

- pad diameter;
- hole diameter;
- nominal annular-ring width;
- hole/registration offset;
- minimum realized ring on the worst side;
- reduced margin and breakout risk.

The figure should support the Section 3.7.2/3.7.3 examples:

- `0.30 mm` hole/drill value with `0.70 mm` pad gives `0.20 mm` nominal ring;
- a simplified `0.05 mm` relative offset reduces the worst-side ring from `0.20 mm` to approximately `0.15 mm`;
- a design that begins at `0.15 mm` nominal ring would fall to approximately `0.10 mm` under the same illustrative offset.

### Central teaching message

> **Annular ring is design margin around a plated hole. A centered CAD hole can lose ring on one side when the drilled hole and copper pattern are not perfectly registered.**

Secondary message:

> **Starting above a limiting capability value provides more margin against manufacturing variation.**

### Overall layout

Use **three top-view panels** arranged left-to-right.

#### Panel A — Centered geometry

Show:

- circular copper pad;

- dimension/label for `Pad diameter, D_p`;

- radial dimension `Nominal ring, a_nominal`;
- numerical example:
  - `D_h = 0.30 mm`
  - `D_p = 0.70 mm`
  - `a_nominal = 0.20 mm`.

This panel establishes the ideal geometry.

#### Panel B — Same design with relative offset

Use the **same pad diameter and same hole diameter** as Panel A.

Shift the hole center by an illustrative amount relative to the pad center.

Show:

- dashed pad centerline or original center;
- shifted hole center;
- arrow labeled `Relative offset, e = 0.05 mm`;
- worst-side realized ring labeled `a_min ≈ 0.15 mm`;
- visibly larger ring on the opposite side.

This panel teaches that offset redistributes annular ring rather than changing the pad/hole diameters.

#### Panel C — Lower-margin design under the same offset

Use a smaller pad while retaining the same `0.30 mm` hole and the same illustrative `0.05 mm` offset.

Recommended example:

- `D_h = 0.30 mm`;
- `D_p = 0.60 mm`;
- nominal ring = `0.15 mm`;
- worst-side realized ring ≈ `0.10 mm`.

Show the worst-side ring as visibly thin and label:

- `Reduced margin`
- `Approaching breakout risk`

Do not show catastrophic breakout unless the geometry is explicitly changed enough to cause it.

### Required technical relationships

The final figure must correctly show that:

1. For centered circular geometry, `a_nominal = (D_p - D_h)/2`.
2. `0.70 - 0.30 = 0.40 mm`, and half gives `0.20 mm`.
3. Relative hole-to-pad offset reduces ring on one side and increases it on the opposite side.
4. With the simplified first-order model, `0.20 - 0.05 = 0.15 mm`.
5. A `0.60 mm` pad around a `0.30 mm` hole has `0.15 mm` nominal ring.
6. Under the same simplified `0.05 mm` offset, its worst-side ring is approximately `0.10 mm`.
7. The `0.05 mm` offset is an **illustrative screening example**, not a universal tolerance.
8. The figure must not imply that copper and drill shift independently in only one manufacturing mechanism; use the broader term `relative hole-to-copper offset`.
9. The figure must not present the `0.15 mm` value as a universal acceptance requirement.
10. The figure should show breakout **risk**, not guarantee breakout at the example values.

### Technical-verification basis

#### Current Section 3.7

The section establishes:

- `a_nominal = (D_p - D_h)/2`;
- equivalent form `D_p = D_h + 2a_nominal`;
- representative minimum reference = `0.15 mm`;
- preferred design range = `0.20-0.25 mm`;
- example `D_h = 0.30 mm`, `a = 0.20 mm`, `D_p = 0.70 mm`;
- simplified screening model `a_min ≈ a_nominal - e`;
- illustrative offset `e = 0.05 mm`;
- `0.20 - 0.05 = 0.15 mm`;
- `0.15 - 0.05 = 0.10 mm`.

#### Current verification already recorded in the section

The current section notes that published fabrication examples vary by supplier and board construction:

- `0.15 mm` is a useful common minimum reference;
- `0.20-0.25 mm` provides more design margin;
- layer count, copper thickness, service/class, and supplier matter.

The figure must preserve this qualification.
