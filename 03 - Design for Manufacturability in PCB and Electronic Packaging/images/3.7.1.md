## Suggested Content for Figure 3.7.1

**Figure title for authoring/reference only:**  
`THT Lead-to-Finished-Hole Fit and Plating`

### Instructional purpose

Show why through-hole component fit is governed by the **finished plated hole**, not simply by the production drill diameter.

The figure should make the Section 3.7.1 geometry visible:

- the production drill creates an opening before plating;
- copper barrel plating reduces that opening to the finished plated hole;
- the component lead must fit inside the finished opening;
- the remaining difference between finished-hole diameter and maximum lead diameter is the **total diametral insertion clearance**.

The figure should support the section's representative example:

- maximum lead diameter = `0.50 mm`;
- selected diametral clearance = `0.20 mm`;
- finished-hole diameter = `0.70 mm`.

It should also reinforce the section's barrel-copper awareness value of approximately `20-25 μm` without implying that value is universal.

### Central teaching message

> **Specify and review the finished plated hole that the component lead actually passes through. The production drill is larger because barrel plating reduces the opening.**

Secondary message:

> **Lead-to-hole fit must include enough diametral clearance for assembly variation without treating one clearance value as universal.**

### Required technical relationships

The final figure must correctly show that:

1. The **production drill opening is larger** than the finished plated opening.
2. Copper is deposited on the hole wall and reduces the open diameter.
3. The component lead is checked against the **finished plated hole**.
4. `C` is **total diametral clearance**, not clearance on one side.
5. With `D_lead,max = 0.50 mm` and `C = 0.20 mm`, `D_f = 0.70 mm`.
6. If the lead is centered, the nominal radial clearance is half the total diametral clearance, but the figure should not relabel `C` as per-side clearance.
7. Barrel plating is shown on both sides of the hole wall.
8. The figure must not imply that 20-25 μm is a universal plating requirement.
9. The figure must not imply that the designer should manually choose a production drill by subtracting/adding one fixed compensation value.
10. The figure must not present solder-fill acceptance criteria.

### Technical-verification basis

#### Current Section 3.7

The current section establishes:

- `D_f = D_lead,max + C`;
- representative diametral clearance = `0.15-0.30 mm`;
- example `0.50 mm + 0.20 mm = 0.70 mm`;
- finished hole is smaller than the production drill after plating;
- barrel-copper awareness value = approximately `20-25 μm`;
- actual drill compensation must follow the selected fabricator's process.

#### Current technical verification already recorded in the section

The section's verification notes state that:

- plated holes are drilled oversize to reach the desired finished size after plating;
- representative plated-hole tolerances are supplier/process dependent;
- `20 μm` minimum hole-wall copper appears in current fabrication guidance;
- the public section intentionally uses `20-25 μm` only as engineering-scale awareness.

The figure must teach the geometric relationship without turning supplier examples into universal design requirements.
