## Suggested Content for Figure 3.10.2

### Figure title for authoring/reference only

`Silkscreen Clearance Text Height and Line Width`

### Instructional purpose

Give readers one controlled geometric figure that connects the **manufacturability of the legend** to three practical dimensions:

1. clearance from a solderable/exposed feature;
2. text height;
3. printed line/stroke width.

The figure should also show why a legend placed too close to a functional feature may be clipped or may intrude on the area that must remain clean and solderable.

### Central teaching message

> **Legend readability and manufacturability depend on both feature size and separation from functional PCB surfaces.**

Use the primary instructional values as **planning references**, not universal standards:

- legend-to-solderable-area clearance: about `0.10–0.15 mm` or more;
- text height: about `1.0 mm` or larger;
- printed line/stroke width: about `0.15 mm` or larger.

Current fabricators may support different values, so the selected supplier's rule definition and capability govern final release.

### Overall layout

Use a **two-part engineering geometry figure**.

#### Left panel — clearance from solderable feature

Show a top-view exposed copper pad / solderable feature with a nearby legend stroke or short reference designator.

Include:

- a clear nearest-edge-to-nearest-edge dimension for legend clearance;
- one acceptable planning example;
- one small poor/too-close example, clearly secondary.

#### Right panel — text manufacturability

Show a simple sample reference designator such as `R12` or `U3` with controlled dimensions:

- overall character/text height;
- representative printed stroke width.

The panels should use a common visual scale language but do not need to represent the same physical component.

### Required objects and labels

#### Left panel

Required:

- `Solderable / exposed copper`
- `Legend stroke`
- dimension arrow labeled `Legend clearance C`
- concise note: `Planning reference: about 0.10–0.15 mm`
- a small comparison showing `Too close` or `Overlap / clipping risk`

Optional:

- a restrained solder-mask field around the exposed copper to make the physical surface relationship obvious.

#### Right panel

Required:

- sample legend text such as `R12`;
- dimension arrow labeled `Text height H`;
- dimension arrow labeled `Stroke width W`;
- concise planning notes:
  - `H ≈ 1.0 mm or larger`
  - `W ≈ 0.15 mm or larger`

Add a small qualifier:

`Representative planning values — verify fabricator capability`

Do not use the word `minimum` inside the figure unless the label is explicitly tied to one named process/vendor, which is not desired for this vendor-neutral OER figure.

### Required technical relationships

- The three numerical values are **teaching/planning references**, not a combined design-rule formula.
- If the left panel includes a solder-mask opening, the clearance dimension should still terminate on the illustrated functional/solderable reference chosen for the panel; do not mix reference definitions in one dimension.
- Clearance must be measured from the **nearest edge of printed legend material** to the nearest edge of the functional/solderable feature shown.
- Do not dimension from text centerline to pad centerline.
- Do not imply that every fabricator uses the exact same geometric reference. The artwork should use one clear conceptual reference, while the qualifier states that the supplier rule controls final release.
- Text height must be the physical printed character height, not font point size.
- Stroke width must indicate the thickness of a printed character line, not total character width.
- The poor example must clearly show why too little separation is undesirable.
- If CAM clipping is illustrated, show the idea qualitatively; do not imply a universal clipping algorithm.

### Spatial / geometry requirements

- Keep the left and right panels visually separated.
- Dimension arrows must not cross object outlines or labels.
- Use short extension lines and conventional engineering-dimension placement.
- The good clearance example should be visually larger than the poor example.
- The poor example may show a legend stroke approaching or crossing the protected functional region, with a small `X` or dashed clipped portion.
- Use the same stroke-width representation in the sample text and the `W` dimension.
- Ensure `0.10–0.15 mm`, `1.0 mm`, and `0.15 mm` remain legible at 900 px.


### Visual style baseline

- Clean engineering-textbook figure, not a poster or marketing graphic.
- White or very light neutral background.
- Flat 2D top-view geometry unless a restrained isometric cue materially improves recognition.
- Consistent sans-serif labels and moderate line weights.
- Restrained PCB palette: muted green/blue-green solder mask, copper/gold pads, neutral gray components, white/light legend, navy/dark-neutral callouts.
- No photorealism, dramatic lighting, glossy rendering, decorative shadows, logos, watermarks, or branded commercial products.
- Do not rely on color alone; geometry, labels, and spatial relationships must remain understandable in grayscale.
- No embedded external figure number, external caption, or oversized overall title inside the artwork.


### Scope boundaries

This figure should **not**:

- imply the planning values are IPC universal requirements;
- compare multiple fabricators by name;
- teach solder-mask expansion/web geometry from Section 3.9;
- teach solder wetting, reflow, or solder-joint acceptance;
- teach AOI/X-ray/test-probe access from Section 3.11;
- teach typography or graphic-design aesthetics;
- use detailed printer-resolution or ink-chemistry discussion.

### Technical-verification basis

Primary instructional material:

- legend/silkscreen is not perfectly aligned to copper or solder mask;
- silkscreen must not overlap solderable areas;
- teaching values:
  - `0.10–0.15 mm` silkscreen-to-pad clearance;
  - `1.0 mm` text height;
  - `0.15 mm` line width.

Current verification checked 2026-09-13:

- JLCPCB publishes approximately `0.15 mm` pad-to-silkscreen clearance, `1.0 mm` minimum text height, and `0.15 mm` minimum line width for its standard capability.
- Eurocircuits publishes `0.10 mm` clearance to exposed/surface-finished copper, `1.0 mm` text height for good readability, and `0.10 mm` minimum legend line width.
- These differences confirm that the lecture values are appropriate **planning references** rather than universal absolute limits.
- Coombs supports checking nomenclature/legend against solder-mask and design layers and treating legend on SMT lands as a producibility concern.

Preferred approach:

4. preserve the selected baseline and make only targeted corrections during cyclic QA;
5. save every meaningful revision under a new filename;
6. do not copy or rename a candidate to the canonical publication filename until explicit project-owner acceptance.

If a generated concept contains text or geometry defects that cannot be corrected reliably, rebuild the figure as controlled SVG rather than repeatedly regenerating the entire composition.
