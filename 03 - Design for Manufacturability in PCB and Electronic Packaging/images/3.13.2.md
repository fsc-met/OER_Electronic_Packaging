## Suggested Content for Figure 3.13.2

### Figure title for authoring/reference only

`V-Score and Tab-Routed Depanelization Geometry`

### Instructional purpose

Compare two common panel-separation strategies at a DFM level:

- straight V-score / V-cut;
- routed outline with breakaway tabs, including a mouse-bite-style tab example.

The figure should make clear that the selected separation method changes the **physical geometry of the panel and the local keep-out region**.

### Central teaching message

> **V-score and tab routing are different physical separation methods. Their break lines, routed gaps, tabs, and nearby keep-outs must be planned differently.**

### Canonical PNG filename

`3.13.2 - V-Score and Tab-Routed Depanelization Geometry.png`

### Intended mdBook display width

`900 px`

### Recommended canvas size / aspect ratio

- Authoring canvas: approximately `1800 x 1050 px`.
- Recommended aspect ratio: approximately `12:7` to `16:9`.
- Use a matched two-panel top-view comparison.

### Overall layout

Left panel:
- `V-score separation`

Right panel:
- `Tab-routed separation`

Left panel should show:
- two or more rectangular boards sharing a straight score line;
- score line continuing fully across the compatible panel direction;
- a local shaded keep-out band adjacent to the score line.

Right panel should show:
- routed board outline with a visible routed gap;
- one or more solid tabs connecting the board to temporary panel material;
- at least one mouse-bite/perforated tab example;
- a local shaded keep-out zone around the break/tab region.

### Required objects and labels

Left-panel labels:

- `V-score line`
- `Straight separation path`
- `Local keep-out near score`

Right-panel labels:

- `Routed gap`
- `Breakaway tab`
- `Mouse-bite / perforated tab`
- `Local keep-out near tab`

Optional:
- `Finished board outline`
- `Temporary panel material`

Do not add numeric score depth, router diameter, perforation diameter, or tab width.

### Required technical relationships

#### V-score panel

- Score line must be straight.
- Boards sharing the score line should have compatible straight edges.
- Score line should visually continue through the panel direction rather than stop in the middle of a board edge.
- Keep-out band should be adjacent to the score line, not centered randomly on the board.
- Do not depict an irregular contour as being fully V-scored.

#### Tab-routed panel

- Routed gap must physically separate most of the board outline from the panel.
- Tabs must be the remaining mechanical bridges.
- Mouse-bite holes, if shown, must be located **within one breakaway tab**, not scattered along the routed gap or mistaken for tooling holes.
- Keep-out zone should be local to the break/tab region.
- Do not imply that tab routing is always superior or that mouse bites are mandatory.

### Spatial / geometry requirements

- Use similar board scale in both panels.
- Make the left board shape intentionally rectangular/straight-edged.
- Make the right board outline slightly irregular or at least routed around multiple sides so the contrast is visually justified.
- Use dashed or contrasting stroke for the V-score line.
- Use white/neutral routed gaps that clearly separate the board from panel material and maintain a continuous routed channel except where deliberate tabs remain.
- Tabs should be few and easy to identify at 900 px.
- Mouse-bite holes should be large enough to read as a row of drilled perforations but should not resemble tooling holes.
- Keep-out regions should be translucent and clearly local.

This figure should **not** teach:

- router diameter/feed/speed;
- exact tab width;
- exact mouse-bite drill diameter/pitch;
- exact component/copper keep-out distances;
- depanelization machine design;
- stress analysis during separation.

The public section prose owns representative routed spacing and qualified component-edge scales.
