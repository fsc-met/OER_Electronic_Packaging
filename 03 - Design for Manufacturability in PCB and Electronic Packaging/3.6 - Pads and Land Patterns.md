# 3.6 - Pads and Land Patterns

Section 3.5 focused on the empty space between PCB features. Section 3.6 focuses on the **solderable copper geometry directly under a component termination**.

A component can be placed at the correct X-Y location and still be difficult to assemble if its PCB lands do not match the actual package terminals.

> **The land pattern is the physical interface between the component termination and the PCB.**

A good land pattern must provide enough solderable area for normal placement and fabrication variation while still preserving spacing, routing, solder-mask, and assembly margin.

---

## 3.6.1 - Land Pattern, Pad, and Footprint

A **land** or **pad** is a conductive PCB area intended to make an electrical and mechanical connection to a component termination.

A **land pattern** is the complete arrangement of these lands for one component.

In PCB CAD, the word **footprint** is often used for the larger library item that may also include:

- the land pattern;
- package outline;
- courtyard;
- silkscreen;
- polarity or pin-1 marking;
- solder-mask and paste definitions;
- assembly information.

The land pattern is therefore one critical part of the footprint.

The geometry must be based on the actual package and termination dimensions, not only on a generic component name or electrical function.

---

## 3.6.2 - Match the Land to the Component Termination

For a leaded SMT component, useful geometric quantities include:

- lead or terminal width;
- lead or terminal length;
- lead pitch;
- land width;
- land length;
- the solderable area extending toward the **toe**, **heel**, and **sides** of the termination.

A land that is too small for the actual termination can reduce placement margin and solderable area. An unnecessarily large land can consume routing/spacing margin or change the intended solder-joint geometry.

The primary instructional material uses the example:

- component lead width = **0.30 mm**;
- PCB land width ≈ **0.45 mm**.

If the lead is centered on the land, the nominal side margin on each side is:

$$
m_{\text{side}}
=
\frac{w_{\text{land}}-w_{\text{lead}}}{2}
=
\frac{0.45-0.30}{2}
=
0.075\ \text{mm}
$$

This is a useful **geometry example**, but it is not a universal rule that every SMT land should be 20-50% wider than its termination. Current manufacturer land-pattern tables show that the required width increase can be smaller, similar, or package-specific depending on the termination and pitch.

For a real component, use the package manufacturer's recommended land pattern or another validated land-pattern method.

---

## 3.6.3 - Pitch, Land Width, and Remaining Gap

For a row of equal rectangular lands centered on a uniform pitch, with land width measured in the pitch direction:

$$
g = p-w
$$

where:

- $g$ = nominal edge-to-edge gap between adjacent lands;
- $p$ = center-to-center pitch;
- $w$ = land width measured in the pitch direction.

For example, if:

- pitch = **0.50 mm**;
- land width = **0.30 mm**;

then:

$$
g = 0.50-0.30=0.20\ \text{mm}
$$

If the land were widened to **0.35 mm** at the same pitch:

$$
g = 0.50-0.35=0.15\ \text{mm}
$$

This simple relationship explains an important DFM tradeoff:

> **Increasing land width can increase solderable area but reduce the spacing available between adjacent lands.**

Do not widen or shrink a manufacturer-recommended land pattern merely to satisfy one geometric preference. The complete package, solder-mask, fabrication, assembly, and routing requirements must still be satisfied.

---

## 3.6.4 - Symmetry and Copper Balance

For a symmetric two-terminal component, the two lands should normally provide similar geometric support to the two terminations.

Important DFM checks include:

- similar land size and shape;
- component centered on the intended land pair;
- no accidental pad asymmetry;
- no gross copper/thermal imbalance that causes one termination region to heat very differently from the other.

This does **not** mean that every connected trace or copper region must be identical. Electrical current, thermal spreading, grounding, and routing requirements may legitimately require different copper geometry.

The design goal is to avoid unnecessary asymmetry that reduces assembly margin.

As discussed in Section 3.4, tombstoning is a **multi-factor** defect. Land symmetry and copper balance are only part of the problem; solder-paste volume, placement, and reflow heating also matter.

Detailed solder-paste and reflow-process troubleshooting belongs in Chapter 4.

---

## 3.6.5 - Start with the Package Manufacturer's Recommended Land Pattern

The safest starting point for a component footprint is usually the current package drawing or land-pattern recommendation from the component/package manufacturer.

Check at least:

- exact package code;
- lead or terminal pitch;
- terminal width and length;
- pin count and pin-1 orientation;
- exposed-pad geometry, if present;
- recommended PCB land dimensions;
- any package-specific solder-mask or paste notes.

Do not assume that two packages with similar body dimensions use the same land pattern.

Do not assume that a generic CAD-library footprint is correct simply because its package name looks similar.

A useful workflow is:

> **Package drawing -> recommended land pattern -> verified CAD footprint -> DFM review -> production release**

Industry land-pattern standards and validated internal libraries can also be useful, but the component-specific package documentation remains an important check.

A practical comparison is:

| Land-pattern condition | Manufacturing implication |
|---|---|
| Land pattern matches the exact package recommendation | Best starting point for placement, solderable area, spacing, and process review |
| Land is smaller/shorter than intended without validation | Less tolerance for placement or package variation; solderable area may be reduced |
| Land is enlarged without checking pitch/spacing | Adjacent gap, mask web, and routing margin can be reduced |
| Footprint copied from a similar package code | Hidden dimensional or pin-1/exposed-pad differences may create assembly defects |

## 3.6.6 - Fine-Pitch, QFN, and BGA Awareness

As pitch becomes smaller, several features interact more strongly:

- land width;
- land-to-land gap;
- solder-mask opening and mask web;
- routing space;
- fabrication tolerance;
- placement tolerance;
- solder-paste deposition and assembly capability.

A current Microchip assembly example for a **0.50 mm-pitch QFN** uses a **0.45 mm × 0.30 mm package terminal** with a recommended **0.85 mm × 0.30 mm PCB perimeter land**.

This example is useful because it shows that land **length** may extend beyond the package termination while land **width** can remain closely matched to the terminal width.

A current Microchip BGA design guide provides another useful scale: for one **0.50 mm-pitch BGA family**, a **0.275 mm PCB ball pad diameter** is suggested.

These are package-specific examples, not universal QFN or BGA rules.

For QFN packages, also check the center exposed pad. Its electrical connection, solderable area, and any thermal-via pattern must follow the specific component documentation.

Do not assume that every exposed pad is ground.

For BGA packages, the pad diameter, ball pitch, solder-mask strategy, and escape-routing method interact. Hidden joints also affect inspection planning, but detailed inspection strategy is covered later in Section 3.11.

## 3.6.7 - Solder-Mask-Defined and Non-Solder-Mask-Defined Lands

For some leadless and array packages, the effective solderable land is described as either **non-solder-mask-defined (NSMD)** or **solder-mask-defined (SMD)**.

> **In this subsection, SMD means solder-mask-defined, not surface-mount device.**

### NSMD

For an NSMD land:

- the solder-mask opening is larger than the copper land;
- the copper geometry defines the solderable land area;
- the copper pad edges are exposed.

### SMD

For an SMD land:

- the copper pad is larger than the solder-mask opening;
- the solder-mask opening defines the exposed solderable area.

</div>

Many package suppliers recommend NSMD construction for common BGA or QFN applications, but this is not a universal rule. For example, very fine pitch can leave too little solder-mask web for an NSMD strategy, and some package/application guidance intentionally uses SMD pads. The package recommendation and the PCB/assembly process should control the choice.

Detailed solder-mask expansion, registration, and mask-dam design are developed in Section 3.9.

## 3.6.8 - Applied Land-Pattern Review

A useful land-pattern review asks whether the PCB geometry actually supports the package and the selected manufacturing process.

| Design observation | DFM interpretation | Typical response |
|---|---|---|
| A 0.30 mm lead is centered on a 0.45 mm land | About 0.075 mm nominal side margin exists on each side in this illustrative geometry | Check land length, pitch, manufacturer recommendation, and process capability before release |
| 0.50 mm-pitch lands are 0.30 mm wide | Nominal adjacent gap is 0.20 mm | Compare with the applicable spacing rule and package recommendation |
| The same 0.50 mm-pitch lands are widened to 0.35 mm | Nominal adjacent gap falls to 0.15 mm | Do not widen the land casually; check whether the complete land pattern remains manufacturable |
| A symmetric chip component uses noticeably different left/right land sizes | The land pattern itself introduces asymmetry | Correct the footprint unless the package documentation intentionally requires the difference |
| One terminal of a two-terminal chip connects directly to a large copper region while the other uses a narrow trace | Thermal behavior can become asymmetric even if the lands are the same size | Review the copper connection and assembly risk without violating electrical/thermal requirements |
| A QFN exposed-pad footprint was copied from a similar-looking package without checking the datasheet | Exposed-pad size, function, and via requirements may be wrong | Use the exact package drawing and recommended land pattern |
| A 0.50 mm-pitch BGA footprint uses arbitrary pad diameters because the body size matches another BGA | BGA pad diameter and mask strategy are package/process dependent | Use the package-specific recommended land pattern and verify PCB/assembly capability |

The practical review logic is:

> **Match the package -> check the land geometry -> check spacing and mask interactions -> check manufacturing margin -> verify the source of the footprint data.**

---

## Section Takeaway

The main ideas from this section are:

- the land pattern is the physical solderable interface between the component termination and the PCB;
- land width and length must match the actual package geometry while allowing normal manufacturing and placement variation;
- the 0.30 mm lead / 0.45 mm land example is useful for understanding side margin, but it is not a universal percentage rule;
- fine-pitch geometry creates a direct tradeoff between land width and the remaining spacing between adjacent lands;
- symmetric components generally benefit from symmetric land geometry and balanced local copper conditions;
- package-manufacturer land-pattern recommendations are an important starting point for real footprints;
- QFN exposed pads and BGA ball lands require package-specific attention;
- NSMD and SMD land definitions describe different relationships between copper and solder mask;
- a footprint should be verified before release rather than trusted only because it exists in a CAD library.

Section 3.7 next develops **holes, annular rings, and vias**, including the design margin needed for finished-hole size, drilling variation, and via structures.

### Fresh technical verification

Verification date: 2026-09-12.

#### Lecture pad-width rule

The primary instructional material states:

- pad width is typically 20%-50% larger than lead width;
- 0.30 mm lead -> about 0.45 mm pad.

Current manufacturer data confirms the **teaching purpose** but not one universal percentage.

Examples:

- TI's land-pattern recommendation table includes PQFP cases such as 0.26 mm lead width -> 0.30 mm land width and 0.27 mm lead width -> 0.32 mm land width;
- other packages use different relationships;
- therefore the public OER retains 0.30 -> 0.45 mm only as an instructional geometry example and explicitly rejects a universal percentage rule.

The side-margin calculation is correct:

`(0.45 - 0.30) / 2 = 0.075 mm`.

#### Fine-pitch QFN example

Microchip AN4498 for a 48-lead, 7 mm x 7 mm QFN gives:

- contact pitch = 0.50 mm;
- package perimeter terminal = 0.45 mm x 0.30 mm;
- recommended PCB perimeter land = 0.85 mm x 0.30 mm;
- contact-pad spacing = 0.20 mm.

This supports the public QFN example and the pitch-gap calculation:

`0.50 - 0.30 = 0.20 mm`.

#### BGA example

Current Microchip BGA guidance suggests NSMD board pads for the referenced package families and lists:

- 1.0 mm ball pitch -> 0.50 mm PCB pad diameter;
- 0.8 mm ball pitch -> 0.40 mm PCB pad diameter;
- 0.5 mm ball pitch -> 0.275 mm PCB pad diameter.

The public section uses only the 0.50 mm / 0.275 mm example to establish engineering scale.

#### NSMD versus SMD

Current Intel BGA guidance defines:

- NSMD: solder-mask opening larger than the copper land;
- SMD: solder-mask opening smaller than the copper land.

Intel recommends NSMD for most referenced applications.

Current TI guidance shows why this should not be made universal: for some 0.4 mm-pitch BGA applications, insufficient mask web with NSMD geometry can create assembly problems and an SMD strategy may be preferred.

Conclusion:

- definitions are stable;
- preferred choice is package/process dependent;
- detailed mask-dam capability belongs in Section 3.9.

#### Reference-book cross-check

Coombs confirms that land size and pad definition can materially affect BGA solder-joint behavior and discusses both NSMD and SMD constructions.

The public OER does not reproduce proprietary acceptance criteria or reliability tables.
