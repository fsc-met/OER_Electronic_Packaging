# 3.9 - Solder Mask

Section 3.8 focused on the manufacturability of the copper geometry itself. The next layer to review is the **solder mask** placed over that copper.

Chapter 2 introduced the physical role of solder mask: it protects most outer-layer copper while leaving intentional openings over pads, test points, contacts, and other features that must remain exposed. During board assembly, the remaining mask also helps constrain the solderable area and reduce unintended bridging between nearby solderable features.

The DFM question is different:

> **How large should each mask opening be, and will the intended mask remain in the correct place after normal registration variation?**

A useful design logic is:

> **Copper geometry + mask registration capability + required exposed solderable area -> solder-mask opening decision**

The main checks are:

- mask opening relative to the copper pad;
- mask expansion or overlap;
- registration margin;
- minimum mask web or dam between adjacent openings;
- fine-pitch openings that may merge;
- via tenting and other via-covering treatments.

Detailed solder-paste printing, reflow behavior, solder-joint shape, and assembly acceptance belong in Chapter 4.

---

## 3.9.1 - Solder-Mask Registration Is Finite

A solder-mask opening is created from design data, but the fabricated mask pattern is not perfectly aligned to the copper.

Small registration differences can occur because of:

- imaging and exposure accuracy;
- board dimensional change;
- process alignment;
- mask material and process capability.

Therefore, a mask opening should not be reviewed only as an ideal centered CAD shape.

If the opening is too small for the selected process, registration shift can cause solder mask to overlap copper that was intended to remain exposed.

If the opening is made unnecessarily large, nearby mask web can narrow or disappear, and adjacent copper features may become unintentionally exposed.

The design goal is not simply “make the opening as large as possible.” The goal is:

> **Expose the required copper while preserving enough mask and registration margin for the selected process.**

---

## 3.9.2 - Mask Expansion Describes Opening Size Relative to the Pad

For a simple symmetric rectangular or circular pad, define the nominal **mask expansion per side** as:

$$
E=\frac{W_{\text{open}}-W_{\text{pad}}}{2}
$$

where:

- $E$ = solder-mask expansion per side;
- $W_{\text{open}}$ = nominal mask-opening width or diameter;
- $W_{\text{pad}}$ = copper-pad width or diameter.

Equivalently,

$$
W_{\text{open}}=W_{\text{pad}}+2E
$$

For **positive expansion**, the mask opening is larger than the copper pad.

For example, a 0.30 mm pad with a 0.05 mm opening expansion on each side gives:

$$
W_{\text{open}}=0.30+2(0.05)=0.40\ \text{mm}
$$

A useful conventional planning scale is approximately **0.05-0.10 mm per side**.

However, this is not universal. Modern direct-imaging processes can support smaller clearances and, in some constructions, a nominal **1:1 opening** may be supported. Package-specific solder-mask-defined (SMD) lands intentionally use an opening smaller than the copper pad.

Therefore:

- **positive expansion** means opening larger than the pad;
- **zero expansion** means nominal 1:1 geometry;
- **negative expansion / overlap** means the mask opening is smaller than the copper pad.

Section 3.6 introduced the geometric distinction between **non-solder-mask-defined (NSMD)** and **solder-mask-defined (SMD)** lands. The package recommendation and fabrication process should control that choice.

## 3.9.3 - Fine-Pitch Pads Create a Mask-Web Tradeoff

The narrow strip of solder mask left between adjacent openings is commonly called a **solder-mask web**, **mask dam**, or **mask segment**.

When a separate web is intended to remain, it must be wide enough to survive the selected solder-mask process.

For two equal adjacent pads on pitch $p$, with pad width $w$ measured along the pitch direction and positive expansion $E$ per side in that same direction, the nominal mask web is:

$$
B=p-w-2E
$$

where $B$ is the nominal mask-web width between the two openings.

This equation shows the tradeoff directly:

- wider pads reduce the available mask web;
- larger mask expansion also reduces the available mask web;
- tighter pitch reduces the available mask web.

For example, with:

- pitch $p=0.50$ mm;
- pad width $w=0.30$ mm;
- expansion $E=0.05$ mm per side;

the nominal mask web is:

$$
B=0.50-0.30-2(0.05)=0.10\ \text{mm}
$$

A **0.10 mm** mask web remains a useful mainstream teaching reference.

Current fabrication processes vary. Some direct-imaging processes can support webs around **0.07-0.075 mm**, while other mask colors, heavier copper, or conventional processes may require more.

If the calculated web is below the fabricator's minimum, the remaining strip may be removed or the openings may be merged during fabrication.

## 3.9.4 - When a Fine-Pitch Mask Web Cannot Be Maintained

A narrow or missing mask web is not automatically a design mistake.

At fine pitch, the design may legitimately require a different mask strategy.

Depending on the package and fabricator, possible approaches include:

- reducing positive mask expansion;
- using tighter-registration direct imaging;
- allowing adjacent openings to merge;
- using a solder-mask-defined land strategy when supported by the package recommendation.

The correct decision is package- and process-dependent.

The important DFM lesson is:

> **Do not force a nominal mask web that the selected process cannot reliably manufacture.**

A 0.50 mm-pitch example illustrates the sensitivity:

- pad width = 0.30 mm;
- copper gap = 0.20 mm.

With 0.05 mm expansion per side, the web is 0.10 mm.

If expansion increases to 0.075 mm per side:

$$
B=0.50-0.30-2(0.075)=0.05\ \text{mm}
$$

That 0.05 mm web is below the common 0.10 mm teaching reference and below the published minimum web widths of several direct-imaging processes, so the design should not assume that the web will remain.

## 3.9.5 - Via Tenting Is a Solder-Mask Treatment, Not the Same as Via Filling

Vias can be handled differently in the solder-mask data.

### Untented / exposed via

An **untented** via has a mask opening over the via region.

The via pad and/or hole remains exposed according to the fabrication data.

### Tented via

A **tented** via has no intended solder-mask opening over the via, so the mask is intended to cover the via region.

However, complete closure of the hole depends on:

- via diameter;
- mask-application process;
- mask material;
- whether the fabricator requires a separate via-filling step.

Therefore, “tented” should not automatically be interpreted as “the hole is guaranteed to be completely sealed.”

### Solder-mask plugged or filled via

Some fabricators use terms such as **plugged via** or **solder-mask-filled via** for a process in which mask material is intentionally driven into the via.

Supplier terminology varies, so the fabrication note should be explicit.

### Filled and plated-over/capped via-in-pad

This is a different, more controlled construction used for applications such as fine-pitch via-in-pad.

In a common process, the via is intentionally filled, often with resin, then planarized and copper capped or plated over to create a controlled solderable surface. Other specialized fill processes also exist, so the fabrication drawing should state the required construction explicitly. Section 3.7 introduced this advanced construction.

> **Tenting is a mask-covering decision. Filled/plated-over via-in-pad is a different fabrication process.**

## 3.9.6 - Applied Solder-Mask Review

The table below uses the representative values in this section for engineering judgment. Final release must use the selected fabricator's actual solder-mask capability and the package recommendation.

| Design observation | DFM interpretation | Typical response |
|---|---|---|
| 0.30 mm pad with 0.05 mm positive expansion per side | Nominal mask opening is 0.40 mm | Reasonable conventional example; verify registration capability |
| 0.30 mm pad with only 0.02 mm positive expansion | Smaller than the conventional 0.05-0.10 mm planning range | Do not automatically fail; verify whether the selected direct-imaging process supports the geometry |
| 0.50 mm pitch, 0.30 mm pad, 0.05 mm expansion | Nominal mask web is 0.10 mm | Common mainstream teaching example |
| Same geometry with 0.075 mm expansion | Nominal mask web falls to 0.05 mm | Likely too small for many standard processes; use a different mask strategy or verified tighter capability |
| Mask web specified as 0.06 mm | Below the common 0.10 mm reference and below the published minimum web of some current DI/LDI processes | Treat as process-sensitive and verify with the fabricator |
| Fine-pitch footprint has no manufacturable web between openings | Separate mask dams may not survive | Consider merged openings, tighter imaging, or package-approved SMD geometry |
| Open ordinary via is intentionally left exposed | Untented via | Ensure the data and fabrication intent are clear |
| Via has no mask opening but no separate fill specification | Tented intent only | Do not assume guaranteed complete hole closure |
| Fine-pitch via-in-pad requires a planar solderable surface | Tenting alone is not the same construction | Use the required filled/planarized/plated-over process |

A practical review sequence is:

> **Check pad exposure -> check expansion/overlap -> check registration margin -> check fine-pitch mask web -> check via mask treatment -> verify package and fabricator rules.**

## Section Takeaway

The main ideas from this section are:

- solder-mask alignment to copper has finite registration accuracy;
- mask expansion describes the nominal opening size relative to the copper pad;
- **0.05-0.10 mm per side** remains a useful conventional planning scale, but modern direct imaging can support tighter or 1:1 geometry in some processes;
- fine-pitch mask web is controlled by pitch, pad width, and opening expansion;
- **0.10 mm** remains a useful common mask-web teaching reference, while tighter processes may support smaller values;
- if a reliable mask web cannot be maintained, use a package- and process-appropriate alternative rather than forcing an unmanufacturable sliver;
- NSMD and SMD are both intentional mask strategies when properly matched to the package and process;
- via tenting is a solder-mask treatment and is not the same as filled/capped or plated-over via-in-pad construction.

Section 3.10 next examines **silkscreen and markings**, where text, polarity indicators, and reference designators must remain readable without interfering with solderable features.
