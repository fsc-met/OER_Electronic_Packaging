# 3.5 - Spacing and Clearance Rules

Section 3.4 introduced the need for physical room around components. Section 3.5 turns that idea into measurable **spacing and clearance rules**.

In DFM, clearance is the intentional empty space between two relevant features. It is used to provide margin for:

- manufacturing tolerances;
- component-placement variation;
- solder-paste and solder behavior;
- routing, scoring, and handling;
- inspection and rework access;
- electrical isolation when required.

> **A spacing number has meaning only when you know which two feature edges are being measured and why that spacing is needed.**

---

## 3.5.1 - What Does a Clearance Dimension Measure?

Clearance is normally measured **edge-to-edge** between the relevant physical or copper features, not center-to-center.

Examples include:

- component body/courtyard to component body/courtyard;
- component/courtyard to board edge;
- pad to pad;
- trace to trace;
- trace to pad;
- copper to board edge.

Some assembly rule sets define a **component manufacturing courtyard** around the maximum component boundary. Neighboring components, hardware, and sometimes the board edge are kept outside that courtyard so the assembly process has usable physical space.

This distinction matters because two components can have the same center-to-center distance but very different body-to-body clearance if their package sizes differ. The same edge-to-edge principle is used later for pad and copper spacing.

---

## 3.5.2 - Minimum and Preferred Spacing Are Different

A **minimum** spacing is the smallest value allowed by the rule set being used for the design.

A **preferred** spacing moves the design away from that limit and provides additional process or access margin.

For example, a component pair may satisfy a minimum spacing rule but still be difficult to inspect or rework.

The practical DFM question is therefore not only:

> **Does it pass?**

It is also:

> **How close is the design to the limit, and is that margin appropriate for the product and manufacturing process?**

Section 3.3 introduced this minimum-versus-preferred idea. Here it is applied to measurable PCB spacing.

---

## 3.5.3 - Component-to-Component and Component-to-Edge Spacing

For the representative instructional DFM rule set used in this chapter, the following values provide a practical starting point for ordinary SMT placement review:

| Feature | Representative review value | Practical interpretation |
|---|---:|---|
| Small SMT component-to-component spacing | **0.5 mm review minimum** | Basic placement clearance within the instructional rule set |
| Component spacing where routine rework access matters | **1.0 mm preferred** | More room for tools and inspection |
| Near connectors, tall parts, or other access-sensitive areas | **2.0 mm preferred planning target** | Extra local access where geometry is less forgiving |
| General component-to-board-edge spacing | **2.5 mm (0.10 in) planning value** | Leaves room for handling, routing/depanelization, and tooling |
| Tall/heavy component to board edge | **3.0 mm preferred planning value** | Additional margin for handling and mechanical risk |

These are **representative instructional review values**, not universal industry requirements.

A real assembly rule may be **tighter or looser** depending on package type, equipment, panelization, inspection method, and rework expectations.

Current assemblers may use different package-specific manufacturing courtyards. For example, an assembly supplier may allow tighter spacing for small passive components while requiring larger courtyards around BGAs, connectors, crystals, canned capacitors, or other packages.

For production release:

> **Use the intended assembler's component-courtyard and board-edge rules.**

## 3.5.4 - Pad-to-Pad Spacing

Pad-to-pad spacing matters because adjacent solderable surfaces can increase the opportunity for solder bridging when pitch becomes tight.

For the representative chapter rule set:

- **0.20 mm (about 8 mil)** is used as a representative minimum review value for ordinary SMT pad-to-pad spacing;
- **0.25 mm** is used as a preferred value where the package geometry allows it.

Fine-pitch packages may require smaller values, but those designs must be evaluated with the actual package, land pattern, solder-mask strategy, stencil/paste design, and assembly process.

A smaller pad-to-pad spacing is therefore not automatically "wrong." It means the design has moved into a different process window.

Detailed land-pattern design is covered in Section 3.6, and solder-mask geometry is covered in Section 3.9.

---

## 3.5.5 - Trace-to-Trace and Trace-to-Pad Spacing

Bare-PCB fabrication also requires minimum copper spacing.

For ordinary 1 oz copper and mainstream fabrication, **0.10 mm (4 mil)** trace-to-trace spacing appears in current standard-capability tables from major PCB fabricators. A larger value such as **0.15-0.20 mm** provides more manufacturing margin when routing density allows it.

For the representative rule set in this chapter:

| Copper spacing | Representative minimum | Preferred planning range |
|---|---:|---:|
| Trace-to-trace | **0.10 mm (4 mil)** | **0.15-0.20 mm** |
| Trace-to-pad | **0.10 mm (4 mil)** | **0.15-0.20 mm** |

Useful unit relationships are:

$$
4\ \text{mil} = 0.1016\ \text{mm} \approx 0.10\ \text{mm}
$$

and

$$
8\ \text{mil} = 0.2032\ \text{mm} \approx 0.20\ \text{mm}
$$

Actual capability depends on:

- copper thickness;
- layer count;
- fabrication process;
- supplier capability;
- feature location;
- required yield/cost.

Section 3.8 returns to trace, copper, and board-edge geometry in more detail.

---

## 3.5.6 - Component-to-Edge Is Not the Same as Copper-to-Edge

A component-to-board-edge rule protects assembly, handling, inspection, and depanelization space.

A **copper-to-board-edge** rule protects copper features from routing, scoring, registration, and exposed-copper problems.

These are different rules.

Current fabricators may publish copper-to-edge values much smaller than the component-to-edge values used for assembly. The exact value can also change between:

- routed edges;
- V-scored edges;
- outer copper layers;
- inner copper layers;
- special edge features.

For this reason, do not use the 2.5 mm component-placement value as a copper-to-edge rule.

Copper-to-edge geometry is developed in Section 3.8.

## 3.5.7 - Manufacturing Spacing Is Not the Same as Electrical Safety Clearance

The DFM values in this section are mainly manufacturing and assembly spacing values.

They do **not** replace voltage-dependent electrical **clearance** or **creepage** requirements.

A product operating at higher voltage may require much larger separation because the required distance can depend on working voltage, transient conditions, insulation system, materials, environment, and the applicable safety standard.

> **When an electrical-safety spacing requirement is larger than a manufacturing minimum, the electrical-safety requirement controls the design.**

Detailed high-voltage insulation design is outside the scope of this introductory section.

---

## 3.5.8 - Applied Spacing Review

The following example uses the representative chapter rule set, not a universal supplier specification.

| Feature checked | Observed value | Representative rule | DFM decision | Engineering interpretation |
|---|---:|---:|---|---|
| Small SMT component spacing | 0.55 mm | 0.50 mm review minimum | Pass within example rule set | Meets the instructional review minimum, but has limited extra access margin |
| Rework-sensitive component spacing | 0.90 mm | 1.00 mm preferred | Review | Manufacturable may still be possible, but routine rework access is below the preferred target |
| Component to routed board edge | 1.8 mm | 2.5 mm planning value | Review | Move inward if practical or verify the intended panel/depanelization process |
| SMT pad-to-pad spacing | 0.22 mm | 0.20 mm minimum; 0.25 mm preferred | Pass / near preferred boundary | Above the instructional minimum but below the preferred value |
| Trace-to-trace spacing | 0.10 mm | 0.10 mm representative minimum | Pass at example limit | Current mainstream fabrication can support this scale, but more spacing gives additional process margin |
| Trace-to-pad spacing | 0.08 mm | 0.10 mm minimum | Fail | Increase spacing or use a verified process that explicitly supports the tighter geometry |

The point of this review is not to memorize the table. It is to practice the reasoning:

> **Identify the feature -> identify the applicable rule -> compare the actual spacing -> judge the margin -> decide whether to revise or verify with the supplier.**

---

## Section Takeaway

The main ideas from this section are:

- spacing and clearance are measured between the relevant feature edges;
- the rule depends on what is being separated: components, board edges, pads, traces, or other copper;
- minimum and preferred spacing are different;
- representative chapter values provide useful engineering scale, but actual supplier/process rules control production release;
- 0.5 mm component spacing and 1.0 mm preferred rework spacing are useful instructional review values for ordinary SMT placement;
- about 2.5 mm is the representative component-to-edge planning value used in this chapter;
- 0.20 mm pad-to-pad and 0.10 mm trace/copper spacing provide useful mainstream instructional scales, while preferred values provide more margin;
- component-to-edge and copper-to-edge rules serve different purposes;
- manufacturing spacing does not replace voltage-dependent electrical safety clearance/creepage requirements.

Section 3.6 next focuses on **pads and land patterns**, where package geometry and solder-joint formation determine the actual pad design.

### Current technical verification

Verification date: 2026-09-11.

#### Component manufacturing courtyard

Eurocircuits currently defines a component manufacturing courtyard around the maximum component boundary.

Published example clearances include:

- 0.25 mm default;
- 0.15 mm for components smaller than 0603;
- 0.5 mm for connectors, canned capacitors, and crystals;
- 1.0 mm for BGA devices.

Conclusion:

- the lecture's 0.5/1.0/2.0 mm spacing sequence is useful as a conservative instructional review rule set, especially for rework/access reasoning;
- it should not be described as a universal assembler capability table.

#### Trace and copper spacing

Current published fabrication examples:

- JLCPCB: 0.10/0.10 mm minimum track width/spacing for 1- and 2-layer 1 oz boards; 0.09/0.09 mm for multilayer 1 oz in its listed capability;
- PCBWay: 0.10 mm (4 mil) minimum trace spacing in its standard capability table;
- Eurocircuits: 0.150 mm default outer-layer isolation distance in its configurator; tighter classes are available.

Conclusion:

- 0.10 mm (4 mil) remains a defensible mainstream minimum teaching value;
- 0.15-0.20 mm is a useful preferred instructional range when density allows;
- copper thickness and fabrication class matter.

#### Copper-to-edge

Eurocircuits currently publishes:

- routed outer-layer copper-to-edge: 0.25 mm;
- routed inner-layer copper-to-edge: 0.40 mm;
- V-score copper-to-edge: 0.45 mm.

Conclusion:

- the lecture's 1.0-1.5 mm `minimum copper-to-edge` statement should not be carried forward as a general industry minimum;
- copper-to-edge should be treated as a separate supplier/process rule and developed in Section 3.8.

#### IPC role

IPC's current revision table lists IPC-2221C as the generic printed-board design standard.

Public Section 3.5 does not reproduce proprietary IPC spacing criteria. It teaches the role of generic design requirements and supplier/process rules without presenting a universal IPC number.
