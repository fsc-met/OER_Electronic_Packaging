# 4.28 - Practice Problems

These problems review the major concepts developed throughout Chapter 4.

Each problem has **one deterministic answer or answer set** based on the Chapter 4 material and any data supplied in the problem. Problems are grouped in the same general sequence as the chapter. Numerical problems use **supplied exercise values or process requirements** when the real value would depend on the product, material, equipment, or approved manufacturing process.

The answer key is provided in **Section 4.29**.

Most problems are short factory-floor checks. They emphasize the **important knowledge points of Chapter 4**, not memorization of vendor-specific recipes. The goal is to verify that you can recognize the process, connect equipment to the physical operation, read basic process evidence, and choose a reasonable next engineering check.

## Authoring and QA Notes

### Purpose

Section 4.28 is a deterministic Chapter 4 knowledge-and-application check for beginning MET students. It is not an expert process-optimization exam.

The set emphasizes:

- recognition of the major PCB assembly processes and equipment;
- physical process understanding;
- basic numerical scale and first-order calculations;
- interpretation of simple process evidence;
- selection of a reasonable first engineering check;
- mixed-process planning and production-readiness awareness.

### Deterministic-problem rule

Every published problem in this section must have:

- one unambiguous answer or bounded answer set;
- sufficient information in the question or Chapter 4 text;
- a matching answer in Section 4.29;
- no grading dependence on open-ended process-design judgment;
- supplied governing limits whenever a real production decision would otherwise depend on product, paste, component, equipment, or company-specific requirements.

### Primary instructional-material fidelity

The practice set preserves the primary MET406 Chapter 4 teaching emphasis on:

- SMT process flow and major equipment;
- solder-paste printing, SPI, pick-and-place, and reflow;
- soldering physics and practical process variables;
- SMT defect recognition and evidence-based troubleshooting;
- THT insertion, wave, selective, and hand soldering;
- mixed SMT/THT process planning;

Lab #2 and Lab #3 reinforce defect identification, physical mechanism, process causes, and corrective-action reasoning. Those skills are represented here through deterministic evidence chains rather than open-ended grading.

Lab #4's mixed SMT/THT route-planning emphasis is retained. Its SPC-chart analysis is intentionally not included because the approved revised outline assigns detailed process-control/statistical work to Chapter 5.

### Numerical/content control

- Aperture-volume, area-ratio, coordinate-correction, residence-time, transfer-efficiency, wave-contact-time, and material-quantity calculations were independently recomputed.
- Product/process-dependent values are supplied inside the relevant problem.
- The $A_R\ge0.66$ value in Problem 8 is explicitly an exercise screening guideline, not a universal stencil acceptance rule.
- The SPI 80-120% windows and reflow limits used in Problems 10, 19, 20, and 28 are supplied instructional requirements for those problems, not universal manufacturing limits.
- Problem 22's 2-4 s wave-contact window is supplied for the exercise and is not stated as a universal recipe.

### Scope control

The practice set intentionally avoids detailed Chapter 5 content, including:

- AOI/X-ray program development or acceptance criteria;
- ICT/flying-probe/functional-test strategy;
- MRB/nonconformance systems;
- detailed rework/repair acceptance;
- yield/FPY/defect-rate metrics;
- SPC/control-chart interpretation;
- Cp/Cpk/process capability;
- formal root-cause/CAPA systems;
- MES/traceability-system depth;
- maintenance/calibration system design.

Chapter 4 retains only the inspection/evidence awareness needed to understand how the board is built and whether the startup process should continue or hold.
