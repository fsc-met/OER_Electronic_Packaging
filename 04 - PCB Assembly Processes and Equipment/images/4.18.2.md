# Figure 4.18.2 Suggested Content - Wave Contact Hole Filling and Solder Drainage

## Status

**Suggested-content specification - Rev2 QA-reviewed candidate**

This file defines the instructional content and figure-generation constraints for Figure 4.18.2 in Section 4.18, **Wave Soldering: Equipment, Process, and Common Problems**.

The figure has one instructional role: **show what physically happens at one representative plated through-hole during wave contact and as the board leaves the wave**.

Do **not** call this specification or generated artwork canonical/final before explicit project-owner acceptance.

## Figure Title for Authoring / Reference Only

**Wave Contact Hole Filling and Solder Drainage**

Do **not** place this overall title or the figure number inside the artwork.

---

## Instructional Purpose

Help students connect the machine-level process to the local solder-joint physics.

The figure should answer:

> **How does molten solder reach and fill the lead-to-barrel gap during wave contact, and why does clean separation/drainage matter as the board exits?**

---

## Central Teaching Message

> **During wave contact, heat plus wetting allow molten solder to enter the lead-to-barrel gap. As the board leaves the wave, excess liquid solder must separate and return to the wave while enough solder remains in the plated through-hole and around the lead to form the joint.**

## Overall Layout

Use **three equal or near-equal sequential panels** arranged left to right:

1. **Approach / first contact**
2. **Wave contact / hole filling**
3. **Exit / drain and separate**

Keep the same representative lead/PTH geometry across all three panels.

Show the board at a modest incline relative to the solder wave. The inclination should be consistent across the sequence and should not be labeled with a universal numeric angle.

Use one clear board-travel arrow across the sequence or one small arrow per panel.

## Required Technical Relationships

### Panel 1 — approach/contact
1. Board underside approaches/contacts the crest of the molten solder wave.
2. Lead protrudes through the PTH.
3. The lead-to-barrel gap is open before solder enters.
4. The board is not submerged in the solder pot.

### Panel 2 — wetting and hole filling
5. Molten solder must contact the bottom pad/lead region.
6. Solder must be shown **wetting the intended metal surfaces**, not simply being forced upward as a solid plug.
7. Molten solder rises into the lead-to-barrel gap while the lead, barrel, pad, and solder remain hot enough for wetting.
8. The visual must show solder contacting **both the lead and plated barrel**, rather than floating in the center of the hole.
9. The plated barrel remains visibly continuous.
10. Do not imply that "capillary action alone" guarantees complete PTH fill; heat, surface condition, flux/solderability, geometry, and contact time matter.

### Panel 3 — drain/separate
11. Board has moved downstream/upward away from the wave crest.
12. A liquid connection/neck to the wave breaks cleanly.
13. Excess solder returns downward toward the wave/pot.
14. Sufficient solder remains around the lead and in the PTH.
15. The final retained solder should be **conceptual**, not an IPC acceptance example.
16. Do not show a mandatory top-side fillet, one exact fill height, or one universal fillet shape.

## Spatial / Geometry Requirements

- Use the same PCB thickness, lead diameter, PTH diameter, and plated-barrel thickness in all three panels.
- Keep the lead centered enough for clear comparison; slight realistic eccentricity is acceptable but should not distract.
- Exaggerate the lead-to-barrel gap only enough to remain visible at 900 px.
- Exaggerate upward solder fill and drainage arrows modestly for teaching readability.
- Keep the wave surface smooth enough to read as a controlled wave, not turbulent splashing.
- Place the wave crest directly below the PTH during the contact panel.
- In the exit panel, visually separate the board/PTH from the wave crest while keeping the breaking solder neck physically plausible.
- Do **not** draw a long horizontal solder bridge trailing from the PTH to the wave; use a short neck at the separation point so the image does not resemble a bridging defect.
- Do not allow drainage arrows to pass through the lead or unrelated labels.
- Keep outer margins around **5-7%** and maintain comfortable panel padding.

## Technical-Verification Basis

**Current Section 4.18:** explains that molten solder contacts the bottom pads/leads, the lead/pad/PTH heat and wet, solder enters the lead-to-barrel gap, and excess solder must separate/drain as the board leaves the wave.

**Primary MET406 instructional material:** teaches wetting, PTH hole filling, representative wave-contact time, gravity/surface-tension effects, and the importance of drainage/separation at the wave exit.

**Approved reference hierarchy already used for Section 4.18:** Coombs supports PTH wetting/hole-fill behavior and the need to treat wave contact plus exit drainage as a coupled process. Current supplier/equipment guidance used in the section confirms that contact time and drainage depend on machine/product geometry.

Use these sources to verify the physics, but do not copy any published cross-section.
