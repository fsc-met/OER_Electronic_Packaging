# Figure 4.19.2 Suggested Content - Selective Soldering Nozzle Access Keep-Out and Programmed Withdrawal

## Status

**Suggested-content specification - Rev2 QA-reviewed candidate**

This file defines the instructional content and generation constraints for Figure 4.19.2 in Section 4.19, **Selective and Hand Soldering: When and How They Are Used**.

The figure has one instructional role: **teach the physical access envelope around a selective-soldering nozzle and show how programmed approach/contact/withdrawal geometry affects whether the local solder wave can reach and leave the intended THT joint cleanly**.

Do **not** call this specification or generated artwork canonical/final before explicit project-owner acceptance.

---

## Figure Title for Authoring / Reference Only

**Selective Soldering Nozzle Access Keep-Out and Programmed Withdrawal**

Do **not** place this overall title or the figure number inside the artwork.

---

## Instructional Purpose

Make the keep-out/access requirement physically obvious.

The figure should allow a reader to answer:

> **Can the selective-soldering nozzle physically reach the target joint, establish local solder contact, and leave the joint without colliding with nearby SMT, fixture walls, or other hardware?**

A second purpose within the same visual role is to show that the **exit path is programmed**, so solder separation can be controlled rather than left to an uncontrolled lift-off.

---

## Central Teaching Message

> **Selective soldering requires both an accessible nozzle envelope and a clean programmed exit. Nearby components and fixtures can block the nozzle, while approach, contact/dwell, and withdrawal motion control how the local solder wave reaches and separates from the joint.**

## Overall Layout

Use **two distinct but coordinated panels**.

### Left panel - Access / keep-out

Use a simplified **bottom-side plan view** of the PCB.

Show:

- selected THT pin or connector group;
- nozzle footprint centered under the target;
- an explicit **keep-out / access envelope** around the nozzle;
- nearby bottom-side SMT;
- a fixture wall/support or other physical obstruction;
- one clear-access example;
- one blocked/collision example, distinguished by geometry plus check/X symbol or explicit label.

### Right panel - Programmed contact and withdrawal

Use a simplified **side view** of the target joint and mini-wave.

Show the motion logic:

> **approach -> contact/dwell -> withdraw / peel-off**

A modest lateral component may be shown as an **example programmed withdrawal**, but label it `Example exit path` and do not imply that all machines require lateral motion.

The right panel should focus on liquid-solder separation, not defect appearance.

## Required Technical Relationships

1. The nozzle footprint must be centered/aligned with the intended THT target in the clear-access example.
2. The keep-out envelope is a **tool/process clearance concept**, not a universal numeric dimension. Its outline must be visually tied to the nozzle/tooling envelope rather than looking like a PCB design-rule boundary.
3. Nearby SMT can exist on the bottom side as long as it remains outside the nozzle/wave access region.
4. Fixture walls/supports can block access and therefore must be included in manufacturability/process planning.
5. The blocked example must show a real physical interference, not merely a red-colored area.
6. The nozzle should remain below the PCB and contact the target with a local solder fountain.
7. Approach leads into contact/dwell; withdrawal occurs after contact.
8. Withdrawal should visibly break the local solder connection cleanly.
9. If both vertical and lateral exits are shown, they must be presented as **alternatives/examples**, not mandatory sequential motions.
10. The figure must not state that lateral motion always reduces bridging or that one path is universally better.
11. Do not embed 3-8 mm nozzle diameter, 1-3 s dwell, 1-2 mm Z scale, pot temperature, or other recipe values.
12. Do not show workmanship acceptance criteria or defect limits.

## Spatial / Geometry Requirements

### Left panel

- Keep the target near the panel center.
- Draw the nozzle footprint as a simple circle/oval beneath the target.
- Draw the keep-out envelope as a larger dashed outline around the nozzle footprint.
- Keep bottom-side SMT and fixture/obstruction clearly outside or intruding into that envelope depending on the example.
- Use check/X symbols plus labels so pass/fail meaning is not color-dependent.
- Ensure the blocked example visibly intersects the required nozzle/access envelope.
- Keep leader lines short and non-crossing.

### Right panel

- Place PCB approximately horizontal.
- Show nozzle below the PCB.
- Use a clear approach arrow toward the joint.
- Show contact/dwell with the local solder fountain touching the PTH region.
- Show withdrawal upward and, if used, a modest lateral component.
- Keep any solder-neck cue short; do not draw a long bridge or icicle.
- The retained joint should be conceptual, not an IPC acceptance example.
- Keep motion arrows away from labels.
- Use approximately **5-7% outer margin**.
