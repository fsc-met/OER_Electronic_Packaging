## Instructional Purpose

Help first-time Engineering Technology readers interpret the worked four-thermocouple example from Section 4.15.8 and recognize that one oven run can produce different measured temperatures at different board locations.

The figure should answer:

> **How can several thermocouple locations on the same assembly satisfy or miss different parts of the supplied reflow process window?**

The figure should make the decision logic visual: three channels meet the example peak/TAL requirements while the high-thermal-mass TC3 channel has both a low peak and insufficient time above liquidus.

---

## Central Teaching Message

> **A recipe is verified by the measured product response at all important locations, not by one passing trace or by oven setpoints alone.**

Supporting messages:

- multiple thermocouples on the same board can produce different traces;
- the same liquidus reference applies to all four channels for this example;
- peak temperature and TAL are separate requirements;
- TC3 can fail because it does not get hot enough and does not stay above liquidus long enough even though other locations pass;
- the hot location must still be watched because it is already near the upper peak limit;
- the example limits are supplied for this specific teaching example and are not universal SAC305 acceptance limits.

## Overall Layout

Use a **primary temperature-versus-time plot** with a compact secondary metric-summary region.

### Main plot

Show four measured product-temperature traces corresponding to the worked example:

- `TC1 - small edge passive`
- `TC2 - center QFN`
- `TC3 - power package / heavy copper`
- `TC4 - sensitive connector`

The four curves should have the same overall reflow shape but different heating response, peak temperature, time above liquidus, and cooling behavior.

Required references on the plot:

- vertical axis: `Product temperature (°C)`;
- horizontal axis: `Time (s)`;
- dashed horizontal `Liquidus reference, 217 °C`;
- a restrained horizontal band or bracket indicating the **illustrative peak-temperature window 235-245 °C**;
- curve legend identifying TC1-TC4;
- a callout highlighting TC3 as the cold/high-mass location that misses both peak and TAL.

Do not use oven-zone setpoint bars as the primary reference. This is measured product-temperature evidence.

### Compact metric summary

Use a small side or bottom summary that reports only the two metrics this figure needs to emphasize:

| Channel | Peak | TAL | Result |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| TC1 | 244 °C | 78 s | Pass |
| TC2 | 241 °C | 66 s | Pass |
| TC3 | 232 °C | 39 s | **Low peak + short TAL** |
| TC4 | 243 °C | 72 s | Pass |

A compact summary is preferred to four separate TAL brackets on the graph, which would become crowded and ambiguous at 900 px.

Include one concise qualification note:

`Illustrative supplied limits for this example - not universal SAC305 limits`

Do not reproduce the full worked-example table from the section; the figure should remain visually focused.

## Required Objects and Labels

### Axes / reference window

Required labels:

- `Product temperature (°C)`
- `Time (s)`
- `Liquidus reference = 217 °C`
- `Illustrative peak window = 235-245 °C`
- `Illustrative TAL requirement = 45-90 s above 217 °C`

The peak window may be shown as a narrow translucent horizontal band or a bracketed range. Do not shade the entire graph as a generic `pass zone`, because TAL is a duration requirement, not simply an x-y rectangle shared by all traces.

### Four measured traces

Use four visually distinct line styles/colors with direct legend labels:

- `TC1 - small edge passive`
- `TC2 - center QFN`
- `TC3 - power package / heavy copper`
- `TC4 - sensitive connector`

Use line style, marker shape, or direct labeling in addition to color so the figure remains interpretable in grayscale.

### TC3 failure emphasis

Use one concise annotation near TC3 peak or beside the summary:

- `TC3: peak 232 °C - below 235 °C limit`
- `TC3: TAL 39 s - below 45 s minimum`

The annotation should not imply that TC3 is universally the coldest possible point on every board; it is the cold location in **this worked example**.

### Hot-location awareness

Use one small note near TC1 or in the summary:

- `TC1 peak 244 °C - passes, but close to upper peak limit`

This supports the later recipe-adjustment discussion without showing the second-run results in this figure.

## Required Technical Relationships

1. All four traces must represent **measured product temperatures**, not oven setpoint curves.
2. Every trace must rise from near ambient, pass through preheat/equalization behavior, peak once, and then cool.
3. The liquidus reference must be at **217 °C** for this illustrative SAC305 example.
4. TC1, TC2, and TC4 must cross above 217 °C long enough to satisfy the supplied TAL requirement and must peak within 235-245 °C.
5. TC3 must peak at approximately **232 °C**, below the 235 °C minimum, and must remain above liquidus for only approximately **39 s**, below the 45 s minimum.
6. The graph must show TC3 crossing above liquidus briefly; do not accidentally draw a curve that never crosses 217 °C if the summary says TAL = 39 s.
7. TC1 must peak near **244 °C**, visibly close to the 245 °C upper limit without exceeding it.
8. Approximate curve geometry should support the relative TAL durations in the summary; do not use identical crossing times for all four traces.
9. The **12 °C first-run peak spread** (244 °C - 232 °C) may be shown as an optional bracket or note if it remains uncluttered.
10. TAL must be described as the time each individual trace remains above liquidus; do not depict 45-90 s as one shared absolute time interval on the x-axis.
11. The figure must clearly qualify the supplied example limits as process/job-specific instructional limits, not universal SAC305 acceptance criteria.
12. Do not add second-run results from Section 4.15.9; this figure belongs beside the **first-run** worked example.
13. Do not diagnose solder defects or infer print/placement/material causes from the traces; Section 4.16 owns that integrated troubleshooting.
14. Do not portray a single passing trace as proof that the recipe is acceptable.
15. Keep the first-run graph internally consistent with the section table: the figure may simplify ramp and cooling values visually, but it must not add a pass/fail claim for those metrics that contradicts the worked data.

## Technical-Verification Basis

Use the following sources for technical verification, not as artwork templates:

### Primary instructional baseline

- MET406 Chapter 4 - Assembly Technologies, **Reflow Soldering 27-28**:
  - record multiple temperature-versus-time curves;
  - analyze ramp, soak, peak, TAL, and cooling;
  - compare measured data with target limits;
  - identify deviations such as low peak or short TAL;
  - re-profile until all important thermocouple locations fit the acceptable process window.

### Current Section 4.15 worked example

Use the first-run values exactly as written in the current QA candidate:

- **TC1:** peak 244 °C, TAL 78 s;
- **TC2:** peak 241 °C, TAL 66 s;
- **TC3:** peak 232 °C, TAL 39 s;
- **TC4:** peak 243 °C, TAL 72 s.

Illustrative supplied limits:

- peak: **235-245 °C**;
- TAL: **45-90 s above 217 °C**.

The section also gives a 12 °C measured peak spread from 244 °C to 232 °C.

### Approved reference hierarchy

- Clyde F. Coombs Jr., *Printed Circuits Handbook*, 6th ed., Chapter 47:
  - product profiling determines heating/cooling ramps, peak process temperature/duration, and board/component heating characteristics;
  - product profile boards use thermocouples at critical locations to ensure difficult locations receive enough heat while lighter components are not overheated;
  - profile verification requires actual product measurement rather than generic oven settings.
- Glenn R. Blackwell, *The Electronic Packaging Handbook*: multiple thermocouple locations and product-profile interpretation for actual assemblies.

### Current solder-paste verification source

- MacDermid Alpha, ALPHA OM-340 SAC305 technical data sheet (2024): representative process scale includes TAL approximately 45-90 s and peak approximately 235-245 °C, with explicit instruction to optimize the profile for the actual board application.

These values are used only to verify that the worked example is realistic. The figure must explicitly preserve the section's statement that the shown limits are **illustrative supplied limits for this example**, not universal SAC305 acceptance criteria.
