## Instructional Purpose

Give first-time Engineering Technology readers a system-level map of where post-reflow defect causes can originate. The figure should answer:

> **Why does a defect found after reflow not automatically mean that reflow caused it?**

It should connect the earlier Chapter 4 sequence—design/material condition, solder-paste printing, component placement, and reflow—to the final solder-joint observation.

---

## Central Teaching Message

> **A post-reflow defect is downstream evidence. Its root cause may originate in design, material condition, paste printing, placement, reflow, or handling. Troubleshooting must work backward from evidence.**

Supporting messages:

- `Print -> Place -> Reflow` is the main physical process sequence.
- Design/land-pattern choices can influence paste deposition, wetting balance, spacing, and local thermal behavior.
- Component/PCB condition and handling/material status can influence more than one assembly stage.
- A visible final defect is an observation to investigate, not a one-step diagnosis.

## Overall Layout

Use a **left-to-right process map** with two visual layers.

**Main process spine:** `Solder-paste printing -> Component placement -> Reflow -> Post-reflow defect observed`. Each stage should use one simple representative engineering object.

**Upstream influence layer:** separate source-category boxes for `Design / land pattern`, `Component / PCB condition`, and `Handling / material status`, connected to relevant stages/final result with restrained influence arrows. Include a small note such as `Work backward from evidence`. Do not turn the figure into a defect gallery or a detailed troubleshooting flowchart.

## Required Technical Relationships

1. Preserve the physical order **print -> place -> reflow -> post-reflow observation**.
2. Design/land pattern is an upstream influence, not a machine/process stage.
3. Component/PCB condition and handling/material status may influence multiple stages.
4. The final defect observation is downstream of all three SMT stages.
5. Do **not** draw one exclusive `Reflow -> Defect` causal arrow.
6. Visually distinguish **physical process-flow arrows** from **possible influence arrows** by line style, grouping, or a small legend.
7. Do not imply every source category contributes equally to every defect.
8. Do not imply the visible defect proves root cause.
9. Do not show THT/wave/selective soldering, detailed inspection/test strategy, standards limits, or defect-specific acceptance criteria.
10. Do not duplicate Figure 4.16.2's defect-gallery role or Figure 4.16.3's hidden-BGA role.

## Technical-Verification Basis

**Primary MET406 baseline:** printing-defect pages, placement-defect pages, reflow-defect pages, and inspection-awareness pages show that defects can originate from print, placement, material condition, geometry, and thermal history.

**Current Section 4.16:** explicitly groups possible sources as design/land pattern, paste printing, placement, component/PCB condition, reflow process, and handling/material status, and uses `Observation -> evidence -> likely mechanism -> check the upstream process -> next action`.

**Approved references:** Coombs supports cross-process contributors including paste geometry, solderability, profile behavior, surface tension, and warpage; Blackwell describes bridges, opens, solder-volume problems, misalignment/tombstoning, and inspection feedback as manufacturing-process evidence. Use these sources for verification only, never as artwork templates.
