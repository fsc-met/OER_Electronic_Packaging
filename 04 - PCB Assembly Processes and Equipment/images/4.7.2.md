## Figure Title for Authoring / Reference Only

Do **not** place this overall title or the figure number inside the artwork. The final public caption belongs outside the artwork.

---

## Instructional Purpose

Help a beginning Engineering Technology reader connect the physical dimensions of a rectangular stencil aperture to the **theoretical volume available for a paste deposit**.

The figure should make three dimensions concrete:

- aperture length, $L$;
- aperture width, $W$;
- stencil thickness, $T$.

It should then communicate that, for a simple rectangular through-aperture,

$$
V_{\text{aperture}} = LWT
$$

and that increasing aperture area or stencil thickness increases the **theoretical aperture volume**.

The figure must also protect the important distinction that theoretical aperture volume is **not automatically equal to the actual deposited paste volume**.

---

## Central Teaching Message

> **Aperture length and width set the opening area, stencil thickness sets the aperture depth, and together they establish the theoretical paste volume available at that location. Actual printed volume can differ because filling and release are not perfect.**

## Overall Layout

Use a clean **two-part instructional composition**.

### Left / primary geometry panel

Show the same rectangular aperture in two coordinated views:

- **top view** identifying $L$ and $W$;
- **cross-section** identifying stencil thickness $T$.

Include the relationship:

$$
V_{\text{aperture}} = LWT
$$

The equation should be visually secondary to the geometry rather than an oversized title.

### Right / comparison panel

Show two simple comparison pairs:

1. **same thickness, larger opening -> larger theoretical volume**;
2. **same opening, thicker stencil -> larger theoretical volume**.

A concise bottom note should distinguish:

- **theoretical aperture volume**; from
- **actual deposited volume**, which also depends on aperture filling and paste release.

The comparison should use idealized geometry, not exact machine settings.

## Required Objects and Labels

### Geometry panel

Required:

- stencil foil shown in top view and cross-section;
- one rectangular aperture;
- labels `L`, `W`, and `T`;
- label `Aperture`;
- optional simple dimension arrows;
- equation $V_{\text{aperture}} = LWT$.

### Comparison cases

Required visual relationships:

- smaller versus larger opening at the same $T$;
- thinner versus thicker stencil at the same $L$ and $W$.

Recommended concise labels:

- `Larger opening area`
- `More theoretical volume`
- `Thicker stencil`
- `More theoretical volume`

### Theoretical-versus-actual note

Required wording or equivalent:

> **Theoretical aperture volume is not guaranteed deposited volume. Actual volume also depends on filling and release.**

Do not add SPI metrics or transfer-efficiency numbers.

---

## Required Technical Relationships

The figure must correctly communicate that:

1. for a simple rectangular aperture, theoretical volume is $LWT$;
2. increasing $L$ or $W$ while holding the other variables constant increases theoretical volume;
3. increasing stencil thickness $T$ while holding the opening area constant increases theoretical volume;
4. the relationship describes **available aperture volume**, not guaranteed deposited paste volume;
5. actual deposit can be reduced or distorted by incomplete filling/release and process variation;
6. the figure must not imply that thicker stencils are always better;
7. the figure must not imply that every pad/aperture uses a rectangular shape.

---

## Spatial / Geometry Requirements

- The top-view rectangle must clearly support $L$ and $W$ dimension labels.
- The cross-section must clearly support $T$ as stencil thickness, not deposit height.
- Dimension arrows must terminate at the correct geometric limits.
- Comparison cases should use matched scales where possible.
- In the thicker-stencil comparison, keep opening dimensions visibly the same.
- In the larger-opening comparison, keep stencil thickness visibly the same.
- Maintain at least **6–8% outer margin**.
- Keep equation and note boxes away from dimension arrows.
- Do not let leaders cross geometry unnecessarily.
