## Instructional Purpose

Give a first-time Engineering Technology reader a concrete picture of what an **ESD protected area (EPA)** is trying to accomplish.

The figure should connect the physical items students may see at an electronics workstation with the core ESD-control idea:

- the operator is controlled through an approved personnel-grounding method;
- the ESD-protective work surface is bonded/grounded;
- both connect through the site's controlled grounding/bonding scheme;
- the exposed ESDS item stays on the controlled work surface;
- approved protective packaging is used for transport/storage;
- ionization is used only where charge cannot be removed effectively by grounding.

This is a **physical workstation/equipotential-control figure**, not a detailed standards-compliance diagram.

---

## Central Teaching Message

> **An EPA reduces damaging potential differences by controlling personnel, conductive/dissipative work surfaces, tools, packaging, and difficult-to-ground items through grounding/bonding and ionization where appropriate.**

The visual should make clear that a wrist strap is only **one part** of the ESD-control system.

## Overall Layout

Recommended composition:

### Main workstation scene

Use one simplified EPA scene occupying most of the canvas.

From left to right:

1. **operator** standing or seated at the bench;
2. **wrist strap + approved grounding cord** connected toward a **common point ground**;
3. **ESD-protective work surface** on the bench connected to the same controlled grounding/bonding point;
4. **exposed ESDS component or small PCBA** on the controlled work surface;
5. **approved ESD protective packaging/container** near the work area;
6. a **process-essential insulating item** or isolated object at one side;
7. an **ionizer** directed toward that difficult-to-ground item.

A subtle dashed/outlined boundary may identify the **EPA**.

### Supporting message

Use one concise callout near the common grounding paths:

**Ground / bond controlled conductive and dissipative items to reduce potential differences**

Keep text short.

## Required Technical Relationships

The final artwork must satisfy all of the following:

1. The operator is connected through an approved personnel-grounding path when handling the exposed ESDS item.
2. The ESD-protective work surface is connected to the same controlled grounding/bonding scheme.
3. The ESDS item is located on the controlled ESD work surface, not on an ordinary table.
4. The figure visually supports **equipotential control**, not simply "everything goes to earth independently."
5. The ionizer is associated with an item that cannot be controlled effectively by grounding alone.
6. The ionizer must **not** be shown as replacing the operator wrist strap or grounding the work surface.
7. Approved packaging is shown as part of the protection system for material transport/storage.
8. Ordinary plastic bags, foam, cups, or other uncontrolled insulators should not appear near the exposed ESDS item.
9. The figure must not imply that any single device—wrist strap, mat, ionizer, or bag—alone creates a complete ESD-control program.
10. No resistance values, test limits, or compliance thresholds are shown.

---

## Spatial / Geometry Requirements

- Keep the operator close enough to the workstation that the wrist-strap cord route is obvious.
- The wrist-strap cord should terminate at the controlled grounding/common-point system, not at the ESDS item.
- The work-surface grounding lead should be a separate path that joins the same common grounding/bonding point.
- Avoid visually tying the ionizer into the electrical ground line.
- Place the ionizer above or to the side of the process-essential insulating/isolated item with airflow/ion-flow arrows directed toward it.
- Place approved ESD packaging nearby but outside the immediate soldering/work area.
- If an EPA boundary is shown, it should enclose the controlled workstation and not suggest that ESD risk disappears instantly outside a line.
- Keep all leaders short and unambiguous.
- Maintain at least **6–8% outer canvas margin**.
- No label should cross the operator, cords, or grounding paths.

## Generation Guidance

### Preferred Production Method

Use a **controlled vector SVG** from the start.

This figure depends on:

- exact grounding/bonding relationships;
- unambiguous cord routing;
- clear ionizer role;
- readable labels at 900 px.

Free-form raster generation may be used for an initial concept if needed, but the publication candidate should be controlled vector artwork.

### Recommended Build Order

1. Create the 1800 × 1000 white canvas.
2. Establish the bench and optional EPA boundary.
3. Draw the operator and wrist strap.
4. Draw the ESD-protective work surface.
5. Add the common point ground.
6. Route the wrist-strap and work-surface connections separately to the common point.
7. Place the exposed ESDS item on the controlled surface.
8. Add approved ESD packaging nearby.
9. Add one process-essential insulating/isolated item.
10. Place an ionizer directed toward that item only.
11. Add concise labels/leaders.
12. Verify that the ionizer cannot be mistaken for the primary grounding method.
13. Render at native, 1600, 1200, and 900 px.
14. Freeze the first strong baseline and make targeted corrections only.

### Revision Behavior

Once the first technically sound layout is selected:

- freeze the workstation composition;
- preserve major object positions and palette;
- change only defective labels, leaders, or control-path geometry;
- save every meaningful revision with a different filename;
- do not regenerate the entire design for a local text correction.
