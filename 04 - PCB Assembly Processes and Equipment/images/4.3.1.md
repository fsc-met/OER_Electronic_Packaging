# Figure 4.3.1 Suggested Content - Recognizing SMT THT Mixed and Double-Sided Assembly

## Status

**Suggested-content specification — Rev2 QA candidate**

This file defines the intended instructional content and figure-generation constraints for Figure 4.3.1 in Section 4.3, **Recognizing SMT, THT, and Mixed-Technology Assembly**.

The figure is not an illustration of a specific commercial product or machine. It is an original teaching diagram for board-level assembly classification.

---

## Figure Title for Authoring / Reference Only

**Recognizing SMT, THT, Mixed SMT/THT, and Double-Sided SMT Assembly**

Do **not** place this overall title or the figure number inside the artwork. The public figure number and caption belong in the Markdown/HTML insertion block.

---

## Instructional Purpose

Help a first-time Engineering Technology reader classify a **complete PCB assembly** by looking at how components are physically mounted.

The figure should let the reader distinguish four board-level cases at a glance:

1. **SMT-only**
2. **THT-only**
3. **Mixed SMT + THT**
4. **Double-sided SMT**

The figure should especially correct one common beginner misconception:

> A board with components on both sides is **not automatically** a mixed SMT/THT assembly.

The figure should complement, not duplicate, the earlier Chapter 1 joint-level SMT/THT illustration.

---

## Central Teaching Message

> **Assembly type is determined by how the components are mounted, not simply by how many sides of the PCB contain components.**

Supporting physical relationships:

- SMT component terminals connect to **surface lands/pads**.
- THT component leads or pins pass through **plated through-holes**.
- Mixed technology uses **both SMT and soldered THT components** on the same PCBA.
- Double-sided SMT can have SMT components on both sides while remaining an entirely SMT assembly.

## Overall Layout

Use four equally weighted comparison panels arranged as:

| Left | Right |
|---|---|
| **SMT-only** | **THT-only** |
| **Mixed SMT + THT** | **Double-sided SMT** |

Each panel should contain:

1. a concise panel label at the top;
2. one simplified PCB assembly drawing;
3. enough visible board-edge/side geometry to show whether terminals remain on the surface or pass through the board;
4. one or two short callouts only where needed to make the classification unmistakable.

Use the **same board size, viewing direction, and general graphic language** in all four panels so students compare mounting type rather than artistic differences.

A slight isometric or shallow oblique presentation is preferred because the figure must show both board-level classification and the mounting relationship. A **top-view-only** solution is not sufficient for the THT-only or double-sided-SMT panels because it can hide the through-board leads or the bottom-side components.

A controlled side/profile strip or inset may be used inside each panel if needed. If used, keep the profile convention consistent across all four panels.

## Required Objects and Labels

### Panel A - SMT-only

Required objects:

- one simplified PCB;
- several SMT chip components;
- at least one SMT IC package;
- visible surface lands/pads;
- no component lead passing through the PCB.

Required panel label:

**SMT-only**

Recommended concise callout:

**Terminations on surface lands**

The drawing should make it visually obvious that the component terminations stop at the board surface.

### Panel B - THT-only

Required objects:

- one simplified PCB;
- at least one through-hole connector/header or similar pin component;
- at least one leaded component;
- component leads/pins visibly passing through the PCB;
- plated-hole geometry represented schematically;
- soldered lead/pin ends visible on the opposite side if the view permits.

Required panel label:

**THT-only**

Recommended concise callout:

**Leads/pins through plated holes**

Do not imply that every PCB hole is a THT component hole.

### Panel C - Mixed SMT + THT

Required objects:

- one simplified PCB;
- several SMT parts on surface lands;
- at least one THT connector/component with leads passing through plated holes;
- visual coexistence of both mounting types on the same PCBA.

Required panel label:

**Mixed SMT + THT**

Recommended concise callout:

**Both mounting types on one PCBA**

The mixed panel should look like one coherent board, not two unrelated half-boards.

### Panel D - Double-sided SMT

Required objects:

- one simplified PCB;
- SMT components on the **top side**;
- SMT components on the **bottom side**;
- no THT component leads passing through the board;
- enough side/profile visibility to make the bottom-side SMT components unmistakable.

Required panel label:

**Double-sided SMT**

Recommended concise callout:

**SMT on top + bottom**

This panel is the most important pedagogical contrast with the mixed SMT + THT panel.

## Required Technical Relationships

The final artwork must satisfy all of the following:

1. **SMT-only**
   - component terminals end on surface lands;
   - no through-board component leads are shown.

2. **THT-only**
   - component leads/pins visibly pass through the PCB;
   - the holes used by those component leads are represented as plated through-holes;
   - the drawing must not look like the component merely sits above an unrelated drilled hole.

3. **Mixed SMT + THT**
   - both SMT surface connections and THT lead-through-hole connections must be visible on the same board.

4. **Double-sided SMT**
   - SMT parts are present on both PCB faces;
   - no THT component lead passes through the PCB;
   - the bottom-side components must be physically attached to the bottom surface rather than floating below the board.

5. The figure must visually support the section statement:

   > **Board side count and mounting technology are different concepts.**

6. Do not represent press-fit as one of the four main panels. Press-fit is only awareness-level content in Section 4.3 and would overload this figure's classification role.

## Spatial / Geometry Requirements

### Shared geometry

- All four PCB drawings should use approximately the same board dimensions.
- Keep the same board thickness representation throughout.
- Keep the same viewing angle or side-profile convention throughout.
- Maintain generous white space between panels.
- Use visible panel boundaries only if they improve comparison; do not create heavy poster-style boxes.

### SMT geometry

- SMT packages should sit directly over surface pads.
- Package terminals should meet the lands.
- Do not draw SMT leads penetrating the PCB.
- Do not show exaggerated solder fillets that become the visual focus.

### THT geometry

- At least two leads/pins should visibly traverse the PCB thickness.
- The component body should remain on one side of the board.
- The pin/lead should align with the plated hole.
- If an underside solder fillet is shown, keep it schematic and modest.

### Double-sided SMT geometry

- The board edge/profile must be sufficiently visible to distinguish top and bottom surfaces.
- Bottom-side parts should be aligned to bottom-side surface pads.
- No through-hole lead should accidentally appear in this panel.

### Panel balance

- Keep the visual complexity similar among panels.
- Use approximately **3–5 representative components** per panel rather than dense populated-board artwork.
- Do not make the mixed panel visually dominant simply because it contains both types.
- Do not use dramatically different component counts that make one technology appear inherently more complex or more important.
- Reserve at least **7–8% of canvas width** as combined outer/column whitespace and enough internal clearance that panel labels cannot collide with board graphics at 900 px.

- consistent line weights;
- legible sans-serif text;
- no photorealistic rendering;

Suggested visual coding:

- copper/solderable pads: muted gold/tan;
- SMT component bodies: neutral gray/charcoal with modest contrast;
- THT connector/component bodies: a second restrained neutral accent;
- leads/pins: dark gray or metallic neutral;
- solder: light neutral metallic tone if shown.

Do **not** rely on color alone for classification. The mounting geometry must carry the meaning.

Panel labels should be prominent enough to scan at 900 px but should not dominate the artwork.

## Technical-Verification Basis

The figure content is grounded in the current Section 4.3 candidate and the approved project hierarchy.

### Primary instructional basis

The Chapter 4 MET406 instructional material establishes:

- SMT and THT as the primary introductory board-level mounting approaches;
- SMT as surface-mounted component assembly;
- THT as component leads inserted through PCB holes;
- mixed SMT/THT assembly as a practical product architecture.

The public figure should preserve that introductory teaching intent without carrying forward broad or unstable numerical/generalized claims.

### Approved reference verification

- Tummala, *Fundamentals of Microsystems Packaging*, Chapter 17:
  - distinguishes surface-mount and through-hole board assembly;
  - supports the physical surface-versus-through-hole distinction.
- Coombs, *Printed Circuits Handbook*:
  - supports SMT, THT, mixed, and specialized board-level assembly approaches.
- Current Section 4.3 technical QA:
  - treats compliant-pin press-fit separately as a solderless board-level attachment method;
  - avoids claiming universal THT mechanical or current superiority;
  - distinguishes double-sided SMT from mixed SMT/THT.

### Existing-book consistency

- Chapter 1 already established the individual-joint geometry.
- Section 4.2 established the factory route.
- Section 4.3 now needs a complete-board classification visual only.

## Generation Guidance

### Preferred production method

A **controlled vector SVG** is preferred over free-form raster image generation because this figure depends on:

- precise board/component geometry;
- consistent comparison panels;
- technically correct lead-through-hole relationships;
- readable short labels;
- exact top/bottom placement in the double-sided SMT panel.

AI image generation may be used only as an early concept stage if desired. If it introduces geometry or text defects, proceed to a controlled vector rebuild rather than repeatedly changing unrelated portions of the design.

### Recommended build order

1. Create the 1800 × 1100 white canvas.
2. Establish the 2 × 2 comparison grid and safe margins.
3. Draw one reusable generic PCB base.
4. Duplicate the base so all four panels use consistent proportions.
5. Add SMT-only components/pads.
6. Add THT-only components, plated holes, and through-board pins.
7. Add both technologies to the mixed panel.
8. Add top- and bottom-side SMT components to the double-sided panel.
9. Add concise panel labels/callouts.
10. Check that no through-hole pin accidentally appears in SMT-only or double-sided SMT.
11. Check reduced-size readability.
12. Export PNG only after the vector geometry is technically correct.

### Revision behavior

Once a strong baseline exists:

- freeze the overall 2 × 2 composition;
- make only targeted corrections;
- do not redesign correct panels while correcting one panel;
- preserve each meaningful revision under a different filename;
- do not call a candidate canonical/final until explicit owner acceptance.
