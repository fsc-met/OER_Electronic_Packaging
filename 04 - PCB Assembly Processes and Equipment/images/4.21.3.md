# Figure 4.21.3 Suggested Content - Common Secondary Assembly Operations on a Completed PCBA

## Status

**Suggested-content specification - Rev1 QA-reviewed candidate**

This file defines the instructional content and generation constraints for Figure 4.21.3 in Section 4.21, **Depanelization and Secondary Assembly**.

The figure has one instructional role: **show the range of common post-solder secondary-assembly items that may be added to a completed PCBA and reinforce their shared requirements for access, alignment, support, and non-interference**.

Do **not** call this specification or generated artwork canonical/final before explicit project-owner acceptance.

---

## Figure Title for Authoring / Reference Only

**Common Secondary Assembly Operations on a Completed PCBA**

Do **not** place this overall title or figure number inside the artwork.

---

## Instructional Purpose

Provide one board-level visual reference for the recognition-level secondary-assembly operations introduced in Section 4.21:

- shield/can;
- bracket + standoff + screw;
- heat sink + TIM layer;
- daughterboard/module with mechanical support;
- identification label;
- localized staking/adhesive.

The figure should help a reader answer:

> **What kinds of physical items may still be installed after the main soldering flow, and what common installation concerns connect them?**

---

## Central Teaching Message

> **Secondary hardware can complete shielding, mechanical support, thermal integration, identification, and module installation, but every added item must preserve required access and avoid damaging or loading the completed PCBA.**

The figure should communicate **recognition and installation awareness**, not detailed mechanical or thermal design.

## Overall Layout

Preferred composition: **one simplified completed PCBA in the center**, surrounded by six concise callout zones.

Recommended callout distribution:

- upper left: **Shield / cover**;
- upper center/right: **Daughterboard / module**;
- right: **Bracket + standoff + screw**;
- lower right: **Heat sink + TIM**;
- lower left: **Identification label**;
- left/center: **Localized staking / adhesive**.

Keep the main PCBA large enough that each hardware item appears physically installed on or adjacent to the board rather than floating as disconnected icons.

Use one small cross-sectional or exploded inset only where needed for the **heat sink + TIM** relationship. Do not turn the whole figure into six separate detailed mini-figures.

The board should contain a few generic SMT/THT components and connectors to reinforce that primary soldering is already complete.

## Required Objects and Labels

Required board-level objects:

- simplified completed PCBA;
- generic SMT components;
- at least one connector or THT component;
- mounting holes or support points where appropriate;
- accessible test point/connector area to support the non-interference message.

Required secondary items and labels:

1. `Shield / cover`
2. `Bracket`
3. `Standoff`
4. `Screw / fastener`
5. `Heat sink`
6. `TIM layer`
7. `Daughterboard / module`
8. `Mechanical support`
9. `Identification label`
10. `Localized staking / adhesive`

Required shared-principle callout or footer:

- `Preserve access, alignment, and support`
- optional secondary cue: `Avoid interference with nearby components / test points`

If a shield is shown as a later-installed secondary item, depict it as a **clip/fastened/removable cover**, not as a reflow-soldered shield can.

## Required Technical Relationships

1. The completed PCBA must visibly pre-exist before the secondary hardware is added.
2. A shield/cover used here must be clearly a later mechanical installation, not an SMT/reflow-soldered shield.
3. Bracket, standoff, and screw must create a physically plausible support stack; do not show a screw floating without a support/load path.
4. A daughterboard/module must mate with a connector or defined interface and include mechanical support where appropriate; do not show it hanging from electrical pins alone.
5. A heat sink must make thermal contact with a device/module through a visible **TIM layer** or interface region.
6. The TIM must be shown between mating surfaces, not floating beside the heat sink.
7. An identification label must be placed on a clear board/product region and must not cover a connector, vent, test point, or fastener.
8. Localized staking/adhesive must touch/support a component, lead, or wire at an intended local region and must not engulf the whole board.
9. Callout leaders should connect directly to the installed item, not to generic board areas.
10. The figure should make clear that installation order/access matters; at least one test/connector/fastener area should remain visibly accessible.
11. No torque, TIM thickness, adhesive amount, cure value, or fastener specification should be embedded.
12. Do not depict press-fit insertion; Figure 4.21.2 owns that concept.
13. Do not depict detailed cleaning, coating, underfill, or potting; those belong in Section 4.22.

---

## Spatial / Geometry Requirements

- Center the PCBA and reserve safe whitespace around it for callouts.
- Keep all leaders outside the densest component regions whenever possible.
- Leader lines must not cross each other. If crossing cannot be avoided, expand the canvas or reposition callouts.
- Make the shield/cover, daughterboard, bracket/fastener, and heat-sink/TIM relationships physically plausible in scale and orientation.
- Keep the heat-sink/TIM inset close to the associated device so the relationship is obvious.
- Place the label on a flat, unobstructed area.
- Place staking/adhesive locally around a component/lead/wire, not under unrelated components.
- Keep the daughterboard support point distinct from the electrical connector.
- Maintain approximately **5-7% outer margin** and comfortable callout padding.
- Do not crowd six labels into a narrow footer; distribute them around the board.

## Scope Boundaries

This figure **should show**:

- common post-solder hardware/secondary operations;
- access, alignment, support, and interference awareness;
- basic heat-sink/TIM relationship;
- localized staking/adhesive;
- board-level physical integration.

It should **not show**:

- detailed press-fit process;
- depanelization geometry;
- fastener torque values;
- structural calculations;
- heat-sink sizing, thermal resistance, or TIM selection calculations;
- vibration/shock design;
- detailed daughterboard connector mechanics;
- label traceability systems;
- conformal coating, underfill, potting, encapsulation, or cleaning;
- workmanship acceptance criteria;
- reliability qualification.

Detailed thermal design belongs in Chapter 7. Detailed mechanical design belongs in Chapter 8. Cleaning/protection belongs in Section 4.22.

## Generation Guidance

Preferred method: **controlled SVG-first vector construction**.

Recommended build order:

1. create one clean generic completed PCBA as the visual anchor;
2. place six secondary items in physically plausible installed locations;
3. add the heat-sink/TIM interface detail;
4. add a mechanically supported daughterboard/module;
5. add bracket/standoff/screw stack;
6. add shield/cover, label, and local staking/adhesive;
7. add concise leaders and one shared planning message;
8. inspect leader routing and callout spacing;
9. render-check at native, 1600, 1200, and 900 px widths;
10. inspect grayscale readability.

If callouts become crowded, first expand the canvas or move callout regions. Do not solve crowding mainly by shrinking text.

Once a promising baseline exists, freeze the board and visual language; revise only defective labels, leaders, or relationships.

Preserve each meaningful revision under a unique filename.
