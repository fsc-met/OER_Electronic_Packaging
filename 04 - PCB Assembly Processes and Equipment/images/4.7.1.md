## Instructional Purpose

Give a first-time Engineering Technology reader a clear visual model of the physical sequence used to place solder paste on PCB pads through a stencil.

The figure should show, in order:

1. a supported PCB aligned beneath the stencil;
2. a bead of solder paste on top of the stencil;
3. squeegee motion that drives paste into an aperture;
4. a filled aperture located over the intended PCB pad;
5. stencil separation that leaves a defined paste deposit on the pad.

The figure should establish that the stencil is a **patterned metering tool**. The squeegee moves a common paste bead; it does not individually paint each pad.

---

## Central Teaching Message

> **Stencil printing works by aligning a supported PCB under a patterned stencil, filling the stencil apertures with paste, and then separating the stencil so controlled deposits remain on the intended PCB pads.**

Supporting ideas:

- the PCB must be stable and supported during printing;
- the stencil aperture determines where paste can pass through;
- the aperture is positioned over the intended pad;
- stencil separation is part of the printing mechanism, not an afterthought;
- the physical process can be understood before detailed printer settings are introduced.

## Overall Layout

Use a **four-stage left-to-right process sequence**.

Recommended panels:

| Stage | Main visual role |
|---|---|
| **1. Align and support** | PCB supported under the stencil; aperture aligned over pad |
| **2. Squeegee moves paste** | paste bead and squeegee motion across stencil surface |
| **3. Aperture fills** | paste fills the stencil opening over the pad |
| **4. Stencil separates** | stencil lifts/separates and a defined paste deposit remains on the pad |

Use a common cross-sectional viewpoint and consistent scale so the student reads the figure as one physical sequence.

Use simple sequence arrows between panels. Sequence arrows must not pass through labels or technical objects.

## Required Objects and Labels

### Shared objects across the sequence

- **PCB** or PCB cross-section;
- **PCB pad** / land directly below the intended aperture;
- **board support** beneath the PCB;
- **stencil** foil above the PCB;
- **aperture** through the stencil;
- **solder paste**;
- **squeegee** in the active printing stages.

### Required labels

Use concise labels such as:

- `PCB`
- `PCB pad`
- `Board support`
- `Stencil`
- `Aperture`
- `Solder paste`
- `Squeegee motion`
- `Stencil separation`
- `Paste deposit`

The stage headings should be short and subordinate to the drawing. They are not an embedded overall figure title.

### Stage-specific details

**Stage 1 — Align and support**

- PCB sits on visible support;
- stencil aperture is vertically aligned with the intended pad;
- stencil is at or approaching the normal printing position against the board.

**Stage 2 — Squeegee moves paste**

- show a paste bead on top of the stencil;
- squeegee contacts the paste/stencil surface;
- horizontal arrow shows squeegee travel;
- paste is driven toward/into the aperture.

**Stage 3 — Aperture fills**

- aperture is visibly filled with paste;
- pad remains directly under the aperture;
- do not show reflow or a solder joint.

**Stage 4 — Stencil separates**

- stencil moves upward away from the PCB;
- paste deposit remains on the PCB pad;
- the deposit is visibly separate from the stencil.

## Required Technical Relationships

The final artwork must satisfy all of the following:

1. The aperture is located over the intended PCB pad.
2. The PCB is visibly supported beneath the printing area.
3. The squeegee moves paste **across the stencil surface** and drives paste into the aperture.
4. Paste fills the aperture before separation.
5. During stencil separation, the stencil moves away while paste remains on the PCB pad.
6. The figure must not imply that paste is painted directly onto each pad by the squeegee.
7. The figure must not imply that the deposited paste is already a solder joint.
8. The figure must not imply that detailed pressure, speed, blade angle, separation speed, or printer recipe values are universal or are being taught here.

## Spatial / Geometry Requirements

- Keep all PCB cross-sections on a common baseline.
