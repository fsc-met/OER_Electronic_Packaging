## Instructional Purpose

Give a first-time Engineering Technology reader a direct visual comparison of three different solder-surface behaviors:

1. **wetting**;
2. **non-wetting**;
3. **dewetting**.

The figure should let the reader connect the words used in Section 4.4 to the actual **shape and surface-contact behavior of molten solder**.

Its main purpose is physical recognition, not workmanship acceptance.

---

## Central Teaching Message

> **Wetting means molten solder spreads over and remains in intimate contact with a solderable surface; non-wetting means effective wetting does not develop, while dewetting means solder initially wets and then retracts.**

Supporting ideas:

- smaller contact angle generally corresponds to better spreading/wetting;
- a rounded bead with a large contact angle is a useful non-wetting visual;
- dewetting must look different from simple non-wetting because wetting occurred first and then receded;
- no universal contact-angle pass/fail values belong in this figure.

## Overall Layout

Use three equally weighted side-by-side panels:

| Left | Center | Right |
|---|---|---|
| **Wetting** | **Non-wetting** | **Dewetting** |

Each panel should use the **same generic solderable metal surface**, same scale, same viewing direction, and same baseline so readers compare the solder behavior rather than different drawings.

Recommended structure in each panel:

1. concise panel label at top;
2. generic horizontal solderable surface / pad;
3. molten-solder shape above the surface;
4. a small contact-angle arc only where useful;
5. one concise physical-behavior callout.

Do not add a long legend or prose paragraph inside the artwork.

## Required Objects and Labels

### Shared Objects

All three panels require:

- one generic solderable metal surface or PCB land shown as a simple horizontal cross-section;
- one molten-solder region;
- a visible solder/surface contact line;
- consistent surface thickness and scale.

The metal should be labeled generically as:

**Solderable surface**

Do not label the surface as bare copper in all panels, because actual PCB/component solderable finishes vary.

### Panel A - Wetting

Required label:

**Wetting**

Required geometry:

- solder spreads across the surface;
- broad contact area;
- smooth transition at the edge;
- visibly smaller contact angle than the non-wetting case.

Recommended concise callout:

**Spreads over surface**

Optional notation:

`θ`

If the angle symbol is used, it must be placed at the solder/surface/gas contact line and must not be given a universal numerical threshold.

### Panel B - Non-Wetting

Required label:

**Non-wetting**

Required geometry:

- solder remains strongly rounded/bead-like;
- smaller contact area than the wetting panel;
- visibly larger contact angle;
- underlying surface remains visibly exposed around the bead.

Recommended concise callout:

**Does not spread effectively**

Do not draw the solder as completely detached from the surface. Non-wetting still involves physical contact.

### Panel C - Dewetting

Required label:

**Dewetting**

Required geometry:

- show evidence that solder had occupied a broader region and then receded;
- remaining solder should form irregular mounds/ridges/islands rather than one clean symmetric bead;
- represent receded areas as irregularly covered regions, optionally with a very thin residual solder film, not as a simple untouched clean pad identical to the non-wetting panel.

Recommended concise callout:

**Initially wets, then retracts**

A subtle dashed outline showing the earlier wetted extent is allowed if it improves understanding. If used, label it:

**Earlier wetted extent**

Do not imply a time sequence with many steps; one clear "before extent / after retraction" visual is sufficient.

## Required Technical Relationships

The final artwork must satisfy all of the following:

1. The **wetting** case has the broadest effective surface contact and the smallest contact angle of the three main profiles.
2. The **non-wetting** case is bead-like and does not establish effective spreading.
3. The **dewetting** case visually communicates **initial wetting followed by recession**.
4. Dewetting must not be rendered as merely another high-contact-angle bead; it must have a distinct irregular/receded appearance.
5. Contact angle is used only as **qualitative wetting intuition**, not as an acceptance criterion.
6. The figure must not imply that shiny appearance, color, or smoothness alone determines solder-joint quality.
7. The figure must not imply that all real solder joints have identical idealized fillet geometry.

---

## Spatial / Geometry Requirements

- Keep all three metal surfaces on the same horizontal baseline.
- Use approximately the same pad/surface width in every panel.
- Keep the solder volume visually comparable enough that shape differences are meaningful; exact equal volume is not required.
- The wetting profile should be shallow enough to communicate spreading but must not look like a paper-thin film.
- The non-wetting profile should be rounded but physically contact the surface.
- The dewetting profile should use irregular asymmetric retreat, not a second symmetric bead.
- Angle arcs, if used, must touch the correct geometric vertex.
- Do not place the angle arc inside the solder body where its meaning becomes ambiguous.
- Maintain at least **6–8% outer canvas margin**.
- Maintain enough panel spacing that the three states remain visually independent at 900 px.

## Technical-Verification Basis

### Section and Outline Basis

The current Section 4.4 candidate requires:

- wetting versus non-wetting/dewetting awareness;
- surface-tension/contact-angle intuition;
- good/poor wetting sketches;
- no advanced metallurgy or acceptance tables.

The approved Chapter 4 outline gives Figure 4.4.1 the distinct role of showing **how molten solder behaves on a surface**.

### Primary MET406 Instructional Basis

MET406 Chapter 4, `Soldering Fundamentals 4–6`, teaches:

- surface tension as a tendency of liquid solder to minimize surface area;
- cohesion versus surface interaction/adhesion as wetting intuition;
- wetting as spreading with lower contact angle;
- non-wetting as bead-like behavior with larger contact angle.

The lecture's numerical contact-angle bands are intentionally **not** carried into this figure as universal acceptance criteria.

### Approved Reference-Book Verification

- Coombs, *Printed Circuits Handbook*, Chapter 42, distinguishes non-wetting from dewetting and notes that wetting-balance behavior can distinguish the two conditions.
- Coombs also supports contact angle as a useful wetting/cleanliness indicator while not making one simple universal angle sufficient for every assembly acceptance decision.
- Tummala board/interconnection discussions support wetting as a necessary part of soldered metallurgical joining.

### Current-Source / Standards Role

Section 4.4 technical QA already identifies current IPC/J-STD solderability standards as method/requirements references. This figure intentionally avoids proprietary or standards-controlled acceptance tables and uses only the stable physical distinction.

No new time-sensitive numerical claim is introduced in the figure.

## Generation Guidance

### Preferred Production Method

Use a **controlled vector SVG** from the start.

This figure depends on:

- exact surface/contact geometry;
- readable contact-angle arcs;
- consistent panel scaling;
- clear dewetting recession.

Free-form raster generation is more likely to introduce inconsistent geometry and should not be the final source.

### Recommended Build Order

1. Create the 1800 × 850 white canvas.
2. Establish three equal panel zones and safe margins.
3. Draw one generic solderable-surface cross-section.
4. Duplicate it so all panels use identical baseline geometry.
5. Draw the wetting solder profile.
6. Draw the non-wetting bead profile.
7. Draw the irregular dewetting profile and optional prior-extent outline.
8. Add concise panel labels.
9. Add contact-angle arcs only where they remain readable.
10. Check that dewetting cannot be mistaken for non-wetting.
11. Render at 1600, 1200, and 900 px.
12. Make targeted corrections only.

### Revision Behavior

Once the first technically sound layout is selected:

- freeze the three-panel structure;
- preserve relative panel sizes and visual language;
- fix only specific technical/text/layout defects;
- save every meaningful revision with a different filename;
- do not redesign the other panels while correcting one panel.
