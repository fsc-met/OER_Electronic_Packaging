## Instructional Purpose

Give a first-time Engineering Technology reader a visual scale for common solder-powder types used in SMT paste.

The figure should help students understand that:

1. powder type refers to a **particle-size range/distribution**;
2. Type 3, Type 4, and Type 5 differ mainly in representative particle size;
3. powder type does **not** define the solder alloy;
4. finer powder can support smaller deposits/features but increases total particle surface area and oxidation sensitivity.

Its main role is **size-scale recognition and tradeoff awareness**.

---

## Central Teaching Message

> **Powder type identifies a representative particle-size range, not the alloy composition. As powder gets finer from Type 3 to Type 5, smaller deposits become more feasible, but total surface area and oxidation sensitivity increase.**

Supporting ideas:

- the figure should show relative scale, not a single exact diameter per type;
- Type 3 should appear coarser than Type 4, and Type 4 coarser than Type 5;
- smaller particles mean more particles for a similar amount of material.

## Overall Layout

Use three comparison panels in one horizontal row or one shared panel divided into three equal labeled zones:

| Left | Center | Right |
|---|---|---|
| **Type 3** | **Type 4** | **Type 5** |

Each zone should show a **representative particle-size range** using a small cluster of approximately spherical particles drawn at an appropriate relative scale.

Recommended structure:

- top: clear labels **Type 3**, **Type 4**, **Type 5**;
- center: representative particles/clusters sized relative to each other;
- below or beside each type: range label;
- bottom or side: one concise tradeoff note, such as:
  - **finer powder -> supports smaller deposits, but total surface area and oxidation sensitivity increase**.

A small note should also state:

**Powder type = particle-size range, not solder alloy.**

## Required Objects and Labels

### Required Type Labels and Ranges

- **Type 3** - **25–45 µm**
- **Type 4** - **20–38 µm**
- **Type 5** - **15–25 µm**

Use the micrometer symbol `µm` correctly.

### Required Visual Objects

- a particle cluster for each type;
- a common scale cue or consistent side-by-side scaling;
- concise tradeoff note.

Optional but recommended:

- a simple horizontal reference scale bar in micrometers;
- a small arrow or statement indicating **smaller particles -> greater total surface area for a given mass**.

Do not show each type as a single perfect particle only. A small cluster better communicates that the paste contains a distribution of particles.

---

## Required Technical Relationships

The final artwork must satisfy all of the following:

1. Type 3 particles are shown larger on average than Type 4 particles.
2. Type 4 particles are shown larger on average than Type 5 particles.
3. The figure makes clear that these are **ranges/distributions**, not exact one-size categories.
4. The figure explicitly states or strongly implies that powder type is **not** the alloy identity.
5. The figure includes the practical tradeoff that finer powder can support smaller deposits/features but increases total exposed surface area and oxidation sensitivity.
6. The figure must not imply that finer powder is always universally better.

---

## Spatial / Geometry Requirements

- Keep the three comparison zones visually balanced.
- Use a consistent baseline or common reference scale.
- Show enough particle-size contrast that the three types remain distinguishable at 900 px.
- Keep labels close to their corresponding particle groups.
- Maintain at least **6–8% outer canvas margin**.
- If a scale bar is used, keep it simple and uncluttered.
