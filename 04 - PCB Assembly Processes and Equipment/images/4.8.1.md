## Instructional Purpose

Give a first-time Engineering Technology reader a simple machine-level view of how an automatic solder-paste printer brings together the physical printing functions introduced in Section 4.7.

The reader should be able to recognize the main machine systems and connect each one to its basic physical role:

- transport the PCB;
- locate and restrain it;
- support it from below;
- register the PCB to the stencil;
- hold the stencil securely;
- move the active squeegee;
- maintain a usable solder-paste bead;
- separate stencil and PCB after printing;
- clean the stencil underside when required.

The figure should answer:

> **What major hardware systems are working together inside an automatic solder-paste printer?**

---

## Central Teaching Message

> **An automatic solder-paste printer is a coordinated system: board transport, support/clamping, vision alignment, stencil holding, squeegee motion, paste management, separation, and understencil cleaning all support the same basic stencil-printing process.**

Supporting ideas:

- no single machine subsystem controls print quality by itself;
- board handling/support/alignment are as important as the squeegee system;
- the architecture should remain generic rather than manufacturer-specific.

## Overall Layout

Use one simplified **cutaway / conceptual machine architecture** rather than a commercial machine photo.

Recommended organization:

- central printing zone containing the PCB, stencil, paste bead, and squeegee;
- left and right conveyor/rail segments showing board entry/exit;
- support/clamping directly beneath/around the PCB;
- stencil frame surrounding the stencil plane;
- vision camera positioned so it clearly represents board/stencil registration;
- separation motion indicated at the stencil/PCB interface;
- understencil cleaning assembly shown below or beside the stencil as a distinct subsystem.

A restrained isometric or 2D sectional schematic is acceptable.

The visual hierarchy should make the **printing zone** the center of attention while keeping all machine functions recognizable.

## Required Technical Relationships

The final artwork must satisfy all of the following:

1. The PCB is transported into a defined printing position.
2. The PCB is shown supported from below.
3. Clamping restrains/locates the PCB and is visually distinct from the support function.
4. The stencil is held above/in contact with the PCB printing surface in the print zone.
5. The squeegee acts on the stencil surface, not directly on the PCB pads.
6. The solder-paste bead is located in front of the active squeegee.
7. The vision/alignment system is associated with registering PCB and stencil features before printing.
8. Separation is shown as relative motion between PCB and stencil after the print stroke.
9. The understencil cleaning system acts on the stencil underside / aperture interface, not on the top of the PCB.
10. Conveyor/board handling is shown as a transport function, not as the printing actuator.
11. The figure must not imply that every commercial printer has identical mechanical packaging.
12. The figure must not imply that cleaning occurs after every print; it is a machine capability used according to the qualified process.

## Spatial / Geometry Requirements

- Keep the PCB, stencil, and squeegee physically aligned in the central print zone.
- Place board support beneath the PCB.
- Place the stencil frame around the stencil plane without blocking the printing interface.
- Position the paste bead directly ahead of the active squeegee.
- Keep the vision camera offset enough that it does not look like part of the squeegee head.
- Keep the understencil cleaning unit visually separated from the board conveyor.
- Use leader lines that terminate clearly at their intended object.
- Avoid crossed leader lines.
- Preserve **6–8% outer canvas margin**.
- Use comfortable internal spacing between hardware labels.
- Do not force every label into the central print area; use external callout regions when needed.
