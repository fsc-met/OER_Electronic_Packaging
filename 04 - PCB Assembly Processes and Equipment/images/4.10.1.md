## Instructional Purpose

Give readers a clean side-by-side physical comparison of the four supply-format categories introduced in Section 4.10:

1. tape-and-reel;
2. matrix tray;
3. tube / stick;
4. selected bulk / vibratory supply.

The figure should answer:

> **How are SMT components physically organized before a feeder or presentation device brings them to the placement head?**

The figure should also prevent a common beginner confusion: a **reel, tray, tube, or bulk container is a component supply format**, while the feeder or presentation mechanism is the equipment that presents a part at a usable pickup location.

---

## Central Teaching Message

> **The supply format organizes and protects components before pickup; a compatible feeder or presentation mechanism must still bring one correctly oriented part to a predictable pickup location.**

Supporting ideas:

- different packages and production situations can use different supply formats;
- one package family is not permanently tied to one format;
- tape-and-reel is common but not universal;
- bulk handling is suitable only for selected parts that can tolerate the handling and be oriented reliably;
- the figure is about physical recognition, not feeder optimization or machine programming.

## Overall Layout

Use a **four-panel horizontal comparison** with equal visual weight.

Recommended left-to-right order:

1. **Tape-and-reel**
2. **Matrix tray**
3. **Tube / stick**
4. **Bulk / vibratory**

Each panel should show the supply container/form first, with only enough presentation context to show how the parts are organized. A small secondary arrow or note such as `to compatible feeder / presentation device` may be used below the panels, but do not draw a full pick-and-place machine.

Use a common visual scale and style so students compare the physical organization of the parts rather than the artistic treatment.

---

## Required Objects and Labels

### Panel 1 - Tape-and-reel

Required objects:

- reel;
- continuous carrier tape leaving the reel;
- repeated component pockets;
- at least several identical components in pockets;
- visible cover-tape concept over the pocketed region;
- sprocket-hole row if space permits.

Required label:

- **Tape-and-reel**

Optional concise labels:

- `carrier tape`
- `component pockets`
- `cover tape`

Do not turn this panel into the detailed indexing diagram reserved for Figure 4.10.2.

### Panel 2 - Matrix tray

Required objects:

- rigid rectangular tray;
- regular row/column pocket grid;
- several components seated individually in pockets;
- one or more empty pockets may be shown if useful for recognition.

Required label:

- **Matrix tray**

Optional concise label:

- `grid of component pockets`

### Panel 3 - Tube / stick

Required objects:

- elongated tube or stick with a controlled cross-section;
- a row of similarly oriented components inside the channel;
- one end or presentation direction visible.

Required label:

- **Tube / stick**

Optional concise label:

- `components constrained in one row`

The components should not appear loose or free to rotate randomly inside the tube.

### Panel 4 - Bulk / vibratory

Required objects:

- generic bulk hopper/bowl/bin containing loose selected components;
- a simplified orientation path, track, or exit showing that components must be separated and oriented before pickup;
- several loose parts in the storage region and fewer, consistently oriented parts at the exit.

Required label:

- **Bulk / vibratory**

Optional concise label:

- `orient and present one part at a time`

Do not imply that every SMT component is suitable for bulk handling.

## Required Technical Relationships

The final artwork must satisfy all of the following:

1. Tape-and-reel components are visibly stored in repeated carrier-tape pockets, not simply glued to a strip.
2. Matrix-tray components are individually located in a regular pocket grid.
3. Tube/stick components are constrained in a one-dimensional row with consistent orientation.
4. Bulk components begin loose and require an orientation/presentation mechanism before pickup.
5. The four panels represent **supply formats**, not four complete placement machines.
6. The figure must not imply that one component package family can only use one supply format.
7. The figure must not imply that a reel itself performs indexing or pickup; the compatible feeder performs that presentation function.
8. The figure must not imply that trays, tubes, and bulk systems always use the same feeder architecture.
9. Component orientation should be visually controlled in all panels except the intentionally loose upstream portion of the bulk panel.
10. No proprietary feeder geometry, branding, or vendor-specific hardware should be shown.

---

## Spatial / Geometry Requirements

- Keep all four panels aligned on a common baseline.
- Use comparable panel widths so no one format appears to be the universal/default solution simply because it is much larger.
- Maintain at least **6-8% outer canvas margin**.
- Maintain clear internal panel padding and avoid labels touching borders or objects.
- Use concise callout leaders only where they improve recognition.
- Keep component orientation visually consistent within each tray/tube/tape panel.
- In the bulk panel, show a clear transition from random/loose storage to ordered presentation; do not show the ordered exit pointing back into the hopper.
- Avoid crossing arrows and ambiguous leader lines.
- If the four panels become too dense at 900 px, enlarge the authoring canvas before reducing label size.

## Technical-Verification Basis

### Section and outline basis

This figure directly supports Section 4.10.2 and the approved Section 4.10 outline requirements to recognize:

- tape-and-reel;
- trays;
- tubes/sticks;
- bulk awareness;
- feeders/presentation devices as a separate concept.

### Current Section 4.10 basis

The current Rev3 section states that:

- supply format, feeder/presentation device, and nozzle are different things;
- tape-and-reel uses repeated carrier-tape pockets and a cover tape;
- matrix trays use regular pocket grids;
- tube/stick supply constrains a row of components;
- bulk/vibratory systems must orient and present selected parts reliably;
- package family alone does not determine one mandatory supply format.

### Primary instructional basis

MET406 Chapter 4 - Assembly Technologies teaches:

- components supplied through tape, tray, tube, and bulk systems;
- tape feeders as common for passives and small ICs;
- tray feeders for larger/fragile components in representative examples;
- tube/stick feeders using gravity or vibration;
- bulk/vibratory feeders that orient components from a hopper.

### Approved secondary verification basis

The relationships are consistent with the Section 4.10 approved verification basis, including:

- Rao R. Tummala, *Fundamentals of Microsystems Packaging*, Chapter 17;
- Clyde F. Coombs Jr., *Printed Circuits Handbook*, assembly-process discussion;
- current manufacturer/component-packaging information used during Section 4.10 verification.

The reference sources are for technical verification only. Do not copy their figure compositions.
