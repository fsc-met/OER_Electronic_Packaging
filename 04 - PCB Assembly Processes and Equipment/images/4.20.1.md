# Figure 4.20.1 Suggested Content - Representative Assembly Routes for Single-Sided Double-Sided and Mixed-Technology PCBAs

## Status

**Suggested-content specification - Rev1 QA-reviewed candidate**

This file defines the instructional content and generation constraints for Figure 4.20.1 in Section 4.20, **Planning Mixed SMT/THT and Double-Sided Assembly**.

The figure has one instructional role: **compare representative high-level process routes so readers can see how board side, SMT/THT content, and joining method change the manufacturing sequence**.

Do **not** call this specification or generated artwork canonical/final before explicit project-owner acceptance.

## Figure Title for Authoring / Reference Only

**Representative Assembly Routes for Single-Sided Double-Sided and Mixed-Technology PCBAs**

Do **not** place this overall title or figure number inside the artwork.

---

## Instructional Purpose

Give readers one visual map of the major assembly-route patterns introduced in Section 4.20. The figure should help a beginning reader compare route **sequence**, not machine internals.

The figure should allow the reader to answer:

> **How does the major process sequence change when the board is single-sided SMT, double-sided SMT, mixed SMT/THT, or uses pin-in-paste for selected through-hole parts?**

---

## Central Teaching Message

> **The manufacturing route follows the physical board and component constraints. Single-sided SMT can use one print/place/reflow pass; double-sided SMT adds a flip and second SMT/reflow pass; mixed technology adds an appropriate THT joining step; and selected compatible THT parts can sometimes be integrated into reflow by pin-in-paste.**

## Overall Layout

Preferred layout: **five horizontal process lanes stacked vertically**, all read left to right.

Recommended lane order:

1. **Single-sided SMT**
2. **Double-sided SMT**
3. **SMT + THT / wave-compatible underside**
4. **Double-sided SMT + THT / selective solder**
5. **Selected THT by pin-in-paste (PiP)**

Use the same visual grammar in every lane:

- short process boxes;
- simple right-pointing arrows;
- one small generic board-state icon where it helps show side change or THT addition;
- a clear `Flip board` cue only in the double-sided lanes;
- a clear `Insert THT` cue only where THT is added after SMT;
- a short qualification cue for PiP indicating **selected compatible THT**.

Do not draw full printer, placement-machine, oven, wave, or selective-solder equipment. Section 4.20 is using already-taught processes to plan routes.

## Required Technical Relationships

1. Every route must read left to right in the actual manufacturing order shown in Section 4.20.
2. Single-sided SMT must contain only one SMT print/place/reflow sequence.
3. Double-sided SMT must visibly include a board flip and a second SMT/reflow sequence.
4. The figure must not imply that `double-sided` means two copper layers; it means components assembled on both board sides.
5. In the wave route, THT insertion must occur after the SMT reflow sequence shown for that lane.
6. The wave route must be qualified as appropriate only when the underside is compatible with broad wave exposure or appropriate tooling/protection is available.
7. In the selective route, double-sided SMT must be completed before the illustrated THT insertion/selective-solder step.
8. The selective route must visually communicate local THT joining rather than broad underside solder exposure.
9. In the PiP lane, paste must be shown as serving both SMT lands and the required PTH region before the compatible THT part is inserted and the assembly is reflowed.
10. PiP must be shown as an **option for selected compatible THT parts**, not a universal replacement for wave/selective/hand soldering.
11. Do not show a route ranking, `best` route, or universal cost/throughput winner.
12. Do not embed temperature, dwell, conveyor-speed, or other process-setting values. Those belong to Sections 4.14-4.19 and validated product recipes.
13. Do not imply that inspection/test is absent from the routes merely because it is not shown in every lane; Figure 4.20.1 is a high-level assembly-sequence comparison.

## Spatial / Geometry Requirements

- Keep all five lane starting points aligned vertically.
- Use equal or nearly equal lane heights.
- Keep process boxes within each lane at a consistent height.
- Use a minimum visible gap between lanes so arrows cannot be mistaken as cross-lane branches.
- Use one consistent arrow direction: left to right.
- Make the `Flip board` marker visually different from a normal process box, for example a curved rotation arrow around a small board icon plus the text `Flip board`.
- Keep `Insert THT` visually distinct from soldering so insertion is not confused with joint formation.
- For the selective lane, a small local-nozzle symbol may be used at the final box, but it should remain schematic and secondary to the route sequence.
- For the wave lane, a simple broad-wave symbol may be used at the final box, but do not reproduce the detailed hardware from Figure 4.18.1.
- For the PiP lane, use a small PTH/lead icon only if needed to distinguish it from ordinary SMT reflow.
- Maintain approximately **5-7% outer margin**.
- Avoid crossing arrows, nested arrows, and diagonal connectors between lanes.
