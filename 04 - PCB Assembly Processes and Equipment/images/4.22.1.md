# Figure 4.22.1 Suggested Content - PCBA Cleaning Sequence and Hidden Residue Moisture Risk

## Status

**Suggested-content specification - Rev1 QA-reviewed candidate**

This file defines the instructional content and generation constraints for Figure 4.22.1 in Section 4.22, **Cleaning and Protection Processes**.

The figure has one instructional role: **show the physical clean/wash -> rinse -> dry sequence and why hidden low-standoff regions can retain residue or liquid even when the exposed board surface appears dry**.

Do **not** call this specification or generated artwork canonical/final before explicit project-owner acceptance.

---

## Figure Title for Authoring / Reference Only

**PCBA Cleaning Sequence and Hidden Residue Moisture Risk**

Do **not** place this overall title or figure number inside the artwork.

---

## Instructional Purpose

Show a representative wet-cleaning process at the physical-process level:

> **clean / wash -> rinse -> dry**

and connect that sequence to one practical manufacturing risk: narrow gaps under low-standoff parts can retain dissolved residue, rinse liquid, or moisture after the exposed board surface looks dry.

The figure should allow a beginning MET reader to answer:

> **What does each cleaning stage physically accomplish, and why must the process be able to rinse and dry hidden regions rather than only the visible top surface?**

---

## Central Teaching Message

> **Cleaning is complete only when the target contamination has been removed, the cleaning medium has been properly rinsed or displaced as required, and difficult hidden regions are dry enough for the next process.**

Do not imply that visual appearance alone proves cleanliness or dryness.

## Overall Layout

Preferred organization: **three process stages across the upper/main area**, followed by a **wide lower inset comparing hidden-gap risk versus a properly rinsed/dried condition**.

### Stage A - Clean / wash

Show a populated PCBA with representative flux/handling residue on selected board/joint regions.

Use a generic cleaning-medium indication rather than a specific machine architecture. The visual should show that the cleaning medium reaches the contaminated surface and **loosens/dissolves/carries away the target residue**.

### Stage B - Rinse

Show the same board being rinsed so that cleaning chemistry plus dissolved contamination are carried away.

The rinse stage must visibly differ from the cleaning stage. It should not look like merely repeating the same wash.

### Stage C - Dry

Show the board after the rinse with controlled drying arrows removing remaining liquid from exposed and hidden regions.

Do not show one universal dryer design. Use a generic drying cue such as controlled air-flow arrows or a neutral drying enclosure.

### Lower inset - Hidden low-standoff region

Use a simplified cross-section under a **representative low-standoff package**.

Compare:

1. **Incomplete rinse/dry** - top surface appears dry, but residue or liquid remains in the narrow package-to-board gap;
2. **Proper rinse/dry** - the same gap is shown free of retained process liquid/residue to the intended conceptual level.

The inset owns the teaching point that a visually dry top surface does not prove that the hidden gap is ready for the next process.

## Required Technical Relationships

1. The process sequence must read **clean/wash -> rinse -> dry**.
2. The cleaning stage must remove or mobilize the **target residue/contamination**; do not imply that water or solvent exposure alone guarantees cleanliness.
3. The rinse stage must remove the cleaning chemistry together with dissolved or suspended contamination where that wet process requires rinsing.
4. The dry stage must remove retained process liquid from both exposed surfaces and difficult hidden locations.
5. The hidden-gap inset must show a realistic narrow clearance beneath a low-standoff package; do not draw a large open cavity that would be easy to dry.
6. The poor state should show residue/liquid retained **inside the hidden gap**, not only on the visible board top surface.
7. The good state should preserve the same package geometry so the comparison is about process outcome, not different hardware.
8. Do not imply that a visibly dry board automatically passes a formal cleanliness or moisture acceptance test.
9. Do not imply that every no-clean assembly must be washed; the figure represents a product/process that **does require** cleaning.
10. Do not imply that every solvent or vapor process literally uses the same three machine stages; this is a representative wet-cleaning teaching model.
11. Do not embed aqueous/solvent/semi-aqueous taxonomy into the figure; the section table owns that recognition content.
12. Do not show conformal coating, underfill, or potting; Figures 4.22.2 and 4.22.3 own those concepts.

## Spatial / Geometry Requirements

- Keep the same PCBA orientation and basic board geometry across the three process stages so the reader sees one assembly moving through the sequence.
- Use equal or near-equal stage widths and align stage headers cleanly.
- Keep process arrows centered between stages and free of label collisions.
- Residue/contamination should be visible but restrained; do not make the board look catastrophically dirty.
- In the rinse panel, show contaminated liquid leaving the board rather than accumulating around components.
- In the dry panel, drying arrows should point across/through the assembly without passing through text.
- The low-standoff cross-section should use enough vertical magnification to make the narrow gap readable at 900 px while remaining obviously schematic.
- Keep the poor/good hidden-gap states immediately comparable, preferably side by side.
- Use approximately **5-7% outer margin** and comfortable internal padding.
- Keep leader lines short, direct, and non-crossing.

This figure **should show**:

- the representative physical clean/wash -> rinse -> dry sequence;
- removal of target contamination;
- removal of cleaning chemistry/dissolved contamination during rinse;
- hidden retained-liquid/residue risk under a low-standoff part;
- the need to dry the difficult location, not only the exposed surface.

- cleaning-equipment taxonomy;
- aqueous-versus-solvent-versus-semi-aqueous process comparison;

- potting/encapsulation;
