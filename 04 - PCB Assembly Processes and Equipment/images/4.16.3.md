## Instructional Purpose

Teach MET students why a bottom-terminated/area-array package can look normal from above while solder-joint problems exist underneath. The figure should answer:

> **Why can the top of a BGA look normal while a solder-joint problem exists underneath?**

Keep this at awareness level; do not turn the section into a detailed X-ray-inspection lesson.

---

## Central Teaching Message

> **BGA solder joints are hidden under the package. A normal top view does not prove that the internal joints are acceptable.**

Supporting messages:

- hidden adjacent joints can be bridged;
- a joint can contain a void not visible from above;
- head-in-pillow/open behavior can leave the package ball and paste/joint insufficiently coalesced;
- internal/X-ray inspection can provide structural evidence unavailable from ordinary top-side viewing;
- no single inspection method detects every possible defect.

## Overall Layout

Use a **left-to-right comparison** with one external-view panel and three internal-condition panels.

**Left:** generic BGA package on PCB from normal top/oblique view, labeled `Top view - joints hidden`; the package should look externally normal.

**Right:** three aligned schematic internal/cross-section panels labeled `Bridge`, `Void`, and `Head-in-pillow / open`. ## Required Objects and Labels

**Top view:** rectangular generic BGA body on a PCB; underside joints not visible in normal viewing.

**Bridge:** two adjacent solder-ball joints with an unintended continuous solder connection between them.

**Void:** one solder joint containing an enclosed internal cavity; do not depict the void as complete separation by default.

**Head-in-pillow / open:** package-side solder ball above a pad-side reflowed paste/joint with visible incomplete coalescence/separation at the interface.

**Inspection cue:** a simple `X-ray / internal inspection` label or icon only; do not draw a detailed machine.

---

## Required Technical Relationships

1. BGA solder joints must be physically beneath the package body.
2. The top-view panel must look normal and must not reveal the hidden defect directly.
3. Bridge must show an unintended conductive connection between adjacent hidden joints.
4. Void must be a cavity within solder and must not automatically be represented as an electrical open.
5. HiP/open must show incomplete coalescence/separation between the package ball and pad-side solder/paste joint.
6. HiP geometry must be visually distinct from a central void.
7. Internal/X-ray inspection may be shown as structural evidence, but must **not** be presented as a guarantee of detecting every possible defect.
8. Do not show numeric void-percentage limits, IPC class criteria, detailed 2D/3D/CT method comparison, machine operation, algorithms, or electrical-test strategy.
9. Do not duplicate Figure 4.16.1's upstream-process role or Figure 4.16.2's visible-defect gallery.

## Technical-Verification Basis

**Primary MET406 baseline:** the reflow-defect material presents head-in-pillow as a BGA condition in which the package solder ball and pad-side paste fail to coalesce; the inspection material states that hidden joints require X-ray/internal inspection awareness and includes bridges, voids, and hidden opens/HiP among internal conditions.

**Current Section 4.16:** states that a normal-looking BGA top view does not prove hidden joints are acceptable and explicitly lists bridges, missing/insufficient connections, voiding, warpage effects, and HiP. It also states that X-ray provides internal structural evidence but no single inspection method detects every defect.

**Approved references:** Blackwell notes that BGA/flip-chip joints have limited visual access, that ordinary visual inspection cannot inspect them adequately, and that solder voids are generally observed by X-ray or cross-sectioning. Coombs discusses area-array hidden joints and package/board warpage that can create opens or shorts. Use these sources for verification only.
