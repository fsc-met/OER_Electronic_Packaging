## Instructional Purpose

Show the physical sequence that turns solder, a PCB land, and a component termination into a **metallurgically bonded solder joint**.

The figure should help a beginning MET reader understand that:

- flux/surface condition matters before solder can wet;
- the solder must become molten;
- molten solder must wet the joined surfaces;
- a thin interfacial reaction region forms;
- the final solidified joint is more than a hardened blob of solder.

This figure teaches **how the bond forms**. It does not teach acceptance criteria or detailed metallurgy.

---

## Central Teaching Message

> **A soldered connection forms through surface preparation, melting, wetting, interfacial reaction, and controlled solidification.**

A useful sequence is:

> **surface condition -> flux + heating -> molten solder wetting/interfacial reaction -> solidified metallurgical joint**

The visual should reinforce the Section 4.4 model:

> **solderable surfaces + suitable flux + sufficient thermal input + molten solder -> wetting + interfacial reaction -> solidified solder joint**

## Overall Layout

Use four sequential panels with clear left-to-right arrows:

1. **Surface condition**
2. **Flux + heating**
3. **Molten solder wets**
4. **Solidified metallurgical joint**

All panels should use the **same simplified joint geometry**:

- PCB land/pad on the bottom;
- component termination above or slightly offset;
- solder introduced between/around the two surfaces;
- same viewing direction and scale.

A simple side cross-section is preferred.

The process arrows indicate **conceptual sequence**, not a universal machine recipe.

## Required Objects and Labels

### Shared Joint Geometry

Required in all applicable panels:

- PCB substrate or simplified board cross-section;
- PCB land/pad;
- component termination;
- solderable surface/finish at the interfaces;
- generic solder material.

Use generic labels:

- **PCB land**
- **Component termination**
- **Solder**
- **Flux**
- **Thin interfacial region** or **Thin IMC/interface region**

Do not label every surface as bare copper.

### Stage 1 - Surface Condition

Required label:

**1  Surface condition**

Required objects:

- PCB land and component termination separated or positioned for joining;
- thin schematic oxide/tarnish layer or contamination indication on the solderable surfaces.

Recommended short callout:

**Oxide / contamination can block wetting**

Represent the oxide film schematically and subtly. It should not look like a thick structural layer.

### Stage 2 - Flux + Heating

Required label:

**2  Flux + heating**

Required objects:

- flux present on/around the soldering surfaces;
- heat-flow arrows approaching the joint;
- oxide layer reduced/partially removed or shown as being controlled.

Recommended short callout:

**Flux removes/reduces oxides and limits re-oxidation**

Do not show the flux as mechanically scraping the surface.

### Stage 3 - Molten Solder Wets

Required label:

**3  Molten solder wets**

Required objects:

- molten solder contacting both joined surfaces;
- clearly spread solder profile;
- interfacial contact beginning along the wetted surfaces.

Recommended short callout:

**Wetting + interfacial reaction**

The molten solder should look like one connected liquid region, not separate blobs.

### Stage 4 - Solidified Metallurgical Joint

Required label:

**4  Solidified joint**

Required objects:

- bulk solidified solder joining the component termination and PCB land;
- thin interface band at the solder-to-surface boundaries;
- PCB land and component termination still solid.

Required labels:

- **Bulk solder**
- **Thin interfacial region**

Recommended short callout:

**Electrical + mechanical interconnection**

Do not show a thick layered metallurgy stack.

## Required Technical Relationships

1. The solder alloy melts while the PCB land and component termination remain solid.
2. Flux acts **before and during heating** to support clean/reactive surfaces and wetting.
3. Molten solder must make intimate contact with the solderable surfaces.
4. Wetting is shown before the final solidified joint.
5. A thin metallurgical/intermetallic reaction region forms at the solder/surface interface.
6. The interface region is necessary to the metallurgical bond, but the figure must not imply that a thicker IMC is better.
7. The interface thickness must be visually **thin compared with the bulk solder**.
8. The final joint connects the PCB land and component termination through bulk solder and interface regions.
9. Flux should not remain as a thick structural layer inside the final metallic connection.
10. The figure must not imply one universal surface finish, one universal IMC chemistry, or one universal joint shape.

---

## Spatial / Geometry Requirements

- Keep PCB land at the bottom in all four stages.
- Keep component termination orientation consistent.
- Maintain approximately the same joint spacing in stages 1–3.
- In stage 3, molten solder must touch both joined surfaces.
- In stage 4, solidified solder must bridge the land and termination continuously.
- The thin interface band should be approximately **1–3% of the visible solder thickness** in the schematic, enough to see but clearly not bulk material.
- Do not use a thick "IMC slab."
- Oxide film in stage 1 should be even thinner and schematic.
- Heat arrows in stage 2 should terminate at the joint region, not at unrelated board areas.
- Sequence arrows should be centered between panels and not cross labels.
- Maintain at least **6–8% outer canvas margin**.

## Technical-Verification Basis

### Section and Outline Basis

Current Section 4.4 requires:

- soldering as metallurgical joining rather than adhesive bonding;
- flux purpose;
- oxidation/contamination awareness;
- wetting;
- basic intermetallic-layer awareness;
- heat input;
- no advanced metallurgy.

The approved outline explicitly calls for a **simple flux/wetting sequence**.

### Primary MET406 Instructional Basis

MET406 `Soldering Fundamentals 1, 7, 8, and 9` establishes:

- soldering as metallurgical bonding;
- wetting/diffusion/IMC formation;
- joint structure with bulk solder and a thin interface layer;
- flux roles in oxide removal, protection during heating, and wetting.

The lecture's detailed IMC thickness statements are not turned into a figure acceptance value.

### Approved Reference-Book Verification

- Tummala, *Fundamentals of Microsystems Packaging*, supports wetting and interfacial reaction as part of soldered electrical/mechanical interconnection.
- Tummala notes that intermetallic formation occurs in joining and that excessive intermetallic growth can degrade mechanical behavior, supporting the beginner-level rule that a thin interface is necessary but "more is not better."
- Coombs, *Printed Circuits Handbook*, soldering/solderability/flux chapters support the distinction between solderability, flux action, wetting, and interfacial metallurgical bonding.

### Current-Source / Standards Role

The current Section 4.4 QA references IPC/J-STD solderability and flux standards for terminology/requirements roles. This figure does not reproduce proprietary acceptance criteria.

No new time-sensitive numerical claim is introduced.

## Generation Guidance

### Preferred Production Method

Use a **controlled vector SVG**.

This figure requires:

- consistent geometry through multiple process stages;
- precise interface placement;
- a deliberately thin metallurgical layer;
- readable process arrows and labels.

A vector source is more reliable than free-form raster generation.

### Recommended Build Order

1. Create the 2000 × 950 white canvas.
2. Establish four equal process regions and safe margins.
3. Draw one reusable PCB-land/component-termination geometry.
4. Duplicate it across all four stages.
5. Add the oxide/contamination indication to stage 1.
6. Add flux and heat arrows to stage 2.
7. Add molten solder and wetting profile to stage 3.
8. Add thin interface bands and final joint labels to stage 4.
9. Add short stage labels and arrows.
10. Check that the interface is visually thin and not confused with bulk solder.
11. Check that flux is not shown as a permanent thick layer in the final joint.
12. Render at native, 1600, 1200, and 900 px.
13. Make only targeted revisions.

### Revision Behavior

Once a strong baseline exists:

- freeze the four-stage composition;
- preserve object scale and color coding;
- fix only specific defects;
- do not redesign all stages to correct one label or interface;
- preserve every meaningful revision under a unique filename.
