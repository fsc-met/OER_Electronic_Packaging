---

## Instructional Purpose

Show how the soldering tip, pad, component lead, solder wire, and molten solder interact in the correct basic sequence.

The figure should allow a reader to answer:

> **Where should the soldering tip contact the joint, where should the solder wire be fed, and in what order should heat, solder, wire removal, iron removal, and cooling occur?**

---

## Central Teaching Message

> **A **thin tinned film** on the tip supports heat transfer, but the working solder supply is fed to the heated joint. The tip heats both the pad and component lead before solder is allowed to flow. After wetting occurs, remove the solder wire first, remove the iron second, and let the joint cool without movement.**

## Overall Layout

Preferred layout: **four sequential panels** using the same simplified THT joint cross-section.

Recommended steps:

1. **Heat pad + lead**
2. **Feed solder to heated joint**
3. **Remove solder wire, then iron**
4. **Cool undisturbed**

The first two panels should carry the strongest instructional detail.

The THT joint should be shown from a simplified side/cross-sectional viewpoint with:

- PCB laminate;
- plated through-hole / copper pad;
- component lead through the hole;
- soldering tip contacting both the pad and lead on the solder side;
- a thin tinned film on the tip;
- solder wire approaching the heated joint near the tip;
- molten solder wetting around the pad/lead/PTH region.

Do not make the last panel look like a formal accept/reject workmanship plate.

## Required Technical Relationships

1. The tip must contact both the component lead and pad/joint region at the same time.
2. Heat arrows must visibly enter both the lead and copper pad/PTH region.
3. A thin tinned solder film may exist on the tip for thermal contact.
4. The **working solder wire** must be fed to the heated joint near the tip, not shown melting only against the iron tip.
5. The wire should approach the joint from a position distinct from the iron tip so the relationship is unmistakable.
6. Molten solder should wet around the lead/pad/PTH region after the joint is heated. Do not exaggerate the fillet into a formal acceptance geometry.
7. The sequence must show **wire removed before iron**.
8. The final step must show the joint cooling without movement.
9. The figure must not imply that `shiny = good`.
10. The final joint geometry must be conceptual, not a standards workmanship criterion.
11. Do not embed universal tip temperature or dwell-time numbers; Section 4.19 text owns those qualified representative values.
12. Do not show desoldering braid, solder sucker, hot air, component removal, or pad repair; those belong to later rework content.

## Spatial / Geometry Requirements

- Keep the same lead/PTH geometry in all panels.
- Show the solder side consistently; do not flip the board between steps.
- Make the tip contact point clear enough that it visibly touches both lead and pad.
- Keep the thin tinned film much smaller than the later working solder volume so it cannot be mistaken for the solder supply.
- Position solder wire so its end contacts the heated joint region, not the tip-only region.
- Use two distinct heat-flow arrows or a split heat path into pad and lead.
- In the removal panel, show the wire-removal arrow first and the iron-removal arrow second using step labels or numbered cues.
- Keep the cooling panel visually stable; no movement arrows should remain.
- Use approximately **5-7% outer margin**.
- Avoid leaders crossing the solder wire, lead, or tip.
