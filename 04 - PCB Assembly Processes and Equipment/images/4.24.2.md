# Figure 4.24.2 Suggested Content - Line-Side Material Status from Issue to Return-to-Stock

## Status

**Suggested-content specification - Rev1 QA-reviewed candidate**

This file defines the instructional content and generation constraints for Figure 4.24.2 in Section 4.24, **Material Kitting and Line-Side Verification**.

The figure has one instructional role: **show that material identity and required status must remain controlled after initial issue, through replenishment/change events, and when partial material leaves the line**.

Do **not** call this specification or generated artwork canonical/final before explicit project-owner acceptance.

---

## Figure Title for Authoring / Reference Only

**Line-Side Material Status from Issue to Return-to-Stock**

Do **not** place this overall title or figure number inside the artwork.

---

## Instructional Purpose

Show that material verification is not a one-time check performed only at the beginning of a production run.

Students should see that material can change state while it is line-side: it can be issued to the active job, replenished or replaced during production, partially consumed, removed when the job changes or stops, and then either returned through the approved process or placed on hold when the identity/status needed for the next decision is missing.

The figure should emphasize the **minimum line-side control idea** rather than warehouse logistics or enterprise genealogy.

---

## Central Teaching Message

> **Material control continues after initial issue: identity and required status must remain usable through replenishment and job closeout.**

A second message should remain visually clear:

> **If the information needed for the next decision is missing, the material goes to hold—not quietly back to unrestricted stock.**

The figure should not imply that every material is always returnable. Return/reuse is conditional on the applicable approved process and material-specific restrictions.

## Overall Layout

Use a **left-to-right material-status flow** with one explicit decision near the right side.

Recommended main path:

`Verified material issued`
→
`Active PCB assembly job`
→
`Use / controlled replenishment during production`
→
`Partial / unused material leaves line`
→
decision:
`Identity + required status preserved?`

From the decision:

- **YES / return permitted** → `Approved return / controlled stock status`
- **NO / unknown or incomplete status** → `HOLD for verification / disposition`

A small note near the successful return branch should state:

- `Return/reuse rules depend on material and approved procedure`

The diagram should show that replenishment itself can introduce another verification event. A subtle loop or check marker may connect `controlled replenishment` back to the idea of verified identity/status, but it must not become a complicated warehouse or genealogy flowchart.

## Required Objects and Labels

### 1. Verified material issued

Required label:

- `Verified material issued`

Recommended representative objects:

- one component reel or tray;
- one generic material-status tag/record;
- optional bare-PCB or solder-paste icon only if it can be included without implying that all materials follow identical return rules.

Recommended small cue:

- `correct identity + usable status`

Do not crowd this stage with all Section 4.24 checklist fields.

### 2. Active PCB assembly job

Required label:

- `Active PCB assembly job`

Recommended visual:

- generic assembly-line/placement-board symbol;
- no detailed printer, feeder, oven, or AOI anatomy.

This stage represents the line-side production context, not a complete factory flow.

### 3. Use / controlled replenishment during production

Required main label:

- `Use / controlled replenishment`

Required secondary cues:

- `new reel / tray`
- `feeder or tray change`
- `paste container as applicable`

Use no more than two or three compact icons/cues.

A small verification symbol should indicate that replenishment is another point where identity/status must be checked before the new material joins the active job.

Do not imply every listed replenishment event occurs in every run.

### 4. Partial / unused material leaves line

Required label:

- `Partial / unused material leaves line`

Recommended representative object:

- a partially used reel/tray or generic material container moving away from the line;
- a status tag/record still associated with it.

Do not imply that partially used solder paste, bare PCBs, and component reels are always handled identically.

### 5. Status-preservation decision

Required decision label:

- `Identity + required status preserved?`

Required representative status fields shown nearby or inside a compact side panel:

- `material identity`
- `remaining quantity`
- `lot / date code`
- `moisture / exposure status as applicable`
- `expiration / use status as applicable`
- `product / process restrictions as applicable`

These are examples of information needed for the next controlled decision. Do not turn them into universal required fields for every material.

### 6. Approved return / controlled stock status

Required labels:

- `YES / return permitted`
- `Approved return / controlled stock status`

Optional supporting note:

- `identity and required status remain usable`

Use a restrained green/teal continuation symbol.

Do not label this path simply `Return to stock` without the qualifier that return must be permitted by the applicable procedure/material rules.

### 7. Hold path

Required labels:

- `NO / status incomplete or unknown`
- `HOLD`
- `Verification / disposition required`

Use a restrained amber/red-orange hold box.

Do not expand the hold path into MRB, nonconformance, CAPA, or disposition-system detail; Chapter 5 owns those topics.

## Required Technical Relationships

1. The figure must begin with **verified material**, not uncontrolled material entering the line.
2. Material control must continue after initial issue; it is not complete once the first setup is loaded.
3. Replenishment/change events during production must be shown as new opportunities for wrong or unknown-status material to enter the process.
4. New or replacement material should rejoin the active job only after the applicable identity/status check.
5. Partial or unused material leaving the line must remain associated with the information needed for the next controlled decision.
6. `Identity + required status preserved?` must be a decision about the information/status needed for safe controlled handling, not a universal quality-acceptance test.
7. The successful branch must be conditional: **return permitted** and required identity/status preserved.
8. The figure must not imply that every material can be returned to stock or reused after opening/issue.
9. The hold branch must be used when required identity/status is missing, incomplete, uncertain, or inconsistent.
10. Missing status must not lead directly to unrestricted stock.
11. Lot/date code must appear as traceability/restriction information, not as a universal expiration limit.
12. Moisture/exposure status must be qualified `as applicable`; do not reproduce component MSL floor-life tables or detailed bake rules.
13. Solder-paste expiration/use status must be qualified `as applicable`; do not invent one universal working-life/storage value.
14. Bare-PCB handling must not be represented as if printed boards use the same component MSL/floor-life scheme.
15. The figure must remain at the minimum Chapter 4 line-side control level and not become a Chapter 5 product-genealogy or nonconformance workflow.
16. The successful return branch should mean **controlled return status established**, not proof that the material is acceptable for every future job without another applicable verification.

- Keep branch labels adjacent to their respective arrows.
- If a replenishment recheck marker is shown, use a short local verification loop or small check symbol; do not draw a large feedback loop across the whole figure.

- Keep all text horizontal.
- Use no more than two or three lines in each main process box.
- Place the representative status-fields panel adjacent to the decision without covering the process path.
- Use approximately **5-7% outer margins** and comfortable internal padding.
- Arrows must not pass through text, reels/trays, tags, or decision labels.
- Ensure the `return permitted` qualification remains readable at 900 px.
