# Figure 4.22.3 Suggested Content - Underfill and Potting Encapsulation Protection and Access Tradeoffs

## Status

**Suggested-content specification - Rev1 QA-reviewed candidate**

This file defines the instructional content and generation constraints for Figure 4.22.3 in Section 4.22, **Cleaning and Protection Processes**.

The figure has one instructional role: **compare the physical coverage of localized underfill beneath an area-array package with broader potting/encapsulation around a PCBA, and connect broader material coverage to reduced direct access for service/rework**.

Do **not** call this specification or generated artwork canonical/final before explicit project-owner acceptance.

---

## Figure Title for Authoring / Reference Only

**Underfill and Potting Encapsulation Protection and Access Tradeoffs**

Do **not** place this overall title or figure number inside the artwork.

---

## Instructional Purpose

Help readers distinguish two protection approaches that are easy to confuse:

- **underfill** - a localized polymer in the small gap beneath a selected package after solder interconnections are formed;
- **potting / encapsulation** - a much larger protective polymer volume around part or all of an electronic assembly.

The figure should answer:

> **Where does the material go, what does it physically protect, and what happens to later access as the protected volume increases?**

---

## Central Teaching Message

> **Underfill localizes reinforcement beneath a selected package, while potting/encapsulation surrounds a much larger portion of the assembly. Broader material coverage can provide broader protection, but it generally reduces direct access for inspection, repair, and rework.**

Do not present either method as universally required or universally superior.

## Overall Layout

Preferred organization: **two large side-by-side technical panels** with one restrained comparison band at the bottom.

### Left panel - Underfill beneath an area-array package

Use a simplified cross-section showing:

- PCB;
- area-array package body;
- visible solder balls/interconnections;
- narrow package-to-board gap;
- liquid underfill dispensed at one package edge;
- capillary-flow arrows carrying material through the gap;
- final cured underfill occupying the gap around the interconnections;
- most of the rest of the board remaining exposed and accessible.

The material should remain localized under the selected package.

### Right panel - Potting / encapsulation

Use a simplified side/cutaway view showing:

- populated PCBA;
- housing or mold wall;
- larger-volume resin surrounding a substantial portion of the assembly/components;
- broad protective coverage around the electronics;
- reduced direct access to components once the material is cured.

Do not fill the entire canvas with resin. The board/housing relationship must remain clear.

### Bottom comparison band

Use short labeled statements, not a decorative scale:

- `Underfill: localized reinforcement; most board remains exposed`
- `Potting / encapsulation: broad coverage; direct component access greatly reduced`

Optional centered statement:

- `More coverage can mean less direct access for service/rework`

Use wording such as `generally` or `can` where needed so the figure does not claim that all materials have identical rework behavior.

## Required Technical Relationships

1. The area-array solder interconnections must already exist before the representative capillary underfill is shown flowing beneath the package.
2. Underfill must occupy the **narrow package-to-board gap** and surround/support the interconnection region; it must not appear as a coating over the entire board.
3. Underfill flow arrows should originate from an edge dispense and move laterally into the gap by capillary action in the representative process.
4. The package and PCB geometry should remain fixed between dispense and cured-underfill states if more than one state is shown.
5. The underfill panel should make clear that most of the board remains exposed even though package-level rework can still be material/process dependent.
6. Potting/encapsulation must use a much larger material volume around part or all of the assembly.
7. The potted/encapsulated board must be shown inside or associated with a housing/mold volume so the broad fill relationship is understandable.
8. The potting material must surround electronics rather than appear as a thin conformal film.
9. The comparison must connect broader material coverage with generally reduced direct physical access for inspection, repair, or rework.
10. Do not imply that underfill is automatically required under every BGA/CSP.
11. Do not imply that every potted assembly is impossible to rework; use `greatly reduced`/`often difficult` rather than an absolute statement.
12. Do not depict detailed thermal-path design, stress analysis, or environmental qualification.
13. Do not show conformal coating; Figure 4.22.2 owns thin-film surface protection.
14. Do not turn the figure into a reliability-test or Chapter 8 structural-design diagram.

## Spatial / Geometry Requirements

- Keep the two main panels equal enough in visual weight for direct comparison.
- Use a clear cross-sectional side view for underfill so the gap and solder balls are readable.
- The underfill gap may be vertically exaggerated for teaching, but it must remain visibly much smaller than the package/board dimensions.
- Show enough solder balls that the reader recognizes an area-array package, but do not overcrowd the gap.
- Keep capillary-flow arrows inside the package-to-board gap and away from label text.
- Use a simple housing boundary in the potting panel and leave a small unfilled margin at the top or around boundaries so the reader can see the resin volume clearly.
- Make the potting material visually distinct from the board/housing without using high opacity that hides all components.
- The bottom comparison band should use concise two- or three-line statements with comfortable padding.
- Use approximately **5-7% outer margin** and consistent internal spacing.
- Keep leader lines short and non-crossing.

## Scope Boundaries

This figure **should show**:

- localized capillary-underfill concept beneath an area-array package;
- cured underfill surrounding solder-interconnection region;
- broad-volume potting/encapsulation around a PCBA;
- direct-access/rework tradeoff as material coverage increases.

It should **not show**:

- conformal-coating coverage/masking/cure;
- detailed underfill chemistry;
- no-flow/molded underfill variants as separate panels;
- potting-resin selection;
- thermal conductivity or thermal-path design;
- cure-shrinkage calculations;
- CTE/modulus/stress analysis;
- environmental qualification testing;
- detailed rework/removal procedures;
- formal workmanship acceptance.

Figure 4.22.1 owns cleaning/hidden-liquid risk. Figure 4.22.2 owns thin conformal coating.
