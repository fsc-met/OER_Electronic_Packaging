## Figure Title for Authoring / Reference Only

**Too Little, Appropriate, and Excessive Soldering Heat**

Do **not** place this overall title or the figure number inside the artwork.

---

## Instructional Purpose

Teach the general relationship between **thermal input at the joint** and soldering outcome before students encounter reflow profiles, soldering-iron settings, wave-pot settings, or selective-soldering parameters.

The figure should help readers understand three conditions:

1. too little thermal input;
2. appropriate thermal input;
3. excessive thermal exposure.

The visual must also reinforce:

> **The machine/heat-source temperature is not automatically the same as the actual solder-joint temperature or joint response.**

---

## Central Teaching Message

> **Soldering requires enough time and temperature at the joint for flux action, solder melting, wetting, and interfacial reaction, but excessive thermal exposure can degrade flux and damage materials or drive unwanted reactions.**

The figure is conceptual, not a process recipe.

## Overall Layout

Use three equal side-by-side panels:

| Left | Center | Right |
|---|---|---|
| **Too little thermal input** | **Appropriate thermal input** | **Excessive thermal exposure** |

Each panel should use the **same simplified PCB-land/component-termination geometry** and the same generic heat-source representation.

Each panel contains:

1. panel label;
2. heat-source symbol/zone;
3. arrows representing heat transfer toward the joint;
4. simplified joint state;
5. one short consequence callout.

Across the bottom, include one concise note:

**Joint response depends on time + temperature + heat transfer, not only the machine setpoint.**

This bottom note is allowed because it is the figure's central distinction and is not a duplicate caption.

## Required Objects and Labels

### Shared Objects

All panels require:

- PCB substrate/land;
- component termination;
- solder or solder-paste region represented generically;
- a generic heat source above or adjacent to the joint;
- heat-flow arrows from source toward joint.

Generic heat-source label:

**Heat source**

Generic joint label where needed:

**Joint**

Do not draw a specific oven, iron brand, solder wave, or commercial machine.

### Panel A - Too Little Thermal Input

Required panel label:

**Too little thermal input**

Required joint state:

- solder shown not fully melted or incompletely wetted;
- joint area with limited spreading/contact;
- flux action shown as incomplete/exhausted only if visually necessary.

Recommended concise callout:

**Incomplete melting / poor wetting risk**

Do not imply that every insufficiently heated joint must have one specific visible defect.

### Panel B - Appropriate Thermal Input

Required panel label:

**Appropriate thermal input**

Required joint state:

- solder fully molten during the joining stage;
- clear wetting of both intended surfaces;
- final small solidified-joint inset or solidification arrow is optional.

Recommended concise callout:

**Flux acts -> solder melts -> wets -> joint forms**

If an arrow sequence is used, keep it short and readable.

### Panel C - Excessive Thermal Exposure

Required panel label:

**Excessive thermal exposure**

Required visual:

- same basic joint geometry;
- visibly prolonged/intense heat indication;
- warning symbols/callouts near **flux**, **PCB/component**, and **interface** rather than a dramatic destroyed board.

Recommended concise callouts:

- **Flux degradation / exhaustion risk**
- **Material damage / warpage risk**
- **Excessive interfacial reaction risk**

The visual should communicate **increased risk**, not guaranteed failure.

---

## Required Technical Relationships

1. Heat moves from a source through the assembly to the actual joint.
2. Heat-source temperature alone does not determine the joint condition.
3. Insufficient time/temperature/heat transfer can prevent complete melting or effective wetting.
4. Appropriate thermal input permits flux action, melting, wetting, interfacial reaction, and subsequent solidification.
5. Excessive time and/or temperature can:
   - exhaust/degrade flux;
   - damage heat-sensitive PCB/component materials;
   - increase unwanted dissolution/interfacial reaction;
   - contribute to warpage/thermal damage.
6. The excessive panel must use **risk** language and should not imply that every overheated joint develops the same defect.
7. The figure must not imply that one universal source temperature is correct.
8. The figure must not introduce a reflow time-temperature profile.

---

## Spatial / Geometry Requirements

- Use identical joint geometry across the three panels.
- Heat source should be in the same relative location in every panel.
- Heat arrows should terminate at/near the joint, showing a thermal path.
- Use arrow thickness/number plus labels—not color alone—to distinguish relative thermal input.
- In the insufficient panel, the solder region may remain partly bead-like or partly solid, but avoid an impossible half-solid decorative effect.
- In the appropriate panel, show continuous wetting between both joined surfaces.
- In the excessive panel, do not draw flames or charred components; use restrained engineering warning symbols.
- Maintain **6–8% outer margin** and enough panel spacing for 900 px legibility.
- Keep the bottom note band visually secondary to the three panels.

## Technical-Verification Basis

### Section and Outline Basis

Current Section 4.4 requires:

- heat transfer to the joint;
- why too little/too much heat causes problems;
- practical hand-solder/reflow examples;
- general mechanism before machine-specific process sections.

Figure 4.4.3 has the distinct role of visualizing **joint-level thermal input**, not a machine profile.

### Primary MET406 Instructional Basis

MET406 soldering/reflow instruction establishes that:

- solder and flux require controlled heating;
- insufficient thermal input can lead to incomplete wetting/melting;
- excessive exposure can exhaust flux and increase thermal/material risk;
- interfacial reaction depends on thermal exposure.

The detailed ramp, soak, peak, and cooling values from later reflow slides are intentionally excluded.

### Approved Reference-Book Verification

- Tummala board-assembly/interconnection discussion supports controlling time and temperature to obtain soldered bonding without excessive intermetallic growth or material damage.
- Coombs soldering/flux chapters support the distinction between solderability and process heating control and emphasize that the process must provide suitable thermal conditions for wetting.

### Current-Source / Standards Role

No figure-specific numerical value is introduced, so no time-sensitive setpoint is required.

The current Section 4.4 technical QA already distinguishes alloy melting temperature from machine settings and defers qualified process windows to later sections/manufacturer/product data.

## Generation Guidance

### Preferred Production Method

Use a **controlled vector SVG**.

The figure depends on:

- identical joint geometry across panels;
- carefully controlled heat-flow arrows;
- clear distinction between joint response and source/setpoint;
- short readable risk labels.

### Recommended Build Order

1. Create the 1800 × 900 white canvas.
2. Establish three equal panel zones and bottom note band.
3. Draw one generic joint and heat source.
4. Duplicate the joint/source geometry into all three panels.
5. Add insufficient-thermal-input arrows and incomplete joint state.
6. Add appropriate-thermal-input arrows and wetting/joint state.
7. Add excessive-exposure arrows and restrained risk callouts.
8. Add bottom note about time + temperature + heat transfer.
9. Check that no panel becomes a reflow profile.
10. Check that excessive exposure shows risk, not guaranteed catastrophic failure.
11. Render at native, 1600, 1200, 900 px.
12. Apply targeted corrections only.

### Revision Behavior

After a strong baseline exists:

- freeze the three-panel comparison;
- preserve identical joint geometry;
- correct only specific labels/arrows/technical defects;
- save every meaningful revision with a new filename;
- do not redesign the complete figure for a local correction.
