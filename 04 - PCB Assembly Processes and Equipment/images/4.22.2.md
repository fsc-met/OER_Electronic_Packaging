# Figure 4.22.2 Suggested Content - Conformal Coating Coverage Masking and Cure Sequence

## Status

**Suggested-content specification - Rev1 QA-reviewed candidate**

This file defines the instructional content and generation constraints for Figure 4.22.2 in Section 4.22, **Cleaning and Protection Processes**.

The figure has one instructional role: **show conformal coating as an intentionally applied thin protective barrier that requires prepared surfaces, deliberate keep-outs/masking, controlled application, and the approved cure process**.

Do **not** call this specification or generated artwork canonical/final before explicit project-owner acceptance.

---

## Figure Title for Authoring / Reference Only

**Conformal Coating Coverage Masking and Cure Sequence**

Do **not** place this overall title or figure number inside the artwork.

---

## Instructional Purpose

Show the physical sequence:

> **clean/dry surface -> mask keep-outs -> apply thin conformal coating -> cure -> protected surfaces with required interfaces still accessible**

The figure should let a beginning reader understand that conformal coating is **selective by design**. The goal is not to cover every visible surface indiscriminately.

---

## Central Teaching Message

> **Conformal coating protects selected surfaces only when the PCBA is properly prepared, required interfaces are kept uncoated, the coating is applied as a thin controlled barrier, and the material receives its approved cure.**

The figure must distinguish thin conformal coating from potting/encapsulation.

## Overall Layout

Preferred organization: **four sequential stages** using one consistent simplified PCBA, followed by or integrated with a **final enlarged coverage view**.

### Stage A - Prepared PCBA

Show a **clean and dry** populated board ready for coating.

Do not repeat the cleaning-process details from Figure 4.22.1. A simple label such as `Prepared: clean + dry` is sufficient.

### Stage B - Mask keep-outs

Show selected areas intentionally masked. Use at least three representative keep-out types:

- connector mating contacts;
- test points;
- one contact/thermal-interface or mechanical-contact surface.

Masking can be represented generically by tape/caps/plugs/removable mask shapes. Do not make one masking method appear mandatory.

### Stage C - Controlled coating application

Show a generic controlled spray/nozzle or dispensing path depositing a **thin layer** over allowed board/component surfaces.

Label the applicator as `Representative controlled application`, not as the only method.

### Stage D - Approved cure and final state

Show the coating after cure as a thin, translucent layer following the contours of the board/components.

The final view must clearly show:

- coated/protected surfaces;
- connector contacts still exposed;
- test points still exposed;
- the selected contact/thermal surface still uncoated/accessible.

Use a simple `Approved cure process` cue. Do not depict one universal heat/UV/moisture cure mechanism.

## Required Technical Relationships

1. The PCBA must be shown **prepared/clean/dry before coating**.
2. Masking must occur before application and must preserve interfaces that need electrical, mechanical, thermal, test, or service access.
3. Connector **mating contacts** must remain uncoated in the final state.
4. Test points must remain exposed in the final state.
5. The representative contact/thermal surface must remain uncoated if it is identified as a keep-out.
6. The coating must appear as a **thin contour-following barrier**, not as a thick mass filling cavities around components.
7. The application stage should show coverage of allowed surfaces without implying that one spray/nozzle method is universal.
8. The cure stage must be generic and labeled as an approved material/process step; do not imply every coating is heat-cured or UV-cured.
9. The final state must visually distinguish protected coated surfaces from intentionally uncoated keep-outs.
10. Do not imply that conformal coating is structural reinforcement.
11. Do not imply that coating can compensate for contamination, retained moisture, or poor surface preparation.
12. Do not show underfill or potting; Figure 4.22.3 owns those protection forms.
13. Do not turn the figure into a workmanship acceptance plate; formal coating acceptance belongs in Chapter 5/applicable requirements.

## Spatial / Geometry Requirements

- Keep the same board orientation and component geometry across all stages.
- Align stages with equal visual weight and clear left-to-right process flow.
- Use visible but restrained masking shapes that sit on the intended keep-out areas without obscuring the whole component.
- Keep connector-contact and test-point keep-outs separated enough to remain readable at 900 px.
- The coating film should be shown with a thin edge/halo or semi-transparent contour layer rather than a large colored fill volume.
- Do not use transparency as the only cue; include `Thin conformal coating` labels/leaders.
- The applicator path should not cross text or imply direct coating into connector contacts.
- In the final view, show enough uncoated geometry to make the keep-outs unmistakable.
- Use approximately **5-7% outer margin** and comfortable internal panel padding.
- Keep leaders short, non-crossing, and clearly terminated.
