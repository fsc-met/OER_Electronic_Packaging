# Figure 4.19.1 Suggested Content - Selective Soldering Hardware and Local Process Sequence

## Status

**Suggested-content specification - Rev2 QA-reviewed candidate**

This file defines the instructional content and generation constraints for Figure 4.19.1 in Section 4.19, **Selective and Hand Soldering: When and How They Are Used**.

The figure has one instructional role: **help first-time readers recognize the major selective-soldering functions and follow the local process sequence from targeted fluxing through mini-wave contact and controlled withdrawal**.

Do **not** call this specification or generated artwork canonical/final before explicit project-owner acceptance.

## Instructional Purpose

Show selective soldering as a **localized, programmable soldering process** rather than as a small version of full-board wave soldering.

The figure should allow a reader to answer:

> **What happens to a mixed-technology PCB during selective soldering, and which major machine functions make the localized process possible?**

---

## Central Teaching Message

> **Selective soldering applies flux where needed, preheats the assembly, positions selected THT joints over a pumped mini-wave/nozzle, and uses programmed withdrawal/drainage to leave the local solder joint while nearby board areas remain outside the solder-contact region.**

## Overall Layout

Use one **four-stage left-to-right process sequence** with the same generic PCB repeated or moved through the stages:

1. **Targeted flux**
2. **Preheat**
3. **Mini-wave contact**
4. **Withdraw / drain**

Recommended composition:

- one consistent simplified PCB/fixture geometry across the sequence;
- bottom-side SMT shown near, but not inside, the selective solder-contact region;
- targeted THT joint or connector group clearly identified;
- a compact process-function label above each stage;
- the solder pot/pump/nozzle exposed in the mini-wave-contact stage;
- programmed positioning awareness shown with restrained X-Y-Z/motion arrows rather than a detailed CNC interface.

The figure may show the PCB moving relative to the tool or the tool moving relative to the PCB. Use neutral relative-motion arrows so the drawing does **not** imply one machine architecture is universal.

## Required Technical Relationships

1. Flux is applied before solder contact.
2. Preheat occurs before mini-wave solder contact.
3. Flux deposition should read as **localized/targeted** rather than full-board flooding.
4. Preheat may cover a larger board region than the flux or solder contact. The artwork should therefore make the **locality of solder contact** visually stronger than the locality of preheat.
5. The mini-wave must be created by a solder pot/pump/nozzle relationship rather than a passive solder pool.
6. The mini-wave contacts only the selected THT location or local group.
7. Nearby bottom-side SMT remains outside the local solder-contact region.
8. The board is supported/located repeatably; selective soldering does not eliminate tooling.
9. Programmed relative motion/positioning must be visually evident, but the figure must not require one specific moving-part architecture.
10. Controlled withdrawal/drainage occurs after solder contact.
11. The figure must not imply that each selective process always solders exactly one pin at a time; a selected local group is valid.
12. Nitrogen, if shown, must be explicitly optional/common rather than universal.
13. No temperature, dwell, Z-offset, nozzle-diameter, or acceptance value should be embedded; those belong in the surrounding text.

---

## Spatial / Geometry Requirements

- Keep the same PCB orientation across the four stages to make the process sequence easy to follow.
- The selected THT joint/group should stay in approximately the same board region across stages.
- Use a clearly different geometry for bottom-side SMT so it cannot be mistaken for the THT target.
- Leave visible clearance between the nearby SMT and the mini-wave footprint.
- Place the solder pot below the PCB in the mini-wave panel/region.
- Show the pump and a clear solder-flow connection to the nozzle.
- The mini-wave should rise only to the local joint area and must not span the full board width.
- Withdrawal/drainage arrows should start at the contacted joint/nozzle relationship and end in a clear separation state.
- Keep stage labels aligned and sufficiently separated.
- Use approximately **5-7% outer margin**.
- Avoid long crossing leader lines; prefer nearby labels or short leaders.
- If a fixture is shown, leave a clear opening for nozzle access.
