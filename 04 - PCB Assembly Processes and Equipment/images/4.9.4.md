## Figure Title for Authoring / Reference Only

**Reading an SPI Volume Trend Across Successive Boards**

Do **not** place this overall title or the figure number inside the artwork.

---

## Instructional Purpose

Show a first-time Engineering Technology reader how a gradual change across successive boards can provide useful process evidence **before** the first result crosses an illustrative inspection limit.

The figure should visualize the exact Section 4.9.8 sequence:

| Board | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 | 8 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Average volume | 99% | 99% | 97% | 96% | 95% | 94% | 92% | 88% |

Use the same **illustrative 90–110% reference window** from the section.

The reader should understand that:

- Boards 1–7 remain above the illustrative lower limit;
- the gradual decline from Boards 3–7 is still useful process evidence;
- Board 8 is below the illustrative 90% lower limit;
- the correct response is to verify the data, check a related physical condition, and then measure the next print after any approved correction.

The figure should answer:

> **Why should I look at the trend instead of waiting for the first failed board?**

---

## Central Teaching Message

> **A gradual SPI volume decline can reveal process drift before the first out-of-limit board. The trend guides a related process check, but the next print must be measured to verify whether an approved correction worked.**

Supporting ideas:

- trend evidence is not a root-cause diagnosis;
- the 90–110% window is illustrative, not universal;
- this is basic trend interpretation, not SPC/control-chart instruction.

## Overall Layout

Use a simple **line chart**.

### Axes

Horizontal axis:

- `Board in sequence`
- ticks 1 through 8.

Vertical axis:

- `Average volume (% of defined reference)`
- suitable range approximately **85% to 112%** so both the illustrative 90–110% window and all data are visible.

### Data

Plot the exact values:

- Board 1 = 99%
- Board 2 = 99%
- Board 3 = 97%
- Board 4 = 96%
- Board 5 = 95%
- Board 6 = 94%
- Board 7 = 92%
- Board 8 = 88%

Use visible point markers joined by a restrained line.

### Illustrative reference window

Show:

- lower reference line at **90%**;
- upper reference line at **110%**;
- optionally a very pale shaded band between them.

Label clearly:

`Illustrative reference window: 90–110%`

Do **not** label these as statistical control limits.

### Trend annotation

A concise annotation should point to Boards 3–7:

`Gradual decline provides early process evidence`

Board 8 should be marked:

`Below illustrative lower limit`

### Process-response callout

Use a small callout outside the chart:

`Verify data -> check related condition -> approved correction if needed -> measure next print`

Do not invent a post-correction board value.

## Required Technical Relationships

The final artwork must satisfy all of the following:

1. The plotted values must exactly match the current Section 4.9 sequence:
   - 99, 99, 97, 96, 95, 94, 92, 88%.
2. Boards 1–7 must plot above the 90% illustrative lower limit.
3. Board 8 must plot below 90%.
4. The chart must visually communicate gradual decline before the limit crossing.
5. The 90–110% band must be labeled **illustrative**, not universal.
6. The two reference lines must not be labeled statistical upper/lower control limits.
7. No Cp/Cpk, sigma limits, moving range, or control-chart terminology should appear.
8. The related process check must be shown as a next action, not as an identified root cause.
9. The figure must show the need to remeasure after any approved correction.
10. No invented recovery point should be plotted.
