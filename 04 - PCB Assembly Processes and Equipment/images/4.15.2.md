## Instructional Purpose

Teach first-time Engineering Technology readers how to look at a populated PCB and choose measurement locations that are likely to reveal meaningful thermal differences or protect critical components during reflow verification.

The figure should answer:

> **Where should temperature be sampled so the measured profile represents the thermal extremes and critical locations of this assembly?**

The figure should visually reinforce that the correct locations come from product construction, thermal mass, copper coupling, board position, component sensitivity, and hidden-joint concerns rather than from a universal fixed sensor pattern.

---

## Central Teaching Message

> **Thermocouples should be placed where the thermal questions are: expected high-mass and low-mass locations, spatial extremes, temperature-sensitive parts, and other critical joints. The actual hottest and coldest locations must be established by evidence.**

Supporting messages:

- high thermal mass or heavy copper coupling can make some locations slower to heat;
- small/lightly coupled locations may respond more quickly;
- center and edge locations are useful candidates but are not guaranteed to be coldest/hottest;
- temperature-sensitive components may need direct verification even if they are not thermal extremes;
- hidden or difficult solder joints can require deliberate measurement when they are important to the process window;
- the number of channels should follow the product's thermal questions, not a universal rule.

## Overall Layout

Use a **top/plan view or restrained isometric view of one populated PCB** as the central object.

Around the board, place four to six concise callouts that point to **representative candidate measurement locations**. Recommended callout categories are:

1. **High thermal mass / heavy copper coupling**
2. **Low thermal mass / small component**
3. **Board center candidate**
4. **Board edge candidate**
5. **Temperature-sensitive component**
6. **Hidden or difficult joint when critical**

These categories may overlap physically. For example, the high-mass component may also be near the board center. Do not force each category into a separate artificial location if the composition becomes unrealistic.

A restrained small note should communicate:

`Examples - actual hot/cold locations must be verified`

or equivalent wording.

Do not turn the figure into a profiler-hardware diagram; Figure 4.15.1 owns the measurement hardware.

## Required Objects and Labels

### PCB and representative components

Show one generic populated PCB with enough component variety to make the thermal choices physically meaningful:

- one large/high-mass package or power device;
- a visible heavy copper region or thermal pad/plane connection associated with that high-mass location;
- several small passive components;
- one medium-size IC or QFN-like package near a central region;
- one connector or other thermally substantial component;
- one clearly identified temperature-sensitive component/location;
- one BGA/QFN/bottom-terminated or otherwise hidden-joint example when space allows;
- board edges and corners clearly visible.

The board should look plausible but generic and unbranded.

### Required callout labels

Use concise labels equivalent to:

- `High thermal mass / heavy copper`
- `Low thermal mass / small component`
- `Board center candidate`
- `Board edge candidate`
- `Temperature-sensitive location`
- `Hidden / difficult joint when critical`

Optional smaller explanatory phrases:

- `may heat more slowly`
- `may respond more quickly`
- `verify actual hot/cold behavior`

Do **not** label any one point `Hottest` or `Coldest` as a universal fact.

### Measurement markers

Use small thermocouple/junction markers or channel dots at the selected points.

Possible labels:

- `TC-A`, `TC-B`, `TC-C`, etc.; or
- simple numbered channels.

If channel labels are used, include a restrained note such as:

`Example channels - number depends on product`

Do not imply that every board requires exactly four, five, or six thermocouples.

## Required Technical Relationships

1. Every marked measurement location must correspond to a physically plausible solder joint/component target on the populated PCB.
2. A high-mass/heavy-copper location must visibly have more thermal mass or copper coupling than the small-component location.
3. The low-mass example must be a genuinely small/light component or lightly coupled joint, not simply another large package near the edge.
4. The board-center and board-edge markers must be presented as **candidate spatial samples**, not guaranteed thermal extremes.
5. The temperature-sensitive location must be shown because of a defined exposure concern, not because it is automatically hottest.
6. A hidden/difficult joint may be represented beneath a BGA/QFN-like package, but the figure must not become a detailed drilling/attachment procedure.
7. The number of measurement channels must be presented as product-dependent.
8. The populated board, not a bare PCB, must be the main object.
9. The figure must not imply that thermal mass alone determines temperature; airflow, copper distribution, board thickness, location, and process conditions also matter.
10. Do not show a fixed universal rule such as `edge = hottest` or `center = coldest`.
11. Do not introduce pass/fail limits, TAL brackets, or recipe adjustments; Figure 4.15.3 and later text own evidence interpretation.
12. Do not introduce solder-defect root-cause diagnosis; Section 4.16 owns that integration.
13. Do not use measured-temperature colors, hot/cold halos, or heat-map shading around the candidate locations; this figure teaches **where to measure**, not the measured result.

- Make the PCB the dominant central object and keep it large enough for component types to be recognizable.
- Place callout boxes around the outside of the board with short, noncrossing leaders.

- Use enough whitespace that every callout remains readable at 900 px.

- Maintain approximately **6-8% outer margin**.
- Preserve comfortable internal padding within every callout box.

## Technical-Verification Basis

Use the following sources for technical verification, not as artwork templates:

### Primary instructional baseline

- MET406 Chapter 4 - Assembly Technologies, **Reflow Soldering 27-28**: place thermocouples on critical locations such as BGA/large copper areas, small components, and board-edge locations; re-profile until all important locations fit the required process window.

### Approved reference hierarchy

- Clyde F. Coombs Jr., *Printed Circuits Handbook*, 6th ed., Chapter 47:
  - product profile board should be populated and instrumented at critical locations;
  - thermocouples may be deployed at leading/trailing edges, sides, middle, disparate package types, and unusually massive components;
  - power/ground-connected locations can be thermally challenging on thick/copper-rich boards;
  - number of thermocouples depends on package types, board thickness, layout complexity, and oven characteristics;
  - there is no substitute for actual product/profile-board measurement.
- Glenn R. Blackwell, *The Electronic Packaging Handbook*: use of product thermocouples to capture high-/low-thermal-mass and spatially different responses.

### Current manufacturer/application guidance

- Indium Corporation, *Attaching Thermocouples to a PCB for Reflow Profiling*:
  - recommended locations include high-thermal-mass solder joints, low-thermal-mass solder joints, middle/edge areas, and components/joints of concern;
  - populated boards are preferred for representative profiling.
- KIC Thermal profiling guidance:
  - selected thermocouple sites should represent the product's mass range;
  - at least one high-mass and one low-mass location are useful for evaluating the process window.

These sources establish the measurement-location logic. The artwork must use a fresh generic PCB composition and must not reproduce any reference figure or vendor screenshot.

## Generation Guidance

Preferred method: **controlled vector / SVG-first** or a carefully controlled hybrid concept followed by vector rebuild.

Recommended workflow:

1. draw one generic populated PCB with clear component-size/copper-coupling variation;
2. choose physically plausible representative measurement markers;
3. add callouts for high mass, low mass, center, edge, sensitive, and hidden/critical locations as space permits;
4. verify that no label claims a universal hot/cold result;
5. route leaders cleanly and maintain comfortable callout spacing;
6. render at 900 px and confirm that component-scale differences and marker endpoints remain visible;
7. freeze the strongest visual baseline;
8. make only targeted corrections to marker location, label wording, leader routing, spacing, or component geometry.

Do not use generative heat-map effects. The figure represents **measurement planning**, not measured temperature data.
