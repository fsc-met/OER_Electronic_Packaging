## Instructional Purpose

Show **why moisture control is needed** for moisture/reflow-sensitive nonhermetic surface-mount packages.

The figure should make the physical mechanism visible:

1. moisture from ambient air diffuses into the polymer package during exposure;
2. the package can still look normal;
3. rapid reflow heating raises internal moisture/vapor pressure and thermal stress;
4. internal interfaces may delaminate or the package may crack/popcorn.

This figure teaches mechanism only. It does not teach MSL labels, floor-life decisions, or bake procedures; those belong in Figure 4.5.3 and the surrounding text.

---

## Central Teaching Message

> **A moisture-sensitive package can absorb water vapor without visible change; rapid high-temperature reflow can then create internal vapor pressure and stress that increase the risk of delamination or package cracking.**

The reader should understand why "the part looks fine" is not evidence that moisture status is acceptable.

### Left panel: moisture absorption

Show a simplified nonhermetic molded surface-mount package cross-section:

- die;

- leads/terminals;
- ambient water-vapor symbols outside;
- arrows showing moisture diffusing into the package.

### Right panel: reflow heating

Use the **same package geometry**.

- heat arrows entering the package;
- internal moisture represented as vapor/pressure expansion;

- one restrained crack or package-separation indication;
- optional label `popcorning risk` near the crack/delamination cue.

Short supporting label:

**Rapid heating -> vapor pressure + internal stress**

Use a large process arrow between panels.

## Required Technical Relationships

1. Moisture enters from the ambient environment into a nonhermetic polymer package over time.
2. Moisture is shown within the package before reflow.
3. The package can remain externally normal before heating.
4. Reflow heat is applied rapidly relative to normal storage.
5. Heating creates vapor/internal-pressure and thermal-stress risk within the package.
6. Damage is shown primarily at internal material interfaces and/or as package cracking/delamination.
7. The right panel communicates **risk**, not guaranteed failure.
8. Do not imply that all absorbed moisture literally forms one large steam bubble.
9. Do not imply that moisture alone is the only stress mechanism; the figure may say `vapor pressure + thermal stress`.
10. Do not imply that all surface-mount packages are moisture sensitive to the same degree.

---

## Spatial / Geometry Requirements

- Use the same package outline, die position, and internal support geometry in both panels.
- In the left panel, moisture arrows should enter the package body from the ambient environment from more than one side.
- Use multiple small moisture symbols/arrows rather than one oversized droplet.
- In the right panel, heat arrows should approach the package from outside and point into the body.
- Vapor/pressure arrows should originate internally and point outward toward susceptible interfaces.
- Delamination should appear as a thin opening/separation along an internal interface, not as a huge missing chunk.
- Any crack should be restrained and localized.
- Do not use an explosion cloud, flame, or shattered package.
- Maintain at least **6–8% outer margin**.
- Ensure all internal labels remain readable at 900 px.

## Technical-Verification Basis

### Section and Outline Basis

Section 4.5.5 requires:

- moisture-sensitive package concept;
- ambient moisture absorption;
- rapid reflow heating;
- internal vapor pressure/stress;
- delamination/cracking/popcorning awareness.

The approved outline identifies this figure's distinct role as **the physical damage mechanism**, not the handling decision.

### Primary MET406 Instructional Basis

MET406 Chapter 4 states that:

- absorbed moisture in boards/components can expand rapidly during heating;
- trapped vapor can contribute to blistering, delamination, cracking, or component popcorning;
- prebake may be used as a mitigation before assembly.

The figure narrows this general teaching to the moisture-sensitive component-package mechanism required by the redesigned outline.

### Approved Secondary-Reference Verification

- Tummala, *Fundamentals of Microsystems Packaging*, explains that plastic packages absorb moisture and that high-temperature reflow can promote vapor-pressure-driven delamination/cracking/popcorning.
- Coombs, *Printed Circuits Handbook*, identifies moisture/reflow-sensitive component control and J-STD-020/J-STD-033 as the industry framework.

### Current Standards / Technical Guidance Basis

The Section 4.5 technical QA used current IPC/JEDEC moisture/reflow-sensitive-device guidance:

- J-STD-020 for moisture/reflow sensitivity classification;
- J-STD-033 for handling, packing, shipping, and use.

The artwork intentionally avoids reproducing any proprietary classification or bake table.

At artwork-generation/acceptance time, wording should be checked against the current applicable revision.

## Generation Guidance

### Preferred Production Method

Use a **controlled vector SVG**.

This figure depends on:

- matching geometry between two stages;
- clear moisture-diffusion arrows;
- restrained pressure/delamination cues;
- readable internal labels.

A vector source is strongly preferred for technical control and later editing.

### Recommended Build Order

1. Create the 1800 × 850 white canvas.
2. Establish two equal panel regions.
3. Draw one generic molded-package cross-section.
4. Duplicate the package geometry into the second panel.
5. Add ambient moisture arrows/symbols to the left panel.
6. Add a few internal moisture markers within the package.
7. Add reflow heat arrows to the right panel.
8. Add restrained internal pressure arrows.
9. Add one thin delamination zone and/or local crack risk.
10. Add short panel labels.
11. Verify that the right panel shows risk rather than explosion.
12. Render at native, 1600, 1200, and 900 px.
13. Freeze the first strong baseline and use targeted corrections only.
