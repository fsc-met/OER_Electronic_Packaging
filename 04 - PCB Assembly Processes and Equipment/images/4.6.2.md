## Instructional Purpose

Give a first-time Engineering Technology reader a process-behavior picture of why solder paste rheology matters.

The figure should make one practical sequence visible:

1. during printing, the paste is subjected to shear and must flow;
2. after deposition, the paste must recover enough body to hold a defined shape;
3. after placement, the paste must provide temporary tack that helps hold the component before reflow.

Its main role is to show the **flow -> recovery -> temporary holding** logic behind shear thinning, thixotropic recovery, and tack.

---

## Central Teaching Message

> **Useful solder-paste rheology allows the material to flow under printing shear, then recover enough structure after deposition to keep the printed shape and temporarily hold the placed component before reflow.**

Supporting ideas:

- shear thinning helps the paste move during printing;
- thixotropic recovery helps the deposit stop flowing after printing;
- tack is a temporary pre-reflow holding property;
- the figure should emphasize behavior, not printer parameter optimization.

## Overall Layout

Use three equal or near-equal sequential panels in one horizontal row:

| Left | Center | Right |
|---|---|---|
| **Under printing shear** | **After deposition** | **After placement before reflow** |

Recommended panel roles:

### Panel A - Under printing shear

- show a simplified squeegee moving across paste;
- show the paste being forced/dragged through a simplified stencil opening or shear zone;
- label the high-shear condition and indicate **flows under shear**.

### Panel B - After deposition

- show a printed paste deposit resting on a PCB pad after the shear has been removed;
- indicate **shape retention / recovers after printing**.

### Panel C - After placement before reflow

- show a component terminal or simple chip component contacting the paste deposit(s);
- indicate **temporary tack holds component**.

Use concise state labels rather than long prose inside the figure.

## Required Objects and Labels

### Shared Required Objects

- generic PCB surface or pad(s);
- solder paste deposit(s);
- simple panel labels;
- arrows showing direction or sequence where useful.

### Panel A - Under printing shear

Required objects/labels:

- simplified **squeegee**;
- optional simplified **stencil** or opening representation;
- paste bead or paste mass;
- movement arrow;
- label such as **Flow under shear** or **Lower apparent viscosity during shear**.

Do not include numerical squeegee pressure, speed, or aperture dimensions.

### Panel B - After deposition

Required objects/labels:

- one or more discrete paste deposits on pad(s);
- clear definition of printed shape;
- concise label such as **Recovers and holds shape**.

Optional concise wording:

- **thixotropic recovery**;
- **reduced slump**.

### Panel C - After placement before reflow

Required objects/labels:

- simple chip-component body or a single termination contacting deposit(s);
- deposits slightly deformed under placement;
- concise label such as **Tack temporarily holds component**.

Optional subordinate label:

- **before reflow**.

## Required Technical Relationships

The final artwork must satisfy all of the following:

1. The left panel clearly shows a **high-shear printing condition**.
2. The center panel clearly shows that after printing the deposit **retains a defined shape**, rather than continuing to flow freely.
3. The right panel clearly shows that tack is a **temporary pre-reflow** holding property.
4. The sequence must communicate behavior change with process state.
5. The figure must not imply that the component is permanently bonded before reflow.
6. The figure must not imply that tack is the same thing as the final solder-joint mechanical strength.
7. The figure must not imply that one viscosity number, one tack value, or one printer setting is universal.
