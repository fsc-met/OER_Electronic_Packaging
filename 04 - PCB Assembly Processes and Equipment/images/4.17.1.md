# Figure 4.17.1 Suggested Content - Common Through-Hole Component Forms and Lead Paths

## Status

**Suggested-content specification - Rev2 QA-reviewed candidate**

This file defines the instructional content and figure-generation constraints for Figure 4.17.1 in Section 4.17, **Through-Hole Components and Insertion**.

The figure has one instructional role: **help students physically recognize common THT component forms and connect them to the lead-through-plated-hole concept before soldering**.

Do **not** call this specification or generated artwork canonical/final before explicit project-owner acceptance.

## Figure Title for Authoring / Reference Only

**Common Through-Hole Component Forms and Lead Paths**

Do **not** place this overall title or the figure number inside the artwork. The public figure number and caption belong in the Section 4.17 Markdown/HTML block.

---

## Instructional Purpose

Give first-time MET students a compact visual answer to two questions:

> **What common component forms count as through-hole components?**

and

> **What physically makes them through-hole before soldering begins?**

The figure should connect familiar package shapes to the common physical idea that a lead, pin, or terminal enters a plated PCB hole.

---

## Central Teaching Message

> **Axial, radial, connector, transformer/odd-form, and power-device packages can look very different, but through-hole assembly places their leads or pins through plated PCB holes before the soldering operation.**

## Overall Layout

Use a two-part layout:

1. **Upper/main area:** four to five simplified component-form examples arranged left to right.
2. **Lower/right teaching inset:** one enlarged PCB cross-section showing a representative component lead or pin entering a plated through-hole **before soldering**.

Required component-form set:

- axial leaded component;
- radial leaded component;
- multi-pin connector;
- transformer / larger odd-form component.

A small power-device example with thicker leads may be added **only if it fits without reducing readability**. It is secondary and must not displace one of the four required categories.

Keep the component-form area dominant. The PTH inset should explain the shared physical relationship without turning the figure into a detailed PCB-fabrication or solder-joint diagram.

## Required Objects and Labels

Required component labels:

- `Axial leaded`
- `Radial leaded`
- `Multi-pin connector`
- `Transformer / odd-form`
- `Power device` if included

Required PTH-inset objects:

- PCB laminate body;
- top and bottom copper pad/land;
- continuous plated barrel;
- finished hole opening;
- component lead/pin passing through the hole;
- visible clearance between an ordinary soldered THT lead and the plated barrel before soldering.

Required PTH-inset labels:

- `Component lead / pin`
- `Plated through-hole (PTH)`
- `Plated barrel`
- `PCB`
- small note: `Before soldering`

Do not show a solder fillet or wave-solder interaction in this figure.

---

## Required Technical Relationships

1. Axial leads exit opposite ends of the component body.
2. Radial leads exit from the same side of the component body.
3. A multi-pin connector uses an organized pin field/housing rather than a two-lead axial/radial form.
4. Transformer/odd-form and power examples may have several leads/tabs and larger bodies.
5. Every example must visually remain compatible with through-hole insertion; do not accidentally draw an SMT-only package.
6. The cross-section must show the lead/pin **entering a plated component hole**, not a non-plated mechanical hole.
7. The plated barrel must be a thin conductive wall around the hole, not solid copper filling the hole.
8. The ordinary soldered THT lead should have assembly/process clearance before soldering; do not draw it as a press-fit interference connection.
9. Do not imply every THT component is mechanically stronger or carries more current than every SMT component.
10. Do not teach solder fill, wave contact, fillet acceptance, or capillary rise; Section 4.18 owns the soldering process.

## Spatial / Geometry Requirements

- Keep the component examples at a similar visual scale while allowing larger odd-form parts to occupy more space naturally.
- Use a consistent near-orthographic or restrained isometric viewing angle.
- Keep category labels directly below or above the relevant component with no ambiguous leaders.
- Keep the PTH inset large enough to distinguish **lead metal, air/process clearance, plated barrel, annular copper, and dielectric** at 900 px.
- The lead must remain centered enough to read clearly but need not be perfectly centered; do not make the clearance so small that the geometry resembles press-fit.
- Maintain approximately **5-7% outer canvas margin**.
- Maintain comfortable separation between component examples and the cross-section inset.
- Do not let the inset leader labels cross the lead, barrel, or unrelated text.
- Avoid showing densely detailed internal package construction.

## Scope Boundaries

This figure **should show** common THT component forms and one clear lead/pin-through-PTH physical relationship before soldering.

This figure **should not show** solder-wave equipment, flux/preheat, solder fillet geometry, barrel-fill acceptance, inspection criteria, detailed hole/annular-ring dimensions, press-fit compliance geometry, or mixed-technology process planning.

---

## Technical-Verification Basis

**Primary MET406 baseline:** the THT introduction identifies the lead-through-plated-hole concept, connectors, transformers, power devices, axial/radial forms, and the general insertion-before-solder sequence.

**Current Section 4.17:** defines through-hole components by leads/pins passing through PTHs, includes axial, radial, connector, transformer/odd-form, and power-device categories, and explicitly keeps detailed hole-design content in earlier PCB/DFM sections.

**Approved references:** Coombs supports axial/radial/odd-form through-hole component categories and assembly context; Blackwell supports continued use of through-hole connectors and larger/power components where geometry/mechanical demands make THT practical. Use these references for verification only; do not copy their illustrations.

---

## Originality / Copyright Requirements

Create an original component-recognition composition. Keep the examples generic enough that students learn **form categories**, not one manufacturer's specific product geometry. Do not trace textbook package drawings, copy vendor product photographs, reproduce connector catalogs, or mimic a protected PCB cross-section layout.

Use generic component geometry with no logos, manufacturer markings, model numbers, or distinctive commercial housings.

---

Preferred method: **controlled vector / SVG-first**. Establish the component silhouettes and category labels first. Once a promising baseline exists, freeze the visual language and make only targeted corrections.

Preserve every meaningful revision under a different filename.

## Editable SVG Requirements

Use a true editable SVG as the controlled source whenever practical. The SVG must use editable text and separate vector objects rather than embedding a raster image as the primary artwork. Define explicit `width`, `height`, and `viewBox`; use `preserveAspectRatio="xMidYMid meet"`; place an explicit full-canvas white rectangle as the first visible object; use `markerUnits="userSpaceOnUse"` for normal engineering arrows/leaders; avoid external fonts/assets; preserve safe whitespace; and follow the project content-first canvas rule.

The SVG is not considered successful merely because it renders. It must also pass the dedicated visual geometry/layout gate: every text block must fit comfortably within its intended region, no text may touch/cross a border, no important objects may overlap unintentionally, leaders/arrows must terminate clearly, margins and internal padding must be balanced, and the source must remain editable after corrections.

Render-check visually at native size and approximately **1600 px, 1200 px, and 900 px** width. After any change to wording, font size, geometry, spacing, or arrow routing, rerun the layout QA.

Keep each component example, the PTH inset, labels, and leaders in separate editable groups so individual corrections do not require redesigning the complete figure.
