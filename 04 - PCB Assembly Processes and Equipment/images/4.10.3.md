## Instructional Purpose

Give first-time Engineering Technology readers a physically intuitive comparison of:

1. a **compatible, centered vacuum pickup**;
2. an **undersized and/or off-center pickup** with reduced holding margin or increased tilt risk;
3. an **oversized or poorly matched pickup** that extends beyond the usable flat pickup surface, leaks, or interferes with package features.

The figure should answer:

> **Why must the nozzle match the component's usable pickup surface rather than simply the overall package outline?**

The figure should support the practical Section 4.10 question:

> **Where can the machine hold this part without losing it, damaging it, or confusing its orientation?**

---

## Central Teaching Message

> **A stable vacuum pickup needs a suitable contact location, enough effective sealed area, adequate centering, and clearance from fragile or functional package features. Nozzle selection is a geometry-and-holding problem, not a simple "bigger is better" rule.**

Supporting ideas:

- a good nozzle opening/contact area sits on a usable package surface;
- off-center or very small contact can reduce holding margin and increase tilt/rotation risk;
- an oversized/poorly matched nozzle can overhang the flat pickup surface, leak, or contact features it should avoid;
- actual performance also depends on nozzle condition, leakage, part mass, acceleration, surface shape, and machine sensing;
- the figure must not imply one universal vacuum pressure requirement.

## Overall Layout

Use **three side-by-side comparison panels** with the same generic component body and similar vertical scale.

Recommended panel order:

1. **Compatible pickup**
2. **Undersized / off-center pickup**
3. **Oversized / poorly matched pickup**

Use a simplified side/oblique or cross-sectional technical view so the contact relationship between nozzle and component top surface is obvious.

Each panel should show:

- nozzle body/tip above the component;
- the nozzle opening/contact footprint;
- component package body;
- usable pickup surface;
- one or two representative package features that must not be contacted (for example leads, edge steps, exposed fragile structures, or an uneven top feature);
- vacuum direction/path indicated by a small upward arrow inside the nozzle;
- a concise outcome cue.

Do not draw a complete placement head or board-placement sequence.

---

## Required Objects and Labels

### Shared required objects in all panels

- same generic component/package family for comparison;
- clear top/usable pickup surface;
- nozzle body and tip/opening;
- package edge/feature context;
- component centerline or visual center cue where useful.

### Panel 1 - Compatible pickup

Required geometry:

- nozzle centered or intentionally located on a stable flat pickup region;
- contact/opening fully contained within the usable pickup surface;
- visually adequate support against tilt;
- no contact with leads/balls/fragile edge features.

Required label:

- **Compatible pickup**

Optional short cues:

- `good seal`
- `centered / stable`

### Panel 2 - Undersized / off-center pickup

Required geometry:

- nozzle contact noticeably smaller and/or shifted away from the preferred pickup region;
- component still technically pickable in the conceptual example, but with visibly lower holding/tilt margin;
- optional slight tilt arrow or moment cue to show instability risk without implying failure is guaranteed.

Required label:

- **Undersized / off-center**

Optional short cues:

- `lower holding margin`
- `tilt / rotation risk`

Do not state that every small nozzle always drops the part.

### Panel 3 - Oversized / poorly matched pickup

Required geometry:

- nozzle opening/contact footprint extends beyond the usable flat pickup surface **or** overlaps a package feature that prevents a good seal/clearance;
- show a small leakage gap or interference region where useful;
- the bad geometry should arise from mismatch, not simply from the nozzle being visually large.

Required label:

- **Oversized / poorly matched**

Optional short cues:

- `leak / interference risk`
- `poor surface match`

Do not imply that every larger nozzle necessarily causes placement offset.

## Required Technical Relationships

The final artwork must satisfy all of the following:

1. The nozzle opening/contact region must physically meet the component's usable pickup surface.
2. The compatible case must show the nozzle footprint contained within a stable, reasonably flat pickup region.
3. The undersized/off-center case must show why reduced effective contact and/or eccentric pickup can reduce stability without presenting failure as guaranteed.
4. The oversized/poorly matched case must show overhang, leakage, or interference with package geometry; size alone is not the teaching mechanism.
5. The nozzle must not contact fragile leads, solder balls, or other functional features in the compatible case.
6. Vacuum direction should be shown through the nozzle, not as a force pushing the component downward.
7. The figure should not imply that a vacuum reading alone proves correct centering.
8. The figure should not show a universal vacuum pressure number or threshold.
9. Nozzle geometry may be round/oval/rectangular/custom in industry, but the comparison should use one simplified family so geometry changes are easy to interpret.
10. The component in all three panels should be the same or clearly equivalent so the difference is the pickup-tool relationship, not a change of package type.
11. Nozzle condition should be acknowledged in a small note only if space permits; detailed contamination/blockage is not the main role of this figure.

## Spatial / Geometry Requirements

- Keep the component body and overall panel scale consistent across all three cases.
- Align the component package on a common baseline.
- Use a centerline or reference mark in the compatible panel if it helps students see centering.
- In the off-center case, make the eccentricity visually obvious but not exaggerated to the point of physical impossibility.
- In the oversized/poor-match case, show a clear geometric reason for leakage/interference.
- Keep vacuum arrows inside the nozzle or directly along the nozzle centerline.
- Avoid arrows that could be interpreted as component motion toward the PCB; this figure is pickup-only.
- Keep callout leaders short and unambiguous.
- Maintain **6-8% outer margins** and enough internal whitespace for labels.
- If package leads/features are shown, keep them mechanically plausible and symmetric where appropriate.

## Technical-Verification Basis

### Section and outline basis

This figure directly supports Section 4.10.7 and the approved Section 4.10 requirements for:

- nozzle recognition;
- vacuum pickup;
- component size/shape constraints;
- feeder/nozzle compatibility.

### Current Section 4.10 basis

The current Rev3 section states that:

- most SMT placement heads use vacuum nozzles;
- the nozzle should match the **available pickup surface** and component mechanical behavior rather than only the package outline;
- stable holding depends on usable contact/seal area, centering, leakage, component mass, acceleration, nozzle condition, and surface geometry;
- common nozzle forms include round, oval, rectangular, and component-specific shapes;
- contamination, chipping, blockage, or nozzle damage can destabilize pickup;
- exact vacuum sensing levels are machine-, nozzle-, and component-dependent.

### Primary instructional basis

MET406 Chapter 4 - Assembly Technologies teaches:

- vacuum nozzles pick components from feeders;
- nozzle geometry should match component size/shape;
- proper fit supports a good seal, minimal tilt, and accurate centering;
- poorly matched nozzles can contribute to dropped or misaligned parts;
- pickup/retention can be monitored by vacuum sensing.

The public specification deliberately corrects the oversimplified implication that nozzle size alone determines success or failure.

### Approved secondary/current verification basis

The relationships are consistent with the Section 4.10 approved verification basis, including:

- Rao R. Tummala, *Fundamentals of Microsystems Packaging*, Chapter 17;
- Clyde F. Coombs Jr., *Printed Circuits Handbook*, assembly-process discussion;
- current ASMPT/Yamaha/JUKI placement-equipment information used during Section 4.10 verification for component-specific nozzle/tooling and pickup-correction practice.

No vendor nozzle chart or machine drawing should be reproduced.

## Generation Guidance

Preferred method: **controlled vector / SVG-first technical comparison**.

Recommended authoring sequence:

1. create one generic package and usable pickup-surface geometry;
2. duplicate the same package into three panels;
3. create the compatible nozzle/contact geometry first;
4. modify only nozzle size/location/fit for the two poor-match cases;
5. add vacuum arrows and minimal outcome callouts;
6. verify that the bad cases are physically meaningful rather than merely visually dramatic;
7. render at 900 px and inspect the seal/contact relationship;
8. freeze the strongest baseline and make only targeted corrections.

If AI-assisted raster generation is used for a concept, do not trust nozzle openings, package leads, contact points, or text without reconstruction/verification. Use controlled vector geometry for the final candidate if those relationships cannot be made exact.
