## Instructional Purpose

Show the **operator/technologist handling logic** for a moisture/reflow-sensitive device after receiving or opening dry-pack material.

The figure should make the manufacturing decision process visible:

1. recognize the dry-pack controls;
2. verify known material status;
3. open and begin tracking exposure;
4. continue to assembly if within the allowed condition;
5. use qualified dry storage or approved repacking when permitted;
6. place material on hold when exposure/humidity status is unknown or unacceptable;
7. follow the approved controlled recovery/dry/bake process when required.

This figure teaches **what to do**, not the physical package-damage mechanism. Figure 4.5.2 owns that mechanism.

---

## Central Teaching Message

> **Moisture-sensitive-device handling depends on preserving known status: identify the MSL and dry-pack evidence, track exposure after opening, and hold the material rather than guessing when the condition is unknown or exceeded.**

The figure should reinforce that resealing or moving a reel does not automatically reset its floor-life history.

## Overall Layout

Use a simple left-to-right manufacturing flow with one major decision branch.

### Stage 1 - Sealed dry-pack recognition

Show a simplified moisture-barrier bag package containing/associated with:

- MSL/caution label;
- desiccant;
- HIC;
- component reel/tray.

Panel/step label:

**1  Verify sealed dry-pack status**

Supporting callout:

**Check label, bag condition, HIC, and material identity**

### Stage 2 - Open and start exposure tracking

Show:

- bag opened;
- reel/tray removed;
- exposure clock/timer icon;
- record/traveler/database symbol.

Step label:

**2  Open + record exposure**

Supporting callout:

**Track floor-life status**

### Stage 3 - Decision

Use one diamond:

**Known status and within allowed exposure?**

Two main branches:

#### YES branch

Leads to:

**Assemble / reflow within allowed condition**

Optional small secondary branch:

**Need to pause? -> qualified dry storage or approved repack when procedure permits**

If dry storage/repack is shown, arrow it back to **tracked material status**, not to a "new" or reset clock.

#### NO / UNKNOWN branch

Leads down to a strong but restrained:

**HOLD**

Then:

**Verify exposure / HIC / storage history**

Then:

**Follow approved recovery, drying/bake, or repack procedure as required**

Do not show a universal bake time or temperature.

## Required Technical Relationships

1. Dry-pack status is verified **before** material is treated as normal line-side stock.
2. The MBB, desiccant, HIC, and MSL/caution label are shown as different control/evidence items with different roles.
3. Opening the bag begins/continues controlled exposure tracking according to the applicable procedure.
4. Floor-life status is based on **known history**, not visual appearance.
5. Material known to be within the allowed condition may proceed to assembly/reflow.
6. Qualified dry storage or approved repacking may preserve/control status when permitted by procedure.
7. Dry storage/repacking must **not** be shown as automatically resetting the floor-life clock.
8. Unknown or exceeded status leads to **HOLD / verify**, not directly to assembly.
9. An HIC is evidence of bag humidity condition, not a direct measurement of moisture inside every component.
10. Recovery/dry/bake action must be shown as controlled and procedure-dependent.
11. No universal bake recipe is shown.
12. No full J-STD-033 table or proprietary standard decision tree is reproduced.

---

## Spatial / Geometry Requirements

- Keep the main flow left to right.
- Use one decision diamond only; avoid a complex flowchart.
- Keep the `YES` path horizontally dominant.
- Send `NO / UNKNOWN` downward into a visually separate hold/recovery branch.
- Use a clearly boxed **HOLD** state so the stop decision is unmistakable.
- The dry-storage/repack branch should reconnect to the tracked-status flow, not to the start as though history disappeared.
- Keep arrow directions unambiguous.
- Avoid crossing arrows.
- Maintain at least **6–8% outer margin**.
- Keep object icons simple enough that text remains readable at 900 px.

## Generation Guidance

### Preferred Production Method

Use a **controlled vector SVG**.

This figure relies on:

- clean branching;
- unambiguous arrow directions;
- readable short labels;
- controlled icon geometry;
- strict distinction between `continue` and `hold`.

Vector construction is strongly preferred over raster generation.

### Recommended Build Order

1. Create the 2000 × 1050 white canvas.
2. Establish the main left-to-right process baseline.
3. Draw the dry-pack recognition group.
4. Draw the open/track-exposure group.
5. Add the single decision diamond.
6. Add the `YES -> Assemble/reflow` branch.
7. Add the optional dry-storage/repack branch and reconnect it to tracked status.
8. Add the downward `NO / UNKNOWN -> HOLD` branch.
9. Add verification and controlled recovery boxes.
10. Check that no path visually resets exposure history.
11. Add concise object labels for MBB/desiccant/HIC/MSL label.
12. Render at native, 1600, 1200, and 900 px.
13. Freeze the first strong baseline and make targeted corrections only.

### Revision Behavior

After a strong baseline exists:

- freeze the main flow direction and branching;
- preserve box/diamond positions;
- correct only specific label, arrow, or icon defects;
- save each meaningful revision with a new filename;
- do not redesign the entire flowchart for a local correction.
