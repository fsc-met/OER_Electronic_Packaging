## Instructional Purpose

Help first-time Engineering Technology readers recognize the basic reflow-profiling hardware and understand that the profiler can only report a useful product temperature when each thermocouple junction is physically coupled to the intended measurement location.

The figure should answer:

> **What hardware records a reflow profile, and what must the thermocouple junction physically measure?**

The figure should connect the measurement chain from the solder-joint location to the thermocouple wire, profiler/data logger, and recorded temperature-versus-time traces without turning into a detailed oven-anatomy figure.

---

## Central Teaching Message

> **A reflow profile is measured on the product: thermocouple junctions contact selected solder-joint locations, and a multichannel profiler records those temperatures as the instrumented assembly passes through the oven.**

Supporting messages:

- the **measurement junction/bead** is the sensing location;
- the junction must remain in intimate thermal contact with the intended target;
- the thermocouple wires connect selected board locations to separate profiler channels;
- the profiler/data logger may travel through the oven inside a thermal barrier designed for the system;
- attachment quality can make an apparently smooth trace physically misleading;
- a thermocouple touching the wrong object, lifting from the target, or being insulated from the target does not represent the intended solder-joint temperature.

## Overall Layout

Use a **two-part engineering-textbook composition**.

### Main region - profiling measurement chain

Show a simplified populated PCB traveling left-to-right with several thermocouple junctions attached to selected solder-joint locations. Route the thermocouple wires neatly to a multichannel profiler/data logger shown in a protective thermal barrier or insulated carrier.

The main region should visually communicate:

`selected solder-joint locations -> thermocouple channels -> profiler/data logger -> recorded temperature traces`

A tiny simplified trace icon may appear at the profiler output only to communicate that multiple temperatures are recorded. Do not reproduce the full profile interpretation that belongs to Figure 4.15.3.

### Secondary region - attachment-quality comparison

Use a compact side-by-side or stacked inset with two enlarged solder-joint measurement details:

1. **Good contact** - thermocouple junction firmly coupled to the intended lead/pad solder-joint region with minimal added mass.
2. **Poor contact** - junction lifted from the target, touching only the package/board away from the intended joint, or covered by insulating material such that the target temperature is not represented.

The attachment inset should be large enough to make the bead/target relationship visible at 900 px.

Do not use a full reflow-oven cutaway. Figure 4.14.1 already owns oven hardware.

## Required Objects and Labels

### Main measurement setup

Required objects:

- one representative populated PCB;
- several generic SMT components;
- at least three thermocouple measurement junctions attached at different board locations;
- fine thermocouple wire pairs routed away from the board;
- one multichannel `Reflow profiler / data logger`;
- one simplified `Thermal barrier` around the profiler when shown as traveling through the oven;
- separate channel connections entering the profiler;
- a restrained multi-trace output cue or small screen/trace symbol.

Required labels equivalent to:

- `Thermocouple junction`
- `Thermocouple wires`
- `Profiler channels`
- `Reflow profiler / data logger`
- `Thermal barrier` or `Protective thermal barrier`
- `Recorded temperature traces`

Optional small label:

- `Representative populated PCB`

Do not label a fixed number of channels as universal.

### Good-contact inset

Show:

- PCB pad;
- component termination/lead;
- solder-joint region;
- fine thermocouple junction placed at the intended measurement location;
- restrained securing material consistent with a durable profiling-board method.

Required label:

- `Good: junction coupled to target`

Optional secondary label:

- `High-temperature solder for durable attachment`

If high-temperature solder is illustrated, show it as the **securing method around the lead/joint region**, not as a second sensing element.

### Poor-contact inset

Show one clear failure mode, preferably:

- thermocouple junction lifted slightly away from the intended solder joint;

and optionally a second subtle cue such as insulating tape covering the bead.

Required label:

- `Poor: junction not measuring the intended target`

Optional concise notes:

- `Loose / lifted bead`
- `Insulating cover over bead`

Do not show polyimide tape as universally prohibited. The Section 4.15 text allows it for securing **wires away from the bead**; the problem is using insulating tape over the measurement junction as the primary thermal attachment.

## Required Technical Relationships

1. Each temperature channel must originate at a **thermocouple junction on the product**, not from an oven-zone sensor.
2. The measurement junction must be depicted as the location where the two thermocouple wires meet.
3. The good-contact junction must physically touch or be embedded at the intended solder-joint/lead-pad region.
4. The good-contact attachment must add only modest local mass and must not visibly bridge unrelated component leads.
5. The poor-contact example must be physically plausible and must clearly measure the wrong thermal condition or weakly couple to the target.
6. Thermocouple wires may be restrained away from the junction, but the artwork must not imply that ordinary insulating tape over the bead is a preferred attachment method.
7. The profiler must receive several separate thermocouple channels and record temperature histories; it must not be shown controlling the oven directly.
8. The thermal barrier, if shown, protects the logger electronics; it does not thermally insulate the thermocouple measurement locations on the PCB.
9. The PCB should be populated. A bare PCB must not be used as the main profiling-board example because it does not reproduce the assembled product's thermal response.
10. Do not imply that every profiler must travel through the oven in exactly the same package configuration; some systems and installations differ.
11. Do not introduce recipe optimization, pass/fail decisions, or defect diagnosis in this figure. Figures 4.15.3 and Section 4.16 own those concepts.
12. Do not imply that one thermocouple can represent the entire board.
13. If the PCB and profiler are shown traveling together, depict the profiler **following** the instrumented board rather than leading it; keep enough visual separation that the profiler does not appear to be part of the board thermal mass. Do not label one universal spacing distance unless the wording is qualified as system/procedure dependent.

## Spatial / Geometry Requirements

- Keep the populated PCB as the dominant object in the main region.
- Route thermocouple wires in smooth, separated paths; avoid a tangled bundle that hides board features.
- Terminate every wire clearly at either a visible junction or a profiler channel.
- Do not let wires cross labels or the good/poor attachment inset.
- Place the profiler downstream or beside the PCB so the visual path reads naturally from measurement point to recording instrument.
- If a board-travel arrow is shown, keep it secondary and simple; Section 4.14.1 already teaches conveyor transport.
- Enlarge the good/poor attachment details enough that the solder joint, junction bead, and poor-contact gap are visible at 900 px.
- Keep leader lines short and terminate them directly on their intended object.
- Maintain approximately **6-8% outer margin** and comfortable internal padding around insets.
- Avoid perspective or 3D effects that make the thermocouple-junction contact ambiguous.
- The figure should remain understandable in grayscale through labels, geometry, line style, and callout structure.

## Technical-Verification Basis

Use the following sources for technical verification, not as artwork templates:

### Primary instructional baseline

- MET406 Chapter 4 - Assembly Technologies, **Reflow Soldering 27-28**: attach thermocouples at critical locations; connect the instrumented board to a profiler; run the full oven cycle; record temperature-versus-time data; analyze ramp, soak, peak, TAL, and cooling.

### Approved reference hierarchy

- Clyde F. Coombs Jr., *Printed Circuits Handbook*, 6th ed., Chapter 47:
  - reflow profiler/tracker as multichannel measurement instrumentation;
  - product profile board as a thermocouple-instrumented populated board;
  - junction contact with the intended solder-joint location;
  - caution about unintended thermocouple junctions;
  - fine-gauge thermocouples and minimum disturbance of the measurement;
  - high-temperature solder as a durable attachment method;
  - caution against tape directly holding/shielding the bead;
  - thermal barrier/protective cover for trackers traveling through the oven.
- Glenn R. Blackwell, *The Electronic Packaging Handbook*: product-temperature measurement on actual assemblies and use of attached thermocouples for reflow-profile verification.

### Current manufacturer/application guidance

- Indium Corporation, *Attaching Thermocouples to a PCB for Reflow Profiling*:
  - thermocouple tip/junction should contact the solder joint rather than a remote package/board location;
  - populated boards should be used for representative product profiling;
  - high-melting-temperature solder is a preferred attachment method;
  - thermally conductive epoxy or aluminum tape may be alternatives;
  - insulating Kapton/polyimide tape should not cover the thermocouple tip as the primary attachment method;
  - profiler thermal mass should be kept from distorting the board measurement.

The figure must preserve these engineering relationships without importing the layout, screenshots, or figure composition of any reference source.

## Generation Guidance

Preferred method: **controlled vector / SVG-first**, because the thermocouple-junction geometry and labels must be exact.

Recommended workflow:

1. create the populated-board + profiler measurement-chain composition;
2. establish clean wire routing and channel connections;
3. add the good-contact enlarged inset;
4. add one clear poor-contact inset;
5. add only the labels necessary to identify hardware and measurement quality;
6. render at 900 px and confirm that the bead/target relationship is still visible;
7. freeze the strongest visual baseline;
8. make only targeted corrections to wire routing, bead geometry, leader placement, wording, or spacing.

AI image generation may be used for an early concept, but if the bead, lead/pad contact, channel wiring, or text cannot be made technically reliable, rebuild the figure as controlled vector artwork rather than repeatedly regenerating the whole design.
