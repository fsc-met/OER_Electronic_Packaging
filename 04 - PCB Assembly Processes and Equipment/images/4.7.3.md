## Instructional Purpose

Help a beginning Engineering Technology reader understand area ratio as a **physical comparison between aperture opening area and aperture-wall area**.

The figure should visually answer:

> **Why can a small aperture or a thick stencil be harder to release even if the top-view opening seems acceptable?**

It should connect the rectangular area-ratio equation to the competing physical surfaces that affect release.

---

## Central Teaching Message

> **Area ratio compares the aperture opening area with the aperture-wall area. When wall area becomes large relative to the opening, paste has more stencil-wall interaction and release generally becomes more difficult.**

Supporting ideas:

- higher area ratio generally provides a more favorable release geometry;
- lower area ratio generally increases sensitivity to paste, wall finish, cleanliness, coatings, and separation behavior;
- the traditional approximately 0.66 guideline is useful context, not a universal pass/fail law.

## Overall Layout

Use three coordinated zones rather than one overloaded drawing.

### Zone A — Geometry definition

Show one simplified rectangular aperture with:

- opening area highlighted;
- four wall surfaces conceptually represented;
- dimensions $L$, $W$, and $T$.

Include the rectangular relationship:

$$
A_R = \frac{LW}{2T(L+W)}
$$

Use concise labels:

- `Opening area = LW`
- `Wall area = 2T(L+W)`

### Zone B — Simple worked geometry anchor

Include the same section example, compactly:

- $L=0.30\text{ mm}$;
- $W=0.30\text{ mm}$;
- $T=0.10\text{ mm}$;
- $A_R=0.75$.

This may be a small boxed calculation or a geometry callout. Do not dominate the figure with arithmetic.

### Zone C — Release comparison

Compare two simplified aperture-release cases:

- **higher area ratio** -> relatively more opening area / less wall dominance -> cleaner/easier release tendency;
- **lower area ratio** -> relatively more wall interaction -> more paste retained on walls / more difficult release tendency.

The comparison must communicate **tendency**, not guaranteed success/failure.

A small qualified note may say:

> **~0.66 has long been used as a conventional starting guideline; qualified modern processes may print below it.**

If this note makes the figure crowded, omit it from the artwork because the public text already supplies the qualification.

## Required Technical Relationships

The figure must correctly show that:

1. area ratio is **opening area divided by aperture-wall area**;
2. for a rectangular aperture, opening area is $LW$;
3. wall area is $2T(L+W)$;
4. decreasing $L$ or $W$ or increasing $T$ generally lowers area ratio;
5. lower area ratio means more stencil-wall interaction relative to opening area;
6. lower area ratio generally makes release more difficult, all else equal;
7. higher area ratio does not guarantee a perfect print;
8. lower area ratio does not guarantee failure;
9. the 0.66 concept must not be shown as a universal acceptance threshold.

## Technical-Verification Basis

### Section and Outline Basis

This figure directly supports Section 4.7.4 and the approved outline requirement for an **area-ratio concept at applied level** plus one simple area-ratio calculation.

### Equation Verification

For a rectangular aperture:

$$
A_R=\frac{LW}{2T(L+W)}
$$

because the aperture opening area is $LW$ and the four wall areas sum to $2T(L+W)$.

The section example:

$$
L=W=0.30\text{ mm},\qquad T=0.10\text{ mm}
$$

gives:

$$
A_R=0.75.
$$

### Approved Reference Basis

The current Section 4.7 authoring notes verify this treatment against:

- IPC-7525C terminology for aperture and area ratio;
- Indium stencil-design guidance for the rectangular formula and the conventional approximately 0.66 guideline;
- current AIM/process evidence showing qualified modern printing can operate below 0.66 in some cases;
- Coombs and Tummala for underlying stencil-printing/release context.

Use these sources for technical correctness only; do not reproduce their figures or layouts.
