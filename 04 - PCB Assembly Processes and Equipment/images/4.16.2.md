## Instructional Purpose

Give MET students a clean visual reference for what six commonly discussed SMT defect categories can physically look like. The figure should answer:

> **What physical features should I recognize when someone says open, bridge, solder balls, tombstone, skew/shift, or poor wetting?**

The figure must stop at **appearance recognition** and intentionally avoid teaching one assumed cause per defect.

---

## Central Teaching Message

> **Defects have recognizable physical appearances, but appearance alone does not identify the process stage that caused them.**

Supporting messages:

- insufficient solder and an electrical open are related but not identical;
- a bridge is an unintended conductive solder connection;
- solder balls are separate solder spheres outside the intended joint;
- a tombstone is a small chip component lifted at one end;
- skew/shift is in-plane rotation/displacement;
- poor/non-wetting is inadequate solder spreading/bonding and exposed/unwetted metal, not merely a dull-looking joint.

## Overall Layout

Use **six equal instructional panels** in a 2 x 3 grid. Recommended order:

Top row: `Insufficient solder / open`, `Solder bridge`, `Solder balls`.

Bottom row: `Tombstone`, `Skew / shift`, `Non-wetting / poor wetting`.

Each panel should use one simplified generic PCB/pad/component example, one concise heading, and at most one short appearance cue. Keep a consistent vector-viewing style across panels.

---

## Required Objects and Labels

**Panel 1 — Insufficient solder / open:** one termination/lead with little or incomplete solder and, if showing an open example, a visible non-contact gap. Do not imply all opens are low-solder defects.

**Panel 2 — Solder bridge:** adjacent pads/leads joined by one continuous unintended solder mass.

**Panel 3 — Solder balls:** one or more separate spherical solder particles on solder mask outside the intended joint; they must not look like designed BGA balls.

**Panel 4 — Tombstone:** small two-terminal chip with one end soldered and the opposite end lifted out of plane.

**Panel 5 — Skew / shift:** component rotated/displaced in plane relative to its footprint; keep it roughly horizontal so it is not confused with tombstoning.

**Panel 6 — Non-wetting / poor wetting:** solder with limited spread, exposed intended metal, and bead/high-contact-angle geometry. Do not use `dull = bad` or `shiny = good`.

---

## Required Technical Relationships

1. Every panel must depict the named defect category distinctly enough for a first-time student to differentiate it from the other five.
2. Insufficient solder/open must not imply the two are universally identical.
3. Bridge must show a continuous conductive solder path between conductors intended to be separate.
4. Solder balls must be separate from intended joints and visually different from BGA balls.
5. Tombstone is out-of-plane lift of one end of a two-terminal chip.
6. Skew/shift is in-plane displacement/rotation.
7. Non-wetting/poor wetting must be shown through spread/contact geometry and exposed surface, not brightness.
8. Do not label any panel `print cause`, `placement cause`, or `reflow cause`; each appearance can have multiple sources.
9. Do not include fixed workmanship limits, hidden-BGA defects, or detailed AOI/X-ray/test content.
10. Do not duplicate Figure 4.16.1's source-map role or Figure 4.16.3's hidden-joint role.

## Technical-Verification Basis

**Primary MET406 baseline:** print-defect pages cover insufficient/excess paste, bridging, slumping, incomplete release, and misalignment; placement-defect pages cover misalignment, tombstoning, and lifted leads; soldering fundamentals cover wetting/non-wetting; reflow-defect pages cover non-wetting/dewetting, solder balling, tombstoning, and open/cracked-joint awareness.

**Current Section 4.16:** distinguishes insufficient solder from open circuits, defines bridges and solder balls, explains tombstoning and skew/shift, and cautions against using shiny/dull appearance alone to judge lead-free wetting.

**Approved references:** Blackwell describes visual inspection targets including misalignment, coplanarity/opens, bridges, insufficient solder, and solder balls; Coombs supports distinct solder-ball, bridge/short, tombstone, wetting, and open outcomes. Use source concepts only; do not copy defect photographs or plate layouts.
