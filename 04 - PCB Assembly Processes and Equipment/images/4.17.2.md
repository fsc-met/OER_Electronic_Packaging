The figure has one instructional role: **show the controlled physical sequence used to prepare, orient, insert, seat, and retain a THT component before soldering**.

## Instructional Purpose

Turn the Section 4.17 insertion discussion into a visual workflow that students can use as a practical pre-solder check.

The figure should answer:

> **What physical checks should occur between receiving a THT component and sending the inserted board to soldering?**

---

## Central Teaching Message

> **THT insertion is controlled preparation and verification, not “push the leads through the holes.”**

The required sequence is:

> **Lead preparation -> orientation check -> insert without forcing -> establish the specified seating/stand-off -> retain only as required**

## Overall Layout

Use two visual bands.

### Upper/main band — controlled insertion sequence

Five numbered stages arranged left to right:

1. `Prepare leads`
2. `Verify orientation / polarity`
3. `Insert without forcing`
4. `Set specified seating / stand-off`
5. `Retain as required`

Use one representative THT part through the sequence where practical so the workflow reads as one continuous physical operation.

### Lower band — conditions to avoid

Use four compact bad-condition examples:

- `Forced / mismatched lead pattern`
- `Reversed polarity`
- `Tilted or uneven seating`
- `Excessive clinching / retention`

Each bad example should use a small neutral warning mark; do not turn the figure into a standards-acceptance poster.

---

## Required Objects and Labels

### Stage 1 — Prepare leads
Show an axial or radial part with leads being formed to match the board hole spacing. Label `Lead form matches hole pattern`. Do not show sharp bends at the package seal.

### Stage 2 — Verify orientation / polarity
Use a polarized radial component or keyed connector. Show a component polarity/key mark and a corresponding board/document orientation cue. Label `Verify before insertion`.

### Stage 3 — Insert without forcing
Show leads aligned with PTHs and entering naturally. Label `Lead pattern aligns with holes`. Include a small `Do not force` note near the bad-example strip rather than the good panel.

### Stage 4 — Set specified seating / stand-off
Show a correct intended seating condition and a **small secondary two-case cue** labeled `Near-flush when specified` and `Stand-off when specified`. Neither case should receive a green "universal good" mark. The main label should read `Use the specified seating condition`.

### Stage 5 — Retain as required
Show a restrained lead-clinch example **plus a small alternative retention cue** such as a connector latch/board lock or fixture symbol. Label `Temporary retention only if required`. Keep the clinch moderate and leave visible clearance around the hole/pad. This prevents the figure from implying that clinching is the only retention method.

## Required Technical Relationships

1. Lead forming changes lead geometry to match the intended footprint; it must not imply that forcing a mismatched component is acceptable.
2. Polarity/orientation verification occurs **before** soldering.
3. The lead/pin should enter the intended PTH without excessive sideways force.
4. Seating is product/package dependent; do not imply `flush = always correct` or `stand-off = always correct`.
5. Heavy/large parts may require support independent of the lead itself.
6. Clinching is optional/process-dependent, not universal.
7. Excessive clinching may stress the lead/hole/pad and can interfere with later solder flow.
8. Lead trimming/cutting may occur at different stages depending on the process; do not define one mandatory trimming point in this figure.
9. Do not present a universal lead protrusion dimension or clinch angle.
10. Do not show wave-solder equipment, molten solder, flux, preheat, or final solder-joint acceptance.

## Spatial / Geometry Requirements

- Give each of the five main steps equal visual weight and aligned baselines.
- Keep process arrows simple and one-directional.
- The bad-condition strip should be visually separate from the main sequence so students do not confuse the incorrect examples with required steps.
- Keep the `reversed polarity` example physically believable: use a polarized part/marking pair, not a generic nonpolar resistor.
- Keep the component/pad/hole geometry large enough to identify tilt, force, and clinch conditions at 900 px.
- Do not route arrows through component bodies or text.
- Maintain approximately **5-7% outer margin** and comfortable internal panel padding.
- Any multi-line labels must be intentionally wrapped and vertically centered.
- Use warning icons sparingly; geometry must carry the technical meaning. The same component style should be reused between the correct and incorrect examples whenever that improves comparison.

## Scope Boundaries

This figure **should show** insertion preparation, orientation/polarity verification, natural lead-to-hole alignment, seating/stand-off, optional retention, and representative incorrect conditions.

## Technical-Verification Basis

**Primary MET406 baseline:** the THT process sequence includes lead forming/preparation, insertion, clinching/retention, stand-off awareness, manual/automated insertion, and later soldering. The lecture also uses representative lead protrusion and clinch-angle examples, but the current Section 4.17 intentionally does not present those values as universal requirements.

**Current Section 4.17:** emphasizes lead preparation, no-forcing, polarity/orientation verification, product-dependent seating/stand-off, optional clinching/retention, lead cutting as process-dependent, and connector/transformer mechanical awareness.

**Approved references:** Coombs supports manual/automatic insertion and component preparation/retention context; current insertion-equipment practice supports controlled insertion/clinch functions rather than one universal clinch geometry; current IPC-controlled workmanship criteria support avoiding one textbook protrusion/clinch number for every product.
