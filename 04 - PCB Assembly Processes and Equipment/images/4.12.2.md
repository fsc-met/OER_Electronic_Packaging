# Figure 4.12.2 Suggested Content - Fiducials Correct Board Shift and Rotation

## Status

**Suggested-content specification - Rev2 QA-reviewed candidate**

This file defines the instructional content and figure-generation constraints for Figure 4.12.2 in Section 4.12, **Placement Data, Vision, Fiducials, and Basic Alignment**.

The figure has one instructional role: show how board-level fiducial recognition allows programmed placement coordinates to be related to a **real PCB that enters the machine shifted and/or rotated**.

Do **not** call this specification or generated artwork canonical/final before explicit project-owner acceptance.

## Figure Title for Authoring / Reference Only

**Fiducials Correct Board Shift and Rotation**

Do **not** place this overall title or figure number inside the artwork.

---

## Instructional Purpose

Help first-time Engineering Technology readers understand why mechanical board positioning is not sufficient by itself and how fiducials establish the relationship between the programmed coordinate frame and the actual PCB position.

The figure should answer:

> **How can the machine place parts correctly when the real PCB enters slightly shifted or rotated?**

The visual must distinguish:

- programmed/ideal board coordinates;
- actual board position in the machine;
- measured board-level fiducials;
- translation/rotation relationship;
- corrected placement targets;
- optional local-fiducial awareness.

---

## Central Teaching Message

> **The placement file describes an ideal board coordinate system. By locating board fiducials on the actual PCB, the machine can relate the programmed coordinates to the board's measured position and orientation before placing components.**

Supporting messages:

- board translation and rotation are different from component pickup offset;
- two widely separated board-level fiducials are a common arrangement, not a universal requirement;
- additional scale/distortion or local corrections depend on the equipment and process;
- local fiducials can provide a nearby registration reference when used;
- the figure is conceptual and should not imply a universal machine tolerance or correction algorithm.

## Overall Layout

Use a **two-part alignment diagram** plus a small local-fiducial inset.

### Left panel - Programmed vs. actual board

Show:

- a light dashed rectangular outline labeled **Programmed board/reference frame**;
- the actual PCB overlaid with a small visible X/Y shift and slight in-plane rotation;
- two board-level fiducials on the actual PCB, widely separated;
- the expected/programmed fiducial locations on the reference board;
- one board-recognition camera cue or camera sight-line symbol;
- concise `ΔX`, `ΔY`, and `Δθ` relationship labels;
- at least two programmed component targets.

The shift/rotation should be large enough to see but not so exaggerated that the board looks physically misplaced.

### Right panel - Corrected placement targets

Show:

- the actual PCB in its measured position/orientation;
- corrected component target symbols aligned with the actual board;
- a simple transformation cue such as:

`programmed target → board transform → corrected target`

Do not show a full transformation matrix.

### Small inset - Local fiducial awareness

Show:

- one fine-pitch/asymmetric component footprint or local region;
- one nearby local fiducial;
- label: **Local fiducial (when used)**;
- brief note: `additional nearby registration reference`.

The inset must not imply that every component requires a local fiducial.

## Required Technical Relationships

1. The programmed/reference board and actual PCB must represent the **same PCB**, not two different products.
2. The actual PCB must be shown shifted and slightly rotated relative to the programmed reference frame. The two outlines must remain identifiable as the **same board geometry**, so the displacement is read as misregistration rather than as two separate boards.
3. Fiducial expected locations and measured/actual fiducials must correspond to one another.
4. Board-level fiducials must be widely separated enough for the rotation relationship to be visually understandable.
5. The board-recognition camera must look at fiducials, not at the component target. If two camera sight lines are shown, each must terminate on a fiducial and must not pass through target labels.
6. `ΔX` and `ΔY` must represent board translation, not component pickup error.
7. `Δθ` must represent in-plane board rotation.
8. Corrected targets must remain attached to the actual board coordinate frame.
9. The figure must not imply that simply adding the same X/Y offset is sufficient when board rotation is present.
10. The figure must not show component pickup X/Y/$\theta$ compensation; that distinct correction was introduced in Section 4.11 and summarized in Section 4.12.8.
11. The two-fiducial arrangement must be labeled or treated as a **common example**, not a universal minimum.
12. Do not imply that three fiducials universally produce scale correction.
13. If scale/distortion awareness is included, it must be a small qualified note only.
14. The local fiducial must be physically near the local component/region it references.
15. The local fiducial must be shown as optional/process-dependent, not mandatory.
16. No machine-specific algorithm, fiducial tolerance, or correction resolution should appear.

## Spatial / Geometry Requirements

- Use the same board dimensions for the programmed and actual board.
- Use a modest shift and rotation so both frames remain easy to compare.
- The programmed/reference board should use a light dashed outline.
- The actual board should use the normal muted PCB style.
- Match each programmed fiducial to its corresponding actual fiducial using short dashed displacement leaders or clearly aligned markers.
- Keep global-fiducial markers visually different from component target markers so students cannot confuse a board reference with a placement target.
- Do not place the local-fiducial inset over the programmed/actual overlay; reserve independent whitespace for it.
- `ΔX` and `ΔY` arrows should be orthogonal.
- `Δθ` should be a curved in-plane rotation arrow near a board corner or reference axis.
- Do not let correction arrows pass through component target labels.
- On the corrected panel, target markers must sit on intended footprint/placement locations in the actual board frame.
- The local-fiducial inset should be spatially separated and clearly labeled as an inset.
- Maintain approximately **6-8% outer margin**.
- If overlay labels become crowded, enlarge the canvas before shrinking text.
- Keep the board shift/rotation visually subtle enough to reinforce the phrase **slightly shifted or rotated**.

## Technical-Verification Basis

### Current Section 4.12 basis

Section 4.12 Rev3 states that:

- mechanical board positioning does not guarantee identical X/Y position and angle;
- board-recognition cameras locate programmed fiducials;
- fiducial measurements can establish X translation, Y translation, in-plane rotation, and on supported systems scale/other geometric compensation;
- two widely separated board-level fiducials are a common arrangement;
- exact fiducial count and correction model are machine/process-dependent;
- local fiducials may provide an additional nearby registration reference;
- when rotation is present, applying the same X/Y translation to every component is not enough.

The section's worked example shows that a small board rotation can create meaningful displacement far from the reference point.

### Approved outline basis

The approved Section 4.12 outline requires:

- board fiducials;
- local fiducial awareness;
- camera alignment;
- board shift/rotation correction.

### Primary instructional basis

MET406 Chapter 4 - Assembly Technologies teaches that:

- the PCB is conveyed into the machine;
- the machine locates fiducial marks;
- the controller establishes board X/Y shift and rotation;
- placement coordinates are adjusted to the actual board position before placement.

### Approved secondary/current verification basis already documented in Section 4.12

- Tummala, *Fundamentals of Microsystems Packaging*: board transport, optical alignment using fiducials, and board X/Y/theta correction.
- Coombs, *Printed Circuits Handbook*: global and local/fine-pitch fiducial concepts.
- Current equipment information reviewed during Section 4.12 drafting confirms that fiducial strategy and correction capabilities vary by system.

No source figure should be traced or closely redrawn.

## Generation Guidance

Preferred method: **controlled vector / SVG-first**.

Recommended workflow:

1. draw the programmed/reference board frame;
2. duplicate the board to create the actual PCB;
3. apply a small controlled translation and rotation to the actual PCB;
4. place corresponding global fiducials on both frames;
5. add translation and rotation cues;
6. add programmed component targets;
7. create the corrected-target panel using the actual board frame;
8. add the small local-fiducial inset;
9. render at 900 px and verify that the two coordinate frames remain distinguishable;
10. freeze the strongest baseline and make only targeted corrections.

If AI-generated imagery is used for an early concept, do not trust its board geometry, fiducial correspondences, rotation arrows, or target positions. Rebuild these as controlled vector geometry.
