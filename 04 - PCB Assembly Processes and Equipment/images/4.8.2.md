## Instructional Purpose

Give a first-time Engineering Technology reader a compact physical model of the printer controls that act directly around the print interface.

The reader should see, in one simplified cross-section:

- an active squeegee contacting the stencil;
- a rolling solder-paste bead ahead of the blade;
- paste filling an aperture;
- the supported PCB and pad beneath the stencil;
- squeegee travel direction;
- applied blade force;
- blade angle;
- later stencil-to-board separation.

The figure should answer:

> **Which few machine controls physically act at the print interface, and what are they trying to accomplish?**

---

## Central Teaching Message

> **Squeegee speed and force create a controlled paste roll and stencil wipe during aperture filling; board support stabilizes the interface, and controlled separation afterward allows the filled paste to release onto the PCB pad.**

Supporting ideas:

- the goal is not “more force” or “slower is always better”;
- blade angle and paste-bead size are part of the qualified setup;
- the control variables interact with stencil geometry and paste behavior.

## Required Technical Relationships

The final artwork must satisfy all of the following:

1. The squeegee blade contacts the **top surface of the stencil**, not the PCB.
2. The blade is inclined at a plausible printing angle and the angle marker refers to the blade/stencil geometry.
3. The paste bead is located **ahead of the active squeegee** in the travel direction.
4. The squeegee travel arrow points in the direction that moves the paste bead across the stencil.
5. The applied-force arrow acts downward toward the stencil/print interface.
6. Paste is shown filling a stencil aperture aligned above a PCB pad.
7. The PCB is supported from below.
8. The main print-stroke state and the later separation state are visually distinguished; separation must not look simultaneous with the blade printing over the same location.
9. Separation is shown as relative stencil/PCB motion after printing, without implying one universal machine mechanism.
10. The figure must not imply that increasing speed or force is inherently beneficial.
11. The figure must not imply one universal separation speed.
12. The figure must not introduce quantitative acceptance criteria for printed deposits.

---

## Spatial / Geometry Requirements

- Maintain a clear vertical stack: **squeegee/paste -> stencil -> PCB pad -> PCB -> support**.
- Keep the aperture directly over the pad.
- Keep the blade angle marker outside the paste bead where possible.
- Route the speed arrow above the blade/stencil region.
- Route the force arrow so it cannot be mistaken for separation motion.
- Keep the separation inset/cue far enough from the main force arrow to avoid ambiguity.
- Avoid leader-line crossings.
- Maintain **6–8% outer canvas margin**.
- Preserve enough vertical space that labels do not crowd the cross-section.
- If representative bead size is indicated, use a symbolic dimension or note rather than drawing an exact scale relationship to the whole printer.
