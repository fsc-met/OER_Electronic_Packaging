# Figure 4.18.1 Suggested Content - Major Hardware and Process Path Through a Wave Soldering Machine

## Status

**Suggested-content specification - Rev2 QA-reviewed candidate**

This file defines the instructional content and figure-generation constraints for Figure 4.18.1 in Section 4.18, **Wave Soldering: Equipment, Process, and Common Problems**.

The figure has one instructional role: **help first-time MET students recognize the main wave-soldering hardware and follow the board through the process in the correct physical order**.

Do **not** call this specification or generated artwork canonical/final before explicit project-owner acceptance.

## Figure Title for Authoring / Reference Only

**Major Hardware and Process Path Through a Wave Soldering Machine**

Do **not** place this overall title or the figure number inside the artwork. The public figure number and caption belong only in the Section 4.18 Markdown/HTML block.

---

## Instructional Purpose

Show the machine as a physical manufacturing line rather than as a list of unrelated equipment names.

The figure should allow a student to answer:

> **Where is the PCB as it moves through a wave-soldering machine, and what does each major module do to it before, during, and after contact with the solder wave?**

---

## Central Teaching Message

> **Wave soldering is a controlled sequence: flux -> preheat -> wave contact -> drain/separate -> cool. The conveyor carries and supports the PCB through each stage, while the solder pot, pump, and nozzle create the standing wave.**

## Overall Layout

Use one continuous **left-to-right side-view machine schematic**.

Recommended process regions:

1. **Flux**
2. **Preheat**
3. **Wave contact**
4. **Drain / separate**
5. **Cool / exit**

The PCB should be shown moving on a conveyor or conveyor rails across all five regions. Use a clear board-travel arrow that does not cross unrelated labels.

The wave-contact region should expose enough of the solder pot/pump/nozzle to make the standing wave physically understandable. The **pump-to-nozzle flow path must be visually explicit**, because the instructional goal is to distinguish a pumped standing wave from a passive solder bath.

A small optional pallet/support cue may be attached to the board or shown in a compact inset/callout. It must remain secondary.

Optional nitrogen awareness may be shown only as a restrained dashed enclosure/hood around the wave region labeled `Optional nitrogen`. It must not become a major subsystem panel.

- solder pot;

- standing molten-solder wave contacting the board underside;

## Required Technical Relationships

1. The board must move **flux -> preheat -> wave contact -> drain/separate -> cool**.
2. Flux is applied before preheat.
3. Preheat occurs before molten-solder contact.
4. The **solder side / underside** of the board contacts the wave; do not show the whole PCB submerged in the solder pot.
5. The solder pot is a reservoir beneath the board.
6. The pump must be visually connected to the nozzle/wave system so students understand that the standing wave is actively pumped, not a passive pool.
7. The nozzle/wave must contact only the required underside region.
8. The conveyor should continue through the contact and exit region, preferably at a modest incline, but no universal angle value should be embedded in the artwork.
9. The drain/separate region must occur **after** wave contact and **before** cooling.
10. The pallet/support, if shown, must not block the intended solder-access opening.
11. Optional nitrogen, if shown, must be visually secondary and must not imply that nitrogen is required for all wave soldering.
12. The figure must not imply that the machine setpoint alone guarantees correct product temperature or solder-joint quality.

## Spatial / Geometry Requirements

- Use one continuous conveyor path across the figure.
- Maintain clear left-to-right separation between the five process regions.
- Show the solder pot below the wave-contact region, not as a floating box disconnected from the wave.
- Show the pump inside or adjacent to the solder pot with a clear flow path to the nozzle.
- Show the standing wave rising from the nozzle toward the board underside.
- Keep the board clearly above the pot except at the intentional wave-contact interface.
- Keep the drainage/separation region visually open so excess solder returning to the wave can later be illustrated without crowding.
- Use approximately **5-7% outer margin**.
- Keep labels outside major equipment where possible; use short leaders rather than placing text on top of hardware.
- Do not route leaders through the PCB, solder wave, or other labels.
- The board-travel arrow should remain visually dominant but not oversized.
- If a pallet/support is included, show it as a simple support/masking element rather than a detailed fixture design.

## Color / Style Guidance

Follow the established EPAC Chapter 4 visual language:

- explicit white background;
- clean, simplified engineering-textbook vector style;
- restrained green/teal PCB where applicable;
- muted copper/gold/tan for pads, plated barrels, and solder-pot/nozzle features where useful;
- neutral grays for machine structure, conveyors, fixtures, and component bodies;
- muted metallic gray for solder;
- dark navy/charcoal editable sans-serif labels;
- restrained accent colors that do not carry meaning alone;
- consistent line weights, balanced spacing, and generous white space;
- no photorealistic factory scene, decorative gradients, logos, watermarks, vendor styling, or poster-like ornament;
- no embedded public figure number, final caption, or oversized duplicate overall title.

The figure must remain understandable in grayscale through geometry, labels, line style, arrows, and grouping rather than color alone.

For this figure specifically:

- molten solder should be muted metallic gray/silver, not bright blue or orange;
- any flow arrow inside the solder pot should be restrained and clearly subordinate to the board-travel arrow;
- heat may be suggested with a restrained warm tint or simple upward heat marks in the preheat region, but avoid decorative flames;
- flux spray/foam should be schematic and subtle;
- machine frames and guards should be simple enough that the equipment path remains immediately readable.

## Technical-Verification Basis

**Approved Section 4.18 outline:** requires recognition of the fluxer, preheat, solder pot, pump/wave, conveyor, pallet/fixture awareness, and cooling/exit, and defines the teaching sequence as `flux -> preheat -> wave contact -> drain/separate -> cool`.

**Current Section 4.18:** explicitly identifies the same hardware and sequence, adds the optional pallet/support role, and treats nitrogen as optional process-window support rather than a required stage.

**Primary MET406 instructional material:** identifies fluxer, preheat zone, solder pot, pump, wave nozzle, conveyor, optional nitrogen hood, cooling, and the same left-to-right wave-soldering process.

**Approved reference hierarchy already used for Section 4.18:** Coombs and current industry guidance support the conveyor -> flux -> preheat -> pumped standing wave -> exit/cooling architecture. Use these sources for technical verification only; do not copy their machine drawings.
