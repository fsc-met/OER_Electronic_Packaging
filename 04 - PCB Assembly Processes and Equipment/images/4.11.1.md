# Figure 4.11.1 Suggested Content - Simplified Anatomy and Motion of a Pick-and-Place Machine

## Status

**Suggested-content specification - Rev1 QA-reviewed candidate**

This file defines the intended instructional content and figure-generation constraints for Figure 4.11.1 in Section 4.11, **Pick-and-Place Machine - How It Works**.

The figure has one instructional role: provide a machine-level visual map of the major physical subsystems and the X-Y-Z-$\theta$ motions that work together during SMT placement.

Do **not** call this specification or generated artwork canonical/final before explicit project-owner acceptance.

## Figure Title for Authoring / Reference Only

## Instructional Purpose

Help first-time Engineering Technology readers recognize the main functional regions of a modern SMT placement machine before they follow a complete placement cycle.

The figure should answer:

> **What are the major physical subsystems inside a placement machine, and how do X, Y, Z, and $\theta$ motion relate to them?**

Students should be able to identify:

- board conveyor/support and the held PCB;
- feeder bank / component presentation area;
- placement head;
- vacuum nozzle;
- X-Y motion system;
- Z pickup/placement motion;
- $\theta$ component rotation;
- board-recognition camera / fiducial recognition;
- component-recognition camera;
- the fact that the controller coordinates these functions even though proprietary control architecture is not shown.

---

## Central Teaching Message

> **A pick-and-place machine coordinates a stable PCB, known component sources, vision, vacuum pickup, and controlled X-Y-Z-$\theta$ motion so a picked component can be placed at the intended board location and orientation.**

The mechanical layout may differ among manufacturers, but the **functions** are common.

## Overall Layout

Use one **simplified open-machine cutaway / plan-oblique schematic**, not a photorealistic machine enclosure.

Recommended composition:

1. PCB/conveyor region near the lower-center portion;
2. feeder bank along one side;
3. horizontal X-Y beam/gantry or equivalent simplified motion structure above the board;
4. placement head suspended from the motion structure;
5. nozzle extending downward from the head;
6. board-recognition camera shown viewing a PCB fiducial;
7. component-recognition camera shown in one **representative** arrangement, preferably a fixed upward-looking example labeled `example arrangement - layouts vary`;
8. X and Y arrows in the horizontal plane;
9. Z arrow beside the nozzle;
10. curved $\theta$ arrow around the nozzle/component axis.

Use short labels close to their intended objects. Avoid a dense callout forest.

## Required Technical Relationships

1. The PCB must be shown supported/held in the placement region; it must not appear free to slide while components are being placed.
2. Feeders present components at a physical source separate from the PCB.
3. The placement head/nozzle must plausibly travel between component source, recognition location, and PCB placement location.
4. X and Y are orthogonal horizontal motions in the placement plane.
5. Z is vertical pickup/placement motion at the nozzle/head.
6. $\theta$ is rotation about the nozzle/component axis.
7. The board-recognition camera must be associated with the PCB/fiducial, not with component pickup.
8. The component-recognition camera must be associated with the picked component/nozzle.
9. The component-camera arrangement shown must be explicitly presented as one representative architecture rather than the only possible orientation/location.
10. Vacuum should be represented as holding the component through the nozzle; do not show vacuum as a downward force.
11. The figure must not imply that component recognition proves exact manufacturer part number or electrical value.
12. The machine should appear to place relative to the actual held PCB, not an abstract floating coordinate system.
13. Do not include detailed fiducial correction geometry or coordinate-transform mathematics.

---

## Spatial / Geometry Requirements

- Keep the PCB/conveyor region visually central enough to serve as the reference workspace.
- Separate the feeder bank from the PCB enough that source and destination are immediately clear.
- X and Y arrows must be orthogonal and visibly lie in the horizontal placement plane.
- Z arrow must be vertical and close to the placement head/nozzle.
- $\theta$ arrow must visibly wrap around the nozzle/component axis.
- The board-camera sight line must terminate on a fiducial mark.
- The component-camera sight line must terminate on the picked component or nozzle-held component.
- Board-camera and component-camera sight lines must be visually distinct and impossible to confuse.
- Do not route leaders through the PCB, feeder bank, or unrelated text.
- Keep the component-recognition camera away from the PCB so students do not confuse its function with board alignment.
- Reserve a clear no-label corridor around the X/Y motion arrows.
- Maintain about **6-8% outer canvas margin** and comfortable internal spacing.
- If gantry, camera sight lines, and labels compete for space, expand the canvas before reducing text size.

Follow the established EPAC Chapter 4 visual language:

- simple 2D or restrained oblique geometry;
- no photorealistic machine enclosure;
- no glossy rendering;
- no decorative factory background;

Suggested coding:

- machine frame/gantry: muted blue-gray;
- PCB: muted green;
- conveyor/support: neutral gray;
- feeder bank: muted blue or gray;

- board-recognition cue: restrained teal;

- X/Y/Z/$\theta$ arrows: dark blue with concise labels.

## Originality / Copyright Requirements

Create an **original functional machine schematic**.

Do not:

- trace a placement-machine brochure or product photograph;
- copy a manufacturer cutaway;
- reproduce a textbook machine diagram;
- preserve a source's distinctive camera/gantry arrangement and merely change colors;
- use branded equipment, logos, proprietary UI, or recognizable industrial enclosure styling.

Use generic engineering geometry derived from the physical relationships.

---

## Generation Guidance

Preferred method: **controlled vector / SVG-first**.

Recommended workflow:

1. establish PCB/conveyor and feeder-bank locations;
2. add simplified motion structure and placement head;
3. add X/Y/Z/$\theta$ motion cues;
4. add board-recognition and component-recognition functions;
5. verify every sight line and arrow termination;
6. render at 900 px;
7. freeze the strongest visual baseline;
8. make only targeted corrections to labels, spacing, arrow routing, or geometry.

If AI-generated raster imagery is used for a concept, treat it only as a concept source. Rebuild exact machine relationships, labels, arrows, and camera sight lines as controlled vector geometry when needed.

## Editable SVG Requirements

The final candidate should include a true editable SVG that:

- uses vector geometry as the primary artwork;
- keeps PCB, conveyor, feeders, gantry, placement head, nozzle, cameras, arrows, leaders, and text as separate editable objects where practical;
- defines explicit `width`, `height`, and `viewBox`;
- uses `preserveAspectRatio="xMidYMid meet"`;
- includes a full-canvas white rectangle as the first visible drawing element;
- keeps text editable;
- uses `markerUnits="userSpaceOnUse"` for ordinary engineering arrows;
- preserves safe outer margins;
- avoids clipped callouts;
- does not rely on external fonts or raster assets for the technical content.

Render-check at native size and approximately **1600 px, 1200 px, and 900 px** width. A successful render alone is not enough; the rendered layouts must be visually inspected.
