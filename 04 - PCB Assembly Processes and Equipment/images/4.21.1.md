# Figure 4.21.1 Suggested Content - Common PCB Depanelization Methods and Board-Stress Control

## Status

**Suggested-content specification - Rev1 QA-reviewed candidate**

This file defines the instructional content and generation constraints for Figure 4.21.1 in Section 4.21, **Depanelization and Secondary Assembly**.

The figure has one instructional role: **help the reader recognize the three depanelization geometries required by the section and connect each method to the need for controlled PCB support and limited board bending**.

## Figure Title for Authoring / Reference Only

**Common PCB Depanelization Methods and Board-Stress Control**

Do **not** place this overall title or figure number inside the artwork.

---

## Instructional Purpose

Show, in one compact textbook figure, how a populated PCB can be separated from a panel by:

1. a **V-score / V-groove**;
2. **routed breakout tabs with mouse-bite holes**;
3. **router depanelization**;

and then reinforce one practical process rule:

> **Support the populated assembly near the intended separation region and avoid unnecessary bending or twisting.**

The figure should allow a beginning reader to recognize the physical geometry before reading a detailed manufacturing drawing or work instruction.

---

## Central Teaching Message

> **Different depanelization methods create different separation paths, but all require control of board strain after components and solder joints are already present.**

The figure should make clear that manual break-off is only appropriate where the panel was intentionally designed for it and where the approved process controls board flex.

## Overall Layout

Preferred organization: **three primary method panels across the top or main body**, plus **one narrow stress-control inset spanning the lower area**.

### Panel A - V-score / V-groove

Show:

- two adjacent PCB units still connected in a panel;
- a simplified board cross-section at the separation line;
- shallow V-grooves from the top and bottom surfaces;
- a thin **remaining web** of laminate;
- a straight intended separation line.

### Panel B - Routed tabs / mouse bites

Show:

- irregular or curved board outline inside a panel;
- routed clearance around most of the outline;
- one or two retained **breakout tabs**;
- one tab containing a row of **mouse-bite holes**;
- the tab clearly identified as the designed remaining connection.

### Panel C - Router depanelization

Show:

- populated board/panel held by support/fixture features;
- a small rotating router cutter at a tab or programmed outline location;
- a concise motion/path arrow;
- support close enough to the cut region to make low-strain separation visually clear.

### Lower inset - Board-stress control

Use a simple **preferred versus poor handling** comparison:

- preferred: board supported close to the score/tab/cut while separation occurs;
- poor: populated board visibly bent or twisted over a large unsupported span.

Use a restrained check/X or `Preferred` / `Avoid` cue so the message does not depend on color alone.

## Required Technical Relationships

1. A V-score must show **two opposed grooves with laminate remaining between them**; do not depict the score as a complete cut.
2. V-score geometry should imply a straight separation boundary.
3. Routed-tab geometry must show that most of the board perimeter has already been routed away and that the **tab is the remaining connection**.
4. Mouse-bite holes must lie **inside the retained tab region**, not randomly along the finished board edge.
5. The router cutter must remove the retaining material by a localized mechanical cutting action while the assembly is supported.
6. Router depanelization should be presented as **controlled / low-strain**, not as literally force-free or stress-free.
7. The stress inset must show that reducing the unsupported lever arm near the separation line reduces gross board bending.
8. The `Avoid` case must show board flex/twist as the undesirable action, not merely a different hand position.
9. No method should be presented as universally superior; the figure teaches recognition and strain control.
10. Manual break-off must not appear to be acceptable for arbitrary PCB edges; it must be tied to a designed V-score or breakout-tab feature.
11. The figure must not imply that components may safely contact the cutter or fixture.
12. Do not depict the board as being depanelized before assembly; the section context is post-solder separation of a populated PCBA/panel.

## Spatial / Geometry Requirements

- Keep three main method panels visually balanced and aligned.
- Use consistent PCB thickness and basic visual language across panels.
- In the V-score cross-section, leave enough vertical space that both grooves and the central web are unmistakable at 900 px.
- In the routed-tab panel, keep the routed gap wide enough to distinguish it from the tab.
- Mouse-bite holes should be small, evenly spaced, and contained in the tab.
- The router cutter should be offset from components and should terminate clearly at the intended cut line/tab.
- The support/fixture should sit beneath or immediately adjacent to the cutting/separation region, not far away where the board would act as a long cantilever.
- The `Avoid` bending example should exaggerate curvature only enough to communicate the risk; do not depict catastrophic fracture.
- Keep leader lines short and direct.
- No leader should cross another label or unrelated object.
- Use approximately **5-7% outer margin** and comfortable internal padding in every panel.
