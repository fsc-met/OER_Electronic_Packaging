# Figure 4.20.2 Suggested Content - First-Side Component Retention During Second-Side Reflow

## Status

**Suggested-content specification - Rev1 QA-reviewed candidate**

This file defines the instructional content and generation constraints for Figure 4.20.2 in Section 4.20, **Planning Mixed SMT/THT and Double-Sided Assembly**.

The figure has one instructional role: **show why previously soldered first-side SMT components can remain attached during the second reflow pass even though their solder joints may become molten again, and why heavier components need more retention margin/process consideration**.

## Figure Title for Authoring / Reference Only

**First-Side Component Retention During Second-Side Reflow**

Do **not** place this overall title or figure number inside the artwork.

---

## Instructional Purpose

Correct the common beginner assumption that the first-side joints must stay solid during second-side reflow.

The figure should allow a reader to answer:

> **What happens to the first-side joints during the second reflow pass, what pulls the first-side component downward, and what physical mechanism can keep many smaller components attached?**

---

## Central Teaching Message

> **During a normal same-alloy second reflow pass, first-side solder joints can re-melt. Gravity acts on the component, while the geometry and surface tension of the molten solder joints can retain many smaller components. Heavier or poorly supported components have less retention margin and may require different side assignment, adhesive, support, or another process strategy.**

## Overall Layout

Preferred layout: **three-step horizontal sequence**.

### Panel A - First-side reflow

Show:

- simplified PCB cross-section;
- one or two representative SMT components on Side 1, above the board for the first pass;
- solder paste/joints shown after first-side reflow as solidified joints;
- label `Side 1 reflow complete`.

### Panel B - Flip and second-side placement

Show:

- the board rotated so the first-side components now hang underneath;
- new Side 2 components placed on the upper side;
- concise `Flip board` rotation cue;
- label `Side 1 components now underneath`.

### Panel C - Second reflow: molten first-side joints

Show the key physical state:

- first-side solder joints visibly molten/rounded again;
- a downward arrow labeled `Gravity` acting through the component body;
- clear meniscus/termination cues labeled `Molten-solder surface tension supports retention`;
- one **smaller/lighter representative component** remaining aligned under the board;
- one **larger/heavier representative component** shown in a separate small comparison zone or adjacent schematic with the annotation `Less retention margin - evaluate side assignment / adhesive / support / alternate process`.

Do not show the heavier component falling off as if failure is inevitable. The message is reduced margin and need for planning, not a universal failure threshold.

## Required Technical Relationships

1. The first-side joints must be shown **solidified after the first reflow** and **molten again during the second reflow** for the same-alloy conceptual case.
2. The figure must not teach `never re-melt the first-side solder`. Section 4.20 explicitly corrects that oversimplification.
3. The board flip must place the first-side components underneath before the second pass.
4. Gravity must point downward from the first-side component body.
5. Surface-tension retention must be represented at the molten solder/termination interfaces, not as an unrelated upward force applied to the component center.
6. The figure should use a qualitative meniscus/retention cue rather than an exact vector-sum force diagram.
7. The smaller/lighter component should remain attached and aligned in the representative successful case.
8. The larger/heavier example should show **reduced retention margin**, not an inevitable drop or a fixed mass threshold.
9. Do not provide a universal component-mass limit, force equation, solder-surface-tension number, or package-specific limit.
10. Adhesive/support must be shown, if at all, as a **possible validated process choice**, not as mandatory for every double-sided SMT board.
11. The figure must not imply that a higher-melting first-side alloy is the default solution.
12. The figure must not imply that all double-sided SMT boards use the same side assignment; actual side order remains product/process dependent.
13. No thermal-profile temperature/time values should be embedded; Sections 4.14 and 4.15 own the qualified reflow-process values and profiling method.
14. Do not make the final artwork a reliability/failure-analysis figure. Cumulative thermal effects are mentioned in the section text but are not this figure's visual role.

## Spatial / Geometry Requirements

- Use the same PCB thickness, pad positions, and component geometry when a given component is repeated across the sequence.
- The board-flip cue should show a clear 180-degree rotation concept without making the reader infer the new side orientation.
- In Panel C, keep the first-side component physically below the PCB and the second-side component above it.
- Show molten solder as rounded meniscus/fillet geometry at the underside terminations; do not draw detached solder droplets.
- Place the gravity arrow through or adjacent to the component body and point straight downward.
- Place surface-tension labels/leaders at the solder/termination menisci so the mechanism is visually tied to the joint.
- Keep the smaller/lighter and larger/heavier examples far enough apart that their labels cannot be confused.
- If a warning/attention symbol is used for the heavier component, pair it with text; do not rely on color alone.
- Maintain approximately **5-7% outer margin**.
- Avoid force arrows crossing labels, the PCB, or component bodies unnecessarily.
