## Instructional Purpose

Give a first-time Engineering Technology reader a clear handling/control picture for solder paste as a **time- and temperature-sensitive process material**.

The figure should visually reinforce that solder paste should move through a controlled material path such as:

1. supplier-specified storage;
2. sealed equilibration/conditioning before opening when required;
3. product-specific preparation;
4. controlled production use with time/status awareness;
5. hold-and-verify response if the material status becomes abnormal or uncertain.

Its main role is to teach **control logic**, not universal numeric storage rules.

---

## Central Teaching Message

> **Solder paste should move through a controlled material path from storage to production use. Product-specific instructions and verified material status govern the path, while expired, contaminated, dried, separated, or otherwise uncertain material should be held and verified rather than guessed into use.**

Supporting ideas:

- refrigerated storage is common for many products, but it is not universal;
- conditioning is commonly performed with the package still sealed when required;
- multiple clocks exist (shelf life, stencil life, open/working time, tack life), and their meanings are product-specific;
- abnormal material condition requires hold/verify, not improvisation.

## Overall Layout

Use a left-to-right or upper-left-to-lower-right **controlled process flow** with a clearly distinguished alternative branch.

Recommended main sequence:

1. **Supplier-specified storage**
2. **Sealed conditioning / equilibration before opening when required**
3. **Product-specific preparation**
4. **Controlled production use and time/status tracking**

Recommended alternative branch:

- **Abnormal or unknown material status -> HOLD / VERIFY / REPLACE according to controlled procedure**

Use a decision point or clearly labeled side branch so that the hold/verify path is visually recognized as an **alternative response**, not as another mandatory sequential process step.

A recommended structure is:

- top or left: storage icon/box/container;
- next: sealed package reaching use temperature;
- next: preparation step such as gentle product-specific preparation / ready for printer;
- next: controlled production use;
- branch off from a status check to a caution box for abnormal material.

A small side note may identify examples of abnormal status:

- expired;
- unknown time-temperature history;
- contamination;
- excessive separation;
- drying / crusting.

A small note may also say:

**Use product label, TDS, and local procedure. Do not memorize one universal rule.**

## Required Objects and Labels

### Required Main-Flow Labels

The following step labels or equivalent concise wording are required:

- **Supplier-specified storage**
- **Sealed conditioning / equilibration before opening when required**
- **Product-specific preparation**
- **Controlled use / time tracking**

### Required Alternative-Branch Label

A clearly visible alternative outcome is required, such as:

- **Hold and verify material status**
- **Replace / do not use if status cannot be verified**

### Required Supporting Labels

At least some of the following concise cues should appear:

- **label / TDS / procedure**
- **shelf life**
- **stencil life / open time / tack life** (can be grouped as examples of product-specific clocks)
- **do not open cold paste early** or equivalent short reminder
- **do not guess** or equivalent short caution

Do not fill the figure with many numerical times or temperatures.

---

## Required Technical Relationships

The final artwork must satisfy all of the following:

1. The figure shows a **controlled material path** from storage to use.
2. Conditioning/equilibration, when shown, occurs **before opening when required**.
3. The figure shows that multiple product-specific clocks/limits exist and that they are **not one universal rule**.
4. A clear alternative branch exists for abnormal or uncertain material status.
5. The figure must not imply that all solder paste requires the same refrigerated range, shelf life, conditioning time, or stencil life.
6. The figure must not imply that separated/dried/contaminated/unknown-status paste should simply be mixed and used.
7. The figure must not imply that the hold/verify branch is just another routine step.

## Spatial / Geometry Requirements

- The main flow path should be visually dominant and easy to follow.
- The alternative hold/verify branch must be clearly separated from the main path.
- If a decision diamond is used, the `yes` and `no` (or equivalent) paths must be unambiguous.
- Maintain at least **6–8% outer canvas margin**.
- Avoid crossing arrows if possible.
- Keep step boxes large enough for comfortable 900 px readability.
- Any icons (container, refrigerator, clock, label, caution symbol) should be simple and subordinate to the instructional text.
