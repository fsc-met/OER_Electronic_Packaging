Do **not** place this overall title or figure number inside the artwork.

---

## Instructional Purpose

Help a beginning reader distinguish a **press-fit connection** from a soldered THT joint and understand the physical installation sequence:

> **align the connector -> support the PCB -> press with suitable tooling -> compliant pin section deforms inside the compatible PTH -> connector seats**

The figure should make the load path visible so the reader understands that the press force should be carried through the intended connector/pin region into local board support rather than by bending the whole populated PCB.

---

## Central Teaching Message

> **Press-fit is a controlled mechanical insertion process, not a generic force-fit operation. The connector, compliant pin, plated through-hole, press tooling, and PCB support must work as one matched system.**

No solder should appear in the connection.

## Overall Layout

Preferred layout: **three sequential cross-sectional panels** using the same connector/PTH geometry.

### Step 1 - Align before pressing

Show:

- simplified connector body above the PCB;
- several compliant press-fit pins aligned with compatible PTHs;
- press tool above the connector, not yet applying significant load;
- local support plate/fixture directly beneath the insertion area;
- an alignment cue showing the pins centered over holes.

### Step 2 - Controlled insertion

Show:

- downward press-force arrow from the press tool;
- pins entering the PTHs;
- compliant sections elastically deforming/compressing inside the plated barrels;
- support directly beneath the board;
- minimal board deflection.

### Step 3 - Fully seated connection

Show:

- connector seated to the intended position;
- compliant sections engaged inside the PTHs;
- support still visible or noted as having carried insertion load;
- **no solder fillet** and no solder-wave/reflow imagery.

Optional small side note:

- `Force/distance limits are product specific`

Do not turn the figure into a force-trace chart.

## Required Technical Relationships

1. The pin must be centered over a compatible PTH before significant press load is applied.
2. The support fixture must be directly below or very close to the insertion region.
3. The press tool must act on an appropriate connector/terminal seating surface and remain aligned with the support.
4. The compliant section must visibly deform or compress **inside the plated through-hole**, not outside it.
5. The PTH wall must remain intact; do not depict gouging, tearing, or crushing as the normal process.
6. The connector body must remain approximately level during insertion.
7. The PCB should remain essentially flat in the correct process depiction; do not show large board bowing.
8. The final connection must contain **no solder**.
9. Do not imply that any pin can be pressed into any drilled hole; use `compatible PTH` terminology.
10. Do not embed universal insertion force, travel, hole diameter, or plating numbers.
11. Do not depict hammering, plier squeezing, or an improvised fixture as acceptable.
12. The figure should communicate recognition/basic process understanding, not detailed connector qualification or force-signature acceptance.

## Spatial / Geometry Requirements

- Use the same pin/PTH geometry in all three panels to avoid conceptual drift.
- Keep the support plate aligned vertically with the press tool and connector footprint.
- Ensure the downward force arrow terminates at the press tool/connector load path, not at unrelated PCB areas.
- The PTH barrel should be large enough at 900 px to show the compliant section changing shape.
- Make the compliant section visibly wider or flexed after insertion without exaggerating it into permanent destructive collapse.
- Keep enough clearance between adjacent pins so each hole remains distinguishable.
- In the seated panel, show the connector resting at a clear installed height without depicting excessive compression into the PCB.
- Use approximately **5-7% outer margin**.
- Keep labels outside the cross-section where possible, using short leaders that do not cross the pin geometry.
