## Instructional Purpose

Give a first-time Engineering Technology reader a clear visual model of what solder paste physically is **before printing and reflow**.

The figure should help the reader recognize that solder paste is not liquid solder. It is an engineered composite process material made of:

1. microscopic **solder-alloy powder**; and
2. a continuous **flux vehicle** that carries the powder and controls the material's processing behavior.

The figure should visually connect this material model to the later process outcome:

- the powder becomes the metallic solder joint during reflow;
- the flux vehicle provides fluxing action and helps control rheology and tack before reflow.

Its main role is **material recognition and physical understanding**, not chemistry detail.

---

## Central Teaching Message

> **Solder paste is a suspension of microscopic solder-alloy particles in a continuous flux vehicle. The powder supplies the future joint metal, while the vehicle supplies the chemistry and flow-control behavior needed before and during reflow.**

Supporting ideas:

- the paste is a two-part engineered material, not one uniform liquid;
- the alloy powder exists as many particles, not as one solid mass;
- the vehicle is continuous around the particles;
- the paste may have a high metal **mass fraction** while still visually looking like particles dispersed in a continuous medium;
- no exact flux recipe or alloy recipe needs to be taught in this figure.

## Overall Layout

Use one main conceptual panel, preferably with two coordinated zones:

1. **main magnified material view** showing many spherical alloy particles dispersed in the surrounding flux vehicle;
2. **concise callout region** identifying the roles of the two major parts.

A recommended structure is:

- center or left-center: enlarged conceptual view of the paste body;
- callout label to a particle cluster: **Solder-alloy powder**;
- callout label to the continuous medium: **Flux vehicle**;
- a small two-item role summary, such as:
  - powder -> future solder joint metal;
  - vehicle -> oxide-removal chemistry + flow/tack control.

An optional small note may indicate:

**Many conventional stencil-printing pastes contain a high-80s to about 90% metal fraction by mass.**

If that note is included, it must also clarify that mass fraction is **not the same as visual volume fraction**.

Do not overload the figure with a process sequence. The figure's role is to explain the material itself.

## Required Objects and Labels

### Shared Required Objects

- one magnified conceptual region representing a sample of solder paste;
- many small, mostly spherical alloy particles;
- a continuous surrounding flux vehicle;
- clean leader lines or callouts.

### Required Labels

The following labels are required or strongly preferred:

- **Solder-alloy powder**
- **Flux vehicle**
- **Particles later melt and become the solder joint** (or equivalent concise wording)
- **Vehicle provides fluxing action and controls paste behavior** (or equivalent concise wording)

Optional concise subordinate labels, if space allows:

- **activators / resins / solvents / rheology modifiers**
- **temporary tack**
- **flow control**

Do **not** list a long chemistry recipe or supplier-specific trademarked ingredients.

---

## Required Technical Relationships

The final artwork must satisfy all of the following:

1. The solder-alloy powder is shown as **many separate particles**, not one continuous metallic blob.
2. The flux vehicle is shown as a **continuous medium** surrounding the particles.
3. The figure clearly distinguishes the **future metallic role** of the powder from the **pre-reflow chemical/rheological role** of the vehicle.
4. The figure must not imply that the particles are already molten.
5. If a mass-fraction note is included, it must not be rendered in a way that falsely suggests the paste is almost entirely solid by visible volume.
6. The figure must not imply that the paste is simply "glue" or simply "liquid solder."

---

## Spatial / Geometry Requirements

- Keep enough particle count to make "powder dispersed in vehicle" immediately obvious.
- Vary particle sizes slightly within a reasonable narrow range so the view looks like a particle distribution rather than perfectly cloned circles.
- Avoid making the particles so small that they disappear at 900 px.
- Maintain at least **6–8% outer canvas margin**.
- Keep leader lines short and clean; do not cross leader lines if avoidable.
- Preserve enough white space that the material view and callouts remain legible.

## Scope Boundaries

The figure must **not** teach:

- detailed flux chemistry formulations;
- solder alloy phase diagrams;
- exact alloy-composition percentages such as SAC305 elemental composition;
- oxidation or wetting physics in detail;
- solder-paste storage rules;
- stencil-printing setup;
- tack-life, shelf-life, or process timing;
- reflow-profile development;
- specific commercial brand formulations.

Do not add decorative process equipment or a full line flow.
