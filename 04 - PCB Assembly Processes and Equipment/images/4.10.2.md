## Instructional Purpose

Give a first-time Engineering Technology reader a physically correct visual explanation of what a tape feeder does:

- accepts carrier tape from a reel;
- advances the tape by the required pocket pitch;
- peels/removes the cover tape before pickup;
- exposes the next component at a known pickup location;
- allows the placement nozzle to pick that component;
- then indexes the next pocket for a later cycle.

The figure should also visually distinguish:

- **tape width, $W$** - measured across the carrier tape;
- **component pitch, $P_1$** - center-to-center distance between successive component pockets along the tape.

The figure should answer:

> **How does a tape feeder repeatedly present the next component at the pickup point?**

---

## Central Teaching Message

> **Repeatable placement starts with repeatable presentation: the feeder indexes the carrier tape by the correct pitch, removes the cover tape, and places the next pocket at a known pickup location beneath the nozzle.**

Supporting ideas:

- tape width and component pitch are different dimensions;
- indexing must move pocket centers to the pickup position, not simply move the tape an arbitrary distance;
- the component must be exposed before pickup;
- the feeder, not the reel alone, performs the presentation function;
- exact feeder mechanics and sensing vary by machine.

## Overall Layout

Use one **left-to-right feeder-path schematic** rather than multiple disconnected panels.

Recommended structure:

### Left - Supply

- generic component reel;
- carrier tape leaving the reel;
- optional simple guide roller/entry.

### Center - Indexing and cover-tape opening

- carrier tape with repeated pockets and sprocket holes;
- one clear dimension for **tape width $W$** across the tape;
- one clear dimension for **component pitch $P_1$** between adjacent pocket centers;
- simplified indexing/drive element aligned with the tape/sprocket region;
- arrow showing the tape advancing one pocket pitch;
- cover tape peeling away before the pickup location.

### Right - Pickup

- one component fully exposed in an indexed pocket;
- a clear vertical or slightly offset nozzle above that component;
- a labeled **pickup position** reference;
- next pocket(s) visible behind the pickup pocket.

The visual flow should be:

> **reel -> index / peel -> exposed pocket at pickup position -> nozzle pick**

Do not show the later board-placement operation.

## Required Technical Relationships

The final artwork must satisfy all of the following:

1. Carrier tape moves from the reel toward the pickup position.
2. Components remain seated inside pockets before pickup.
3. The cover tape is shown over upstream pockets and peeling away **before** the component reaches the pickup point.
4. Sprocket holes are located along the carrier tape edge and are used only as a generic indexing cue; do not invent proprietary drive geometry.
5. The **tape-width dimension $W$** is perpendicular to the tape travel direction.
6. The **component-pitch dimension $P_1$** is parallel to tape travel and spans center-to-center between adjacent pockets.
7. The nozzle is above the exposed component at the pickup location, not over a still-covered pocket.
8. The pickup location is a fixed/known reference in the feeder coordinate sense; the figure may acknowledge programmed/learned correction conceptually but should not show detailed software correction.
9. The index arrow should show motion by one pocket pitch for the illustrated cycle.
10. The reel itself must not appear to place the component directly onto the PCB.
11. The figure must not imply that every tape feeder uses the same motor, sensor, cover-tape route, or mechanical architecture.
12. No machine-specific acceptance tolerances or calibration values should appear.

---

## Representative Scale to Show

Use one clear instructional example consistent with Section 4.10:

- carrier tape width: **8 mm**;
- component pitch: **4 mm**.

The geometry does not need to be precisely to scale, but the labels should reinforce:

> **8 mm tape width does not mean 8 mm component pitch.**

If the numerical values create clutter, show `W = 8 mm (example)` and `P1 = 4 mm (example)` in small secondary text near the dimension labels.

Do not imply these values are universal for all 8 mm carrier tape or all components.

---

## Spatial / Geometry Requirements

- Use a straight or gently guided carrier-tape path; avoid decorative looping.
- Keep at least three pocket centers clearly visible around the pitch dimension.
- Place the width dimension on a local top-view or plan-view portion where it can be measured across the tape unambiguously.
- If combining a perspective side view with dimensions becomes confusing, use a mostly top/oblique technical view rather than photorealistic perspective.
- Keep the peel path clearly above/away from the component pockets after separation.
- The cover tape must not visually intersect the nozzle at the pickup position.
- Place the pickup-position reference directly under the nozzle centerline.
- Keep dimension arrows and flow arrows visually different in purpose but consistent in style.
- Maintain **6-8% outer margins** and adequate internal whitespace.
- Avoid leader-line crossings.

- tape-and-reel;
- feeders;
- feeder indexing;
- pickup location;
- nozzle;

### Current Section 4.10 basis

The current Rev3 section states that:

- a tape feeder accepts pocketed carrier tape from a reel;
- sprocket holes support controlled indexing;
- cover tape is removed before pickup;
- tape width $W$ and component pitch $P_1$ are different dimensions;
- an 8 mm carrier-tape example may use 2 mm or 4 mm feed pitch;
- the feeder advances the next pocket to a known pickup location;
- modern feeder details vary and may include pickup-position correction.

### Primary instructional basis

MET406 Chapter 4 - Assembly Technologies provides the teaching sequence:

> **feeder advances -> part available/present -> head aligns -> vacuum nozzle picks -> feeder indexes next pocket**

It also identifies tape feeders, cover tape/pockets, feeder sensors, and repeatable pickup position as core placement-supply concepts.

The relationships are consistent with the Section 4.10 approved verification basis, including:
