## Instructional Purpose

Give a first-time Engineering Technology reader one clear visual model of how a 3D solder paste inspection system relates a printed paste deposit to a programmed reference.

The figure should help the reader recognize:

1. **area** as the detected top-view footprint of the paste deposit;
2. **X/Y offset** as lateral displacement of the measured deposit from its programmed target;
3. **height** as paste height above the chosen local reference surface;
4. **volume** as the three-dimensional amount of paste represented by the measured deposit surface.

The figure should answer:

> **What does a 3D SPI system actually measure on one printed paste deposit?**

---

## Central Teaching Message

> **3D SPI combines top-view position/footprint information with measured surface height so the system can report area, X/Y offset, height, and volume relative to the programmed inspection reference.**

Supporting ideas:

- the programmed reference must be visible conceptually;
- the measured deposit does not have to coincide perfectly with the reference outline;
- height is measured from a chosen local reference surface;
- volume is based on the measured 3D deposit shape, not simply a top-view image;
- the figure must not imply one universal acceptance limit.

## Overall Layout

Use **two coordinated views of the same deposit**.

### Left panel - Top view

Show:

- one PCB pad or inspection region;
- a **dashed programmed target/reference outline**;
- the **measured paste footprint** as a filled irregular/rounded shape;
- a target center/reference point;
- a measured-deposit centroid or reference point;
- X and Y axes or clear orthogonal directions;
- X/Y offset arrows or one offset vector with X and Y components;
- a label identifying the measured **area**.

The measured paste footprint should be slightly shifted from the programmed target so offset is visible, but not grossly displaced.

### Right panel - Side / 3D conceptual view

Show:

- PCB/reference surface;
- paste deposit with a non-flat surface profile;
- vertical **height** dimension from the local reference surface to the deposit;
- a shaded or filled deposit body representing **volume**;
- optional small note: `volume from measured 3D surface`.

The right panel should teach geometry, not the optical hardware used to reconstruct the surface.

## Required Technical Relationships

The final artwork must satisfy all of the following:

1. Area is represented as the measured **top-view footprint**, not as PCB-pad area by default.
2. The programmed target/reference and measured deposit are visibly distinct.
3. X/Y offset represents the lateral displacement between the measured deposit and its programmed target/reference.
4. Height is measured from a clearly identified local reference surface.
5. Volume is represented by the complete three-dimensional paste body.
6. The figure must not imply that a single peak-height value multiplied by pad area is the general SPI volume algorithm.
7. The figure must not imply that the programmed reference must always equal the copper-pad outline.
8. The figure must not imply that every offset or height variation is a defect.
9. No universal pass/fail thresholds should appear.
10. The same conceptual deposit should be represented in both coordinated views.

## Spatial / Geometry Requirements

- Keep the two panels visually balanced.
- Keep the target/reference outline clearly distinguishable from the measured deposit.
- Offset arrows must terminate at meaningful reference points and not float ambiguously.
