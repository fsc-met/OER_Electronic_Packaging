# Figure 4.20.3 Suggested Content - Bounded Process Planning Case for a Mixed Double-Sided PCBA

## Status

**Suggested-content specification - Rev1 QA-reviewed candidate**

This file defines the instructional content and generation constraints for Figure 4.20.3 in Section 4.20, **Planning Mixed SMT/THT and Double-Sided Assembly**.

The figure has one instructional role: **connect a specific board's geometry and component distribution to a reasonable process route, showing why local selective soldering is a suitable starting choice for this bounded example**.

Do **not** call this specification or generated artwork canonical/final before explicit project-owner acceptance.

**Bounded Process Planning Case for a Mixed Double-Sided PCBA**

Do **not** place this overall title or figure number inside the artwork.

## Instructional Purpose

Turn the Section 4.20 planning checklist into one concrete engineering case.

The figure should allow a reader to answer:

> **How do the locations of bottom-side SMT and the two THT component groups influence side order and the decision to use selective soldering in this example?**

---

## Central Teaching Message

> **A process route should be selected from the actual board geometry. In this bounded case, small bottom-side SMT is reflowed first, larger/complex top-side SMT is reflowed second, THT parts are inserted afterward, and selective soldering locally reaches the two THT groups while the existing bottom-side SMT stays outside molten-solder contact.**

## Overall Layout

### Upper-left - Top-side board view

Show a simplified, intentionally non-proprietary PCB outline with:

- label `Top side`;
- one BGA package;
- several larger/complex SMT packages represented by QFN/SOIC-style generic shapes;
- the body of one 20-pin THT connector near a board edge;
- one 2-lead THT power component;
- concise callout: `Larger/complex SMT assigned to second reflow side`.

Do not draw traces or detailed routing.

### Upper-right - Bottom-side board view

Show the same board outline from the opposite side with:

- label `Bottom side`;
- about 18 small passive SMT components shown as a grouped field or exact set of simple chip rectangles;
- protruding pins for the 20-pin THT connector;
- two protruding leads for the THT power component;
- clear local access zones around the connector pins and power-component leads;
- simple selective-solder nozzle symbol positioned below one access zone, with a second access indication for the other group;
- concise callout: `Existing bottom SMT remains outside local solder-contact region`;
- concise callout: `Selective-nozzle access available`.

The visual should make clear that the bottom-side SMT exists **before** THT selective soldering and is not intended to be contacted by molten solder.

### Bottom strip - Selected route

Show one continuous route:

> `Bottom SMT: Print / Place / Reflow -> Flip -> Top SMT: Print / Place / Reflow -> Insert THT -> Selective solder -> Downstream verification`

Use process boxes/arrows rather than equipment drawings.

A compact reason box may state:

- `Why selective here?`
- `Two local THT groups`
- `Bottom SMT should avoid wave contact`
- `Usable nozzle access`

Keep this reason box secondary to the board geometry and selected route.

## Required Technical Relationships

1. The board must contain both SMT and THT and must visibly use SMT on both sides.
2. The bottom side must show only small passive SMT components as the representative first-side group, consistent with the bounded case.
3. The top side must show the BGA and larger/complex SMT group as the representative second-side group.
4. The 20-pin THT connector must be near a board edge, as specified in the case.
5. The 2-lead THT power component must be a separate THT group from the connector.
6. THT insertion must occur **after both SMT reflow passes** in the selected route.
7. Selective soldering must occur after THT insertion.
8. The selective-solder concept must be local: only the THT pin/lead regions receive solder contact in the figure.
9. The bottom-side SMT group must remain visually outside the local molten-solder/nozzle-contact regions.
10. The selective-nozzle access region must be physically plausible and not blocked by the connector body, bottom-side passives, or a fictional fixture wall.
11. Do not imply that selective soldering is universally better than wave soldering. It is the **reasonable starting choice for this case** because only two THT groups need soldering, bottom SMT should avoid wave contact, and access is available.
12. If the optional wave note is included, state only that wave would require suitable underside compatibility and pallet/tooling; do not add cost/throughput rankings.
13. Do not depict PiP as the selected route in the main figure. The section text lists PiP as an alternative check, not the selected initial route for this case.
14. Do not show exact inspection acceptance criteria. `Downstream verification` remains a route checkpoint only.
15. Do not include a depanelization step, secondary hardware, cleaning, coating, NPI, kitting, MES, SPC, or Chapter 5 quality/test detail.

## Spatial / Geometry Requirements

- Top and bottom board views must use the **same board outline and relative THT-hole locations** so readers can mentally connect the two sides.
- Mirror/flip the board appropriately between top and bottom views; do not accidentally move the connector or power-component holes to unrelated locations.
- Place the 20-pin connector close enough to the edge that a local nozzle-access path is visually credible.
- Keep bottom-side SMT passives away from the two selective-solder access zones so the selected route is understandable without a dense pallet/masking explanation.
- The selective-nozzle symbol should be small and schematic, positioned below the board rather than drawn like a full machine.
- Use dashed or lightly outlined access zones around the THT groups, but pair them with labels so color is not required.
- The process-route strip should be visually separated from the board views by white space or a light rule.
- Keep all route arrows left to right.
- Maintain approximately **5-7% outer margin**.
- Do not use detailed copper traces, vias, fiducials, dimensions, or DFM spacing values; this is a route-planning sketch, not a PCB-layout drawing.

## Generation Guidance

Preferred method: **controlled vector / SVG-first**.

Recommended build order:

1. create one simple board outline and fixed THT-hole locations;
2. duplicate/mirror the board for top and bottom views while preserving hole correspondence;
3. place the BGA/larger SMT group on the top side;
4. place the 18 bottom-side passives in a clean non-proprietary arrangement outside the THT access areas;
5. add the connector body/pin field near the edge and the separate 2-lead power component;
6. add two selective-solder access zones and a schematic nozzle cue;
7. verify that bottom-side passives remain outside the local contact zones;
8. add the selected route strip;
9. add no more than three short reason callouts;
10. render-check at required sizes.

Once the first technically correct board geometry is established, freeze the board outline, component-side assignments, and THT locations. Correct only specific defects.
