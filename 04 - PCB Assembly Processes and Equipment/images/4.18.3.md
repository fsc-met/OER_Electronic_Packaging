# Figure 4.18.3 Suggested Content - Common Wave Soldering Problem Appearances

## Status

**Suggested-content specification - Rev2 QA-reviewed candidate**

## Instructional Purpose

Give students a compact visual reference for the defect/problem vocabulary used in Section 4.18.

> **What does each common wave-soldering problem physically look like, before I decide what process variable or geometry to investigate?**

## Required Objects and Labels

### Panel 1 — Bridging
Show:

- two adjacent THT leads/pads;
- unintended continuous solder connection between them.

Label:

- `Bridging`

The bridge must visibly connect two conductors that should remain separate.

### Panel 2 — Insufficient hole fill
Show:

- one PTH cross-section;
- lead inside plated barrel;
- solder rising only partway through the barrel.

Label:

- `Insufficient hole fill`

Do not show or label a numeric fill percentage.

### Panel 3 — Icicle / excess solder
Show:

- bottom-side lead/pad;
- excessive retained solder;
- pointed downward or trailing projection/spike.

Label:

- `Icicle / excess solder`

The projection should be clearly different from a normal small fillet.

### Panel 4 — Poor wetting
Show:

- solder **actually contacting** the intended pad/lead region;
- solder pulled back, beaded, or failing to spread normally over the intended metal;
- exposed intended wettable area remaining visible;
- geometry that makes the poor-spread condition clear even in grayscale.

Do not depict this panel as simply `no solder present`, because that would visually collapse it into the `Skip` panel.

Label:

- `Poor wetting`

Do **not** use dull/rough surface color alone as the defect indicator.

### Panel 5 — Skip
Show:

- a short row of otherwise similar wave-soldered joints;
- one local intended joint/area with little or no solder contact while adjacent joints are soldered;
- enough board/lead context to show that the missed location belongs to the same process pass as the neighboring successful joints.

Label:

- `Skip`

The skip must be visually distinct from poor wetting: the intended location is largely **missed**, rather than contacted by solder that fails to spread.

### Panel 6 — Shadowing
Show:

- wave/board-travel direction arrow;
- one taller or obstructing component/fixture feature clearly identified as the upstream obstruction;
- a repeated weak or missed solder region **behind it relative to travel**;
- at least one unaffected joint outside the shadowed region so the geometric pattern is visually evident.

Label:

- `Shadowing`

The panel should show a pattern/context, not one isolated joint with no spatial relationship.

## Required Technical Relationships

1. Bridging is a continuous unintended solder path between adjacent conductors.
2. Insufficient hole fill is partial PTH filling; do not embed one universal acceptance threshold.
3. Icicle/excess solder includes excess retained solder with a pointed projection or spike.
4. Poor wetting must be represented by **failure to spread/bond normally on intended metal**, not simply by a dull surface.
5. Skip must look like a local area that received little/no useful solder contact.
6. Shadowing must include an upstream obstruction and a downstream/trailing weak region relative to board/wave travel.
7. None of the six panels may assign one automatic cause.
8. Do not visually imply that all bridges come from low conveyor angle, all skips from low wave height, or any similar one-to-one mapping.
9. Keep the examples representative and conceptual rather than standards acceptance images.
10. Do not include blowholes/voids or lifted pads/barrel cracks in the six-panel figure because the approved Section 4.18 outline prioritizes the six categories above.

---

## Spatial / Geometry Requirements

- Use six equal or near-equal panel frames.
- Keep panel titles in consistent positions.
- Use one common scale within pairs where comparison helps, but do not force identical geometry when a cross-section is needed.
- Keep the bridge, partial fill, spike, poor-wetting coverage, missed joint, and shadowing pattern visually large.
- Do not rely on a mathematically precise contact-angle arc; use simple spread/bead geometry that is readable to MET students without introducing wetting-angle theory.
- Do not crowd each panel with multiple arrows or causes.
- For the shadowing panel, the obstruction and trailing weak region must be clearly separated in space and aligned with the travel arrow.
- Maintain approximately **5% outer margin** and comfortable panel padding.
- Avoid microscopic details that will disappear at 900 px.
- Avoid leaders crossing neighboring panels.

**Approved Section 4.18 outline:** explicitly requires the six categories used in this figure.

**Current Section 4.18:** defines the appearance and likely investigation areas for all six categories, emphasizes that visible problems are observations rather than one-cause diagnoses, and explicitly warns that dull-looking lead-free solder alone is not evidence of poor wetting.

**Primary MET406 instructional material:** contains wave-solder examples for bridging, icicles, insufficient barrel fill, skips/non-wetting, and other related problems, plus drainage and shadowing-related THT process concepts.

**Approved reference hierarchy already used for Section 4.18:** Coombs supports the general wave-solder defect mechanisms/appearances and process dependence. Use references to verify geometry and terminology only; do not copy published defect plates or inspection photographs.
