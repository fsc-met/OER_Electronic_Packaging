## Part B - Stencil Printing and Solder Paste Inspection

### Problem 7 - Theoretical Aperture Volume

1. **0.0144 mm³**

$$
V=(0.40)(0.30)(0.12)=0.0144\ \text{mm}^3
$$

2. **0.0120 mm³**

$$
V=(0.40)(0.30)(0.10)=0.0120\ \text{mm}^3

3. **B - 0.12 mm stencil**

For the same aperture length and width, increasing stencil thickness increases theoretical aperture volume.

2. **B - No**

The supplied 0.66 value controls this exercise only. It is a common teaching/process-screening scale, not a universal acceptance rule for every stencil, paste, aperture, or qualified process.

### Problem 10 - Transfer Efficiency versus SPI Volume Percentage

1. **80% transfer efficiency**

$$
TE=\frac{0.0192}{0.024}\times100\%=80\%
$$

2. **96% of the SPI nominal target**

$$
\frac{0.0192}{0.0200}\times100\%=96\%
$$

3. **A - Yes**

The supplied SPI window is 80-120% of the program's nominal target, and 96% is within that window.

4. **F**

Transfer efficiency compares measured volume with theoretical aperture volume. An SPI program's volume percentage can use a different defined nominal target, which is why this problem intentionally produces 80% TE but 96% of the SPI nominal target.

### Problem 18 - Conveyor Speed and Residence Time

1. **0.50 min**

$$
t=\frac{450}{900}=0.50\ \text{min}
$$

2. **30 s**

$$
0.50(60)=30\ \text{s}
$$

3. **45 s**

$$
t=\frac{450}{600}=0.75\ \text{min}=45\ \text{s}
$$

4. **F**

Changing conveyor speed changes the board's travel time through the oven, so it affects residence time through all downstream physical zones, not only the peak region.
