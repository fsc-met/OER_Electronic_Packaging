## Suggested Content for Figure 1.2.2

**Instructional purpose:**  
Show that packaging decisions continue to matter throughout the entire electronic-product life cycle, from initial design through field operation and maintenance. The figure should help students see packaging as an ongoing engineering concern rather than a final enclosure or assembly step.

**Filename:** `1.2.2 - Packaging Across the Product Life Cycle.png`  
**Intended width:** 850 px in Markdown  
**Aspect ratio:** 16:9

### Objects/components to show

Create a clean left-to-right engineering workflow containing the following eight stages:

1. Design
2. Fabrication
3. Assembly
4. Inspection & test
5. Qualification
6. Production
7. Field operation
8. Maintenance / repair

Include a simple feedback path from the later life-cycle stages back toward **Design** to represent lessons learned, field feedback, corrective action, and redesign.

### Required labels

Use only these concise labels inside the artwork:

- Design
- Fabrication
- Assembly
- Inspection & test
- Qualification
- Production
- Field operation
- Maintenance / repair
- Feedback

Do not add explanatory sentences inside the image.

### Required states / values

- No numerical values are required.
- Each stage should be presented as part of one continuous product-development and support process.
- The stages should be visually equal enough that no stage appears optional by default.
- The **Feedback** path should be secondary to the main left-to-right flow.

### Arrow directions and relationships

- Main arrows should proceed left to right:
  **Design → Fabrication → Assembly → Inspection & test → Qualification → Production → Field operation → Maintenance / repair**
- Include one secondary feedback arrow from the right side of the workflow back toward **Design**.
- The feedback arrow should communicate learning and corrective action, not physical return of the product through every manufacturing step.

### Spatial relationships

- Use one primary horizontal flow across the image.
- Keep stage boxes or icons evenly spaced.
- A modest curved feedback arrow may run beneath or above the main flow.
- Avoid circular layouts that obscure the chronological product-development sequence.
- Keep the lower area visually clean for the external Markdown caption.

### Color restrictions

- White or very light neutral background.
- Restrained technical palette.
- Use consistent stage styling, with only modest color variation if needed for readability.
- The feedback arrow may use a secondary accent color.
- Avoid gradients, decorative shadows, or bright commercial-infographic styling.

### Suggested visual style

- Clean engineering workflow diagram.
- Flat 2D presentation preferred.
- Consistent line weights and box/icon sizes.
- Legible sans-serif labels.
- Minimal icons are acceptable if they clearly represent the stage and do not replace the written labels.
- No embedded overall figure title.

- Do not show a specific company's proprietary development process.
- Do not use company logos, branded equipment, or recognizable commercial products.
- Do not add cycle times, yield values, cost figures, or reliability numbers.

- Do not reproduce or closely imitate a published product-development diagram.
- Do not add a footer legend, paragraph explanation, or caption inside the artwork.
