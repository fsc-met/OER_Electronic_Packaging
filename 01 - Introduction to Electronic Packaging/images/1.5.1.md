## Suggested Content for Figure 1.5.1

**Instructional purpose:**  
Show one representative way an integrated resistor can be fabricated on a semiconductor die. The figure should help students understand that a diffused resistor is a **doped semiconductor current path with controlled resistivity and geometry**, not a tiny packaged resistor embedded in the silicon.

**Filename:**  
`1.5.1 - Example of an Integrated Diffused Resistor.png`

**Intended width:** 900 px in Markdown

**Recommended aspect ratio:** 16:9

### Representative structure

Show a clean conceptual cross-section of a **diffused resistor**.

Required elements:

- `Terminal 1`;
- `Terminal 2`;
- metal contacts at both ends;
- insulating oxide at the semiconductor surface;
- a continuous diffused/doped resistive region between the contacts;
- semiconductor substrate or well;
- a clearly indicated current path through the resistive region.

Suggested labels:

- `Terminal 1`
- `Terminal 2`
- `Metal contact`
- `Insulating oxide`
- `Diffused resistive region`
- `Current path`
- `Semiconductor substrate or well`

### Required conceptual message

The figure should visually support the statement:

`Resistance comes from the resistivity and geometry of the doped path.`

A secondary note may state:

`Longer path → larger resistance`

The current path should run **between the two metal contacts through the doped resistive region**.

### Physical interpretation

The conceptual structure should communicate that:

1. a selected semiconductor region is doped to provide a controlled resistive path;
2. the two metal contacts provide electrical access to the ends of that path;
3. the surface oxide provides insulation except at the intended contact openings;
4. current flowing through the finite-resistivity doped region produces the resistor behavior.

The figure does not need to specify whether the doped region was formed strictly by thermal diffusion. Modern processes may use diffusion, ion implantation with subsequent thermal treatment, or another process-specific doping method. The term **diffused resistor** is used here as the conventional device description.

### Important scope note

This is **one representative integrated-resistor structure**.

Do not imply that all integrated resistors are diffused resistors. Other common configurations include:

- polysilicon resistors;
- thin-film resistors;
- other process-specific resistive structures.

These additional resistor technologies may be discussed later in the book.

### Prohibited or misleading elements

- Do not show a packaged resistor buried inside the die.
- Do not show the current path passing through the oxide.
- Do not leave either metal contact electrically disconnected from the resistive region.
- Do not imply that the resistor must always use one specific dopant type.
- Do not assign universal dimensions, doping concentrations, resistivity, or resistance values.
- Do not imply that the surrounding substrate is intended to be the main resistor current path.
- Do not claim that all integrated resistors use this structure.
