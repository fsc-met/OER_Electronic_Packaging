### Part C – Electric-Field Control

Use a small conceptual comparison to explain the role of the gate.

For NMOS:

- show a gate voltage that is sufficiently positive relative to the source;
- show an electric-field indication through the dielectric;
- show an electron-rich conducting channel forming at the semiconductor surface;
- label the channel `N-type inversion channel`.

For PMOS:

- show that the gate is driven sufficiently lower than the source;
- show a hole-rich conducting channel;
- label it `P-type conducting channel`.

This portion should remain qualitative.

The primary message should be:

`Gate voltage -> electric field -> channel formation -> source-drain conduction`

### Required terminal interpretation

The figure should support the following terminology:

- **Gate:** insulated control electrode.
- **Source:** terminal from which majority carriers enter the controlled channel in the normal operating convention used here.
- **Drain:** terminal toward which majority carriers move through the channel.
- **Body:** semiconductor region in which source and drain are formed.

If space permits, include the short note:

`Source and drain can be structurally similar; their circuit roles depend on device structure and bias.`

Do not make this note visually dominant.

---

### Required technical relationships

The figure must show all of the following correctly:

- NMOS uses N+ source/drain regions in a P-type body or well.
- PMOS uses P+ source/drain regions in an N-type body or well.
- The gate is electrically separated from the semiconductor by a dielectric.
- Normal channel control occurs through the **electric field**, not through a steady current flowing through the gate dielectric.
- The conducting channel forms at the semiconductor surface beneath the gate.
- NMOS and PMOS have complementary gate-polarity behavior.
- Do not show the source and drain directly touching each other.
- Do not represent the conducting channel as a physical wire inserted into the semiconductor.

### Prohibited or misleading elements

- Do not show a continuous N-type channel in an unbiased enhancement-mode NMOS.
- Do not show a continuous P-type channel in an unbiased enhancement-mode PMOS.
- Do not show electrons normally flowing through the intact gate dielectric.
- Do not show negative gate voltage turning on the simplified enhancement-mode NMOS.
- Do not describe the conducting channel as a depletion region.
- Do not assign one universal threshold voltage.
- Do not imply that the planar cross-section represents every modern MOSFET.
- Do not show source/drain as tiny packaged components inside the silicon.
- Do not imply that MOSFETs only exist inside ICs; they may also be discrete packaged devices.
