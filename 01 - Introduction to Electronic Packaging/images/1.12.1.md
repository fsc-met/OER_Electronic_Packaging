## Suggested Content for Figure 1.12.1

**Figure title for authoring/reference only:**  
`Overall Electronic Manufacturing Pathway from Semiconductor to Finished Product`

**Instructional purpose:**  
Provide one clear visual summary of the **overall electronic manufacturing pathway** recapped at the beginning of Section 1.12.

This figure should help readers consolidate the Chapter 1 progression developed so far and preserve one of the most important distinctions in the chapter:

> **Semiconductor/component manufacturing** and **PCB fabrication** are different manufacturing streams.

Those streams remain separate until they converge during:

> **PCB assembly**

The figure should therefore answer the question:

> **How is an electronic product built from semiconductor fabrication to the finished product?**

This figure is different from the packaging-hierarchy figure. It should show a **manufacturing flow**, not a hierarchy of physical scales.

The main pathway to show is:

### Semiconductor / component stream

> **Semiconductor fabrication -> Wafer / device structures -> Bare die -> IC packaging -> Packaged components**

### PCB stream

> **PCB materials -> PCB fabrication -> Bare PCB**

### Convergence and later integration

> **Packaged components + bare PCB -> PCB assembly -> PCBA -> System integration -> Finished electronic product**

The figure may also imply that discrete components and connectors join the assembly stream along with packaged ICs, but the main structural distinction must remain clear.

**Filename:**  
`1.12.1 - Overall Electronic Manufacturing Pathway from Semiconductor to Finished Product.png`

**Intended width:** 900 px in Markdown

**Recommended aspect ratio:** 16:9 or 5:3

---

### Overall layout

Use a clean **two-stream manufacturing flow diagram**.

Recommended structure:

1. upper or left stream for **semiconductor / component path**;
2. lower or parallel stream for **PCB path**;
3. a visually obvious **convergence point at PCB assembly**;
4. a final progression from **PCBA** to **system integration** to **finished electronic product**.

The figure should visually communicate:

- two separate upstream manufacturing streams;
- one clear convergence point;
- continued downstream integration beyond the PCBA.

Do **not** make the figure look like a packaging-level hierarchy. The purpose is to show **how the product is built**, not to organize the product by physical scale.

Do **not** place the figure number, external caption, or an oversized overall title inside the artwork.

---

### Stage group 1 - Semiconductor fabrication and wafer/device structures

Show the beginning of the semiconductor stream with a wafer-focused representation.

Required progression:

- `Semiconductor fabrication`
- `Wafer / device structures`

Preferred visual cues:

- semiconductor wafer;
- repeated die pattern on the wafer;
- a subtle suggestion of microscopic device/interconnect structures.

Optional concise sublabels:

- `Integrated circuits fabricated on wafer`
- `Semiconductor devices and interconnect`

The wafer must look like a semiconductor wafer, not a PCB.

Do not show a packaged component at this stage.

---

### Stage group 2 - Bare die

Show a transition from the wafer to one separated die.

Required stage:

- `Bare die`

Optional connector label:

- `Wafer test / singulation`

Preferred visual cues:

- one small rectangular IC die;
- visible bond pads or a simple pad pattern;
- clear distinction from the wafer and from the later packaged component.

The bare die should be visibly:

- unpackaged;
- fragile-looking or minimal;
- much smaller than the later board/product stages.

---

### Stage group 3 - IC packaging and packaged components

Show the die becoming a practical board-assembly component through IC packaging.

Required progression:

- `IC packaging`
- `Packaged components`

Preferred visual cues:

- a simplified package body, such as a QFN-, QFP-, or BGA-like component;
- optional cutaway or conceptual view that suggests the die is inside;
- optional note that discrete components and connectors also become part of the assembly input.

Optional concise sublabels:

- `Die-to-package interconnection`
- `Packaged ICs and other components`

The figure must reinforce that **IC packaging** is a separate activity from **PCB fabrication**.

Do not imply that the IC package is itself a PCB.

---

### Stage group 4 - PCB materials, fabrication, and bare PCB

Show the second manufacturing stream in parallel to the component stream.

Required progression:

- `PCB materials`
- `PCB fabrication`
- `Bare PCB`

Preferred visual cues:

- laminate/copper sheet or a generic PCB-material cue;
- a fabricated board showing traces, pads, vias, and solder mask;
- a board without mounted components.

Optional concise sublabels:

- `Patterned conductors, pads, vias`
- `Board without components`

The PCB must remain clearly distinct from:

- a semiconductor wafer;
- an IC package;
- a populated PCBA.

This stream should visually remain independent until the convergence point.

---

### Stage group 5 - PCB assembly and PCBA

Show the explicit convergence of the two streams.

Required progression:

- `PCB assembly`
- `PCBA`

Required conceptual relationship:

> **Packaged components + bare PCB -> PCB assembly -> PCBA**

Preferred visual cues:

- packaged components moving onto a bare PCB;
- one populated board after assembly;
- clear evidence that the result is now a **PCBA**.

Optional concise sublabels:

- `SMT / THT assembly`
- `Assembled board`

This convergence is the most important structural relationship in the figure and must be visually obvious.

The figure must clearly distinguish:

- `PCB = bare board`
- `PCBA = assembled board`

Do not let the PCBA look like the final product.

---

### Stage group 6 - System integration and finished electronic product

Show that a PCBA is usually integrated into a larger product rather than being the end of the story.

Required progression:

- `System integration`
- `Finished electronic product`

Preferred visual cues:

- PCBA combined with enclosure, cooling, power, wiring, interfaces, or mechanical supports;
- a final generic finished product or system.

Optional concise sublabels:

- `PCBA + enclosure + power + cooling + interconnections`
- `Final integrated product`

The product representation should be generic and unbranded.

Do not use a recognizable commercial device.

---

### Required conceptual relationships

The figure must correctly communicate all of the following:

- semiconductor fabrication produces wafer/device structures and ultimately the **bare die**;
- IC packaging converts the die into a **packaged component**;
- PCB fabrication separately produces the **bare PCB**;
- the semiconductor/component stream and the PCB stream remain separate until **PCB assembly**;
- PCB assembly combines packaged components and the bare PCB to form a **PCBA**;
- the PCBA is often only an intermediate subsystem;
- system integration creates the **finished electronic product**;
- the figure is a **manufacturing pathway**, not a universal packaging-level numbering scheme.
