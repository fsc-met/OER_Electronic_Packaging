## Suggested Content for Figure 1.8.4

**Instructional purpose:**  
Show the final transition from a completed semiconductor wafer to individual bare IC dies.

The figure should help readers understand:

- how wafer-level electrical testing evaluates individual die locations;
- how test results can be represented on a wafer map;
- what scribe lanes / streets are;
- how dicing / singulation separates the wafer;
- why the resulting bare die is not yet a normal PCB-mountable packaged component.

This figure should prepare readers directly for Section 1.9 on IC packaging.

**Filename:**  
`1.8.4 - Wafer Probe and Dicing into Bare IC Dies.png`

**Intended width:** 900 px in Markdown

**Recommended aspect ratio:** 16:9

---

### Overall layout

Use a clean **three-stage left-to-right sequence**:

1. Wafer probe
2. Wafer map / die status
3. Dicing / singulation -> bare IC dies

Optional final small note:

`Next: IC packaging`

Do not show the bare dies already inside packages.

---

### Stage 1 – Wafer probe

Show:

- a finished circular wafer;
- visible repeated die grid;
- one die location highlighted;
- a simplified probe card or several fine probe needles touching that die's I/O test pads.

Required labels:

- `Wafer probe`
- `Probe contacts one die location`
- `Finished wafer`

Optional labels:

- `Test pads`
- `Probe card`

The probe system should appear temporary and external.

Do not show permanent wire bonds.

---

### Stage 2 – Wafer map

Show:

- a simplified circular wafer map;
- repeated die cells;
- a small number of cells marked differently to indicate pass / fail / not tested.

Required labels:

- `Wafer map`
- `Pass`
- `Fail`

Use symbols as well as color so the figure remains understandable in grayscale.

Recommended approach:

- check mark = pass;
- X = fail.

Optional note:

- `Selected electrical tests`

Do not imply the map proves long-term reliability.

---

### Stage 3 – Dicing / singulation

Show:

- close-up of the die grid;
- visible scribe lanes / streets between neighboring dies;
- one conceptual dicing tool path along a street;
- several separated rectangular dies after singulation.

Required labels:

- `Scribe lanes / streets`
- `Dicing / singulation`
- `Bare IC dies`

Optional tool depiction:

- small wafer saw blade;
- laser line;
- generic singulation symbol.

Keep the tool generic rather than implying one universal dicing technology.

---

### Final transition note

At the far right, add:

- `Next: IC packaging`

Optional small schematic:

`Bare die -> packaged IC`

If included, the packaged IC must be visually secondary and clearly shown as the **next stage**, not as part of wafer fabrication.

---

### Required technical relationships

The figure must correctly show that:

- wafer probe occurs while the dies are still physically part of the wafer;
- probe contacts are temporary;
- test results can be stored by die location;
- not every die necessarily passes;
- scribe lanes exist between die regions;
- singulation follows the scribe lanes / streets conceptually;
- dicing produces individual bare dies;
- bare dies remain fragile and require later packaging or controlled die-level assembly;
- wafer probe is not the same as final product qualification.

---

### Spatial relationships

- Keep wafer-level testing on the left.
- Place wafer map in the middle as a logical information representation, not a separate physical wafer-processing step.
- Place dicing and individual dies on the right.
- Use one clear arrow sequence.
- Keep the final bare dies large enough to visually distinguish them from die locations still attached to the wafer.

---

### Color restrictions

Use:

- wafer: pale gray / lavender;
- die grid: dark thin lines;
- probe needles: metallic gray;
- pass / fail symbols: high-contrast symbols plus restrained color;
- dicing path: strong neutral or muted accent;
- bare dies: muted semiconductor tones.

Do not rely on red and green alone.

---

### Suggested visual style

- clean engineering-textbook infographic;
- flat 2D or restrained isometric style;
- no photorealistic probe station;
- no manufacturer-specific tool;
- large readable labels;
- strong visual difference between attached die locations and separated dies;
- no figure number or overall title inside artwork.

---

### Prohibited or misleading elements

Do not:

- show a wafer map as a guarantee of reliability;
- show probe needles permanently bonded to the die;
- show every die passing;
- show dicing through the active circuitry instead of along streets;
- imply all products use one exact dicing technology;
- show the final die already soldered to a PCB;
- show the wafer turning directly into a packaged component without a separate packaging stage.
