## Suggested Content for Figure 1.3.1

**Instructional purpose:**  
Give readers with limited electrical background one visual reference for voltage, conventional current, electron motion, resistance, and circuit reference. The figure should establish the minimum circuit language needed before semiconductor behavior is introduced.

**Filename:** `1.3.1 - Basic Electrical Quantities in a Simple DC Circuit.png`  
**Intended width:** 850 px in Markdown  
**Aspect ratio:** 16:9

### Objects/components to show

Create one simple closed DC circuit containing:

- a generic DC voltage source or battery on the left;
- a single resistor/load on the right;
- conducting wires forming a complete loop;
- a circuit-reference/ground symbol connected to the negative node, chosen as the 0 V reference for this example;
- a clearly marked voltage difference across the source or load;
- a conventional-current arrow around the loop;
- a separate electron-motion arrow along one metallic conductor in the direction opposite conventional current.

### Required labels

Use only concise labels needed for interpretation:

- Voltage V
- Current I
- Resistance R
- Conventional current
- Electron motion in metal
- 0 V reference (chosen for this example)

Do not add explanatory paragraphs inside the image.

### Required relationships / arrows / states

- The conventional-current arrow must leave the positive terminal of the source, pass through the resistor/load, and return toward the negative terminal.
- The electron-motion arrow in the metallic conductor must point opposite the conventional-current direction.
- The 0 V reference symbol must be connected to a real circuit node, not floating.
- The circuit must be visibly closed.
- Voltage must be indicated as a difference between two nodes rather than as a quantity located at one isolated point.

### Spatial relationships

- Use a simple rectangular loop or similarly clear circuit layout.
- Keep the voltage source on one side and the resistor/load on the opposite side.
- Place the current arrow outside or along the conductor without obscuring the circuit.
- Keep the electron-motion arrow visually distinct from conventional current.
- Leave adequate whitespace for labels.

### Color restrictions

- White or very light neutral background.
- Restrained engineering-textbook palette.
- Use one accent treatment for conventional current and another for electron motion if needed.
- Do not rely on color alone; arrow direction and labels must remain clear in grayscale.

### Suggested visual style

- Clean 2D engineering schematic.
- Exact electrical symbols should be drawn as controlled vector shapes rather than left to free-form image generation.
- Consistent line weights.
- Concise sans-serif labels.
- No photorealistic battery.
- No decorative lightning bolts or marketing-style effects.
- No embedded overall title.

### Prohibited or misleading elements

- Do not show conventional current and electron motion pointing in the same direction in a metal wire.
- Do not imply that circuit ground is necessarily Earth ground.
- Do not show an open circuit while also showing steady current.
- Do not imply that voltage is a flow.
- Do not add AC waveforms, semiconductor devices, or advanced circuit elements.
