## Suggested Content for Figure 1.12.2

**Figure title for authoring/reference only:**  
`Electronic Packaging Hierarchy from Die to System`

**Instructional purpose:**  
Provide one clear visual summary of the **physical packaging hierarchy** introduced in Section 1.12 after the chapter has already followed the manufacturing pathway from semiconductor material to the finished electronic product.

The figure should help readers distinguish two related but different ideas:

> **Manufacturing pathway = how the product is built**

and

> **Packaging hierarchy = the physical scale at which the product is being analyzed**

The figure should organize the physical integration levels used in this OER without implying that one universal industry numbering scheme exists.

The main hierarchy to show is:

> **Wafer / device -> Bare die -> IC package / component -> PCB / PCBA -> Module / motherboard / subassembly -> Product / system -> Rack / cluster, where applicable**

The figure should also communicate that the **interfaces between levels** carry electrical signals, power, heat, and mechanical loads, so engineering problems often cross more than one packaging level.

**Filename:**  
`1.12.2 - Electronic Packaging Hierarchy from Die to System.png`

**Intended width:** 900 px in Markdown

**Recommended aspect ratio:** 16:9

---

### Overall layout

Use a clean **physical-scale hierarchy diagram** progressing from smaller integration scales on the left to larger integration scales on the right.

Recommended structure:

1. `Wafer / device`
2. `Bare die`
3. `IC package / component`
4. `PCB / PCBA`
5. `Module / motherboard / subassembly`
6. `Product / system`
7. `Rack / cluster` shown as **optional / where applicable**

Use one clear directional progression, but do not make the diagram look like a detailed manufacturing process flowchart. The purpose is to show **levels of physical integration**, not to repeat all manufacturing operations from Sections 1.8 through 1.11.

Do **not** place the figure number, external caption, or an oversized overall title inside the artwork.

### Level 1 - Wafer / device

Show a simple semiconductor wafer containing repeated die locations, with one small conceptual inset or highlighted region suggesting microscopic semiconductor devices and on-die interconnect.

Required label:

- `Wafer / device`

Optional concise sublabel:

- `Semiconductor devices and on-die interconnect`

The wafer should clearly look like a semiconductor wafer, not a PCB.

Do not show packaged components at this level.

---

### Level 2 - Bare die

Show one individual rectangular IC die separated from the wafer.

Required label:

- `Bare die`

Optional concise sublabel:

- `Integrated circuit on silicon`

Show small I/O pads or similar fine external connection features if useful.

The die should remain visibly:

- unpackaged;
- much smaller than the package and PCB levels;
- distinct from a PCB.

Do not draw a package body around it.

---

### Level 3 - IC package / component

Show a generic packaged IC containing or conceptually enclosing the die.

Preferred representation:

- one cutaway or simplified package such as a QFN-, QFP-, or BGA-like package;
- visible external leads, lands, or solder balls;
- optional die-to-package interconnection indication.

Required label:

- `IC package / component`

Optional concise sublabel:

- `Die + package interface`

The visual should reinforce that the package creates a practical interface between the microscopic die and the board level.

Do not show the IC package as a miniature PCB.

---

### Level 4 - PCB / PCBA

Show a clearly recognizable populated printed circuit board.

Required label:

- `PCB / PCBA`

Recommended visual content:

- PCB substrate;
- traces/pads;
- packaged ICs;
- discrete components;
- connector;
- visible solder-joint concept where practical.

Optional small distinction:

- `PCB = bare board`
- `PCBA = assembled board`

If both a bare PCB and PCBA are shown, keep the distinction extremely clear and concise. Do not allow this detail to crowd the hierarchy.

The populated board must be visibly larger than the IC package and must not look like part of the package substrate.

---

### Level 5 - Module / motherboard / subassembly

Show one larger electronic subassembly containing more than one board or module, or one motherboard with a smaller daughterboard/module attached.

Required label:

- `Module / motherboard / subassembly`

Representative structures may include:

- motherboard + daughterboard;
- multiple PCBAs connected together;
- board-to-board connector;
- small cable or backplane connection.

Use only enough detail to show that multiple electronic assemblies can be integrated at this level.

Do not imply that every product must have a motherboard or multiple PCBAs.

---

### Level 6 - Product / system

Show a generic finished electronic product or system enclosure containing the electronic subassembly.

Required label:

- `Product / system`

Representative visible or implied elements may include:

- enclosure / chassis;
- electronics;
- power;
- interconnections;
- cooling hardware where needed;
- display/control or external interface.

The figure should show that the system level integrates electronic, mechanical, thermal, and protective structures.

Do not use a recognizable commercial product or company-specific enclosure.

---

### Level 7 - Rack / cluster, where applicable

Show this as an **optional extension**, not as a mandatory final level.

Possible representation:

- equipment rack containing several systems;
- data-center-style rack concept;
- industrial cabinet containing multiple electronic units;
- cluster of interconnected systems.

Required label:

- `Rack / cluster`

Required qualifier:

- `Where applicable`

Use a visually lighter border, dashed outline, small `Optional` note, or another restrained treatment to make clear that not every product extends to this level.

Do not imply that all electronic products must be installed in a rack or cluster.

---

### Required scale relationship

The figure should visually communicate increasing physical integration scale from left to right:

`microscopic device structures -> individual die -> packaged component -> board assembly -> multi-board/module assembly -> complete product -> larger interconnected installation`

The major objects should therefore become progressively larger or more encompassing across the figure.

Exact geometric scale is not required, but the relative progression should be visually intuitive.

Do not make the wafer appear physically smaller than a single die simply to fit the layout. If necessary, use a small `not to scale` note or a zoom/highlight transition between wafer and die.

---

### Interfaces between levels

Use restrained arrows or interface markers between adjacent levels.

Recommended interface examples:

- wafer/device -> bare die: `Singulation`
- bare die -> IC package/component: `Die-to-package interface`
- IC package/component -> PCB/PCBA: `Package-to-board interface`
- PCB/PCBA -> module/subassembly: `Board-to-board / cable interface`
- module/subassembly -> product/system: `System integration`
- product/system -> rack/cluster: `System-to-system interface`

These labels are optional if they make the figure too dense. If only some are used, prioritize the **physical interface concept** over process terminology.

Do not overload the figure with the detailed manufacturing steps already covered earlier in Chapter 1.

Recommended concise labels:

- `Signals`
- `Power`

A simple continuous band or four small arrows may extend beneath the hierarchy to reinforce that these functions do not stop at one level.

Keep this secondary to the main hierarchy.

Do not add long descriptions inside the figure.

---

### Required conceptual relationships

The figure must correctly communicate all of the following:

- the hierarchy organizes **physical integration levels**;
- the semiconductor wafer/device level is different from the bare-die level;
- the bare die is different from the packaged IC;
- the IC package/component is different from the PCB/PCBA;
- a PCB/PCBA can be integrated into a larger module or subassembly;
- a module/subassembly can be integrated into a complete product/system;
- rack/cluster integration is application dependent;
- the levels are connected by physical interfaces;
- electrical signals, power, heat, and mechanical loads can cross several levels;
- the hierarchy is an organizational model rather than one universal numbered standard;
- modern packaging technologies may blur boundaries between levels even though the hierarchy remains useful.

---

### Important manufacturing distinction

The figure must remain consistent with the manufacturing pathway established earlier in Chapter 1:

#### Semiconductor/component stream

`Semiconductor fabrication -> IC die -> IC packaging -> packaged component`

#### PCB stream

`PCB materials -> PCB fabrication -> bare PCB`

#### Assembly convergence

`Packaged/discrete components + bare PCB -> PCB assembly -> PCBA`

The hierarchy figure should **not** redraw these as one misleading manufacturing stream.

In particular, do not imply:

- semiconductor fabrication produces a PCB;
- an IC die becomes a PCB;
- an IC package is a PCB;
- PCB fabrication is part of IC packaging;
- a PCBA is automatically the complete finished product.
