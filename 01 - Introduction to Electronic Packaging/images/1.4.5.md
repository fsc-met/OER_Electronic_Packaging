## Suggested Content for Figure 1.4.5

**Instructional purpose:**  
Show, at a beginner-friendly applied level, how an enhancement-mode NMOS changes from an OFF condition to an ON condition. The figure should build directly on the MOSFET structure introduced in Figure 1.4.3 and clearly distinguish:

1. the gate-to-source voltage that **controls channel formation**;
2. the drain-to-source voltage that **drives current through the channel**.

**Filename:**  
`1.4.5 - How an NMOS Transistor Acts as a Voltage-Controlled Switch.png`

**Intended width:** 900 px in Markdown

**Recommended aspect ratio:** 16:9

### Overall layout

Use two matched side-by-side NMOS cross-sections:

1. **OFF**
2. **ON**

The two structures should use identical physical geometry so readers can easily see what changes when the gate is driven.

A small circuit-level switch analogy may appear beneath the two structures if space permits.

Do not embed an overall figure title or figure number inside the artwork.

---

### Required Physical Structure in Both Panels

Show:

- P-type body or well;
- N+ source on the left;
- N+ drain on the right;
- gate electrode;
- gate dielectric;
- semiconductor surface;
- source terminal `S`;
- drain terminal `D`;
- gate terminal `G`.

The body terminal may be shown if it does not create unnecessary clutter.

Use the same semiconductor colors and terminology as Figure 1.4.3.

---

### OFF Panel

Show an enhancement-mode NMOS with:

- gate-to-source voltage below the required threshold condition;
- no continuous inversion channel between source and drain;
- source and drain visibly separated by the P-type semiconductor surface region;
- no large drain-source current arrow.

Required labels:

- `OFF`
- `$V_{GS}$ below threshold condition`
- `No continuous inversion channel`
- optional: `Only small leakage in a real device`

A small open-switch symbol may be included below the cross-section.

Do not label the gate as necessarily 0 V because the OFF condition depends on voltage **relative to the source**.

---

### ON Panel

Show the same NMOS geometry with:

- gate sufficiently positive relative to source;
- positive charge indication on the gate if useful;
- electric-field arrows directed from the positively biased gate toward the semiconductor;
- an electron-rich inversion channel directly beneath the gate connecting source and drain.

Required labels:

- `ON`
- `Positive $V_{GS}$`
- `Electric field`
- `N-type inversion channel`
- `Electron-rich channel`

If carrier motion is shown:

- electron motion must point **source → drain** for the operating condition used in the figure;
- conventional current must point **drain → source**.

If both directions make the figure crowded, omit both rather than risk ambiguity. The surrounding text already explains the directions.

---

### Distinguish Control Voltage from Driving Voltage

The figure should visually reinforce:

`$V_{GS}$ controls channel formation`

and

`$V_{DS}$ drives current through the channel`

A small callout or two-line note may be used:

- `$V_{GS}$ → controls the channel`
- `$V_{DS}$ → drives drain-source current`

Do not imply that the positive gate voltage itself pushes the drain current through the channel.

---

### Electric-Field Requirements

- The gate must remain physically separated from the semiconductor by the gate dielectric.
- Electric-field arrows may pass conceptually through the dielectric.
- Do **not** show electrons flowing through the gate dielectric as the normal turn-on mechanism.
- The field should act on the semiconductor surface beneath the gate.

---

### Channel Requirements

The ON-state channel must:

- appear at the semiconductor surface directly beneath the gate;
- connect the source-side and drain-side N+ regions;
- be labeled as an **inversion channel**;
- be visibly absent in the OFF panel.

Do not:

- call the channel a depletion region;
- draw the channel as a metal wire;
- show the source and drain physically touching without the gate-controlled channel.
