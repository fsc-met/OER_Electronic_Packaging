## Suggested Content for Figure 1.17.1

**Figure title for authoring/reference only:**  
`Moore's Law and Process Node Scaling`

**Instructional purpose:**  
Give readers one compact visual that connects three historical ideas:

1. representative chip transistor counts increased dramatically over time;
2. semiconductor process-node labels generally moved toward smaller values over time;
3. modern node names are **technology-generation labels**, not one literal transistor dimension.

The figure should support Section 1.17.1 without implying a strict one-to-one relationship between node name and transistor count.

**Filename:**  
`1.17.1 - Moore's Law and Process Node Scaling.png`

**Intended Markdown width:** 900 px

**Recommended source-art size:** at least 1800 × 1000 px

**Recommended aspect ratio:** approximately 16:9 or 9:5

---

### Core teaching message

The figure should visually communicate:

> **Historically, transistor counts increased by orders of magnitude while semiconductor manufacturing generations moved toward smaller feature scales.**

It must also communicate:

> **Modern node names such as 5 nm, 3 nm, and 2 nm-class are process-generation labels rather than one exact transistor measurement.**

Do not show a simple equation such as:

`transistor count ∝ 1 / node size`

because that would be misleading.

---

### Recommended layout

Use a **two-part textbook chart**, not a poster.

#### Upper panel — transistor-count trend

Use:

- horizontal axis: `Year`
- vertical axis: `Representative chip transistor count`
- logarithmic y-axis strongly preferred
- a restrained upward trend line or series of representative data points

The visual should show the broad progression:

> `thousands → tens of thousands → millions → hundreds of millions → billions → tens of billions`

The point is the **orders-of-magnitude increase**, not memorization of specific processor models.

If named representative chips are used, keep the list short and technically verified. Do not fill the figure with product logos or marketing imagery.

A preferred educational treatment is 5–7 labeled points maximum.

Possible representative milestones, only after verification:

- early-1970s commercial microprocessor: a few thousand transistors;
- late-1970s processor: tens of thousands;
- early-1990s processor: a few million;
- around 2000: tens of millions;
- early 2010s: around one billion;
- early 2020s leading commercial SoCs: tens of billions.

Exact chip names and counts are optional.

#### Lower panel — process-node trend

Show a clean horizontal progression beneath the chart:

`µm-class → submicron → 180 nm → 90 nm → 45 nm → 22 nm → 14 nm → 7 nm → 5 nm → 3 nm → 2 nm-class`

This lower panel should visually move from **larger node labels** to **smaller node labels** as time moves forward.

Do not imply that every manufacturer used exactly the same node at the same date.

---

### Required annotation

Include one clearly readable note inside the figure:

> `Modern node names are technology-generation labels, not one literal transistor dimension.`

This note is required.

A second optional note may say:

> `Representative historical trend — chip design, die area, architecture, and manufacturer also affect transistor count.`

Use concise wording.

---

### Relationship between the two panels

The upper and lower panels should be aligned by time, but the visual relationship should be **historical correlation**, not a strict causal formula.

The figure may use a light vertical alignment grid across both panels.

Do not draw arrows directly from each node label to a specific transistor count unless the exact chip/process pairing has been verified.

---

### Moore's law treatment

A light dashed guide line may be shown on the transistor-count chart to suggest the historical Moore's-law trend.

If used, label it:

- `Historical Moore's-law trend`

Do not label the line as a guaranteed future law.

Do not extrapolate it far into the future.

Do not state that every chip doubled exactly every two years.

---

### Recommended visual style

- clean engineering textbook chart;
- white or very light background;
- sans-serif labels;
- restrained navy / gray palette;
- one muted accent for the transistor-count trend;
- one different muted accent for node labels;
- no 3D chart effects;
- no gradients unless very subtle;
- no stock photographs;
- no company logos;
- no marketing language;
- no oversized title inside artwork;
- no embedded figure number or caption.

The figure should read like a technical teaching chart rather than an industry poster.

---

### Prohibited or misleading elements

Do not:

- imply node name equals literal gate length;
- imply all transistor dimensions equal the node name;
- imply transistor count depends only on node size;
- imply all chips at a given node contain the same transistor count;
- imply every manufacturer changes nodes at the same time;
- treat Moore's law as an exact physical law;
- extend an exponential trend indefinitely into the future;
- claim transistor scaling has completely stopped;
- use transistor counts that have not been technically verified;
- mix chip counts from incomparable products without labeling them as representative examples;
- use logarithmic scaling without labeling the axis clearly;
- place a company logo, processor photograph, or branded package image in the figure;
- embed the external figure number or Markdown caption in the artwork.

---

### Technical verification requirements

Before final figure acceptance, verify any numerical data against authoritative sources.

Recommended source categories:

- Computer History Museum / manufacturer historical documentation for early microprocessor transistor counts;
- Intel historical processor documentation where Intel processors are used;
- Apple or other manufacturer official technical releases if modern SoC transistor counts are used;
- ASML or semiconductor-industry references for the definition of `node`;
- current Section 1.17 technical-verification notes.

If exact chip data are difficult to verify consistently, use **orders-of-magnitude bands** instead of specific chip names. That is preferable to using uncertain data.

---

### Final caption

A note states that modern node names are technology-generation labels rather than one literal transistor dimension.`
