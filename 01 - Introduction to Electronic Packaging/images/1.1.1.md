## Suggested Content for Figure 1.1.1

**Instructional purpose:**  
Show, at a glance, why a correct circuit design is not yet a usable electronic product. The figure should contrast electrical design intent with the physical realization created through electronic packaging.

**Filename:** `1.1.1 - From Circuit Design to Physical Electronic Product.png`  
**Intended width:** 850 px in Markdown  
**Aspect ratio:** 16:9

### Suggested visual style

- Clean engineering-textbook illustration.
- White or very light neutral background.
- Restrained technical color palette.
- Simple 2D or restrained isometric presentation; do not use photorealistic rendering.
- Consistent line weights and concise sans-serif labels.
- Adequate whitespace.
- No decorative clutter, watermark, logo, or overall title embedded in the artwork.
- Use callout leader lines only where they improve clarity.

### Required visual content

- **Left side:** a simplified schematic-style circuit representing electrical design intent. It should contain:
  - one generic IC symbol;
  - a few generic passive-component symbols;
  - simple electrical connection lines.
- **Center:** a clear transition from the schematic to the physical implementation.
- **Right side:** a generic, original PCB assembly illustrating the physical realization of the circuit. It should visibly include:
  - an IC package mounted on a PCB;
  - solder joints between the package/component terminations and PCB pads;
  - PCB traces and at least one via as physical interconnect features;
  - a board-edge connector or other mechanically plausible connector;
  - a simple heat-spreading or heat-sink element associated with a heat-producing component;
  - a simple mechanical support or partial enclosure/chassis element.

The physical assembly should communicate that packaging provides more than electrical connection: it also supports cooling, mechanical support, manufacturability, and protection.

### Required labels

Use only concise labels that are needed to interpret the physical product:

- Circuit / schematic
- Physical realization
- IC package
- Solder joints
- PCB
- Traces and vias
- Connector
- Thermal path / heat sink
- Mechanical support

Do not add paragraph-length explanations inside the image.

### Required relationships / arrows / states

- A left-to-right arrow should show the transition from the schematic to the physical product and be labeled **Physical realization**.
- The IC package must be physically mounted to the PCB through visible solder-joint connections.
- PCB traces and vias must appear as features of the board, not as floating wires.
- The connector must be physically attached to the PCB at a plausible location.
- The thermal element must be physically associated with the heat-producing component/package and should imply a heat-flow path away from the component.
- Mechanical support should visibly support or constrain the PCB rather than float independently.
- The schematic and physical product should represent two views of the same general engineering system, without implying a literal one-to-one detailed circuit layout.

### Do not show

- Do not reproduce or closely imitate the composition of a textbook, manufacturer, or online reference figure.
- Do not use a recognizable commercial product, company logo, trademarked enclosure design, or branded component.
- Do not make semiconductor wafer fabrication or the internal semiconductor die the focus; those topics are introduced later in Section 1.3.
- Do not imply that every electronic product requires a heat sink, fan, or enclosure.
- Do not draw impossible electrical or mechanical connections.
- Do not show loose traces floating above the PCB.
- Do not add an embedded figure title; the caption belongs in the Markdown.
- Do not include unnecessary numerical values or dimensions.
- Do not use decorative lighting, dramatic perspective, or artistic effects that reduce technical clarity.

### Technical verification sources

- earlier Chapter 1 instructional materials, used to verify the intended book framing of electronic packaging as the physical realization that supports electrical, thermal, mechanical, manufacturing, and reliability needs.
- Rao R. Tummala, *Fundamentals of Device and Systems Packaging: Technologies and Applications*, used to verify the broad scope and multidisciplinary functions of electronic packaging.
- Rao R. Tummala, *Fundamentals of Microsystems Packaging*, especially the introductory packaging chapters, used to verify the relationship between devices, packages, boards, and systems.
- Glenn R. Blackwell, ed., *The Electronic Packaging Handbook*, used to verify the applied design/manufacturing/system perspective.

These sources are for technical verification only. The figure composition must be independently created for this OER.
