## Suggested Content for Figure 1.3.4

**Instructional purpose:**  
Show how a PN junction physically forms when P-type and N-type semiconductor regions are brought together. The figure should bridge the earlier discussion of doped semiconductor material and the later discussion of equilibrium, forward bias, and reverse bias.

The figure should emphasize the sequence:

**separate P-type and N-type regions → carrier diffusion → recombination near the junction → exposed fixed ionized dopants → depletion region → built-in electric field → equilibrium**

The purpose is to help readers understand that the depletion region and built-in electric field arise naturally from carrier diffusion and recombination rather than from a simple statement that electrons and holes are merely attracted to each other.

**Filename:**  
`1.3.4 - Formation of a PN Junction from Diffusion to Equilibrium.png`

**Intended width:** 900 px in Markdown

**Recommended aspect ratio:** 16:9

### Overall layout

Use a clean three-panel horizontal progression:

1. **Before contact**
2. **Diffusion and recombination**
3. **Equilibrium PN junction**

Keep P-type material on the **left** and N-type material on the **right** throughout all panels.

Use the same carrier symbols and visual language as the surrounding semiconductor figures in Section 1.3.

---

### Panel 1 – Before Contact

Show separate P-type and N-type semiconductor regions before a junction is formed.

#### P-type region

Show:

- high concentration of mobile holes;
- relatively few electrons need not be shown;
- neutral bulk semiconductor.

Label:

- `P-type`
- `High hole concentration`

#### N-type region

Show:

- high concentration of mobile electrons;
- relatively few holes need not be shown;
- neutral bulk semiconductor.

Label:

- `N-type`
- `High electron concentration`

The two semiconductor regions should be visibly separated before contact.

Do not show a depletion region or fixed space charge yet.

---

### Panel 2 – Diffusion and Recombination

Show the P-type and N-type regions brought into contact.

Immediately after contact:

- holes diffuse from the P side toward the N side;
- electrons diffuse from the N side toward the P side;
- electrons and holes recombine near the junction.

Use clearly labeled arrows:

- `Hole diffusion` pointing **P → N**
- `Electron diffusion` pointing **N → P**

Near the junction, show the beginning of uncovered ionized dopants:

- fixed negatively charged acceptor ions on the P side;
- fixed positively charged donor ions on the N side.

Show these ions as fixed at lattice locations, not as mobile particles.

Indicate that recombination occurs near the junction.

Recommended label:

- `Recombination occurs near the junction`

Do not imply that recombination removes the dopant atoms themselves.

---

### Panel 3 – Equilibrium PN Junction

Show the completed equilibrium junction.

Include:

- P-type neutral region on the left;
- N-type neutral region on the right;
- depletion region centered around the junction;
- fixed negative acceptor ions within the P-side portion of the depletion region;
- fixed positive donor ions within the N-side portion of the depletion region;
- mobile holes primarily outside the depletion region on the P side;
- mobile electrons primarily outside the depletion region on the N side.

Label the central region:

- `Depletion region`

The depletion region should be shown as semiconductor material with relatively few mobile majority carriers, **not as an empty physical gap**.

Show the built-in electric field with an arrow pointing:

**N side → P side**

because the field direction is from the fixed positive donor ions toward the fixed negative acceptor ions.

Label:

- `Built-in electric field`
- optional clarification: `(from N side to P side)`

At the bottom of the equilibrium panel, state:

`Diffusion and field effects balance → no net current`

This represents thermal equilibrium.

---

### Required carrier and charge legend

Use a consistent legend containing:

- open red circle: `Holes (mobile)`
- filled blue circle: `Electrons (mobile)`
- red circled minus: `Fixed acceptor ions (−) (immobile)`
- blue circled plus: `Fixed donor ions (+) (immobile)`
- lightly shaded central region: `Depletion region (space-charge region)`

The symbols must be large enough to remain clear at normal mdBook display width.

---

### Required physical relationships

The following relationships must be technically correct:

- holes diffuse from **P to N** before equilibrium is established;
- electrons diffuse from **N to P**;
- recombination occurs near the junction;
- acceptor ions exposed on the P side are **negative and fixed**;
- donor ions exposed on the N side are **positive and fixed**;
- fixed dopant ions do not move through the semiconductor;
- the depletion region contains relatively few mobile majority carriers;
- the built-in electric field points **N → P**;
- the electric field opposes continued majority-carrier diffusion;
- at equilibrium, diffusion and electric-field effects balance and the **net current is zero**.

---

### Spatial relationships

- Keep P-type on the left and N-type on the right in all panels.
- Use consistent semiconductor-block height and scale.
- Keep the junction approximately centered in Panels 2 and 3.
- Place mobile carriers mainly in the neutral regions in the equilibrium panel.
- Keep fixed ions visually confined to the depletion region.
- Do not allow arrows or labels to overlap carrier symbols.
- Use aligned panels so readers can visually follow how the junction develops from one stage to the next.

---

### Color restrictions

- White or very light neutral background.
- P-type region may use a subtle warm tint.
- N-type region may use a subtle cool/green tint.
- Depletion region should use a subtle pale violet or neutral shaded band.
- Holes: red outline.
- Electrons: filled blue.
- Fixed acceptor ions: red circled minus.
- Fixed donor ions: blue circled plus.
- Built-in field: black or another high-contrast neutral arrow.

Do not rely on color alone. Charge signs and labels must remain understandable in grayscale.

---

### Suggested visual style

- Clean engineering-textbook infographic.
- Three aligned panels.
- Large, readable labels.
- Flat 2D conceptual semiconductor cross-sections.
- Consistent with Figures 1.3.3 and 1.3.4.
- Restrained professional palette.
- No photorealistic semiconductor imagery.
- No decorative effects.
- No embedded overall figure title beyond panel headings.

---

### Prohibited or misleading elements

- Do not show the depletion region as an air gap.
- Do not depict electrons and holes as simply being electrostatically attracted to one another as the primary explanation.
- Do not show fixed dopant ions moving.
- Do not reverse the diffusion directions.
- Do not reverse the built-in electric-field direction.
- Do not imply that P-type semiconductor is positively charged overall or N-type semiconductor is negatively charged overall.
- Do not show large mobile-carrier populations remaining inside the equilibrium depletion region.
- Do not introduce forward or reverse external bias in this figure; those belong in the next figure.
- Do not introduce energy-band diagrams, Fermi levels, carrier-concentration equations, or depletion-width equations.

### Final caption

*Figure 1.3.4 – Formation of a PN junction. After P-type and N-type regions are brought together, electrons and holes diffuse across the junction and recombine. The remaining fixed ionized dopants form a depletion region and create a built-in electric field that opposes further majority-carrier diffusion, producing equilibrium with no net current.*

### Alt-text intent

Describe three stages of PN-junction formation: separate P-type and N-type regions, carrier diffusion and recombination after contact, and an equilibrium junction containing a depletion region, fixed ionized dopants, and a built-in electric field directed from the N side toward the P side.
