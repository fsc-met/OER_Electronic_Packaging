## Suggested Content for Figure 1.3.5

**Instructional purpose:**  
Show the physical origin of the depletion region and how external bias changes PN-junction behavior. The figure should correct the common oversimplification that electrons are merely "attracted to holes" and should instead emphasize diffusion, recombination, fixed ionized dopants, the built-in electric field, and changing depletion width.

**Filename:** `1.3.5 - PN Junction at Equilibrium, Forward Bias, and Reverse Bias.png`  
**Intended width:** 900 px in Markdown  
**Aspect ratio:** 16:9

### Objects/components to show

Create three side-by-side PN-junction panels with P-type material on the left and N-type material on the right:

1. **Equilibrium**
   - P region and N region;
   - depletion region centered on the junction;
   - fixed negative acceptor ions on the P side of the depletion region;
   - fixed positive donor ions on the N side;
   - mobile holes farther into the P region;
   - mobile electrons farther into the N region;
   - built-in electric-field arrow from the positive donor-ion side toward the negative acceptor-ion side.
2. **Forward bias**
   - positive external terminal connected to P;
   - negative external terminal connected to N;
   - narrower depletion region;
   - carrier crossing / strong conventional-current direction indicated.
3. **Reverse bias**
   - negative external terminal connected to P;
   - positive external terminal connected to N;
   - wider depletion region;
   - no large majority-carrier current arrow; if leakage is shown, make it visibly small and secondary.

A small diode symbol may be included beneath the forward/reverse panels only if it can be rendered with exact controlled geometry.

### Required relationships / arrows / states

- At equilibrium, the electric field must point from the fixed positive donor ions on the N side toward the fixed negative acceptor ions on the P side.
- Forward bias must connect positive to P and negative to N.
- Reverse bias must connect negative to P and positive to N.
- Forward bias must show a narrower depletion region than equilibrium.
- Reverse bias must show a wider depletion region than equilibrium.
- Fixed ionized dopants must remain in the crystal and must not be shown moving across the junction.
- Mobile electrons and holes should be concentrated primarily outside the depletion region.
- Any conventional-current arrow in forward bias should be consistent with positive-current direction.

### Spatial relationships

- Keep P on the left and N on the right in all three panels.
- Use identical panel scales so depletion-width changes are visually meaningful.
- Place polarity symbols outside the semiconductor blocks.
- Keep fixed-ion symbols visually distinct from mobile carriers.

### Color restrictions

- White/light neutral background.
- Use consistent colors/symbols for electrons, holes, donor ions, and acceptor ions across all panels.
- Do not rely on red/blue color alone to distinguish charge sign; include + and - markings.
- Use a subtle shaded band for the depletion region.

### Suggested visual style

- Clean engineering textbook diagram.
- Flat 2D semiconductor cross-section.
- Controlled vector labels and polarity symbols.
- No photorealistic effects.
- No embedded overall title.

### Prohibited or misleading elements

- Do not describe or depict the PN junction merely as electrons being electrostatically attracted to holes.
- Do not show the depletion region as an air gap or empty physical space.
- Do not show the built-in electric field pointing from P to N.
- Do not reverse the forward-bias or reverse-bias battery polarity.
- Do not show zero reverse current as an absolute physical rule.
- Do not imply that the junction has one universal fixed forward voltage.
- Do not depict fixed dopant ions as mobile.
