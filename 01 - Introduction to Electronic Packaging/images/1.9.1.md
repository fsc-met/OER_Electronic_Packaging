## Suggested Content for Figure 1.9.1

**Instructional purpose:**  
Explain **why IC packaging is needed** by comparing a fragile bare semiconductor die with a practical packaged IC component.

The figure should make clear that the package is not merely a protective shell. It provides several engineering functions simultaneously:

- electrical fan-out / redistribution;
- mechanical support and handling;
- environmental protection;
- thermal paths;
- manufacturable external terminals for later PCB assembly.

The figure should visually support the Section 1.9 transition:

> **bare IC die -> IC packaging -> packaged IC component**

**Filename:**  
`1.9.1 - Why a Bare IC Die Needs Packaging.png`

**Intended width:** 900 px in Markdown

**Recommended aspect ratio:** 16:9

---

### Overall layout

Use a clean **left-to-right comparison/transformation**.

Recommended structure:

- **Left:** bare IC die
- **Center:** five package functions represented by concise callouts/arrows
- **Right:** packaged IC component with board-compatible external terminals

A restrained transformation arrow may connect:

`Bare IC die` -> `IC packaging` -> `Packaged IC component`

Do not draw a PCB as the final object. The package should clearly remain a separate component.

Do **not** place the figure number, external caption, or an oversized overall title inside the artwork.

---

### Left side – Bare semiconductor die

Show:

- one small rectangular semiconductor die;
- visible top-side I/O pads around the die edge or other plausible pad pattern;
- thin, fragile geometry;
- no molded body;
- no PCB;
- no external package leads.

Required label:

- `Bare IC die`

Optional labels:

- `Fine I/O pads`
- `Fragile silicon`

The die should look clearly different from a packaged IC and from a PCB.

---

### Center – Why packaging is needed

Show five concise functional callouts.

#### 1. Electrical fan-out / redistribution

Visual concept:

- very fine die pads on the left;
- larger external package terminals on the right;
- arrows showing scale transition.

Required label:

- `Electrical fan-out`

Optional note:

- `Fine die pitch -> practical package pitch`

Do not imply that fan-out always means one specific package technology.

#### 2. Mechanical support

Visual concept:

- die attached to a stable support/carrier.

Required label:

- `Mechanical support`

Optional note:

- `Handling and assembly`

#### 3. Environmental / physical protection

Visual concept:

- protective body, lid, or encapsulation around the die.

Required label:

- `Protection`

Optional note:

- `Contamination and handling`

Do not imply ordinary plastic molding is hermetic.

#### 4. Thermal path

Visual concept:

- heat generated in the die;
- arrows toward package substrate, exposed pad, lid, or other heat-flow route.

Required label:

- `Thermal path`

Do not imply one universal heat-flow direction for all package types.

#### 5. Manufacturable external interface

Visual concept:

- package terminals that could later connect to a PCB.

Required label:

- `Board-compatible interface`

Optional note:

- `Leads, lands, pins, or solder balls`

---

### Right side – Packaged IC component

Show one generic packaged IC that is easy to recognize but not tied to a commercial product.

Preferred visual:

- generic QFN, QFP, or small BGA-like component;
- clear package body;
- visible external terminals;
- no brand marking.

Required label:

- `Packaged IC component`

Optional callout:

- `Ready for handling, test, and PCB assembly`

Do not imply the package itself is a PCB.

---

### Required technical relationships

The figure must communicate correctly that:

- the electronic circuit already exists on the bare die;
- packaging does not create the transistor circuitry;
- packaging connects microscopic die-level I/O to larger external terminals;
- packaging adds physical support and protection;
- packaging contributes to heat removal;
- the package provides a repeatable manufacturing and test interface;
- the output is a **component**, not a PCB;
- specialized bare-die assembly exists, but ordinary PCB assembly commonly uses packaged components.
