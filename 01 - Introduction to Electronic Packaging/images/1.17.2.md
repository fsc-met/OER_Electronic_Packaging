## Suggested Content for Figure 1.17.2

**Figure title for authoring/reference only:**  
`Evolution of Transistor Structures`

**Instructional purpose:**  
Help students visually understand why transistor architecture changed as semiconductor scaling became more difficult.

The figure should show the simplified progression:

> **Planar MOSFET → FinFET → GAA / nanosheet → future CFET concept**

The figure is for **structural recognition**, not semiconductor-device physics.

**Filename:**  
`1.17.2 - Evolution of Transistor Structures.png`

**Intended Markdown width:** 900 px

**Recommended source-art size:** at least 1800 × 900 px

**Recommended aspect ratio:** approximately 16:9

---

### Overall layout

Use a clean **four-panel horizontal engineering schematic**.

From left to right:

1. `Planar MOSFET`
2. `FinFET`
3. `GAA / nanosheet`
4. `CFET — future concept`

Each panel should:

- use the same general viewing direction;
- use consistent gate, channel, source/drain, and substrate colors;
- be simplified enough for first-course recognition;
- show how gate control becomes progressively more three-dimensional.

Do not use a poster layout, large banners, decorative badges, or long explanatory text.

---

### Panel 1 — Planar MOSFET

Required label:

- `Planar MOSFET`

Show:

- flat semiconductor substrate;
- source region;
- drain region;
- planar channel near the top surface;
- gate above the channel;
- thin gate dielectric represented schematically.

Concept to communicate:

> The channel is mainly in the wafer plane and the gate controls it primarily from above.

Optional small date label:

- `mainstream CMOS for decades`

Do not overcomplicate the device cross-section.

---

### Panel 2 — FinFET

Required label:

- `FinFET`

Show:

- a vertical fin-shaped channel rising above the substrate;
- source and drain at opposite ends of the fin;
- gate wrapping around approximately three sides of the fin.

Concept to communicate:

> The gate gains better electrostatic control by wrapping around multiple sides of a vertical channel.

Optional concise time note:

- `industry adoption ~2011–2012`

This date should be labeled approximate.

---

### Panel 3 — Gate-All-Around / nanosheet

Required label:

- `GAA / nanosheet`

Show:

- two or three stacked horizontal nanosheet channels;
- gate material fully surrounding each nanosheet;
- source/drain regions at the ends of the stacked sheets.

Concept to communicate:

> The gate surrounds the channel on all sides for stronger electrostatic control.

Optional concise time note:

- `industry production from 2022; broader adoption mid-2020s`

Keep the wording compact.

Do not depict a nanowire-only structure if the label says nanosheet unless the distinction is made clear.

---

### Panel 4 — CFET

Required label:

- `CFET — future concept`

Show:

- one n-type transistor structure vertically stacked with one p-type transistor structure;
- clear vertical integration;
- no implication that this is already mainstream manufacturing.

Concept to communicate:

> Future logic scaling may increase density by stacking complementary transistor structures vertically.

Required qualifier:

- `future / roadmap`

Optional small date note:

- `active research in the 2020s`

Do not label CFET as a current mainstream production technology.

---

### Consistent color language

Recommended:

- gate: muted purple;
- channel / nanosheet / fin: muted blue;
- source/drain: dark gray or muted green-gray;
- substrate: pale gray;
- dielectric: light neutral;
- p-type vs n-type in CFET: use two subtle tones plus explicit labels.

Do not rely on color alone. Label key structural regions where useful.

---

### Required labels

At minimum:

- `Gate`
- `Channel`
- `Source`
- `Drain`

These can be shown once in a small common legend or selectively in each panel.

For CFET, also label:

- `nFET`
- `pFET`

Avoid dense semiconductor-physics labeling.

---

### Optional timeline cue

A thin horizontal arrow beneath the panels may show:

> `1960s–2000s → 2010s → 2020s → future`

This is optional.

If included, add a note:

- `Approximate development / adoption periods`

Do not imply an instantaneous industry-wide transition.

---

### Required conceptual relationship

The figure must make the architectural progression visually obvious:

- Planar: gate primarily above the channel;
- FinFET: gate around multiple sides;
- GAA: gate surrounding the channel;
- CFET: vertical stacking of complementary transistor structures.

The visual should reinforce the Section 1.17 teaching point:

> **The industry has changed transistor geometry, not merely shrunk the same planar structure.**

---

### Visual style

- original engineering schematic;
- white background;
- restrained 2D or restrained isometric view;
- consistent geometry and line weights;
- no photorealism;
- no microscopic TEM-style images;
- no company logos;
- no branded transistor names such as RibbonFET unless used only in internal notes;
- no large title inside the artwork;
- no figure number or external caption inside artwork.

The figure should look like a textbook schematic, not a semiconductor-company roadmap poster.

---

### Prohibited or misleading elements

Do not:

- show GAA as simply a smaller FinFET without gate-all-around geometry;
- show a FinFET gate controlling only the top surface;
- show the GAA gate failing to surround the nanosheet;
- present CFET as current mainstream production;
- imply the dates are exact universal transition years;
- add semiconductor equations;
- add quantum-mechanical diagrams;
- add detailed fabrication steps;
- label node sizes on the devices as if transistor geometry equals node name;
- use company-specific proprietary cross-sections;
- copy a manufacturer or textbook transistor-evolution figure;
- embed the external caption or figure number.

---

### Technical verification sources

Use the current Section 1.17 verification notes as the primary technical grounding:

- historical planar MOSFET references;
- UC Berkeley FinFET historical materials;
- Intel 22 nm / Tri-Gate materials for high-volume FinFET-era adoption;
- Samsung 3 nm GAA production information;
- Intel 18A RibbonFET information as current GAA confirmation;
- TSMC N2 nanosheet information;
- imec logic-scaling and CFET roadmap materials.

These are technical references only. The final composition must be independently authored.
