## Suggested Content for Figure 1.5.3

**Instructional purpose:**  
Show one representative way inductance can be integrated on an IC using a planar patterned-metal spiral.

**Filename:**  
`1.5.3 - Example of an Integrated Planar Spiral Inductor.png`

**Intended width:** 900 px in Markdown

**Recommended aspect ratio:** 16:9

### Representative structure

Show a **top view of a planar spiral metal inductor**.

Required elements:

- outer terminal;
- patterned metal spiral;
- inner terminal;
- an electrically valid connection from the inner terminal using:
  - a via and lower metal layer,
  - an underpass,
  - or another clearly indicated secondary metal connection.

Suggested labels:

- `Outer terminal`
- `Patterned metal spiral`
- `Inner terminal`
- `Via`
- `Underpass / lower metal`
- `Current path`

A restrained magnetic-field indication may be added if it remains clear at 900 px.

### Required concept

The figure should communicate:

`Current through a looped metal conductor → magnetic field → inductance`

The spiral must be electrically continuous.

### Important scope note

This is **one representative integrated-inductor structure**.

Do not imply that all integrated inductors use the same geometry. Multilayer/stacked spirals and other patterned-metal configurations are also possible.

### Visual style

- clean top-view engineering diagram;
- white or very light background;
- clear metal paths and terminal connections;
- large readable labels;
- minimal text;
- no decorative effects;
- no embedded figure number or overall title.

### Prohibited or misleading elements

- Do not show a doped spiral carved into silicon.
- Do not leave the inner end electrically unconnected.
- Do not show impossible metal crossings without a via/layer change.
- Do not imply that every IC contains an integrated inductor.
- Do not assign universal inductance, dimensions, or frequency values.
