## Suggested Content for Figure 1.7.1

**Instructional purpose:**  
Show that the same general electronic circuit function can be physically realized in two very different ways:

1. as a **discrete-component circuit** built from separate packaged components mounted on a PCB; and  
2. as a **monolithic integrated circuit (IC)** in which many devices and interconnections are fabricated together on one semiconductor die.

The figure should help readers understand that the difference is not mainly the schematic function, but the **physical implementation**.

This figure supports the key Section 1.7 message:

> **Same circuit function, different physical realization**

It also reinforces two important distinctions:

- an **integrated circuit is not a PCB**;  
- a monolithic IC still needs **IC packaging** before normal PCB-level use.

**Filename:**  
`1.7.1 - Discrete Circuit versus Monolithic Integrated Circuit.png`

**Intended width:** 900 px in Markdown

**Recommended aspect ratio:** 16:9

### Overall layout

Use a clean side-by-side comparison with a central bridging statement.

- **Left side:** discrete-component implementation on a PCB
- **Right side:** monolithic IC implementation on one semiconductor die
- **Center:** a clear comparison statement such as  
  `Same circuit function`  
  `Different physical realization`

A clean two-column engineering-textbook infographic is preferred.

Do **not** embed an oversized overall figure title or figure number inside the artwork. The external Markdown caption provides that information.

---

### Left side – Discrete-component circuit

Show a simplified PCB assembly with several **separate packaged components** mounted on a board.

Required elements:

- a small PCB or PCB region;
- at least one packaged transistor or logic IC package;
- several discrete passive components such as resistor and capacitor packages;
- copper traces on the PCB;
- visible solder joints or pad connections;
- a physically plausible arrangement showing that the circuit is created by connecting separate parts on the board.

Required labels:

- `Discrete-component circuit`
- `Separate packaged components`
- `PCB`
- `Copper traces`
- `Solder joints`

Optional additional labels if they remain uncluttered:

- `Packaged transistor`
- `Discrete resistor`
- `Discrete capacitor`

The discrete side should visually communicate:

- the components are separate physical parts;
- the PCB provides interconnection and support;
- the circuit exists at the **board-assembly level**.

Do not overcrowd the left side with too many components or make it look like a finished consumer product.

---

### Right side – Monolithic integrated circuit

Show a simplified semiconductor die or die cross-section/top-view hybrid concept representing a **monolithic IC on one semiconductor die**.

Required elements:

- one clearly bounded die region;
- representative integrated transistor structures;
- representative integrated passive structures where appropriate;
- patterned on-die metal interconnections;
- the sense that multiple circuit elements are fabricated together inside or on the same die;
- small bond pads or I/O pads at the die edge if helpful.

Required labels:

- `Monolithic IC on one semiconductor die`
- `Integrated transistors`
- `Integrated passive structures`
- `On-die metal interconnect`

Optional additional label:

- `I/O pads`

The integrated side should visually communicate:

- many devices exist together on the same semiconductor die;
- the connections are **on-die interconnects**, not PCB traces;
- the circuit exists at the **semiconductor-die level**.

Do **not** draw miniature packaged resistors, capacitors, logic-gate chips, or other board-level components inside the die.

---

### Central comparison message

Between or above the two sides, include a short comparison statement such as:

- `Same circuit function`
- `Different physical realization`

A small directional arrow or linking graphic may be used if it improves clarity, but the figure should present a **comparison**, not a time-sequence manufacturing flow.

---

### Required secondary note

Include a small secondary note near the integrated-circuit side, such as:

- `The die still requires IC packaging before PCB assembly`

This note is important because Section 1.7 is preparing readers for the later progression:

- semiconductor fabrication → IC die;
- IC packaging → packaged component;
- PCB fabrication → bare PCB;
- components + bare PCB → PCB assembly.

Keep this note secondary so it does not dominate the main comparison.

---

### Required conceptual relationships

The figure must support all of the following ideas correctly:

- the **left side** uses separate individually manufactured components;
- the **right side** uses many devices fabricated together on one semiconductor die;
- the two sides may represent a similar **general circuit function**, even though the physical implementation is different;
- the discrete circuit depends on **PCB traces and solder joints** for interconnection;
- the monolithic IC depends on **on-die metal interconnect**;
- the IC is **not** a miniature PCB;
- the IC die is **not yet** a packaged board-mountable component;
- the figure must **not** imply that the left-side PCB is manufactured from the IC or that the IC turns into a PCB.

---

- Keep the left-side PCB and right-side die clearly separated.
- Keep the center comparison statement visually obvious.
- Ensure the right-side die is visually recognized as a semiconductor structure, not a board.
- Maintain sufficient whitespace for readability at 900 px mdBook display width.
- Reserve space below the figure for the external Markdown caption.

### Color restrictions

Use a restrained engineering-textbook palette.

Suggested approach:

- **PCB side:** green, blue, or neutral PCB tones with visible metallic trace accents;
- **IC side:** light silicon/die neutral tones with distinct but subtle region colors for device structures and interconnect layers;
- **comparison text/arrows:** neutral high-contrast colors.

Do not rely on color alone to distinguish the two implementations. Labels and structure must also make the distinction clear in grayscale.

Avoid:

- bright marketing-style gradients;
- decorative glow effects;
- photorealistic textures;
- dramatic perspective.

---

### Suggested visual style

- clean engineering-textbook infographic;
- flat 2D or restrained isometric style;
- concise sans-serif labels;
- consistent line weights;
- moderate abstraction rather than photorealism;
- simple original geometry;
- no watermark, logo, or brand-specific product appearance.

The goal is **clarity**, not artistic complexity.

---

### Prohibited or misleading elements

Do **not** include any of the following:

- a recognizable commercial product or branded PCB;
- a processor logo or manufacturer package marking;
- miniature packaged components drawn inside the integrated-circuit die;
- logic-gate symbols presented as literal physical objects inside the die;
- a die that visually looks like a printed circuit board;
- a claim or implication that all components should be integrated;
- a claim or implication that integration automatically makes every design cheaper, cooler, or better;
- packaging details so elaborate that they distract from the main comparison;
- a fabrication-process flowchart;
- a completed consumer device enclosure.

Also do **not** imply that:

- the discrete circuit is obsolete or incorrect;
- the monolithic IC eliminates the need for packaging;
- the IC directly becomes the PCB.
