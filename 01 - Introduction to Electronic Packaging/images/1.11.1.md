## Suggested Content for Figure 1.11.1

**Figure title for authoring/reference only:**  
`From PCBA to Finished Electronic Product`

**Instructional purpose:**  
Show that a **PCBA is often only one subsystem** inside a finished electronic product and that **system integration** combines the PCBA with the mechanical, electrical, thermal, power, interface, and protective elements required for the complete product.

The central teaching transformation is:

> **PCBA + system elements -> system integration -> finished electronic product**

The figure should make clear that:

- a PCBA is not automatically the finished product;
- the enclosure alone is not the product;
- system integration involves much more than placing a board inside a box;
- multiple packaging functions continue at the system level.

**Filename:**  
`1.11.1 - From PCBA to Finished Electronic Product.png`

**Intended width:** 900 px in Markdown

**Recommended aspect ratio:** 16:9

---

### Overall layout

Use a clean **convergence / integration diagram**.

Recommended structure:

#### Left side – Central electronic subassembly

Show:

- one clearly recognizable **PCBA**;
- optionally, one smaller second PCBA or module for recognition.

#### Around or adjacent to the PCBA – System elements

Show representative elements that must be integrated with the PCBA:

- enclosure or chassis;
- cables and connectors;
- power supply or battery;
- display and controls;
- sensors and/or actuators;
- representative cooling hardware;
- structural mounting hardware.

#### Middle – System integration

Show a clear convergence into:

- `System integration`

#### Right side – Finished electronic product

Show one integrated product enclosure containing or clearly representing the major subsystem categories.

Do **not** place the figure number, external caption, or an oversized duplicate overall title inside the artwork.

---

### 1. Central PCBA

Show one generic populated PCB containing representative:

- packaged IC;
- discrete components;
- connector;
- PCB traces/pads where appropriate.

Required label:

- `PCBA`

Optional smaller note:

- `Electronic subsystem`

The PCBA should look like the accepted Chapter 1 board-level concept from Section 1.10.

Do not show a bare PCB.

---

### 2. Optional second PCBA or module

A second board or module may be shown if it helps students recognize that many products contain multiple electronic subassemblies.

Possible label:

- `Additional PCBA / module`

Keep this secondary.

Do not make the figure look like every product requires multiple boards.

---

### 3. Enclosure / chassis

Show a simplified:

- enclosure;
- chassis;
- structural frame;
- or shell.

Required concept:

- mechanical support and product boundary.

Suggested label:

- `Enclosure / chassis`

The enclosure should be clearly one **system element**, not the finished product by itself.

---

### 4. Cables and connectors

Show representative:

- cable harness;
- board-to-board or external connector;
- wiring connection.

Suggested label:

- `Cables / connectors`

The visual should communicate that system-level electrical interconnection extends beyond the PCB.

Do not turn this into a detailed wiring diagram.

---

### 5. Power source / power hardware

- battery;
- AC/DC power supply;
- DC power module.

Suggested label:

- `Power`

Do not imply every product uses a battery.

---

### 6. Display / controls

Show representative user-interface hardware such as:

- display;
- button;
- switch;
- knob;
- indicator.

Suggested label:

- `Display / controls`

Keep it conceptual.

---

### 7. Sensors / actuators

Show one or two simple representative devices, such as:

- sensor;
- motor;
- fan;
- actuator.

Suggested label:

- `Sensors / actuators`

Do not overpopulate the figure.

---

### 8. Cooling hardware

Show one representative system-level thermal solution.

Preferred examples:

- heat sink;
- fan;
- heat spreader;
- cold plate.

Suggested label:

- `Cooling hardware`

The purpose is to show that the thermal path continues beyond the component and PCB.

Do not imply all products require active cooling or liquid cooling.

---

### 9. Structural mounting elements

Show representative:

- standoff;
- bracket;
- screw/fastener;
- board rail or frame.

Suggested label:

- `Mounting hardware`

This should visually reinforce that the PCBA must be mechanically supported.

---

### 10. System integration stage

The individual system elements should converge at one clearly identified stage:

- `System integration`

The integration stage should communicate:

- electrical interconnection;
- mechanical mounting;
- thermal integration;
- enclosure integration;
- user-interface integration.

Do not expand it into a detailed manufacturing recipe.

---

### 11. Final integrated product

Show a generic finished electronic product.

The product may be represented as:

- a compact industrial control unit;
- a generic electronics enclosure;
- a simplified instrumentation product;
- another non-brand-specific electronic system.

The final product should visibly or conceptually contain:

- electronics;
- power;
- interconnections;
- cooling where needed;
- mechanical structure;
- enclosure;
- external interface.

Required label:

- `Finished electronic product`

Avoid using a recognizable commercial product or brand.

---

### Required technical relationships

The figure must correctly communicate that:

- a PCBA is often only one electronic subsystem;
- multiple system elements are manufactured or selected separately;
- system integration combines them into a complete product;
- packaging continues at the system level;
- electrical, mechanical, thermal, and environmental functions must coexist;
- the enclosure alone does not define the product;
- not every product requires every possible system element shown.

---

### Spatial relationships

- Keep the PCBA visually central to the incoming electronic side.
- Arrange system elements around the integration stage or as parallel inputs.
- Use arrows that converge toward `System integration`.
- Use one clear arrow from `System integration` to `Finished electronic product`.
- Avoid crossing arrows.
- Do not imply the PCBA transforms directly into an enclosure.
- Do not imply the enclosure transforms into the finished product without the other subsystems.

Use a restrained engineering-textbook palette.

- PCBAs: muted green / blue-green;
- enclosure/chassis: neutral gray;

- power element: muted blue;
- cooling hardware: metallic gray;
- arrows: navy;
- final product: neutral enclosure with visible or suggested subsystem integration.

Do not rely on color alone to convey meaning.

- clean engineering-textbook infographic;

- white or very light background;
- consistent line weights;

- no embedded figure number or external caption.

---

### Prohibited or misleading elements

Do not:

- imply the PCBA is automatically the complete product;
- imply the enclosure alone is the product;
- show only a PCB going into an empty box;
- imply all products require batteries;
- imply all products require fans or liquid cooling;
- imply all products contain multiple PCBAs;
- show detailed cable/harness design rules;
- show detailed EMI/EMC analysis;
- show detailed thermal calculations;
- show detailed enclosure design;
- show a brand-specific computer, phone, vehicle controller, or other commercial product;
- add manufacturing steps that belong in a separate process-flow figure;
- add an oversized title inside the image.
