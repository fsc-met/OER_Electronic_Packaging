## Suggested Content for Figure 1.2.1

**Instructional purpose:**  
Show that a single electronic-packaging decision can influence many product outcomes at the same time. The figure should reinforce the central idea of Section 1.2: packaging decisions are system-level engineering decisions rather than isolated physical choices.

**Filename:** `1.2.1 - Packaging Decisions Affect the Entire Product.png`  
**Intended width:** 850 px in Markdown  
**Aspect ratio:** 16:9

### Objects/components to show

- A simple, generic PCB assembly or compact electronic module in the center.
- A small highlighted callout on the central assembly labeled **Packaging decision**. The highlighted decision should be generic rather than tied to one specific product; examples could be component placement, PCB structure, connector location, cooling hardware, or enclosure arrangement.
- Seven surrounding outcome areas connected to the central packaging decision:
  - Performance
  - Thermal
  - Mechanical
  - Manufacturability & yield
  - Inspection & service
  - Reliability
  - Cost & schedule

### Required labels

Use only these concise labels inside the artwork:

- Packaging decision
- Performance
- Thermal
- Mechanical
- Manufacturability & yield
- Inspection & service
- Reliability
- Cost & schedule

Do not include paragraph-length explanations inside the figure.

### Required states / values

- No numerical values are required.
- The central electronic assembly should appear generic and original.
- Each surrounding outcome should be visually distinct but equal in importance; do not imply that one category is always dominant.

### Arrow directions and relationships

- Use outward arrows or clearly directed connectors from **Packaging decision** to each surrounding outcome.
- The arrows should communicate that one packaging choice can affect multiple engineering outcomes simultaneously.
- Do not use arrows that imply a one-way causal chain among the seven outcomes.
- If a secondary interaction is shown, keep it minimal so the main message remains visually clear.

### Spatial relationships

- Place the generic PCB assembly near the center of the figure.
- Place **Packaging decision** directly on or immediately adjacent to the central assembly.
- Arrange the seven outcome labels around the assembly in a balanced radial or semi-radial layout.
- Keep adequate whitespace between labels and connector lines.
- Do not crowd the lower edge because the published caption will appear below the image in Markdown.

### Color restrictions

- White or very light neutral background.
- Restrained technical palette.
- Use a limited number of muted colors to differentiate outcome areas.
- Avoid bright gradients, decorative shadows, or dramatic lighting.
- Maintain sufficient contrast for print and typical laptop/tablet viewing.

### Suggested visual style

- Clean engineering-textbook infographic.
- Simple 2D or restrained isometric central PCB assembly.
- Consistent line weights.
- Concise sans-serif labels.
- No photorealistic rendering.
- No decorative icons unless they materially improve recognition of an outcome category.
- No embedded overall figure title.

### Prohibited or misleading elements

- Do not use a recognizable commercial product, company logo, trademarked enclosure, or branded component.
- Do not imply that all packaging decisions affect every outcome equally in every product.
- Do not imply that improving one outcome automatically improves all others.
- Do not add numerical cost, temperature, reliability, or yield values.
- Do not reproduce or closely imitate the composition of a textbook, manufacturer, or online reference figure.
- Do not add a footer legend, explanatory paragraph, or caption inside the artwork.
- Do not include arrows or components that appear to float without a clear physical or conceptual relationship.

### Technical verification sources

- earlier Chapter 1 instructional materials, used to verify the intended book framing that packaging affects performance, reliability, manufacturability, cost, and product operation.
- Rao R. Tummala, *Fundamentals of Device and Systems Packaging: Technologies and Applications*, used to verify the multidisciplinary and system-level nature of packaging decisions.
- Rao R. Tummala, *Fundamentals of Microsystems Packaging*, used to verify the coupling among electrical, thermal, mechanical, materials, manufacturing, and reliability considerations.
- Glenn R. Blackwell, ed., *The Electronic Packaging Handbook*, used to verify the concurrent-engineering and design/manufacturing/test perspective.

These sources are for technical verification only. The figure composition must be independently created for this OER.

Create the figure from the underlying engineering relationships described above. Do not trace, closely redraw, or preserve the distinctive arrangement of any source figure. Use a new composition with a generic central electronic assembly and independently arranged outcome callouts.
