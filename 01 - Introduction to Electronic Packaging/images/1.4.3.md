## Suggested Content for Figure 1.4.3

**Instructional purpose:**  
Help beginners understand the key BJT switching idea:

> **A small base current controls a larger collector-emitter current.**

This figure should build on the earlier BJT structure figure by focusing specifically on **NPN transistor operation**. It should visually explain:

1. what happens when the base is **not driven**;
2. what happens when a **small base current** is applied;
3. why the transistor can be treated as a switch;
4. that the **base current controls** the larger collector-emitter current but does **not supply the electrical energy** for it.

**Filename:**  
`1.4.3 - How Base Current Controls an NPN Transistor.png`

**Intended width:** 900 px in Markdown

**Recommended aspect ratio:** 16:9

### Overall layout

Use a clean two-panel comparison:

- **Left panel:** `OFF`
- **Right panel:** `ON`

The two panels should use the **same NPN transistor geometry** so students can easily see what changes.

A conceptual engineering-diagram style is preferred.

Do **not** embed an overall title or figure number inside the artwork.

---

### Required content

#### Panel 1 – OFF state

Show an **NPN transistor** in an approximate OFF state.

Required ideas:

- `Base not driven` or `Base current ≈ 0`
- transistor approximately `OFF`
- `Very small collector-emitter current`
- the collector-emitter current path should appear weak, blocked, or nearly inactive

Use clear labels:

- `Collector (C)`
- `Base (B)`
- `Emitter (E)`

The OFF panel should communicate that without sufficient base-emitter drive, the transistor does not strongly conduct through the collector-emitter path.

---

#### Panel 2 – ON state

Show the same **NPN transistor** in an approximate ON state.

Required ideas:

- `Small base current`
- `Base-emitter junction forward biased` or equivalent beginner-friendly phrasing
- `Larger collector-emitter current`
- transistor approximately `ON`

Again label:

- `Collector (C)`
- `Base (B)`
- `Emitter (E)`

The ON panel should make it visually obvious that:

- a **small current enters the base**;
- a **larger current flows through the collector-emitter path**.

If arrows are used, the larger collector-emitter current arrow should be visibly more prominent than the base-current arrow.

---

### Required conceptual message

The figure must reinforce all of the following:

- the **base** is the control terminal;
- the **collector-emitter path** is the main current path;
- a **small base current** can enable a **larger collector-emitter current**;
- the transistor is therefore useful as a **switch**;
- the base current does **not** act as the power source for the larger current.

A central callout or bottom note may say:

`Small base current controls larger collector-emitter current`

A second concise note may say:

`The collector circuit provides the main current path.`

These should be short and highly legible.

---

### Optional structure support

If space permits, the transistor may be shown using either:

- a simplified **circuit-symbol-based view**, or
- a simplified **semiconductor-structure-based view**, or
- a hybrid approach combining the symbol with a simple conceptual current explanation.

For this figure, the priority is **operating concept**, not detailed semiconductor cross-section.

Because Figure 1.4.2 already introduces NPN/PNP structure, this new figure should focus more on **how current control works** than on region-by-region device anatomy.

### Technical requirements

The final figure must satisfy all of the following:

- it must clearly show an **NPN** transistor, not PNP;
- the **base** must be the control terminal;
- the figure must distinguish between:
  - **small base current**, and
  - **larger collector-emitter current**;
- the OFF state must show **very small** collector-emitter current, not necessarily exactly zero;
- the ON state must show that the transistor allows substantial collector-emitter current;
- the figure should support the explanation that the collector-emitter current depends on the external circuit and the transistor state;
- the visual logic must not imply that the base current itself becomes the entire collector current;
- if current arrows are shown, they must be technically reasonable for an NPN transistor.

---

### Prohibited or misleading elements

- Do not imply that the transistor is an ideal mechanical switch.
- Do not imply that the base current is the sole source of the collector-emitter current.
- Do not imply that the collector and emitter are interchangeable.
- Do not make the base current appear larger than the collector-emitter current.
- Do not show the transistor as only a packaged PCB part if that distracts from the device operation concept.
- Do not overload the figure with semiconductor equations or advanced carrier-transport details.
- Do not include decorative art, unnecessary icons, or a large embedded title.

---

### Visual style

- large readable labels;
- strong visual contrast between OFF and ON;
- enough whitespace for 900 px mdBook readability;
- no embedded figure number or overall title.
