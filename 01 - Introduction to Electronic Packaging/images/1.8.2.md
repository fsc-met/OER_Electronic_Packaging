## Suggested Content for Figure 1.8.2

**Instructional purpose:**  
Show the core repeated fabrication cycle used throughout semiconductor manufacturing.

The figure should help students understand that IC fabrication is not a single one-way list of steps. Instead, many layers and device regions are created through repeated cycles of:

> **form a layer -> coat photoresist -> define a pattern -> etch or modify selected regions -> remove resist / clean -> inspect -> repeat**

This figure should focus on **process logic and pattern transfer**, not on one specific transistor recipe.

**Filename:**  
`1.8.2 - Repeated Patterning and Material Processing Cycle.png`

**Intended width:** 900 px in Markdown

**Recommended aspect ratio:** 16:9

---

### Overall layout

Use a clean **six-panel horizontal sequence** or **circular repeated-cycle layout**.

Recommended six-panel sequence:

1. Form / deposit a material layer
2. Coat photoresist
3. Lithography: expose and develop
4. Etch **or** dope through openings
5. Strip resist and clean
6. Inspect / measure -> repeat

Use the **same wafer cross-section geometry** in each panel so students can directly compare what changes from step to step.

At the end, include a curved arrow back toward the first panel labeled:

`Repeat for the next feature or layer`

The figure should not imply that every cycle uses identical materials or parameters.

---

### Panel 1 – Form or deposit material layer

Show:

- silicon substrate;
- one thin added layer on top.

Required labels:

- `1. Form / deposit layer`
- `Silicon`
- `New material layer`

Optional note:

- `Dielectric, conductor, or process layer`

---

### Panel 2 – Coat photoresist

Show:

- the same underlying wafer structure;
- a photoresist layer coating the top surface.

Required labels:

- `2. Coat photoresist`
- `Photoresist`

The resist should be visually distinct from the underlying process layer.

---

### Panel 3 – Lithography and development

Show:

- patterned reticle / mask above the wafer;
- exposure direction;
- developed resist with selected openings.

Required labels:

- `3. Expose and develop`
- `Reticle / mask`
- `Patterned photoresist`

Optional label:

- `Lithography`

The resist openings should clearly define where later processing will act.

---

### Panel 4 – Selective processing

Split the panel conceptually into two possible outcomes or use a branch label.

Option A: **Etching**
- exposed material removed through resist openings.

Option B: **Doping / implantation**
- dopant ions enter exposed semiconductor regions.

Required labels:

- `4. Selective processing`
- `Etch`
- `or`
- `Dope / implant`

Optional label:

- `Pattern controls where processing occurs`

Do not imply that etching and implantation must always occur in the same cycle.

---

### Panel 5 – Strip resist and clean

Show:

- photoresist removed;
- patterned or modified underlying structure remaining.

Required labels:

- `5. Strip resist and clean`
- `Patterned / modified structure remains`

The result should visibly preserve the process effect created in Panel 4.

---

### Panel 6 – Inspect / measure and repeat

Show:

- simple optical / metrology icon or measurement lines;
- the completed local feature ready for the next fabrication cycle.

Required labels:

- `6. Inspect / measure`
- `Repeat for next feature or layer`

Optional label:

- `Metrology / process control`

---

### Required technical relationships

The figure must correctly show:

- a layer exists before it can be patterned;
- photoresist is a temporary process material, not part of the final circuit structure by default;
- lithography defines where later processing acts;
- etching removes selected exposed material;
- doping modifies selected semiconductor regions;
- resist is removed after its pattern-transfer role is complete;
- cleaning and inspection are recurring process activities;
- the cycle repeats many times using different materials and masks.

---

### Important distinction to show

Use one short note such as:

`Lithography defines the location; later processing creates or modifies the physical structure.`

This should be concise and secondary.

It helps prevent the common misconception that exposure alone physically forms the final transistor or interconnect.

---

### Spatial relationships

- Keep all six wafer cross-sections aligned.
- Maintain the same scale and orientation.
- Use clear arrows between panels.
- Do not change the wafer geometry unnecessarily.
- Use one return arrow from the final panel back toward the beginning to emphasize repetition.
- Keep labels outside dense cross-sectional regions where possible.

---

### Color restrictions

Suggested visual convention:

- silicon: pale gray / lavender;
- new material layer: pale blue / yellow;
- photoresist: muted magenta / purple;
- removed region: white / background;
- doped region: warm or cool accent;
- etch / ion arrows: neutral high-contrast colors.

Do not rely on color alone. Use labels and shape changes.

---

### Suggested visual style

- clean 2D engineering cross-sections;
- no photorealistic fab equipment;
- white or very light background;
- consistent layer thickness conventions;
- concise labels;
- strong panel-to-panel alignment;
- no overall title inside artwork;
- no logos or watermarks.

---

### Prohibited or misleading elements

Do not:

- show photoresist remaining permanently in the finished structure;
- imply lithography alone creates the final device;
- show ion implantation as metal deposition;
- show etching removing material underneath protected regions;
- imply every cycle includes both etch and implantation;
- imply one exact number of repeated cycles;
- introduce process-specific dimensions or chemistry;
- use a transistor cross-section so detailed that students mistake the figure for a universal MOSFET process recipe.

### Final caption

*Figure 1.8.2 – Many IC structures are created through repeated pattern-transfer cycles. A layer is formed, photoresist defines where processing should occur, material is selectively etched or semiconductor regions are modified, and the wafer is cleaned before another cycle begins.*

### Alt-text intent

Describe a repeated six-step wafer-processing cycle in which a material layer is formed, photoresist is applied, lithography patterns the resist, selected regions are etched or doped, the resist is removed and the wafer is cleaned, and the result is inspected before the cycle repeats.
