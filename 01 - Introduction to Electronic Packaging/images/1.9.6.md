## Suggested Content for Figure 1.9.6

**Instructional purpose:**  
Give readers a high-level manufacturing overview of how a **tested bare die becomes a packaged IC component**.

The figure must show that IC packaging is **not one universal linear recipe**.

The central flow should be:

> **tested bare die -> die attach -> internal interconnection -> protection -> external-terminal formation -> singulation where required -> marking / inspection / final test -> packaged IC**

The internal-interconnection stage must branch into **wire bond OR flip-chip**, rather than showing them as mandatory sequential operations.

**Intended width:** 900 px in Markdown

### Required technical relationships

The figure must correctly communicate that:

- packaging begins after semiconductor fabrication;
- die attach precedes the internal electrical interconnection for conventional die-first flows;
- wire bond and flip-chip are alternative interconnection routes;
- protection follows internal assembly in many conventional flows;
- external terminal formation depends on package family;
- singulation may occur at different points and is not always required in the same way;
- final inspection and test occur after major package construction;
- wafer-level packaging can deviate from this conventional die-first sequence;
- the final output is a packaged component, not a PCB.
