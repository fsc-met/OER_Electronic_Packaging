## Suggested Content for Figure 1.8.3

**Instructional purpose:**  
Show how microscopic semiconductor devices near the wafer surface become a complete integrated circuit through contacts, vias, and multiple metal-interconnect layers.

This figure should connect the earlier Chapter 1 device discussions to the fabrication process by showing the physical hierarchy:

> **semiconductor device structures -> contacts -> local interconnect -> multiple metal layers -> I/O interface**

The figure should also visually support the FEOL / MOL / BEOL terminology introduced in Section 1.8.

**Filename:**  
`1.8.3 - Building Devices and Multilevel Metal Interconnects.png`

**Intended width:** 900 px in Markdown

**Recommended aspect ratio:** 16:9

---

### Overall layout

Use one clean **conceptual IC cross-section** spanning the width of the figure.

The cross-section should contain three clearly distinguished vertical zones:

1. **FEOL** – semiconductor device region near the silicon surface
2. **MOL** – contacts and local device connections
3. **BEOL** – multiple dielectric-separated metal-interconnect layers

At the top, show:

- passivation;
- one I/O pad opening.

Use subtle brackets or side labels for FEOL / MOL / BEOL.

Do not draw a PCB underneath or around the die.

---

### FEOL region – semiconductor devices

At the bottom, show:

- silicon substrate / body;
- several representative transistor structures near the surface;
- optional integrated resistor or capacitor structure if space permits;
- device isolation between neighboring devices.

Required labels:

- `Silicon`
- `Integrated transistors`
- `Device isolation`

Optional labels:

- `Integrated passive structure`
- `Source / drain regions`
- `Gate`

Keep the transistor geometry conceptual and consistent with earlier Section 1.4 figures.

Do not attempt to depict a specific modern process node.

---

### MOL region – contacts and local connections

Directly above the devices, show:

- contact plugs connecting device terminals upward;
- local interconnect elements.

Required labels:

- `Contacts`
- `Local interconnect`

Optional bracket:

- `MOL`

The contacts must physically connect device regions to the first wiring level.

Do not leave contacts floating.

---

### BEOL region – multilevel interconnect

Show at least three or four metal layers separated by dielectric.

Required elements:

- metal lines on each level;
- vertical vias between selected levels;
- some long/wide upper-level conductors;
- dielectric separating adjacent metal layers.

Required labels:

- `Metal 1`
- `Metal 2`
- `Upper metal`
- `Vias`
- `Interlayer dielectric`

Optional bracket:

- `BEOL`

The figure should make clear that metal layers may pass above one another while remaining isolated unless a via intentionally connects them.

---

### Planarization indication

At one or more layer interfaces, use straight aligned horizontal surfaces and a small note:

- `Planarized surface`

Optional note:

- `CMP helps maintain surface flatness`

Do not make CMP a separate dominant process diagram.

---

### Top surface – passivation and I/O interface

Show:

- final passivation layer;
- an intentional opening above one pad;
- exposed I/O pad.

Required labels:

- `Passivation`
- `I/O pad`

Optional note:

- `Connection to later packaging level`

The I/O pad should be visibly connected through the interconnect stack to the device circuitry below.

---

### Required technical relationships

The figure must communicate correctly that:

- active semiconductor devices exist near the wafer surface;
- contacts connect device terminals to the wiring system;
- multiple metal layers are separated by dielectric;
- vias connect selected metal levels;
- upper interconnect layers can distribute signals, power, and ground over longer distances;
- passivation protects the top surface but leaves selected I/O regions accessible;
- the I/O pad is the boundary to the later IC-packaging interconnection;
- IC metal layers are not PCB copper layers.

---

### FEOL / MOL / BEOL terminology

If shown, use the terms carefully:

- `FEOL – device formation`
- `MOL – contacts / local connections`
- `BEOL – multilevel metal interconnect`

Include a small note:

`Terminology boundaries can vary by process.`

Keep this secondary.

---

### Spatial relationships

- Silicon at bottom.
- Devices immediately at or near silicon surface.
- Contacts directly above device terminals.
- Metal levels stacked upward.
- Vias vertical between selected levels.
- Passivation at top.
- I/O pad opening at the top interface.

Use a clear vertical hierarchy so readers can understand how the IC becomes a three-dimensional interconnect structure.

---

### Color restrictions

Suggested convention:

- silicon: pale gray / lavender;
- N-type / P-type regions: restrained cool / warm accents;
- gate / local conductor: dark gray;
- contacts and vias: metallic neutral;
- metal layers: muted gold / copper;
- dielectric: pale blue / cream;
- passivation: subtle green / blue;
- I/O pad: slightly stronger metallic accent.

Do not rely on color alone.

---

### Suggested visual style

- clean engineering-textbook cross-section;
- flat 2D presentation;
- consistent line weights;
- large readable labels;
- no decorative perspective;
- no photorealistic semiconductor micrograph;
- no package body;
- no PCB;
- no overall title inside artwork.

---

### Prohibited or misleading elements

Do not:

- draw PCB traces as the die interconnect;
- place miniature packaged parts inside the die;
- show every metal layer electrically connected to every other layer;
- show vias where no connection is intended;
- imply one universal number of metal layers;
- imply FEOL / MOL / BEOL boundaries are identical across all fabs;
- show passivation as a hermetic package seal;
- show an I/O pad disconnected from internal circuitry.

---
