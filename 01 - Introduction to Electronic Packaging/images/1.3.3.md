## Suggested Content for Figure 1.3.3

**Instructional purpose:**  
Show how a small number of donor or acceptor impurity atoms changes the mobile carrier population in silicon. The figure should help students distinguish mobile electrons/holes from the fixed ionized dopant atoms left in the crystal.

**Filename:** `1.3.3 - Doping Silicon to Create N-Type and P-Type Material.png`  
**Intended width:** 850 px in Markdown  
**Aspect ratio:** 16:9

### Objects/components to show

Create three side-by-side lattice-style conceptual panels:

1. **Intrinsic silicon**
   - repeating silicon atoms with four covalent bonds;
   - no intentional dopant highlighted.
2. **N-type silicon**
   - one silicon site replaced conceptually by a phosphorus donor;
   - four bonds maintained;
   - one extra mobile electron shown nearby;
   - fixed donor site shown as positively ionized after donation.
3. **P-type silicon**
   - one silicon site replaced conceptually by a boron acceptor;
   - one missing bond electron represented as a mobile hole;
   - fixed acceptor site shown as negatively ionized after accepting an electron.

The atomic layout is conceptual and should not imply exact bond lengths or dopant concentrations.

### Required labels

- Intrinsic silicon
- N-type
- P-type
- Silicon (Si)
- Donor (P)
- Acceptor (B)
- Mobile electron
- Mobile hole
- Fixed positive donor ion
- Fixed negative acceptor ion

### Required relationships / arrows / states

- In the N-type panel, the donor must be associated with an additional mobile electron.
- In the P-type panel, the acceptor must be associated with a mobile hole.
- Fixed dopant ions must remain at lattice sites.
- Do not depict N-type silicon as carrying a net negative bulk charge or P-type silicon as carrying a net positive bulk charge.

### Spatial relationships

- Use the same lattice geometry and scale in all three panels.
- Keep the dopant atom near the center of the N-type/P-type panels.
- Place the mobile electron/hole close enough to show the relationship but not as if permanently attached to the dopant.
- Keep labels outside dense bond geometry when possible.

### Color restrictions

- White/light neutral background.
- Silicon atoms one consistent neutral color.
- Donor and acceptor atoms may use two restrained accent colors.
- Use a consistent symbol/color for electrons throughout the chapter.
- Represent holes differently from positive ions so they cannot be confused.

### Suggested visual style

- Clean textbook lattice schematic.
- Conceptual 2D bonding representation.
- No quantum orbital clouds or energy-band diagrams.
- No embedded overall title.

### Prohibited or misleading elements

- Do not show the mobile electron as an extra proton or as a fifth covalent bond.
- Do not show a hole as a physical positively charged particle inserted into the lattice.
- Do not show the donor/acceptor ions moving through the crystal.
- Do not imply that every silicon atom is replaced by a dopant.
- Do not use wording such as "N-type is negatively charged" or "P-type is positively charged."

### Technical verification sources

- OpenStax, *University Physics Volume 3*, section on semiconductors and doping.
- Rao R. Tummala, *Fundamentals of Microsystems Packaging*, semiconductor-material/device introduction.

### Copyright/originality note

Create a new lattice composition based on the underlying doping concept. Do not trace a textbook's silicon-bond diagram.

### Final caption

*Figure 1.3.3 – Doping silicon with donor or acceptor impurities changes the dominant mobile carrier. N-type silicon has electrons as majority carriers, while P-type silicon has holes as majority carriers.*

### Alt-text intent

Describe intrinsic silicon, N-type silicon with a phosphorus donor and mobile electron, and P-type silicon with a boron acceptor and mobile hole, while fixed ionized dopants remain at lattice sites.
