## Suggested Content for Figure 1.8.1

**Instructional purpose:**  
Give readers a first visual overview of the entire integrated-circuit fabrication sequence, beginning with a **prepared semiconductor wafer** and ending with **individual bare IC dies**.

The figure should be especially accessible to readers who may not yet know what a wafer is. It should establish the physical scale and sequence before the section discusses individual fabrication processes in more detail.

The figure should reinforce these ideas:

- a wafer is a thin circular semiconductor manufacturing platform;
- many die locations are processed together on the wafer;
- IC fabrication is based on repeated material formation, patterning, etching, doping, cleaning, and inspection;
- active devices are formed near the wafer surface;
- multiple metal interconnect layers are then built above the devices;
- wafer-level testing occurs before singulation in the simplified sequence used here;
- dicing separates the completed wafer into individual bare dies;
- the output of this section is a **bare IC die**, not a PCB and not yet a normal board-mountable packaged IC.

**Filename:**  
`1.8.1 - From Semiconductor Wafer to Individual IC Dies.png`

**Intended width:** 900 px in Markdown

**Recommended aspect ratio:** 16:9

---

### Overall layout

Use a clear **left-to-right process overview** with approximately 8 to 10 visually distinct stages.

Recommended flow:

1. Prepared semiconductor wafer
2. Die locations / repeated fields
3. Dielectric or material layer formation
4. Photolithography
5. Etching and/or doping
6. Repeated device-building cycles
7. Multilevel metal interconnect
8. Passivation and I/O pads
9. Wafer probe / wafer map
10. Dicing into bare IC dies

The middle steps should visually communicate that several operations are **repeated**, not performed only once.

A loop or callout such as:

`Repeated patterning and processing cycles`

may span the middle fabrication stages.

Do not present the figure as if one pass through each step is sufficient to fabricate an IC.

---

### Stage 1 – Prepared semiconductor wafer

Show:

- a circular wafer;
- a clean polished surface;
- a subtle flat/notch if desired;
- no finished devices yet.

Required label:

- `Prepared semiconductor wafer`

Optional secondary label:

- `Silicon wafer`

Do not imply that the wafer is a PCB.

---

### Stage 2 – Repeated die locations

Show the same wafer with a grid of repeated rectangular die areas.

Required labels:

- `Many die locations`
- `Repeated IC pattern`

The rectangles should remain part of the wafer. Do not depict them as already separated chips.

---

### Stage 3 – Material / dielectric formation

Use a simplified zoomed cross-section or wafer segment.

Show:

- silicon base;
- one newly formed thin layer on top.

Required label:

- `Form / deposit material layer`

Optional labels:

- `Dielectric`
- `Conductive or process layer`

Keep this generic because different fabrication steps use different materials.

---

### Stage 4 – Photolithography

Show:

- photoresist coating on top;
- a patterned reticle/mask above the wafer;
- exposure indication;
- resulting patterned resist after development.

Required label:

- `Photolithography defines the pattern`

Optional sublabels:

- `Photoresist`
- `Reticle / mask`
- `Expose and develop`

Do not imply that lithography itself creates the complete transistor.

---

### Stage 5 – Etching and / or doping

Show two conceptual outcomes or one split icon:

- selective material removal through patterned openings;
- selective dopant introduction into exposed semiconductor regions.

Required labels:

- `Etch selected regions`
- `Dope selected semiconductor regions`

Optional label:

- `Ion implantation / diffusion`

Do not depict doping as metal deposition.

---

### Stage 6 – Repeated device-building cycles

Show a more developed wafer cross-section with representative device structures near the surface.

Required label:

- `Repeat to build device structures`

Optional examples:

- transistor regions;
- integrated resistor / capacitor structures.

The figure should make clear that device structures emerge from repeated cycles of:

`form layer -> pattern -> etch / modify -> clean -> repeat`

---

### Stage 7 – Multilevel metal interconnect

Show:

- contacts above devices;
- several dielectric-separated metal layers;
- vias connecting metal layers.

Required label:

- `Build multilevel metal interconnect`

Optional sublabels:

- `Contacts`
- `Vias`
- `Metal layers`

The interconnect should look microscopic and layered, not like PCB traces.

---

### Stage 8 – Passivation and I/O pads

Show:

- top protective layer;
- openings at selected external pad locations;
- visible bond / bump pad regions.

Required labels:

- `Passivation`
- `I/O pads`

Optional note:

- `Protects die surface`

Do not show a molded IC package at this stage.

---

### Stage 9 – Wafer probe

Return to a wafer-level view.

Show:

- a probe system contacting one die location;
- a small wafer map identifying passing and failing dies.

Required labels:

- `Wafer probe`
- `Wafer map`

Optional labels:

- `Pass`
- `Fail`

Do not imply wafer probe guarantees lifetime reliability.

---

### Stage 10 – Dicing / singulation

Show:

- scribe lanes between die locations;
- conceptual saw / laser / singulation indication;
- several separated rectangular bare dies.

Required labels:

- `Dicing / singulation`
- `Bare IC dies`

At the far right, add a small transition note:

- `Next: IC packaging`

This final note should be secondary but clearly visible.

---

### Required technical relationships

The figure must communicate all of the following correctly:

- fabrication begins with a wafer, not an individual packaged chip;
- die locations exist on the wafer before singulation;
- patterning and material-processing operations are repeated many times;
- photolithography defines where later processing acts;
- etching removes selected material;
- doping modifies selected semiconductor regions;
- device structures are created near the semiconductor surface;
- contacts, vias, and metal layers connect devices into circuits;
- passivation protects the finished die surface while leaving I/O areas accessible;
- wafer-level testing occurs before dicing in the simplified flow shown here;
- dicing produces individual **bare dies**;
- bare dies still require IC packaging or another controlled die-level assembly method.

  - start = circular wafer;
  - end = individual bare dies.

### Color restrictions

Use a restrained engineering-textbook palette.

Suggested convention:

- silicon / wafer: light gray, muted blue, or pale lavender;
- dielectric layers: pale neutral / light yellow;
- semiconductor doped regions: restrained warm/cool tints;
- metal interconnect: muted gold / copper;
- photoresist: restrained magenta / purple;
- pass/fail wafer map: use distinct but muted symbols and labels, not color alone.

Do not rely on red/green alone for pass/fail interpretation.

Avoid:

- dramatic gradients;
- glow effects;
- photorealistic wafer reflections;
- marketing-style imagery.

---

### Suggested visual style

- clean engineering-textbook process infographic;
- flat 2D or restrained isometric style;
- concise sans-serif labels;
- consistent line weights;
- clear process arrows;
- simple cross-sectional insets;
- no logos;
- no watermarks;
- no figure number inside the artwork.

---

### Prohibited or misleading elements

Do not:

- show an IC package before the dicing step;
- show a PCB as part of wafer fabrication;
- show the wafer becoming a PCB;
- imply one universal fabrication recipe;
- imply all process steps occur only once;
- assign one fixed number of metal layers;
- show every die as guaranteed good;
- show wafer probe as a complete reliability qualification;
- show dicing before the wafer is fully processed;
- use modern commercial logos, fab equipment brands, or recognizable product artwork.
