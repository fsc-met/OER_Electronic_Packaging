## Suggested Content for Figure 1.10.2

**Instructional purpose:**  
Provide a simple physical comparison between **surface-mount technology (SMT)** and **through-hole technology (THT)** at the PCB-assembly level.

The figure should teach the difference through the actual geometry of the board-level connection:

- **SMT:** component terminals join to pads on the PCB surface;
- **THT:** component leads pass through plated holes and are soldered to the plated-hole structure.

The figure should reinforce that SMT and THT are **board-level assembly approaches**, not IC package families.

**Filename:**  
`1.10.2 - Surface Mount and Through Hole Assembly Concepts.png`

**Intended width:** 900 px in Markdown

**Recommended aspect ratio:** 16:9

---

### Overall layout

Use a simple **side-by-side comparison**.

Recommended arrangement:

- **Left:** SMT cross-section
- **Right:** THT cross-section

Both sides should use the same conceptual PCB style so students can compare the mounting geometry directly.

A small bottom note may say:

`Many PCBAs use mixed SMT/THT assembly.`

Do **not** place the figure number, external caption, or an oversized overall title inside the artwork.

---

### Left side – Surface-Mount Technology (SMT)

Show a simplified PCB cross-section containing:

- PCB substrate / laminate;
- copper surface pads;
- optional solder mask around the pads;
- one generic surface-mount component;
- solder joints between component terminals and surface pads.

Required labels:

- `SMT`
- `Surface-mount component`
- `PCB surface pad`
- `Solder joint`

Optional label:

- `Solder mask`

The component must sit **on the PCB surface**.

No component lead should pass through the board.

---

### SMT component choice

Use one generic and easily understood surface-mount form.

Preferred options:

- chip resistor/capacitor;
- small leaded IC package;
- QFN-like package.

A chip resistor/capacitor is preferred for clarity because it makes the surface-only connection easy to see.

Do not use a BGA unless hidden joints are necessary to the teaching purpose. The main goal is SMT-versus-THT geometry, not package inspection.

---

### Right side – Through-Hole Technology (THT)

Show a simplified PCB cross-section containing:

- PCB substrate / laminate;
- plated through hole;
- copper annular pad;
- one component lead passing completely through the hole;
- solder joining the lead to the plated-hole structure.

Required labels:

- `THT`
- `Component lead`
- `Plated through hole`
- `Solder joint`

Optional label:

- `Annular pad`

The lead must visibly pass through the PCB.

---

### THT component choice

Use a simple representative part such as:

- axial resistor;
- radial component;
- connector pin;
- simple leaded device.

A resistor or connector pin is preferred for clarity.

Do not imply that THT assembly must be performed manually.

---

### Required technical relationships

The figure must correctly show that:

- SMT terminals remain on the PCB surface;
- THT leads pass through plated holes;
- solder forms the electrical and mechanical connection in both representative cases;
- the PCB is the same type of board-level interconnection platform on both sides;
- SMT and THT are assembly/mounting approaches, not package families;
- both methods can appear on the same PCBA.

---

### Optional small comparison labels

If space permits, add one concise line below each panel.

Under SMT:

- `Terminal stays on the surface`

Under THT:

- `Lead passes through the board`

These are optional but useful if they do not crowd the cross-sections.

### Optional mixed-assembly note

A small centered note may be included:

> `Many PCBAs use both SMT and THT.`

Do not add a third full panel unless necessary.

---

### Spatial relationships

For SMT:

- component above board;
- terminals aligned to surface pads;
- solder directly between terminal and pad;
- no through-hole lead.

For THT:

- component body above board;
- lead extends downward through the board;
- plated barrel surrounds the lead;
- solder is visible at the board/lead interface.

Keep both PCB cross-sections at similar scale.

---

### Color restrictions

Suggested:

- PCB laminate: muted green / blue-green;
- copper pads / plated holes: copper/gold;
- solder: metallic gray;
- component body: neutral gray;
- component lead/terminal: metallic gray;
- labels/arrows: navy or dark neutral.

Do not rely on color alone; the geometry must communicate the difference.

---

### Suggested visual style

- clean conceptual PCB cross-section;
- flat 2D engineering illustration;
- readable labels;
- moderate line weights;
- restrained colors;
- white/light background;
- no photorealism;
- no commercial markings;
- no figure title/caption inside artwork.

---

### Prohibited or misleading elements

Do not:

- show SMT component leads passing through the board;
- show a THT component only touching surface pads;
- depict the THT hole as unplated;
- imply all SMT components use the same package family;
- imply all THT assembly is hand soldered;
- imply SMT and THT are mutually exclusive on one PCBA;
- add reflow-profile details;
- add wave-solder-machine details;
- add detailed solder metallurgy;
- add acceptance criteria or defect examples;
- turn this into a manufacturing-process flowchart.
