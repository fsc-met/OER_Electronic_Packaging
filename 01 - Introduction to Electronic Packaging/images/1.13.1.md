## Suggested Content for Figure 1.13.1

**Figure title for authoring/reference only:**  
`Multiple Functions Performed by an Electronic Package`

**Instructional purpose:**  
Show that electronic packaging is not responsible for only one physical task. A single packaging structure or assembly often performs several functions at the same time.

This figure supports the central Section 1.13 idea:

> **One packaging structure often performs several functions simultaneously.**

The figure should help students connect the major packaging functions discussed in Section 1.13:

- electrical interconnection;
- thermal management;
- mechanical support;
- environmental protection;
- manufacturability;
- inspection / testability;
- reliability / serviceability.

The visual should also reinforce that **cost is an important design constraint**, but cost is not a physical packaging function in the same sense as electrical, thermal, mechanical, or protective functions.

The figure should prepare students for Section 1.14 by making clear that these functions interact and therefore require input from multiple engineering disciplines.

**Filename:**  
`1.13.1 - Multiple Functions Performed by an Electronic Package.png`

**Intended width:** 900 px in Markdown

**Recommended aspect ratio:** 16:9

---

### Overall layout

Use a clean **central-assembly with surrounding functional callouts** layout.

Recommended structure:

- **Center:** one generic representative electronic packaging assembly.
- **Around the center:** seven functional categories arranged in a balanced radial or semi-radial pattern.
- **Outside or around the seven functions:** a subtle secondary indication that `Cost` constrains the design.
- Use short connectors from the central assembly to each function.
- Do not show the functions as a one-way sequential process.
- Do not imply that one function is always more important than the others.

The central object should look like an original textbook engineering illustration rather than a commercial product.

Do **not** place the figure number, external caption, or an oversized overall title inside the artwork.

---

### Central representative packaging structure

Use one generic **electronic module / PCBA-based packaging assembly** that visually contains several packaging features without becoming overcrowded.

Recommended central elements:

- one PCB or PCBA;
- one packaged IC;
- visible solder joints or package-to-board attachment;
- PCB traces / vias;
- one connector;
- one simple mechanical support feature such as a standoff or bracket;
- one representative thermal path or heat-spreading feature;
- a partial enclosure / protective boundary or cover.

The central assembly should be conceptual and should not imply that every electronic product uses exactly this construction.

Recommended central label:

- `Representative packaging structure`

Optional smaller note:

- `One physical assembly can perform several functions`

Do not label the central assembly as a specific commercial package family or product.

---

### Function 1 - Electrical Interconnection

Required label:

- `Electrical interconnection`

Optional concise sublabel:

- `Signals • Power • Reference`

Use a connector line from this callout to an appropriate electrical feature in the central assembly, such as:

- PCB trace;
- solder joint;
- connector;
- package terminal.

The figure should communicate that packaging creates the physical paths used by electrical signals and power.

Do not add signal-integrity equations or detailed circuit diagrams.

---

### Function 2 - Thermal Management

Required label:

- `Thermal management`

Optional concise sublabel:

- `Heat transfer path`

Connect this callout to a physically meaningful thermal feature such as:

- packaged IC;
- exposed pad / heat spreader;
- PCB copper region;
- heat sink or enclosure heat path.

A few restrained heat-flow arrows may be used.

The thermal path should appear physically plausible.

Do not imply that every product requires a fan, heat sink, or liquid cooling.

---

### Function 3 - Mechanical Support

Required label:

- `Mechanical support`

Optional concise sublabel:

- `Support • Alignment • Load transfer`

Connect this callout to:

- PCB support;
- bracket;
- standoff;
- package attachment;
- enclosure mounting feature.

The figure should communicate that packaging maintains physical position and transfers loads.

Do not turn the figure into a stress-analysis diagram.

---

### Function 4 - Environmental Protection

Required label:

- `Environmental protection`

Optional concise sublabel:

- `Moisture • Dust • Contamination`

Connect this callout to a protective boundary such as:

- enclosure section;
- package body;
- coating / cover;
- sealed interface concept.

The visual should communicate protection from the intended environment without implying that every product is hermetically sealed.

Do not use dramatic weather icons or poster-style decorative effects.

---

### Function 5 - Manufacturability

Required label:

- `Manufacturability`

Optional concise sublabel:

- `Build repeatedly and efficiently`

Connect this callout to an assembly-relevant feature such as:

- component placement;
- solder joint;
- accessible board geometry;
- connector installation.

The concept should remain high level:

> the design must be practical to fabricate and assemble repeatedly.

Do not add detailed DFM rules, stencil geometry, solder-process parameters, or factory equipment.

---

### Function 6 - Inspection / Testability

Required label:

- `Inspection / testability`

Optional concise sublabel:

- `Verify assembly and function`

Connect this callout to:

- visible solder joints;
- test point;
- accessible connector;
- inspection region.

A very simple probe or inspection symbol may be used if it remains uncluttered.

Do not introduce detailed AOI, SPI, X-ray, or acceptance criteria.

---

### Function 7 - Reliability / Serviceability

Required label:

- `Reliability / serviceability`

Optional concise sublabel:

- `Survive life • Maintain where required`

Connect this callout to one or more interfaces that can become reliability critical, such as:

- solder joint;
- connector;
- mounting point;
- thermal interface.

The figure should communicate that packaging must continue to perform during product life and, where the product strategy requires it, allow maintenance or repair.

Do not imply that every product is designed for field repair.

---

### Cost as a surrounding design constraint

Show `Cost` as a **secondary constraint**, not as an equal physical function.

Recommended treatment:

- one subtle outer band;
- one small corner callout;
- or one restrained note below the functional ring.

Required label:

- `Cost constraint`

Optional concise sublabel:

- `Materials • Manufacturing • Test • Rework • Service`

The visual should communicate:

> packaging functions must be achieved within realistic cost and product requirements.

Do not make cost the dominant message.

Do not use currency symbols, dollar values, or marketing-style graphics.

---

### Required relationships

The figure must correctly communicate all of the following:

- one physical packaging assembly can perform several functions simultaneously;
- the seven function categories are interconnected rather than isolated;
- electrical interconnection, thermal management, mechanical support, and environmental protection are physical packaging functions;
- manufacturability and inspection/testability are important design and production requirements;
- reliability depends on the long-term performance of packaging structures and interfaces;
- serviceability is product dependent;
- cost constrains how packaging functions are implemented;
- changing one packaging feature can affect several functions at once.

### Color guidance

Use the established MET406 OER engineering-textbook style.

Suggested approach:

- white or very light background;
- central PCB / assembly: restrained green or blue-green;
- packaged IC: neutral dark gray;
- copper features: muted copper / gold;
- thermal feature: restrained warm accent;
- structural hardware: neutral gray;
- protective boundary: pale neutral / blue-gray;
- callout connectors: dark neutral or navy;
- functional callouts: restrained muted colors with similar visual weight.

Do not use bright rainbow or red-yellow-green status coding.

Do not rely on color alone. Labels, geometry, and connector relationships must remain understandable in grayscale.

- moderate abstraction;
- no photorealism;
- no commercial product;
- no logos;

---

### Prohibited or misleading elements

Do not:

- depict electronic packaging as only an enclosure;
- depict electronic packaging as only the IC package;
- imply every product requires the same thermal solution;
- imply every product requires hermetic sealing;
- imply every product must be field repairable;
- show detailed signal-integrity or power-integrity equations;
- show detailed thermal equations;
- show stress/strain calculations;
- show detailed DFM rules;
- show detailed inspection acceptance criteria;
- show reliability statistics;
- make cost appear as a physical packaging function equivalent to heat transfer or interconnection;
- present the seven functions as a chronological process;
- imply that improving one function automatically improves all others;
- use a recognizable commercial PCB, product, package, or company logo;
- reproduce or closely imitate a textbook, lecture-slide, manufacturer, or web figure;
- add long explanatory paragraphs inside the artwork;
- embed the external figure title, number, or caption inside the image.
