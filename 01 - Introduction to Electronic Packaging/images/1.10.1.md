## Suggested Content for Figure 1.10.1

**Instructional purpose:**  
Show that the **component stream** and the **PCB stream** are manufactured separately and only converge during **PCB assembly**.

The figure should reinforce one of the central manufacturing ideas in Chapter 1:

> **components + bare PCB -> PCB assembly -> PCBA**

The figure must make it visually impossible to confuse:

- semiconductor fabrication;
- IC packaging;
- PCB fabrication;
- PCB assembly.

**Filename:**  
`1.10.1 - Component and PCB Manufacturing Streams Converge at PCB Assembly.png`

**Intended width:** 900 px in Markdown

**Recommended aspect ratio:** 16:9

---

### Overall layout

Use a clean **two-stream convergence diagram**.

Recommended structure:

#### Upper stream – Component stream

`semiconductor fabrication -> IC die -> IC packaging -> packaged IC`

Also show representative separately manufactured board-level components joining this stream before PCB assembly, such as:

- resistor;
- capacitor;
- connector;
- discrete transistor or diode.

#### Lower stream – PCB stream

`PCB materials -> PCB fabrication -> bare PCB`

#### Convergence

Both streams should visibly merge only at:

`PCB assembly`

The final output should be:

`PCBA`

The convergence point should be visually strong and easy to understand.

Do **not** place the figure number, external caption, or an oversized overall title inside the artwork.

---

### Upper stream – Component manufacturing

Show four main stages.

#### 1. Semiconductor fabrication

Use a simple wafer/chip-fabrication icon or wafer-with-die-grid concept.

Required label:

- `Semiconductor fabrication`

Do not show PCB fabrication here.

#### 2. IC die

Show:

- one separated bare die;
- no package body;
- small die pads or fine connection features.

Required label:

- `IC die`

#### 3. IC packaging

Show a simplified packaging transformation or package assembly icon.

Required label:

- `IC packaging`

Do not show a PCB as part of the package.

#### 4. Packaged IC

Show a recognizable generic packaged IC, such as a small QFN/QFP/BGA-like component.

Required label:

- `Packaged IC`

Optional nearby examples:

- `Discrete components`
- `Connectors`

These additional components should appear as separate manufactured parts joining the component stream before PCB assembly.

---

### Lower stream – PCB manufacturing

Show three main stages.

#### 1. PCB materials

Show:

- copper-clad laminate or simplified laminate stack;
- no components.

Required label:

- `PCB materials`

#### 2. PCB fabrication

Show a simplified board-fabrication process icon or patterned copper-board concept.

Required label:

- `PCB fabrication`

#### 3. Bare PCB

Show:

- a clearly recognizable bare printed circuit board;
- copper pads/traces;
- plated holes or vias if useful;
- solder mask;
- no mounted electronic components.

Required label:

- `Bare PCB`

The bare PCB should look clearly different from the final PCBA.

---

### Convergence – PCB assembly

The two streams should converge at one clearly labeled stage:

- `PCB assembly`

Recommended visual:

- packaged IC, discrete components, and connector moving toward the bare PCB;
- a placement/joining icon;
- board being populated.

The convergence must communicate:

> **separately manufactured components + separately fabricated bare PCB**

not:

> **IC package becomes PCB**

or:

> **semiconductor fabrication produces PCB**

---

### Final output – PCBA

Show a populated PCB containing:

- packaged ICs;
- discrete components;
- connector;
- visible board traces/pads where appropriate.

Required label:

- `PCBA`

Optional note:

- `Populated and electrically interconnected PCB`

The PCBA should be visibly different from the bare PCB by showing mounted components.

---

### Required technical relationships

The figure must correctly communicate that:

- semiconductor fabrication produces the IC die;
- IC packaging produces the packaged IC component;
- PCB fabrication produces the bare PCB;
- discrete components and connectors are manufactured separately from the PCB;
- packaged components and the bare PCB remain separate until PCB assembly;
- PCB assembly physically and electrically connects components to the board;
- the result is a PCBA;
- a PCBA is not necessarily the finished electronic product.

---

### Spatial relationships

- Keep the **component stream** and **PCB stream** clearly separated before convergence.
- Use parallel horizontal or diagonal paths.
- Converge them only at `PCB assembly`.
- Use arrows with one clear direction.
- Avoid crossing arrows.
- Place `PCBA` after the convergence, not inside either manufacturing stream.
- Make the bare PCB and PCBA visually easy to compare.

---

### Color restrictions

Use a restrained engineering-textbook palette.

Suggested:

- semiconductor / dies: gray or muted blue;
- packaged components: dark neutral;
- copper features: muted copper/gold;
- PCB: restrained green or blue-green;
- arrows: navy;
- final PCBA: same PCB color as the bare PCB plus clearly visible components.

Do not rely on color alone to distinguish the two manufacturing streams. Use labels and spatial separation.

---

### Suggested visual style

- clean engineering-textbook infographic;

- concise labels;

- consistent line weights;

### Prohibited or misleading elements

Do not:

- draw the packaged IC transforming into the PCB;
- draw semiconductor fabrication feeding directly into PCB fabrication;
- show components already mounted on the bare PCB;
- show the bare PCB as a package substrate;
- imply PCB fabrication is part of IC packaging;
- imply the PCBA is automatically the complete finished product;
- add detailed Chapter 2 PCB-fabrication steps;
- add detailed Chapter 4 assembly-process steps;
- use brand-specific components or boards;
- add long explanatory paragraphs inside the artwork.

### Final caption

*Figure 1.10.1 – Electronic components and the bare PCB are manufactured in separate streams. They converge during PCB assembly, where components are attached and electrically connected to the board to create a PCBA.*

### Alt-text intent

Describe two separate manufacturing streams: a component stream progressing from semiconductor fabrication through IC die and IC packaging to packaged components, and a PCB stream progressing from PCB materials through PCB fabrication to a bare PCB. Both streams converge only at PCB assembly, producing a populated PCBA.
