# 1.0 - Chapter Overview

Electronic packaging is where an electronic design becomes a physical product.

A circuit may work correctly in a schematic or simulation, but a useful product must also be interconnected, supported, cooled, protected, manufactured, inspected, tested, and able to survive its intended environment. These physical requirements connect electronics to materials, manufacturing, thermal behavior, mechanics, reliability, cost, and product use.

Chapter 1 provides the foundation for the rest of this book. The goal is not to make the reader an expert in semiconductor physics, circuit design, or manufacturing. Instead, the chapter builds enough electrical and physical understanding to follow how electronic devices become packaged components, PCB assemblies, and complete products.

## What This Chapter Covers

The chapter develops the subject in four stages.

1. **Start with electronic packaging and why it matters.**  
   Sections 1.1 and 1.2 define the field and show why packaging decisions affect real products.

2. **Build the minimum electronics foundation needed for packaging.**  
   Sections 1.3 through 1.7 review basic electrical quantities, semiconductors, active and passive devices, circuits, logic, and integrated circuits at an applied level.

3. **Follow the physical manufacturing path.**  
   Sections 1.8 through 1.11 follow the semiconductor/component stream from wafer to bare die to packaged component, and the separate PCB stream that produces the bare board. These streams converge during PCB assembly to produce a printed circuit board assembly (PCBA), which can then be integrated into a finished product.

4. **Step back and view packaging as an engineering field.**  
   Sections 1.12 through 1.17 introduce the packaging hierarchy, major packaging functions, multidisciplinary engineering roles, product environments, and important industry trends.

The chapter closes with a summary and a set of practice problems for checking the major concepts.

## Chapter 1 Learning Objectives

After completing Chapter 1, you should be able to:

- define electronic packaging in practical engineering terms and distinguish it from an individual electronic package;
- distinguish electronic/circuit design from the physical realization of an electronic product;
- explain why packaging can affect product performance, manufacturability, cost, reliability, inspection, service, and product life;
- use basic electrical and semiconductor concepts to describe the devices and circuits that packaging must support;
- distinguish discrete devices, integrated devices, circuits, and integrated circuits at an introductory level;
- trace the physical progression from semiconductor wafer and die to packaged component, bare PCB, PCBA, and finished electronic product;
- distinguish semiconductor fabrication, IC packaging, PCB fabrication, PCB assembly, and system integration;
- describe the major functions and physical levels of electronic packaging and recognize the roles of different engineering disciplines and engineering technologists;
- explain why packaging priorities change with the product environment and recognize important industry trends at an introductory level.

## Chapter Path

A useful way to view Chapter 1 is:

> **Semiconductor/component stream:** wafer -> bare die -> packaged component  
> **PCB stream:** PCB materials -> PCB fabrication -> bare PCB  
> **Assembly and integration:** packaged/discrete components + bare PCB -> PCB assembly -> PCBA -> finished product

These pathways provide the vocabulary and physical picture used throughout the remaining chapters.

Section 1.1 begins by defining **electronic packaging** more precisely and separating the broader engineering field from the individual physical structures often called packages.
